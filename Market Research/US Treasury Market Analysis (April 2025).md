# US Treasury Market: Recent Performance, Causes, and Near-Term Outlook (April 2025)

**1. Executive Summary**

This report analyzes the recent performance of the US Treasury market, focusing on the events of April 2025. The market experienced a period of significant volatility, marked by a notable spike in Treasury yields in early April before some stabilization occurred later in the month. The primary catalyst for this turbulence was the announcement of new US tariffs, which triggered a complex set of market reactions. These reactions included technical adjustments by leveraged investors and growing concerns regarding inflation and government deficits. The near-term outlook for the Treasury market remains uncertain, with expectations of potential future interest rate adjustments by the Federal Reserve and continued sensitivity to evolving economic data and policy decisions. This report will also highlight the key risks and opportunities present in the current environment, as well as assess the prevailing investor sentiment towards US Treasury bonds.

**2. Recent Performance of the US Treasury Market**

-   Significant Yield Changes in April 2025:
    > In early April 2025, the US Treasury market initially responded to the announcement of new tariffs with a downward movement in yields.1 This initial reaction likely reflected concerns about the potential negative impact of tariffs on overall economic growth. Typically, when investors anticipate slower economic activity, there is an increased demand for safe-haven assets like US Treasury bonds. This increased demand pushes bond prices higher, which in turn causes yields to fall. This behavior was evident as the market absorbed the news of the impending trade restrictions.1
    > However, this initial downward trend in yields was short-lived. Starting around April 4th, the market witnessed a sharp and somewhat unexpected increase in longer-term Treasury yields, particularly the 10-year and 30-year maturities.<sup>1</sup> The yield on the benchmark 10-year Treasury note, which influences mortgage rates and other long-term borrowing costs, rose significantly, reaching a peak of approximately 4.5% during this period.<sup>1</sup> This increase in yields occurred despite the initial concerns about a potential economic slowdown, suggesting that other factors were beginning to exert a stronger influence on the market.<sup>1</sup> This counterintuitive rise in yields indicated that investors were not solely focused on recession risks but were also considering the potential inflationary consequences of the newly announced tariffs and other underlying market dynamics.<sup>1</sup>
    > Following this peak in early April, Treasury yields experienced some easing, particularly after President Trump announced a 90-day delay in the implementation of tariffs for certain countries.<sup>2</sup> This partial rollback of immediate trade war concerns likely provided some reassurance to the market, leading to a decrease in yields from their earlier highs. Furthermore, successful auctions of US Treasury bonds during this period also helped to stabilize the market.<sup>2</sup> Strong demand at these auctions indicated continued investor appetite for US government debt at the prevailing yield levels, which helped to prevent further significant increases in yields and contributed to the subsequent moderation.<sup>2</sup>

In this instance, the bear steepening suggested that the market was pricing in increased uncertainty and potentially higher inflation over the longer term as a result of the new trade policies.\[8\]

**3. Factors Influencing Recent Treasury Market Activity**

-   Impact of Macroeconomic Data Releases:
    > Concerns about rising inflation played a significant role in the increase in bond yields during April 2025.1 The announcement of tariffs raised expectations that the cost of imported goods would increase, potentially leading to higher consumer prices across the board.1 This anticipation of higher inflation erodes the real value of fixed-income investments, prompting investors to demand higher yields on Treasury bonds to compensate for this increased risk.1 Notably, the two-year breakeven inflation rate, which reflects the market's expectation of inflation over the next two years, saw a significant increase in early April, rising from 2.54% at the end of 2024 to 3.36% on April 8th.1 This indicates that short-term inflation worries were particularly pronounced following the tariff announcement.
    > While slower economic growth expectations typically lead to lower bond yields, the tariff shock introduced a layer of complexity by raising the specter of stagflation - a scenario characterized by slow growth coupled with high inflation.<sup>9</sup> Initial concerns about the negative impact of tariffs on economic activity likely contributed to the initial dip in yields.<sup>1</sup> However, as the potential for tariff-induced inflation became more apparent, the market's focus shifted, leading to the subsequent rise in yields.<sup>1</sup> The Federal Reserve itself acknowledged the potential for tariffs to contribute to higher inflation.<sup>12</sup>
    > Surveys of consumer and business sentiment also reflected the heightened economic uncertainty. The University of Michigan Consumer Sentiment Survey for April showed a significant plunge in economic expectations, reaching its lowest point since the early 1980s.<sup>9</sup> Similarly, the S&P Global Purchasing Manager Index (PMI) data for April suggested a moderating pace of economic expansion in the US.<sup>14</sup> This deterioration in sentiment likely amplified the volatility in the Treasury market as investors grappled with the implications of weaker economic prospects alongside the risk of rising prices.<sup>9</sup>

-   The Role of Federal Reserve Policy:
    > Throughout this period of Treasury market volatility, the Federal Reserve largely maintained a steady stance on interest rates, opting to wait and assess the full economic impact of the newly announced tariffs.9 At its March 2025 meeting, the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) kept the federal funds rate steady at 4.5%.12 Fed Chair Jerome Powell emphasized the central bank's intention to remain cautious and await greater clarity on the economic outlook before considering any further policy adjustments.11 This "wait-and-see" approach likely contributed to the market's uncertainty, as investors sought clearer signals from the central bank regarding its potential response to the evolving economic landscape.17
    > The Federal Reserve did announce a further slowing in the pace of its quantitative tightening (QT) policy, reducing the monthly cap on US Treasury redemptions to $5 billion starting in April, down from $25 billion.<sup>11</sup> This adjustment to QT, while not directly aimed at influencing short-term interest rates, could provide some support to the Treasury market by reducing the amount of government debt that the market needs to absorb each month.<sup>11</sup> By decreasing the supply of Treasury bonds coming onto the market from the Fed's balance sheet, this action could potentially help to limit further increases in yields, particularly during a period of heightened market sensitivity.<sup>11</sup>
    > The Federal Reserve's Financial Stability Report, released around this time, also highlighted concerns about low liquidity in the Treasury market, which had worsened further in April.<sup>18</sup> Low liquidity can amplify market volatility, making it more difficult for investors to execute trades without causing significant price movements.<sup>18</sup> This concern about market functioning likely contributed to the overall sense of unease in the Treasury market following the tariff announcement.<sup>2</sup>
    > In addition to its broader policy tools, the Federal Reserve Bank of New York announced plans to conduct small value operations in the secondary market for US Treasury securities between April 14th and May 13th, with a total planned amount of approximately $150 million.<sup>19</sup> These routine operations are primarily for maintaining operational readiness and are not intended to have a significant impact on overall market yields or prices.<sup>19</sup>

-   Influence of Global Economic and Political Events:
    > The announcement of new tariffs by President Trump on April 2nd served as the primary catalyst for the significant volatility observed in the US Treasury market during that month.1 The "harsher-than-expected" global tariff regime caught many investors by surprise, creating a wave of uncertainty across financial markets.1 The abrupt nature and broad scope of these tariffs raised immediate concerns about their potential to disrupt global trade, increase costs for businesses and consumers, and ultimately slow down economic growth.1
    > These tariff announcements also fueled concerns about potential retaliatory actions from other countries, which could further escalate trade tensions and negatively impact the demand for US Treasuries.<sup>1</sup> As foreign investors hold a significant portion of US government debt, any indication that these investors might reduce their holdings in response to trade disputes could put upward pressure on Treasury yields.<sup>20</sup> The possibility of a trade war leading to a decline in foreign demand for US assets was a key concern cited by market participants.<sup>8</sup>
    > The International Monetary Fund (IMF) also contributed to the overall economic outlook by slashing its forecast for global economic growth during this period.<sup>3</sup> This weaker global growth outlook, while typically a factor that could increase demand for safe-haven assets like US Treasuries, was overshadowed in this instance by the more immediate concerns related to US trade policy and its potential inflationary consequences.<sup>3</sup>

-   Technical Market Factors:
    > The sharp spike in Treasury yields in early April was likely exacerbated by technical factors within the market, particularly the unwinding of leveraged trading positions.2 Many investors, particularly "fast money" levered trading shops, had built up long positions in US Treasuries through strategies like swap spread wideners and basis trades.8 These trades involve taking long positions in cash bonds and short positions in related derivatives. The sudden increase in market volatility triggered by the tariff announcement led to losses on these positions and prompted margin calls, forcing investors to rapidly deleverage by selling their Treasury bond holdings.2 This forced selling added significant downward pressure on bond prices and upward pressure on yields, amplifying the initial market reaction to the tariff news.8 This phenomenon was likened to previous episodes of market stress, such as the COVID-19 selloff of March 2020.8
    > There were also reports of potential stop-loss orders being triggered at large financial institutions, such as Japanese banks, as Treasury yields spiked.<sup>8</sup> Stop-loss orders are pre-set instructions to automatically sell an asset if its price reaches a certain level. In this case, the rapid increase in yields may have triggered these orders, leading to further selling pressure and contributing to the swiftness of the yield increase.<sup>8</sup>

**4. Near-Term Outlook and Expert Perspectives**

-   **Uncertainty and Sensitivity to Policy:** The near-term outlook for the US Treasury market is characterized by significant uncertainty, largely stemming from the ongoing trade tensions and the potential for further policy shifts.<sup>5</sup> The market is expected to remain highly sensitive to any new announcements or developments related to trade policy, as these have already demonstrated a substantial impact on both economic expectations and investor behavior.<sup>13</sup> The increased compensation demanded by investors due to this policy uncertainty is likely to keep Treasury yields at elevated levels.<sup>24</sup>

-   **Expectations of Federal Reserve Policy:** While the Federal Reserve has remained on hold in the immediate aftermath of the tariff announcement, market participants are closely watching for any signals regarding future monetary policy.<sup>8</sup> As of late April 2025, bond markets were pricing in expectations for three or four cuts to the federal funds rate later in the year.<sup>11</sup> This suggests that investors anticipate a potential economic slowdown necessitating a response from the Fed. However, the Federal Reserve's own forecasts at the time indicated a more modest expectation of two rate cuts for the year.<sup>12</sup> This divergence highlights the uncertainty surrounding the Fed's future actions, which will likely depend on the evolution of both inflation and economic growth in the coming months.<sup>11</sup>

-   **Inflation Expectations and Economic Growth Projections:** Experts anticipate that the interplay between inflation expectations and economic growth projections will be a crucial factor influencing the Treasury market in the near term.<sup>1</sup> The Federal Reserve itself revised its core inflation projections upward for 2025, partially reflecting the expected impact of the newly implemented tariffs.<sup>12</sup> At the same time, forecasts from institutions like Comerica suggest a potential growth scare in the second and third quarters of 2025 as a result of the tariff shock.<sup>9</sup> If inflation remains elevated due to the tariffs, the Fed may be hesitant to implement significant rate cuts, potentially keeping Treasury yields higher. Conversely, a more pronounced economic slowdown could compel the Fed to ease monetary policy more aggressively, which would likely put downward pressure on yields.<sup>1</sup>

-   **Expert Forecasts on Yields:** Expert opinions on the near-term direction of Treasury yields are somewhat divided. As of late April 2025, the 10-year Treasury yield was hovering around 4.25% to 4.32%.<sup>14</sup> Some analysts believe that yields are likely to trend lower as the economy slows due to the uncertainty surrounding trade policy and the actual effects of the tariffs.<sup>1</sup> They anticipate that a potential recession could prompt the Federal Reserve to lower interest rates, further boosting Treasury prices and cutting yields.<sup>1</sup> However, others foresee continued volatility and the potential for yields to remain elevated due to concerns about rising government deficits and persistent inflationary pressures.<sup>1</sup>

-   **Potential Regulatory Relief:** There is ongoing discussion among policymakers, including both the Treasury and the Federal Reserve, about potentially speeding up regulatory relief for banks.<sup>8</sup> One specific measure being considered is an amendment to the Supplementary Leverage Ratio (SLR) rule. Temporarily excluding US Treasuries from the calculation of this ratio could incentivize banks to increase their holdings of government bonds, as it would not negatively impact their capital requirements.<sup>8</sup> Such a move could increase demand for Treasuries and potentially help to stabilize the market and cap further increases in yields.<sup>8</sup>

**5. Risks and Opportunities in the US Treasury Market**

-   Potential Risks:
    > One of the primary risks facing the US Treasury market in the near future is the potential for a further escalation of trade tensions and the imposition of additional tariffs.5 The average effective US tariff rate has already reached its highest level since 1909.21 Any further increases in tariffs could exacerbate concerns about inflation, economic growth, and international trade relations, leading to increased volatility and potentially higher Treasury yields.9
    > Another significant risk is a substantial decline in foreign demand for US Treasuries.<sup>1</sup> Foreign investors currently hold a significant portion of the US Treasury market, around 30%.<sup>20</sup> If trade disputes or concerns about the long-term fiscal health of the US were to lead to a significant reduction in this demand, it could put sustained upward pressure on Treasury yields, making it more expensive for the US government to finance its debt.<sup>9</sup>
    > The persistent issue of rising government deficits and the associated increase in Treasury issuance also poses a risk.<sup>1</sup> The US House of Representatives voted to allow for legislation that could add trillions to the national deficit over the next decade.<sup>27</sup> A larger supply of Treasury bonds could potentially push prices down and yields up, especially if demand does not keep pace with the increased borrowing needs of the government.<sup>1</sup>
    > The Treasury market has also experienced periods of low liquidity, and there is a risk that this could worsen, leading to market dysfunction.<sup>2</sup> Liquidity in the Treasury market was already reported as low and worsening in April.<sup>18</sup> Reduced liquidity can amplify yield swings and make it more difficult for investors to trade without significantly impacting prices, potentially leading to instability.<sup>22</sup>
    > While not an immediate threat, a longer-term risk involves a potential loss of confidence in the US dollar as the world's reserve currency.<sup>2</sup> The US dollar has already depreciated against a basket of other currencies.<sup>10</sup> A gradual erosion of the dollar's status could reduce global demand for dollar-denominated assets, including US Treasuries, potentially leading to higher yields over time.<sup>20</sup>

-   Potential Opportunities:
    > The recent increase in Treasury yields could present potentially attractive entry points for long-term investors seeking higher fixed-income returns.8 While yields have come down somewhat from their April peaks, they remain at levels that could offer compelling returns if held to maturity.14
    > If the US economy experiences a slowdown or if the Federal Reserve decides to cut interest rates in response to weakening growth, there is a potential for capital appreciation in the Treasury market.<sup>1</sup> Bond prices move inversely to yields, so a decline in yields would lead to an increase in the value of existing Treasury bonds.<sup>1</sup>
    > The widening of the spread between short-term and long-term Treasury yields, known as a steepening of the yield curve, could also create opportunities for investors in different maturity segments of the market.<sup>8</sup> This could allow investors to strategically allocate their investments across various maturities to potentially enhance returns or manage interest rate risk.<sup>8</sup>
    > Finally, if hedging costs for international investors decline, it could lead to increased demand for US Treasuries from overseas, providing support for bond prices and potentially putting downward pressure on yields.<sup>8</sup> There is some indication of potential for lower hedging costs for Japanese investors, for example.<sup>8</sup>

**6. Investor Sentiment and Emerging Trends**

Investor sentiment towards US Treasury bonds in April 2025 appeared cautious and uncertain, reflecting the significant policy shifts and market volatility.<sup>5</sup> Consumer expectations about the economy fell sharply, indicating a broader sense of unease.<sup>16</sup> There were also signs of a flight to safety, with increased demand for Treasury bonds observed during periods of heightened uncertainty, suggesting that they still retain some of their safe-haven appeal despite the recent turbulence.<sup>28</sup>

However, there were also emerging concerns and doubts about the long-term safe-haven status of US Treasuries.<sup>2</sup> Trading in traditional safe havens like US Treasuries had become erratic and unpredictable.<sup>10</sup> The policy unpredictability surrounding trade, coupled with the growing national debt, has led some investors to question the reliability of US government bonds as the ultimate safe asset in the global financial system.<sup>13</sup>

The market also demonstrated an increased focus on policy headlines, with dramatic swings in asset prices often driven more by news related to trade and government policy than by traditional economic fundamentals.<sup>13</sup> This heightened sensitivity to policy announcements underscores the significant uncertainty and the potential for rapid shifts in investor sentiment in response to political developments.<sup>13</sup>

Finally, there were indications of a potential for capital rotation, with some investors considering shifting capital away from US dollar assets, including Treasuries, and into other global markets.<sup>9</sup> This potential diversification reflects concerns about the dollar's strength, the US economic outlook, and the impact of the current trade policies.<sup>27</sup>

**7. Conclusion**

The US Treasury market experienced a period of significant volatility in early April 2025, primarily triggered by the announcement of new US tariffs. This policy shock, combined with technical market factors such as the unwinding of leveraged trades and concerns about potential inflationary pressures and future economic growth, led to a notable spike in Treasury yields. While yields subsequently eased somewhat, the near-term outlook remains uncertain and highly sensitive to further policy developments, particularly in the realm of trade, as well as incoming economic data that will shed light on the actual impact of these tariffs. The Federal Reserve's response to these evolving conditions will also be a key determinant of the market's trajectory. Investors face both risks and opportunities in this environment, and continued monitoring of market conditions and policy decisions will be crucial for navigating the US Treasury market in the near future.
