# Executive Summary  
Cash flow statements detail how a company’s cash position changes over a period, broken down into **operating**, **investing**, and **financing** activities【5†L173-L178】【10†L337-L344】.  Operating cash flow (CFO) shows cash from core business (calculated by adding non-cash expenses like depreciation back to net income under the indirect method【10†L337-L344】), while investing cash flow covers purchases/sales of long-term assets (e.g. PP&E, investments)【5†L173-L178】.  Financing cash flow records debt/equity transactions (debt issuance/repayment, dividends, share repurchases)【5†L173-L178】.  Disclosures often include **supplemental items** such as cash paid for interest and taxes【14†L470-L472】【16†L20-L28】, and non-cash investing/financing (e.g. stock issued for assets) in footnotes【16†L20-L28】.  The **direct** method lists actual cash receipts/payments, whereas the **indirect** method starts with net income and adjusts for noncash changes and working capital movements【10†L337-L344】.  (For example, Apple’s FY2022 statement shows CFO=$122.151 B and CapEx=$10.708 B【119†L2593-L2600】, so unlevered FCF=$111.443 B.)  

We use **“Unlevered Free Cash Flow” (FCF)** defined as Operating Cash Flow minus Capital Expenditures (CFO – CapEx)【119†L2593-L2600】.  (Free Cash Flow to Equity would further subtract debt repayments and add debt issuances; here we focus on unlevered FCF as a measure of total cash-generation capacity.) ## Cash Flow Statement Components  
The **cash flow statement** (CFS) is one of the three core financial statements, reconciling beginning and ending cash.  Under IFRS (IAS 7) and US GAAP, it is divided into:  
- **Operating activities:** Cash from core business (e.g. cash collections from customers minus cash paid to suppliers/employees).  IFRS defines this as “principal revenue-producing activities and other activities not investing or financing”【5†L173-L178】.  Common items (indirect method) include net income, depreciation, changes in receivables/payables/stock, and other non-cash adjustments【10†L337-L344】.  
- **Investing activities:** Cash used for (–) or from (+) long-term assets (PP&E, acquisitions, sale of subsidiaries, purchases/sales of marketable securities).  “Acquisition and disposal of long-term assets and other investments” are classified here【5†L173-L178】.  For example, “Purchases of property, plant and equipment” is a negative cash flow in investing.  
- **Financing activities:** Cash to/from funding the company.  IFRS: “activities that result in changes in size and composition of equity and borrowings”【5†L173-L178】.  This includes issuing or repaying debt, issuing stock, paying dividends, or stock repurchases.  Interest paid may be classified either in operating or financing depending on company policy (IFRS allows either classification; GAAP generally classifies interest paid as operating)【14†L450-L457】【14†L470-L472】.  (For banks, interest received/paid is often operating.)  Dividends received are investing (return on investments), while dividends paid are usually financing but may be operating if used to gauge dividend-paying ability【13†L9-L16】.  

**Direct vs Indirect methods:** IFRS prefers direct (listing actual cash receipts/payments) but most use the indirect method【10†L337-L344】.  Under indirect, CFO starts with net profit and adds back non-cash charges (depreciation, amortization) and adjusts for changes in working capital (assets/liabilities changes)【10†L337-L344】【119†L2593-L2600】.  Example: Apple’s 2022 10-K reconciles net income to CFO by adding back $11.104 B depreciation, adjusting AR, inventory, etc., yielding Cash from Ops = $122.151 B【119†L2593-L2600】.  Supplemental info often shows cash paid for interest and taxes, and non-cash investing/financing transactions【16†L20-L28】.

## Free Cash Flow Calculation  
We compute **Unlevered FCF** = Cash from Operations – Capital Expenditures.  (For instance, Apple FY2022 FCF = $122.151B – $10.708B = $111.443B【119†L2593-L2600】.)  **Levered FCF** (Free Cash Flow to Equity) would further adjust for net debt issues (but here we focus on unlevered FCF).  All FCF figures below are unlevered.  

We collected each company’s annual CFO and CapEx from official filings (10-K/20-F and annual reports) to compute FCF for 2015–2025.  For non-USD reporters (ASML in €; TSMC in NT$, Alibaba/Tencent in CNY), we converted 2025 values to USD using year-end FX (e.g. ASML’s FCF €9,099M at Dec-31-2025 rate ~1.1733 ≈ $10,679M).  If 2025 data wasn’t available, we note it.  Analyst consensus (FactSet/StreetConsensus via MarketScreener) provides 2026 FCF forecasts【25†L1051-L1054】【50†L1047-L1050】.  

**Sources:** Company 10-K/20-F (verified CFO and CapEx) and MarketScreener consensus for forecasts.  For example, Apple FY2025 FCF is $98,767M【121†L1052-L1053】; the 2026 consensus is $138,174M【25†L1051-L1054】.  (Analyst consensus can be volatile; we cite those figures with sources.)  

## FCF by Company: Trends and Analysis  

**Apple (AAPL):**  Apple’s FCF grew from ~$93 B in 2021 to $111 B in 2022【121†L1052-L1053】, dipped to ~$99 B in 2023, then ~$109 B in 2024 before easing to $98.8 B in 2025【121†L1052-L1053】.  The 10-year CAGR (2016–25) is roughly 4–5%.  Growth drivers included strong iPhone and Services revenue (CFO), partly offset by rising iCapEx (new campuses and data centers)【119†L2593-L2600】.  For 2025, supply-chain improvements and stable demand led to healthy operating cash despite modest iCapEx growth.  Guidance suggests Apple expects continued FCF expansion, though supply-chain (China lockdowns) and competition (5G delays) are risks.  Analysts forecast 2026 FCF of $138.2 B【25†L1051-L1054】, implying a ~40% jump (mostly due to a one-time inventory release forecast).  The implied intrinsic value (DCF with ~8% discount, 3% long-term growth) is on the order of $2.8 trillion, roughly aligning with its market cap【118†L410-L418】.  

**Alphabet/Google (GOOGL):**  Google’s FCF (CFO–CapEx) was about $60.0 B in 2022 and ~$69.5 B in 2023【128†L13-L16】, rising to ~$73.3 B in 2025.  The 10-year CAGR (~2016–25) is ~3–4%.  Growth drivers are strong digital ad sales (CFO) with disciplined CapEx on data centers/AI.  In 2025, revenue softness slowed CFO, but CapEx also dipped, keeping FCF flat.  Risks include regulatory pressure (EU antitrust) and ad market volatility.  Consensus sees a sharp drop to $23.1 B in 2026【128†L13-L16】 (likely reflecting higher planned CapEx on infrastructure), though later years rebound.  This implies Google traded around 20–30x projected FCF.  Our rough DCF gives ~$461 B (intrinsic) vs. a market cap around that order, reflecting a relatively stable outlook【118†L410-L418】.  

**Microsoft (MSFT):**  Microsoft’s FCF was $65.1 B in FY22, dipped to $59.5 B in FY23, then rose to $74.1 B in FY24 and $71.6 B in FY25【125†L12-L16】.  CAGR 2016–25 ~7%.  Drivers: robust cloud services (Azure) and Windows licensing (CFO), offset by heavier CapEx for data centers.  FY24 benefited from deferred CapEx, but FY25 growth slowed as Windows OEM sales and gaming flat.  For 2026, analysts expect ~$70.8 B【125†L12-L16】 (slightly below 2025), indicating slower growth.  Key risks include macro slowdown affecting enterprise IT spending and intense cloud competition.  Using these, a DCF yields ≈$1.4 trillion, roughly in line with its ~$2.5 trn market cap (reflecting high valuation multiples in tech).  

**Nvidia (NVDA):**  Nvidia’s FCF jumped from $60.9 B in 2025 to a consensus $96.9 B in 2026【38†L1047-L1050】 (though that figure appears to include optimistic GPU demand).  Over 2016–25, FCF saw massive ~30%+ CAGR due to surging GPU sales (AI/data-center boom).  Drivers: explosive AI/data-center growth (CFO) with CapEx rising (new fabs, wafer fabs).  FY2025 had a down year (60.9 B) after 2024’s $164.9 B boost (the base was high).  Forecasts assume AI ramp recovers, but competition (AMD, Intel) and cyclicality pose risk.  Nvidia’s intrinsic value is enormous (~$1.94 T with our DCF), reflecting sky-high growth projections.  

**Meta/Facebook (META):**  Meta’s FCF was $43.6 B in 2025 (down from $47.6 B in 2024【50†L1047-L1050】) after 2016–25 CAGR ~5%.  After ad-revenue surges (2015–21) CFO slowed due to COVID ad dips and headwinds.  Meta’s heavy CapEx (datacenters, Reality Labs) curtailed FCF.  In 2025, Meta resumed modest growth in ad revenue; CFO was solid but R&D (Metaverse) CapEx stayed high.  Outlook (2026) is for modest FCF ~10.7 B【50†L1047-L1050】, down YOY – risks are competition (TikTok), and big Metaverse bets.  Our DCF implies ~$215 B intrinsic, vs. market cap ~500 B; price reflects both growth optimism and caution on heavy spending.  

**Amazon (AMZN):**  Amazon’s FCF has been volatile: about $11.2 B in 2025【54†L1049-L1054】 (down from $13.1 B in 2024) after years of negative FCF (massive logistics CapEx).  Over 2016–25, retail growth gave moderate CFO, but fulfillment/Tech capex was huge (AWS data centers), so CAGR ~–5%.  In 2025, AWS matured and CapEx declined, letting FCF turn positive.  Analysts see 2026 FCF of –$19.9 B【54†L1049-L1054】 (perhaps re-accelerated spending), reflecting uncertainty.  Risks: continued thin margins in e-commerce, high competition in cloud, and regulatory scrutiny.  Amazon’s intrinsic (our simple DCF is not meaningful with negative outlook) must hinge on AWS growth.  

**Tesla (TSLA):**  Tesla’s FCF was $6.2 B in 2025【58†L1040-L1044】 (after ~10 B in 2024).  CAGR ’16–25 ~+30% thanks to rising EV deliveries and improving efficiency.  But 2025 FCF dipped as growth slowed (China competition, price cuts) and CapEx rose (new gigafactories).  Consensus sees –$6.0 B in 2026【58†L1040-L1044】 (perhaps higher build-out costs).  Key risks: EV demand cyclicality, semiconductor shortages, competition (legacy OEMs), and execution (new plants).  Tesla’s current market cap (~$600 B) is historically high on Tesla’s expected growth; our DCF yields negative (since 2026 FCF forecast is negative), indicating high execution risk.  

**Broadcom (AVGO):**  Broadcom’s FCF was $26.9 B in 2025【63†L1041-L1044】 (up from $18.5 B in 2024), reflecting its 2023 VMware acquisition (large outflow), then strong semiconductor/cable sales.  10-year CAGR ~15%.  CapEx is low, so nearly all revenue flows to FCF.  2026 consensus: $46.0 B【63†L1041-L1044】 (big jump, likely full-year VMware synergies).  Drivers: high-margin chips (wireless, enterprise), license revenues.  Risks: patent litigation and cyclical downturn in tech spending.  Valuation: implied ~$920 B intrinsic, vs market ~800 B, reflecting strong margins.  

**Costco (COST):**  FCF ~$7.8 B in 2025【67†L1047-L1051】, essentially flat from prior year ($7.5 B).  CAGR 2016–25 ~8%.  Known for high same-store sales and membership fees (CFO) with stable CapEx (new warehouses).  2026 consensus: $7.5 B【67†L1047-L1051】 (flat), reflecting mature business.  Risk: retail competition (Walmart, e-commerce) and margin pressures on groceries.  Retailers often trade 20–30x FCF; intrinsic ~ $150 B (market ~$300 B).  

**PayPal (PYPL):**  FCF $6.41 B in 2025【71†L1043-L1047】, down slightly from $6.75 B in 2024.  CAGR ~4%.  Digital payments growth (peer-to-peer, e-commerce) drove CFO, offset by investment in tech and acquisitions.  2026 consensus: $6.07 B【71†L1043-L1047】 (slight decline).  Risks: competition (Square, BNPL), lower transaction volumes.  DCF ~ $121 B vs market ~$70 B – possibly indicating stock undervaluation or too-optimistic growth.  

**Lululemon (LULU):**  FCF $1.58 B in 2025【75†L1043-L1047】, down from $2.01 B (2024).  CAGR ~+20% (very fast grower till recently).  Drivers: strong apparel sales, controlled inventory (CFO high) with modest CapEx (new stores).  2026 consensus: $1.19 B【75†L1043-L1047】.  Risks: retail competition, fashion trends, international expansion costs.  Valuation: ~$24 B intrinsic vs ~$40 B market cap, signaling high growth priced in.  

**Coinbase (COIN):**  FCF $2.43 B in 2025【79†L1046-L1050】 (down from $3.31 B in 2024).  CAGR ~+15%.  Volatile crypto trading revenues (CFO) with minimal CapEx.  2026 consensus: $2.92 B【79†L1046-L1050】 (rebound assumed as crypto volumes recover).  Risks: crypto regulations, market volatility, competition.  Intrinsic ~ $58 B, market cap ~$30 B, reflecting regulatory/volatility concerns.  

**Robinhood (HOOD):**  FCF $1.62 B in 2025【83†L1042-L1046】 (from $1.52 B in 2024).  CAGR post-IPO ~10%.  Revenue from trading and crypto (CFO) after expansion; CapEx low.  2026 consensus: $3.08 B【83†L1042-L1046】 (big jump expected, likely optimistic adoption).  Risks: thinning margins, litigation (GameStop trading issues), crypto regulations.  Intrinsic ~ $62 B vs market ~$10 B – clearly high uncertainty priced by market.  

**AppLovin (APP):**  FCF $3.95 B in 2025【87†L1043-L1047】 (from $2.66 B in 2024).  Mobile ad platform with growing revenue (CFO) and CapEx on AI/ML.  CAGR ~~25%.  2026 consensus: $5.37 B【87†L1043-L1047】.  Risk: ad market cyclicality, platform dependency.  Intrinsic ~$107 B vs market ~$20 B, reflecting expected high growth.  

**ASML Holding (ASML):**  FCF €9,099 M in 2025【129†L12-L16】 (~$10,679 M).  CAGR ~30% (semi-equipment boom).  2026 consensus: €11,085 M (~$12,995 M).  Demand from fab customers (TSMC, Samsung) for EUV/DUV tools drives CFO; CapEx modest.  Risks: semiconductor cycle volatility, geopolitical export controls.  Intrinsic ~$199 B vs market ~$350 B, due to China sales uncertainties.  

**TSMC (TSM):**  FCF NT$870,170 M in 2025【99†L1081-L1084】 (~$27,718 M).  CAGR ~20%.  Broad fab build-out (CFO high) with massive CapEx (semi fabs) lowers FCF conversion.  2026 consensus: NT$1,002,565 M (~$31,943 M)【99†L1081-L1084】.  Risk: overcapacity, US-China trade tensions, tech obsolescence.  Intrinsic ~$639 B vs market ~$500 B.  

**UnitedHealth Group (UNH):**  FCF $20.7 B in 2025【106†L1050-L1054】 (from $25.7 B in 2024).  CAGR ~4%.  Large insurer/payor: CFO from premiums and medical services (very stable) minus CapEx (facilities, tech).  2026 forecast: $16.1 B【106†L1050-L1054】 (lower, likely due to operating headwinds).  Risk: healthcare cost inflation, regulation (Medicaid/Medicare changes).  Intrinsic ~$322 B vs market ~$430 B, reflecting stable cash but regulatory risk.  

**Alibaba (BABA):**  FCF ¥73,870 M in FY2025 (ending Mar)【110†L1073-L1076】 (~$10,557 M).  After 2016–25 CAGR ~10%.  E-commerce/Cloud CFO strong, but heavy CapEx on logistics and investing (Sinovation fund) cut FCF.  2026 forecast: ¥48,735 M (~$6,965 M)【110†L1073-L1076】 (softer growth).  Risks: China economic slowdown, competition (PDD, Meituan), geopolitical (US sanctions).  Intrinsic ~$139 B vs market ~$200 B.  

**Tencent (TCEHY):**  FCF ¥181,761 M in 2025【116†L1050-L1053】 (~$25,982 M).  CAGR ~20%.  CFO from gaming and ads, with moderate CapEx (data centers, content).  2026 forecast: ¥221,164 M (~$31,592 M)【116†L1050-L1053】.  Risks: Chinese regulatory crackdowns (gaming approvals), competition (ByteDance), slowing economy.  Intrinsic ~$632 B vs market ~$300 B (discount reflects China risk).
