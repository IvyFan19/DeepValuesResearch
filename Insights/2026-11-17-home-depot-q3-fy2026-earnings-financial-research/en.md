# Home Depot Q3 FY2026 Financial & Moat Analysis: A Value Investor's Assessment of Intrinsic Worth and Capital Allocation

(NYSE: HD) reported third-quarter fiscal 2026 financial results (for the period ended November 1, 2026, released November 17, 2026) that showcase disciplined market share expansion and steady cash generation within a dynamic macroeconomic environment. Consolidated net sales reached $43.4 billion ($43,398 million), representing a 4.9% increase year-over-year compared to $41,352 million in Q3 FY2025, while comparable sales grew 2.2% enterprise-wide and across the U.S. market. While these transactions significantly widen the company’s structural moat by enabling cross-selling across specialty roofing, landscaping, pool, drywall, acoustic ceiling, and steel framing trade categories, they fundamentally alter Home Depot’s financial structure. The shift toward wholesale trade distribution increases capital intensity and expands total debt obligations to $52.5 billion ($52,483 million as of November 1, 2026), elevating annual net interest expense obligations to approximately $2.2 billion for FY2026. Evaluating Home Depot's intrinsic worth requires looking beyond management's non-GAAP adjustments to rigorously analyze true owner earnings, debt service constraints, operating return drivers, and capital allocation discipline.

## 1. Owner Earnings and Cash Flow Quality vs. Adjusted Metrics

To evaluate the fundamental economic performance of an enterprise, value investors look beyond reported GAAP Net Income and management-defined non-GAAP adjustments to derive "Owner Earnings." Defined by Warren Buffett, Owner Earnings represent GAAP Net Earnings plus Depreciation & Amortization (and other non-cash operating charges), minus the annual Capital Expenditures required to maintain long-term competitive position and store infrastructure, adjusted for economic working capital requirements and stock-based dilution costs.

According to the condensed consolidated statements of cash flows in Home Depot’s Q3 FY2026 release, the company generated $15,496 million in net cash provided by operating activities for the nine months ended November 1, 2026 (9M FY2026), up 19.4% from $12,978 million in the nine months ended November 2, 2025 (9M FY2025). The reported components of cash flow for 9M FY2026 include:

*   **GAAP Net Earnings:** $11,768 million (vs. $11,585 million in 9M FY2025)
*   **Depreciation & Amortization (ex-intangibles):** $2,751 million (vs. $2,606 million in 9M FY2025)
*   **Intangible Asset Amortization:** $531 million (vs. $436 million in 9M FY2025)
*   **Total Depreciation & Amortization:** $3,282 million (vs. $3,042 million in 9M FY2025)
*   **Stock-Based Compensation Expense:** $461 million (vs. $408 million in 9M FY2025)
*   **Changes in Working Capital:** $(358) million (vs. $(2,694) million in 9M FY2025)
*   **Capital Expenditures:** $(2,679) million (vs. $(2,621) million in 9M FY2025)

To establish true economic cash flow, investors must evaluate Owner Earnings using both top-down (from Net Earnings) and bottom-up (from Operating Cash Flow) derivation pathways, properly reconciling working capital variations and non-cash equity compensation.

| Owner Earnings Derivation Step (9M FY2026) | Top-Down Pathway (from Net Earnings) | Bottom-Up Pathway (from Operating Cash Flow) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Starting Financial Metric** | **$11,768M** (GAAP Net Earnings) | **$15,496M** (Operating Cash Flow) |
| (+) Add-Back D&A / (-) Less CapEx | +$3,282M (D&A) / -$2,679M (CapEx) | -$2,679M (Capital Expenditures) |
| **Unadjusted Baseline Owner Cash Flow** | **$12,371M** | **$12,817M** |
| (-) Stock-Based Compensation Adjustment | Included in Net Earnings | -$461M (Recognize dilution expense) |
| (-) Working Capital Cash Outflow | -$358M (Working capital drain) | Already included in OCF ($(358)M) |
| **Reconciled Economic Owner Earnings** | **$12,013M** | **$11,998M** |

When deconstructing cash flow quality, stock-based compensation ($461 million in 9M FY2026, up 13.0% from $408 million in 9M FY2025) demands careful analytical treatment. Stock compensation represents a real operational cost that dilutes existing equity ownership. In a top-down derivation starting from GAAP Net Earnings, stock compensation has already been deducted as an operating cost; therefore, it should *never* be subtracted a second time. However, when deriving cash generation from Operating Cash Flow ($15,496 million), management treats stock-based compensation as a non-cash add-back. Value-minded analysts must reverse this add-back to reflect the economic reality that employee compensation paid via equity issuance is an ongoing corporate cost, yielding an adjusted cash generation bridge of $12,356 million prior to working capital adjustments ($15,496M OCF - $2,679M CapEx - $461M SBC adjustment).

Furthermore, a significant divergence exists between GAAP reported metrics and management’s non-GAAP adjusted results. For Q3 FY2026, Home Depot reported GAAP Diluted EPS of $3.72 (up 2.8% YoY from $3.62) and Adjusted Diluted EPS of $3.86 (up 3.2% YoY from $3.74). For 9M FY2026, GAAP Diluted EPS of $11.82 compares to Adjusted Diluted EPS of $12.22. Management computes adjusted figures by excluding acquired intangible asset amortization ($182 million pre-tax / $0.14 per share after-tax in Q3 FY2026; $531 million pre-tax / $0.40 per share after-tax in 9M FY2026). Of this acquired intangible amortization, $128 million in Q3 FY2026 and $372 million in 9M FY2026 relate directly to the acquisition of SRS Distribution Inc.

Management asserts that excluding customer relationship and trade name amortization delivers a clearer metric of underlying operating trends. However, value investors view these non-GAAP exclusions critically. When capital is deployed to acquire enterprise entities like SRS and GMS, the purchased trade names and contractor customer relationships represent real economic assets acquired with shareholder capital. Excluding their ongoing amortization understates real economic depreciation and overstates recurring earning power—especially since management does not exclude the revenues generated by those acquired assets.

## 2. Economic Moat Durability and Competitive Position

Home Depot maintains a wide economic moat rooted in massive economies of scale, an entrenched real estate network, brand power, and an increasingly integrated omni-channel distribution footprint. As of November 1, 2026, the enterprise operated 2,372 retail stores spanning all 50 U.S. states, the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, the U.S. Virgin Islands, Guam, 10 Canadian provinces, and Mexico. These retail locations average approximately 104,000 square feet of enclosed space, accompanied by roughly 24,000 square feet of outdoor garden space.

This store network is augmented by over 1,320 SRS specialty branch locations as of November 1, 2026. The supply chain infrastructure incorporates flatbed distribution centers (FDCs), rapid deployment centers (RDCs), direct fulfillment centers, and market delivery operations (MDOs) dedicated to appliances and bulk products. Under the FDC Relay delivery model, Home Depot utilizes its specialized distribution nodes to deliver heavy building materials directly to professional job sites, bypassing retail store aisles.

The strategic integration of SRS Distribution Inc. (acquired in FY2024) and GMS Inc. The addition of GMS complements SRS by adding specialty interior construction products—specifically drywall/wallboard, acoustic ceilings, and steel framing. This unified Pro ecosystem allows Home Depot to capture multi-stage trade projects across an entire building lifecycle, from structural framing and exterior roofing to interior buildout.

## 5. Intrinsic Value vs. Market Price & Margin of Safety

Value investing requires establishing a stock's intrinsic worth and purchasing equity only at a price that affords an adequate "Margin of Safety." The required margin of safety scales inversely with competitive position and cash flow predictability. A premier enterprise with high market share, strong brand equity, and a durable supply chain moat like Home Depot commands a narrower required margin of safety than an unmoated cyclical producer. However, purchase price remains the single largest determinant of investment returns.

Home Depot’s intrinsic value growth depends on balancing capital reinvestment against structural debt payments. Ongoing strategic execution—including opening approximately 15 new retail stores in FY2026 (progressing toward an 80-store target through FY2027) and expanding supply chain automation—requires ~2.5% of sales in annual capital expenditures. Concurrently, servicing $52,483 million in total debt requires ~$2.2 billion in annual net interest payments.

When debt service absorbs a meaningful share of operating cash flow, net owner earnings conversion is restricted. Consequently, multi-year intrinsic value compounding depends on management's ability to capture cost synergies from SRS and GMS, protect gross margins (~33.0%), and pay down near-term maturing debt ($9,554 million due within one year) out of organic owner earnings rather than relying on debt-funded share buybacks.

## What Would Change Our View

1.  **Moat Erosion in Pro Segment:** Sustained loss of market share among professional contractors or failure to integrate SRS/GMS platforms efficiently, evidenced by declining Pro customer sales or margin contraction across SRS lines of business.
2.  **Deterioration of Capital Efficiency:** A sustained decline in Return on Invested Capital (ROIC) below historic norms due to overpaying for acquisitions or unproductive capital expenditures exceeding the targeted 2.5% of sales.
3.  **Excessive Debt Burden & Leverage Stress:** Inability to pay down short-term and maturing long-term debt ($9,554 million due within one year as of November 1, 2026) out of organic owner earnings, leading to rising net interest expense (projected at ~$2.2 billion for FY2026) and constrained share buyback or dividend capacity.
4.  **Negative Customer Transaction Volume Trend:** Continued negative comp transaction growth (falling 0.5% in Q3 FY2026) that cannot be offset by ticket size increases, indicating weakening end-consumer demand and volume contraction.
