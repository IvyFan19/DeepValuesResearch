# AI Infrastructure and Industrial Bottlenecks: Analyzing Steve Eisman’s Discussion with Analyst Steve Tusa

The rapid expansion of artificial intelligence infrastructure has fundamentally transformed the capital goods sector, converting legacy, low-growth industrial manufacturers into high-growth derivative plays on data center capital expenditure. However, the physical realities of power grid interconnections—characterized by transformer supply constraints, direct-to-chip liquid thermal management requirements, and multi-year facility construction timelines—create substantial execution lags relative to market pipeline expectations. While paper project filings suggest exponential industry expansion, actual energized capacity remains tightly constrained by utility transmission availability, physical equipment shortages, and localized permitting friction. Consequently, hardware suppliers trading at valuation premiums face potential compressed multiples if hyper-scaler spending schedules experience deferral or extension.

## What was written

Episode 85 of *The Real Eisman Playbook* (published November 23, 2026) featured Wells Fargo industrials equity analyst Steve Tusa. The conversation centered on how the global data center capex surge has bifurcated the industrial coverage universe between AI-exposed suppliers and traditional cyclical capital goods.

### Industrial Sector Transformation
Historically, the industrial capital goods group was characterized as a slow-growth sector, growing in line with baseline U.S. gross domestic product (GDP) at roughly 3% annually. The influx of hyper-scaler capital expenditures—estimated at an annual global scale between $850 billion and $1.4 trillion, with current-year spending near $1.05 trillion—has accelerated organic growth for exposed hardware vendors to twice normal GDP rates. Within Tusa’s 16-company coverage universe, 8 names are now directly tied to the AI infrastructure buildout, benefiting from continuous earnings beats and upward guidance revisions.

### Equipment Supply Chain Roles
The discussion outlined the explicit hardware taxonomy required to power and cool dense, modern AI data centers:

*   **Power Conditioning & Step-Down Infrastructure:** Electricity drawn from high-voltage transmission lines cannot be directly routed into server architectures. Manufacturers supply transformers, switchgear, and distribution boards to step down and condition electrical current. Forgent maintains approximately 55% revenue exposure to electrical equipment and data center end-markets, while Eaton derives over 20% of its revenues from data center power infrastructure.
*   **Thermal Management & Direct-to-Chip Liquid Cooling:** Legacy data centers relied on central HVAC systems blowing chilled air across server racks (fan walls). However, power densities have escalated from historical 10-kilowatt racks to 200-kilowatt arrays, progressing toward 1-megawatt configurations housing up to 70 GPUs per rack (such as NVIDIA Blackwell B200/B300 architectures). Because air cooling is physically incapable of dissipating this thermal load, facilities require direct-to-chip liquid cooling utilizing cold plates attached directly to semiconductors and Coolant Distribution Units (CDUs). The direct liquid cooling market has expanded from a $1 billion niche application into a $7 billion to $10 billion addressable market. Chilled liquid loops reject thermal load into heavy external industrial chillers, driving equipment demand for legacy vendors including Johnson Controls, Trane, and Carrier. Vertiv represents the primary pure-play in this vertical, generating 85% of its total revenue from data center cooling and power infrastructure, while nVent (Invent) derives 20% to 25% of sales from electrical and liquid cooling solutions.
*   **Adjacent Industrial Exposure:** Rockwell Automation derives approximately 3% of revenue directly from data center hardware, contributing roughly 2 percentage points of growth to its baseline ~8% growth rate, alongside indirect exposure through semiconductor manufacturing machinery.

### Macro Implications and Economic Sensitivity
According to analysis presented on the YouTube channel **Steve Eisman**, broader U.S. economic growth has become disproportionately anchored to AI-driven capital expenditures. ### Grid Bottlenecks and Realization Lags
*   **Historical Base vs. Paper Pipelines:** Prior to the current AI cycle, the total U.S. installed data center power capacity stood at roughly 20 gigawatts (GW)—a footprint built over two decades that consumed approximately 1% of total U.S. electrical generation. In contrast, current databases tracking land acquisitions and municipal permits report a theoretical pipeline of 450 GW.
*   **The Texas Utility Realization Gap:** In Texas, official utility documentation shows 375 GW in formal queue requests. However, as of mid-2026, only 5 GW were actively lit up, with an additional 3 GW approved for energization—representing an operational base of 8 to 10 GW against the 375 GW paper queue.
*   **Execution Durations:** Infrastructure delivery requires multi-year lead times. The Abilene "Stargate" project (an 800 MW to 1 GW facility involving Crusoe, CoreWeave, and Oracle) broke ground in June 2024 but reached only 75% construction completion by late 2026. Energizing a single gigawatt requires up to three years due to supply chain friction extending down to basic distribution components, including high-voltage transformers and utility poles.

## The Buffett reading

Evaluating data center equipment providers through classical value-investing principles highlights essential dynamics concerning economic moats, capital deployment, and valuation safety margins.

### Business Quality & Moats
Sustainable competitive advantages in capital goods depend on specialized engineering integration, switching costs, and installed-base servicing:
*   **Integrated Thermal Systems:** High-density liquid cooling via CDUs and direct-to-chip cold plates demands high manufacturing precision to eliminate fluid leakage risks in high-density server clusters. Suppliers delivering integrated end-to-end thermal loops—linking internal rack cooling directly to heavy central chillers—maintain stronger economic moats than non-integrated component vendors.
*   **Recurring Service Revenues:** Complex industrial hardware deployed in mission-critical facilities creates durable, high-margin service networks. Johnson Controls, for example, generates 30% of its total corporate revenue from recurring field services, establishing a cash-flow buffer against broader industrial cycles.

### Capital Allocation & Operational Execution
Value expansion is frequently driven by internal operational execution and organizational turnarounds independent of end-market tailwinds. Johnson Controls’ operational restructuring over a 1.5-year period under updated executive leadership illustrates how internal execution and margin optimization can compound earnings quality during complex market cycles.

### Price vs. Value and Margin of Safety
A structural risk for value investors involves paying peak-cycle valuation multiples on cyclically inflated earnings. Capital goods companies trading at steep premiums to the S&P 500 discount uninterrupted capital expenditure expansion. If grid constraints or component shortages defer facility commissioning dates, high-beta equities face potential valuation multiple compression. In the podcast discussion, guest analyst Steve Tusa outlined a modeled valuation scenario framework for Vertiv ranging from a $175 downside case to a >$300 upside case relative to its ~$200 baseline price, underscoring high stock sensitivity to continuous spending execution.

## What is not known

1.  **End-User Monetization & ROI:** It remains unverified whether downstream software applications and commercial AI monetization models will deliver adequate returns on invested capital (ROIC) to sustain long-term hyper-scaler capex at the $1 trillion+ annual scale.
2.  **Grid Bottleneck Limitations:** The absolute physical throughput capacity of regional utility grids, high-voltage transmission lines, and utility distribution hardware (down to basic transformers and power poles) remains untested against current project timeline expectations.
3.  **Cyclical Downside Margin Impact:** The exact degree to which equipment supplier revenues and operating margins would contract if hyper-scalers initiate sudden capex cuts or project deferrals has not been stress-tested in the current market environment.

## The lesson for a patient investor

*   **Distinguish Derivative Demand Cycles from Permanent Moats:** Capital goods providers function as macro derivatives of end-market capex cycles. Accelerated short-term revenue growth driven by acute supply-demand imbalances must not be misinterpreted as permanent organic growth expansion.
*   **Focus on Energized Grid Connections Over Headline Pipelines:** Fundamental evaluation requires tracking lit-up gigawatts and formal utility interconnections rather than speculative land purchases, municipal permit filings, or unverified queue requests.
*   **Require a Strict Margin of Safety:** Value discipline mandates avoiding premium entry multiples on hardware vendors whose current cash-flow projections depend on the uninterrupted execution of multi-hundred-billion-dollar infrastructure spending plans.
