## 1. The Investor: Philosophy, Fund, and Lifetime Work

Bill Ackman (William Albert Ackman), born May 11, 1966, in Chappaqua, New York, is the founder and Chief Executive Officer of Pershing Square Capital Management. Raised by Ronnie I. and Lawrence David Ackman—the former chairman of real estate financing firm Ackman-Ziff Real Estate Group—Ackman attended Horace Greeley High School (graduating in 1985) before earning a Bachelor of Arts magna cum laude in social studies from Harvard College in 1988 (authoring a senior thesis on Harvard admissions) and a Master of Business Administration from Harvard Business School in 1992.

Ackman launched his investment career in 1992 by co-founding Gotham Partners alongside David P. Berkowitz. Gotham made small public equity investments and grew assets under management (AUM) to $500 million by 1998 following a high-profile joint bid for Rockefeller Center in 1995 with real estate firm Leucadia National. However, by 2002, Gotham became entangled in shareholder litigation, leading to its unwind. During this transitional period, Ackman executed one of his signature research campaigns by challenging Standard & Poor’s AAA bond rating of financial insurer MBIA. Digging through 725,000 pages of subpoenaed records at a legal cost exceeding $100,000, Ackman exposed MBIA's use of an "orphaned transformer" entity (LaCrosse Financial Products) to conceal credit default swap liabilities on mortgage-backed collateralized debt obligations. Ackman purchased credit default swaps against MBIA corporate debt, covering his short position for a massive profit on January 16, 2009, during the financial crisis.

In 2004, Ackman established Pershing Square Capital Management with $54 million seeded from his personal net worth and former partner Leucadia National. Operating from its headquarters at 787 Eleventh Avenue (9th Floor) in New York City, Pershing Square grew into an institutional powerhouse managing approximately $35 billion as reported in 2026. The firm maintains an unprecedented organizational structure: despite managing tens of billions in assets, Pershing Square employs just 48 staff members and boasts a zero-percent employee turnover rate (as reported by *Fortune* on August 8, 2026). Firm loyalty is cultivated through multi-million dollar equity ownership granted to all personnel—including receptionists and janitors—complemented by extensive corporate wellness benefits and a unique two-month summer remote work policy in the Hamptons.

Over its two-decade history, Pershing Square’s investment style has evolved from high-visibility activist warfare into a compounder model inspired by Warren Buffett. In its early activist era, Ackman achieved historic operational transformations, most notably taking control of Canadian Pacific Railway (CP) through a bitter 2011–2012 proxy fight that replaced the CEO and board, earning Ackman *Report on Business Magazine's* "CEO of the Year" award. Similarly, after acquiring a core stake in Chipotle Mexican Grill in 2016, Ackman engineered the turnaround of the restaurant franchise by helping recruit Brian Niccol as CEO in 2018. However, aggressive activism also delivered severe setbacks, including a nearly $4 billion loss on Valeant Pharmaceuticals (liquidating 27.2 million shares in March 2017 for $300 million alongside a $193.75 million Allergan legal settlement), an unsuccessful proxy fight at Automatic Data Processing (ADP), a failed $1 billion five-year short campaign against Herbalife (exited on February 28, 2018), and the withdrawal of the Pershing Square USA closed-end fund IPO on August 1, 2024, after raising $2 billion.

Ackman’s modern investment model focuses on "permanent capital" structures, long-term corporate holding companies, and macro asymmetry. In March 2020, anticipating economic disruptions from COVID-19 after consulting Yale biostatistician Nicholas Christakis, Ackman deployed $27 million into credit default swap hedges, generating $2.6 billion in profit in under a month while preserving his core long equity portfolio. Today, with an estimated net worth of $8 billion (*Bloomberg*, April 2026), Ackman operates as a global investor, philanthropist (co-trustee of the Pershing Square Foundation alongside spouse Neri Oxman, having granted over $750 million), and Executive Chairman of Howard Hughes Holdings.

From digital advertising platforms like Meta to global music copyright catalogs like Universal Music Group, Pershing Square prioritizes businesses with structural economic moats. Ackman’s transition toward corporate holding structures (evidenced by his Executive Chairmanship and 47% stake in Howard Hughes Holdings) underscores the advantage of permanent capital. Open-ended funds face redemption pressure during market panics, forcing managers to sell depressed stocks. ## 8. Limitations of 13F Filings & Institutional Disclaimer

### Limitations of Form 13F Data
SEC Form 13F-HR filings are backward-looking documents published up to 45 calendar days after the close of each reporting quarter. They report U.S.-listed long equity holdings exclusively as of the final day of the quarter. Form 13F filings provide no visibility into:
*   Non-U.S. equity holdings traded on foreign exchanges (e.g., Euronext-listed Universal Music Group).
*   Short equity positions, options, put/call hedges, or credit default swaps.
*   Cash reserves, commercial paper, treasury holdings, or debt instruments.
*   Intra-quarter trading activities, executed buys/sells occurring between filing dates.

Consequently, 13F filings offer an incomplete snapshot of an institutional fund’s total balance sheet, risk exposure, and real-time portfolio management strategy.
