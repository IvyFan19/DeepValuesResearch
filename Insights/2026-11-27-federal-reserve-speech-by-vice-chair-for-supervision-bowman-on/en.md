# Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman on Modernizing U.S. Banking Supervision

In a pre-recorded address delivered on November 25, 2026, Federal Reserve Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman presented a strategic framework for modernizing U.S. banking regulatory and supervisory structures. Bowman argued that the mere identification of financial risks within banking institutions is insufficient to preserve systemic stability. Instead, effective supervision requires equipping regulators with the proactive authority, accountability, and independent judgment necessary to intervene before firm-specific or contagion risks fully materialize.

## Who wrote it

Michelle Bowman serves as Vice Chair for Supervision on the Federal Reserve Board of Governors, a position she assumed on June 9, 2025, following her initial appointment as Governor on November 26, 2018. Within the federal banking oversight architecture, the Federal Reserve Board and the 12 regional Reserve Banks directly supervise approximately 900 state member banks and 5,000 bank holding companies. This jurisdiction operates alongside the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC), which serves as the primary federal regulator for national banks, and the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC), which oversees state-chartered non-member institutions. For equity analysts at DeepValues Research and patient bank investors, the Vice Chair for Supervision’s policy stance represents a critical variable. Supervisory rigor and examination priorities directly dictate bank asset quality evaluations, compliance overhead, capital buffer maintenance, and ultimately the institution's capacity for shareholder capital deployment.

## What was written

In pre-recorded remarks published on the YouTube channel *Federal Reserve* on November 25, 2026, Bowman set forth a strategic posture for oversight during a macro environment characterized by a target federal funds rate range of 3.50–3.75%, an interest on reserve balances (IORB) rate of 3.65%, and a primary credit bank rate of 3.75%. Her remarks established three core pillars for modernizing supervisory operations:

*   **Learning from Supervisory Failures:** Advancing beyond passive risk identification by empowering supervisors with the explicit authority, accountability, and independent judgment required to take decisive action before financial risks are realized.
*   **Keeping Pace with Financial Evolution:** Frequently updating regulatory and supervisory frameworks to focus resources on material risks, facilitate timely decision-making, and adapt to shifting market structures and technology, in active coordination with international standard-setting bodies such as the Financial Stability Board (FSB) and the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS).
*   **Regional and International Cooperation:** Strengthening cross-border supervisory alignment throughout the Americas alongside key host institutions—including Banco Central do Brasil, the Association of Supervisors of Banks of the Americas (ASBA), the BCBS, and the Financial Stability Institute (FSI)—to reflect deeply interconnected trade and financial networks.

## The Buffett reading

At DeepValues Research, evaluating Vice Chair Bowman’s supervisory vision through a classic value-investing lens provides essential clarity on the structural dynamics governing financial equities:

### Business Quality
Rigorous, forward-looking supervision preserves total banking system integrity and mitigates systemic run risks, protecting high-quality financial franchises. However, in an elevated interest rate regime—with a 3.50–3.75% target federal funds rate and a 3.65% IORB—banks face heightened duration risk and unrealized asset losses. Proactive supervisory examinations increase compliance friction and operational expenditures, which can compress return on equity (ROE) for institutions lacking operating efficiency.

### Economic Moat
Escalating regulatory complexity and proactive examination burdens widen the competitive moat for money-center banks and large bank holding companies. These large institutions leverage compliance scale advantages to efficiently absorb regulatory overhead across vast asset bases, creating formidable structural barriers to entry for smaller regional and community competitors.

### Capital Allocation
Because the Federal Reserve set reserve requirements to 0% for all depository institutions in March 2020, supervisory focus has pivoted from mechanical reserve ratios to qualitative examinations of credit underwriting, asset-liability management, and capital strength. Stricter supervisory scrutiny under this framework directly restricts management's discretion in returning capital to shareholders via dividends or share buybacks if examiners identify unmitigated risks, preferring capital retention over shareholder distributions.

### Price vs. Value
For equity analysts at DeepValues Research, market mispricings arise when supervisory laxity obscures duration risk and credit degradation on bank balance sheets. When latent supervisory gaps are exposed or sudden regulatory interventions occur, market panics force equity prices to trade at severe discounts to intrinsic asset value—particularly when institutions are forced to access emergency liquidity via the discount window at the 3.75% bank rate.
