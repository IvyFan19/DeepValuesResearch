# Lowe's Companies, Inc. (LOW) Q3 FY2026 Earnings: An Owner Earnings and Intrinsic Value Analysis

Lowe's Companies, Inc. demonstrated strong core operational resilience during the third quarter of fiscal 2026 (ended October 30, 2026), delivering total net sales growth of 9.3% to $22.756 billion and comparable sales growth of 1.8%—marking its sixth consecutive quarter of positive comp sales, underpinned by impressive momentum in Pro, Online (+17.4%), and Home Services. However, a rigorous financial statement analysis requires unbundling core cash flow generation from non-recurring accounting items and non-operating windfalls. Third-quarter reported results included a substantial pre-tax IEEPA tariff refund of approximately $250 million (~$0.34 per share net benefit), which was partially offset by $96 million in pre-tax intangible asset amortization ($0.12 per share after tax) associated with the strategic acquisitions of Foundation Building Materials (FBM) and Artisan Design Group (ADG). For the long-term, value-minded investor, Lowe's fundamental operational engine remains exceptionally potent—generating $7.026 billion in true baseline Owner Earnings (Free Cash Flow) over the first nine months of FY2026. This cash flow engine is protected by a wide economic moat backed by high physical store ownership (~89%), strong scale advantages in a fragmented $1 trillion home improvement sector, and exemplary capital deployment discipline, even as gross margin faces minor cost pressures and the balance sheet carries significant financial leverage.

## 1. Owner Earnings and Cash Flow Quality vs. Adjusted EPS

Evaluating Lowe's underlying economic earning power through Warren Buffett’s Owner Earnings framework offers a far more accurate assessment of financial health than relying on reported GAAP or non-GAAP EPS figures. For the nine months ended October 30, 2026, Lowe's reported Net Cash Provided by Operating Activities of $8.690 billion. Deducting Capital Expenditures of $1.664 billion incurred over the same nine-month period yields a baseline Owner Earnings (Free Cash Flow) figure of $7.026 billion. Under strict Owner Earnings principles, Stock-Based Compensation expense ($204 million for 9M FY2026) must not be added back to free cash flow nor deducted again from net income, as share-based payment expense has already been properly recognized as an operating cost in arriving at Net Income ($5.699 billion for 9M FY2026).

For Q3 FY2026, Lowe's reported GAAP Diluted EPS of $2.98 (up 3.5% from $2.88 in Q3 FY2025) and Adjusted Diluted EPS of $3.10 (up 1.3% from $3.06 in Q3 FY2025). The variance between GAAP and non-GAAP metrics stems from adjustments itemized in Exhibit 99.1:
*   **Acquisition Intangible Amortization:** Pre-tax expenses of $96 million ($0.17 per share pre-tax; $0.12 per share after-tax based on a $0.05 tax benefit) associated with intangible asset amortization from the acquisitions of FBM and ADG. In Q3 FY2025, acquisition-related transaction costs and intangible amortization totaled $129 million pre-tax ($0.18 per share after tax).

A vital insight for value analysts is that both reported GAAP Diluted EPS ($2.98) and Adjusted Diluted EPS ($3.10) incorporate a temporary, non-operating tailwind from $250 million in pre-tax IEEPA tariff refunds. To understand the precise net EPS impact, we examine the tax mechanics: dividing the $250 million pre-tax refund across ~560 million diluted shares yields a pre-tax benefit of ~$0.446 per share. Applying the company's full-year effective tax rate of approximately 24.5% results in a net after-tax cash benefit of $188.75 million ($250M × [1 - 0.245]), which translates directly to $0.337—or approximately **$0.34 per share**—in net earnings contribution. 

Excluding this $0.34 non-operating tariff refund, Lowe's normalized operational EPS for Q3 FY2026 was approximately **$2.64 on a GAAP basis** ($2.98 - $0.34) and **$2.76 on an Adjusted basis** ($3.10 - $0.34). Striping away the tariff windfall reveals that underlying quarterly organic earnings power is more moderate than headline numbers indicate.

## 2. Durability of the Economic Moat

Lowe's operates within a highly consolidated dual-monopoly market structure alongside its major primary competitor. This dual-duopoly commands significant economies of scale, immense purchasing leverage over manufacturers, nationwide logistics infrastructure, and dominant brand awareness, creating formidable barriers to entry in the U.S. retail home improvement industry.

Management continues to enhance this moat through the disciplined execution of its "Total Home Strategy," which focuses on five core operational pillars as outlined in the Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended January 30, 2026:
1.  **Drive Pro Penetration:** Expanding product assortments, enhancing job-site delivery capabilities, broadening the Pro extended aisle, and integrating the specialized branch networks of FBM and ADG to capture larger commercial and residential contractors.
2.  **Accelerate Online Sales:** Investing in omnichannel infrastructure, expanding Lowes.com catalog options, streamlining user interfaces, and offering flexible fulfillment options.
3.  **Expand Home Services:** Simplifying complex installation project solutions for homeowners via centralized selling teams and a vetted network of independent third-party contractors.
4.  **Create a Loyalty Ecosystem:** Cultivating customer retention through the MyLowe's Rewards ecosystem tailored for both DIY consumers and Pro trade specialists.
5.  **Increase Space Productivity:** Elevating product assortments through localization, balancing value-oriented private label brands (e.g., Kobalt, STAINMASTER, allen+roth) with national power brands, and expanding rural store offerings.

Q3 FY2026 operational indicators validate the execution of these initiatives:
*   **Omnichannel Growth:** Online sales surged 17.4% year-over-year, marking the third consecutive quarter of digital sales growth exceeding 15%.
*   **Geographic Sales Depth:** Comparable sales of +1.8% were supported by positive comp store growth in 12 of Lowe's 15 geographic operating regions.
*   **Pro Customer Reach:** Commercial and residential Pro market penetration expanded dramatically through the integration of FBM and ADG, adding over 540 specialized branch locations across the U.S. and Canada to complement the core retail store footprint.

Lowe's physical real estate backing provides an structural asset shield. As of January 30, 2026, approximately 89% of Lowe's 1,759 store locations were owned (including stores situated on long-term ground leases). Ownership of nearly nine-tenths of its physical infrastructure insulates Lowe's operating model from commercial rent inflation, contains real estate expense growth, and provides superior balance sheet asset protection relative to pure-lessee competitors.

### Equity Deficit & Return on Equity (ROE) Analysis
As of October 30, 2026, Lowe's reported a total shareholders' deficit of $6.408 billion and an accumulated deficit of $7.215 billion. From a financial analysis perspective, traditional Return on Equity (ROE) is rendered mathematically negative and analytically meaningless. Specialists must note that this negative accounting equity is not the product of cumulative operating losses. Rather, it reflects decades of aggressive, value-accretive share repurchases financed through robust cash flows and low-cost debt issuances, which reduced accounting equity while significantly boosting per-share earning power.

### Liquidity & Net Leverage Assessment
While cash flow generation remains high ($8.690 billion in 9M FY2026 operating cash flow), leverage requires continuous monitoring. Gross debt obligations stand at $37.548 billion ($34.478 billion long-term debt plus $3.070 billion current maturities). Accounting for $3.428 billion in Cash and Cash Equivalents, Lowe's carries **Net Debt of $34.120 billion**. 

Net interest expense reached $1.132 billion over the first nine months of FY2026, with management updating full-year net interest expense guidance to ~$1.5 billion (improved from ~$1.6 billion). Generating annualized operating income in excess of $10 billion ensures strong interest coverage (>6.8x), though refinancing incoming maturities without inflating borrowing costs remains vital for maintaining net income margins.

## 8. What Would Change Our View

The following observable operational or financial developments would demand a fundamental reassessment of our investment stance:

1.  **Pro Customer Comp Degradation:** Failure to integrate FBM and ADG branch operations effectively, leading to flat or negative Pro comp sales and market share losses to primary home improvement competitors.
2.  **Structural Gross Margin Erosion:** A sustained drop in Gross Margin below 32.0% resulting from unmitigated vendor price increases, escalating tariffs without pass-through pricing power, or elevated promotional clearance activity.
3.  **Refinancing & Interest Strain:** Escalation of net debt maturities without proportional operating cash flow expansion, pushing annualized net interest expense above $1.6 billion and straining interest coverage.
4.  **Macro Housing Stagnation:** Prolonged contraction in comparable store sales caused by falling home turnover rates, declining home price appreciation, and weak consumer discretionary spending on large home projects.
