Between 2002 and 2004, he accumulated a landmark stake in NetEase, becoming the company's second-largest individual shareholder after founder William Ding Lei, an investment that yielded massive long-term capital appreciation.

H&H's management style exemplifies Duan’s "do less, do well" investment philosophy. The entity maintains extreme portfolio concentration, multi-year holding periods, and exceptionally low turnover, focusing capital almost exclusively on moat-protected economic compounders such as Apple Inc., Berkshire Hathaway Inc., Alphabet Inc., Kweichow Moutai, and Tencent Holdings Ltd.

Duan maintains an active qualitative presence on the Chinese investor platform Xueqiu (Snowball) under the handles "@大道至简" and "大道无形我有型." His concise commentary on business economic moats and corporate management is widely studied by institutional and retail value investors alike. ## 4. The Biggest Moves, Valued: Owner Earnings, DCFs, and Margins of Safety

To analyze the strategic logic driving Duan Yongping's portfolio adjustments, DeepValues evaluates underlying business cash flows using ten years of SEC XBRL filings (Forms 10-K and 10-Q). 

Under the DeepValues framework, Owner Earnings are defined as:
$$\text{Owner Earnings} = \text{Operating Cash Flow} - \text{Maintenance Capex} - \text{Stock Compensation}$$

A critical analytical nuance lies in the structural definition of Maintenance Capex, calculated as:
$$\text{Maintenance Capex} = \min(\text{Capital Expenditures}, \text{Depreciation \& Amortization})$$

Capital spent above Depreciation & Amortization (D&A) represents growth investment. Subtracting *all* capex provides a stricter liquidity view. When capital expenditures exceed D&A by extraordinary margins—such as in corporate buildouts of AI infrastructure—evaluating both metrics reveals whether growth spending is building true economic value or functioning as mandatory defensive capital expense.

All DCF models employ a standard 10.0% discount rate, project growth over Years 1–5 ($g$), fade growth toward 2.5% across Years 6–10, and apply a 2.5% perpetual terminal rate.
