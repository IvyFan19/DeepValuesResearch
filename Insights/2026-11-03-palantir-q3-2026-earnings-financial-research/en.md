## Owner Earnings and Free Cash Flow over Adjusted EPS

A rigorous application of value investing principles requires evaluating a business through an uncompromised owner earnings lens, explicitly rejecting management’s reliance on non-GAAP constructs such as "Adjusted EPS" ($0.44 in Q3 2026) and "Adjusted Income from Operations" ($1.427 billion). These non-GAAP figures routinely add back real, ongoing economic costs—most notably stock-based compensation (SBC)—creating an inflated baseline of economic profitability.

Stock-based compensation represents a genuine expense to equity holders through the dilution of corporate ownership. Per Exhibit 99.1 to the press release dated November 2, 2026, Palantir recognized SBC expenses of $318.9 million for the three months ended September 30, 2026, and $785.7 million for the nine-month period then ended. Under standard accounting rules, SBC is correctly categorized as an operating expense in the Condensed Consolidated Statements of Operations, reducing GAAP Net Income to $1.128 billion for the quarter. 

Because stock-based compensation is a non-cash expense, management re-adds SBC when calculating Cash Flow from Operations ($1.205 billion in Q3 2026) and Adjusted Free Cash Flow ($1.216 billion, which adjusts for $19.5 million in employer payroll taxes on SBC and $8.0 million in capital expenditures). However, in evaluating fundamental owner earnings—the true cash return generated by business operations attributable to equity holders—SBC must *never* be added back to GAAP net income, as doing so treats equity dilution as if it were costless.

## Durability of the Economic Moat

Palantir’s widening economic moat rests upon exceptional switching costs and high installation barriers across its four core software platforms: Foundry, Gotham, Apollo, and AIP. As detailed in the Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2025, Palantir provides foundational data infrastructure. The architectural core of these platforms is the Palantir Ontology—a dynamic software layer that maps disparate enterprise data sources into operational contexts, binding business logic, algorithms, automated action triggers, and artificial intelligence models into a unified system.

When an enterprise integrates its core operational workflows into the Palantir Ontology, replacing the software becomes operationally disruptive and financially prohibitive. On the Q3 2026 earnings call transcript, Chief Technology Officer Sham Sankar emphasized that the fundamental unit of an enterprise is not the individual knowledge worker, but the workflow itself. By utilizing AIP as an enterprise harness, clients deploy AI models directly into production pipelines to generate measurable operational return on investment (ROI).

Technical disclosures from management demonstrate how customer switching costs are expanding beyond basic enterprise software lock-in into proprietary AI model weights. CTO Sham Sankar highlighted that post-training open-weight models on operational data within the Ontology enables superior, domain-specific performance at lower cost. For example:
*   **L3Harris** fine-tuned open-source models on its proprietary Ontology data, producing a sovereign model that outperformed its prior frontier model in under 48 hours while reducing execution costs by 95%.
*   **McCarthy Building Companies** implemented post-trained Neatron on 4-billion parameter models to automate contract risk review and request-for-information (RFI) classification, saving an estimated 30,000 hours annually.

This architectural integration is accelerating commercial adoption. Per Exhibit 99.1, U.S. commercial revenue grew 148% year-over-year and 29% sequentially to $984 million in Q3 2026. On the earnings call, Chief Revenue Officer Ryan Taylor illustrated how AIP Bootcamps compress sales cycles into rapid contract conversions:
*   An industrial materials company converted a $3 million pilot from Q2 2026 into a $29 million annual contract value (ACV) deal in Q3 2026.
*   A global athletic brand converted a $1.66 million pilot into an estimated $21 million ACV contract.
*   A home appliance manufacturer converted a $1 million pilot into a $3 million ACV deal in Q2 2026, expanding to a $15 million ACV contract by Q3 2026.

In the public sector, defense integration provides a durable baseline. U.S. government revenue expanded 96% year-over-year to $953 million in Q3 2026. Key government programs—such as the U.S. Army awarding Palantir a prime agreement to produce eight TITAN (Tactical Intelligence Targeting Access Node) ground station systems and the expansion of the Maven program past 100,000 active users—reflect deep defense anchoring. Customer retention across all segments remains robust, with net dollar retention reaching 165% in Q3 2026 (up 800 basis points sequentially per Exhibit 99.1).
