# Amgen Q3 2026 Analysis: Owner Earnings, Capital Allocation, and Intrinsic Value Under a Buffett Framework

In the third quarter of 2026, Amgen Inc. demonstrated robust operational volume growth across its core commercial execution, offset by structural headwinds from mature biological patent losses and expanding government price regulation. Total revenues grew 8% year-over-year to $10.4 billion, propelled by an 8% expansion in product sales that was driven entirely by volume gains across growth pillars such as Repatha (+37%), EVENITY (+39%), UPLIZNA (+191%), and IMDELLTRA (+128%). However, long-term owner wealth creation remains caught between this high-volume momentum across 17 growth therapies and severe pricing and volume erosion in legacy blockbusters—specifically Prolia (-45%) and XGEVA (-47%) due to international biosimilar competition, alongside mandatory U.S. Medicare Part D price reductions on Enbrel (-14% net price drop) under the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA). While GAAP net income rose 20% year-over-year to $3,864 million, quarterly operating cash flow contracted from $4,684 million in Q3 2025 to $4,241 million in Q3 2026, and Free Cash Flow declined to $3,654 million due to working capital timing around customer rebates and collections. Burdened by $54.0 billion in total debt outstanding against $13.2 billion in cash and cash equivalents, Amgen’s intrinsic value growth depends on management’s ability to pay down leverage and efficiently reinvest owner capital into high-conviction pipeline programs like MariTide without compromising balance sheet strength.

## Owner Earnings and Free Cash Flow over Adjusted EPS

To evaluate Amgen’s cash generation through Warren Buffett’s owner-oriented framework, investors must look past Wall Street’s preferred non-GAAP adjusted metrics and scrutinize the underlying cash earnings of the enterprise. For the third quarter of 2026, Amgen reported GAAP Net Income of $3,864 million, an increase of 20% year-over-year from $3,216 million in Q3 2025. GAAP Diluted EPS grew 19% year-over-year to $7.08 from $5.93. Conversely, non-GAAP Diluted EPS was reported at $6.45, up 14% from $5.64 in the prior-year period.

The divergence between GAAP net income and non-GAAP metrics stems from several significant adjustments detailed in the GAAP to Non-GAAP Reconciliations of the Q3 2026 earnings release:

*   **Acquisition-Related Amortization:** Non-GAAP cost of sales excludes $881 million in acquisition-related expenses for Q3 2026 (down from $1,420 million in Q3 2025). Management notes in footnote (a) that these non-cash adjustments relate primarily to the amortization of intangible assets acquired in business combinations, such as the October 2023 purchase of Horizon Therapeutics plc.
*   **Net Gains on Equity Investments:** Non-GAAP reconciliations strip out market value fluctuations in equity holdings. For Q3 2026, GAAP income includes $1,465 million in pre-tax net unrealized gains from equity investments (compared to $1,963 million in Q3 2025), which was deducted from GAAP figures to arrive at non-GAAP results. Notably, the $1,174 million figure cited in the release reconciliations represents the nine-month YTD pre-tax adjustment, rather than a Q3 post-tax figure.
*   **Intangible Asset Impairments:** GAAP operating expenses in Q3 2026 reflect $59 million in non-cash intangible asset impairments, compared to $400 million recognized in Q3 2025 for Otezla.

From an owner's perspective, Cash Flow from Operations provides a more authentic baseline than Net Income or Non-GAAP EPS. In the third quarter of 2026, Amgen generated $4,241 million in operating cash flow, compared to $4,684 million in Q3 2025. After deducting capital expenditures of $587 million (reported as $0.6 billion in summary cash flow tables), reported Free Cash Flow stood at $3,654 million ($3.7 billion), down from $4,248 million ($4.2 billion) in Q3 2025. Per management’s commentary in the earnings release, this quarterly cash flow contraction was driven primarily by changes in working capital timing, as the prior-year period benefited significantly from favorable rebate timing and collection schedules.

When calculating true Owner Earnings, value investors must properly account for Stock-Based Compensation (SBC). Stock-based compensation is a real operating expense that dilutes existing owners' equity over time. Under GAAP accounting, SBC is already recognized as an operating expense within cost of sales, R&D, and SG&A, thereby reducing GAAP Net Income. Therefore, when evaluating owner earnings starting from GAAP Net Income, SBC should never be added back or deducted a second time. However, when evaluating owner earnings starting from Cash Flow from Operations—where GAAP cash flow statements routinely add back SBC as a non-cash item—analysts must subtract SBC to reflect the true ongoing economic cost of compensating personnel with equity.

## Durability of the Moat

Amgen’s competitive advantage (economic moat) rests on its proprietary biopharmaceutical intellectual property, complex biological manufacturing infrastructure, and commercial scale. However, product sales dynamics in Q3 2026 illustrate a moat undergoing structural transition: rapid volume expansion in newer growth assets is actively working to offset severe erosion in legacy biological blockbusters.

### Growth Pillars

Product sales grew 8% overall during Q3 2026 to $9,883 million, up from $9,137 million in Q3 2025. Growth was anchored by 17 products delivering double-digit volume growth, with 17 products annualizing at over $1 billion in sales based on Q3 results. Key commercial drivers reported in the Q3 2026 release include:

*   **Repatha (evolocumab):** Sales increased 37% year-over-year to $1,085 million, driven primarily by strong global volume demand.
*   **EVENITY (romosozumab-aqqg):** Sales expanded 39% year-over-year to $751 million, propelled by continuous volume expansion.
*   **UPLIZNA (inebilizumab-cdon):** Sales surged 191% year-over-year to $451 million, driven by commercial adoption in rare disease indications.
*   **IMDELLTRA (tarlatamab-dlle):** Sales reached $406 million, representing a 128% year-over-year increase driven by rapid volume adoption in extensive-stage small cell lung cancer (ES-SCLC).
*   **TEZSPIRE (tezepelumab-ekko):** Sales grew 43% year-over-year to $538 million due to robust volume expansion in severe asthma.
*   **BLINCYTO (blinatumomab):** Sales increased 27% year-over-year to $496 million, supported by volume gains in oncology.

### Moat Erosion & Headwinds

Counterbalancing these growth pillars are two powerful sources of moat degradation: international biosimilar competition on key biologics and mandatory U.S. government price setting under the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA).

*   **Biosimilar Impact on Bone Health Blockbusters:** Prolia (denosumab) sales fell 45% year-over-year to $632 million in Q3 2026 (down from $1,139 million in Q3 2025) due to global biosimilar entries. Per the Q3 2026 release, this decline was driven by a 23% drop in volume, with the remainder split equally between net selling price reductions and changes in estimated sales deductions. Similarly, XGEVA (denosumab) sales fell 47% year-over-year to $288 million (down from $539 million in Q3 2025), driven by 32% lower volume and an 11% net selling price decline.
*   **Regulatory Pricing Dynamics (IRA):** Enbrel (etanercept) reported a 5% increase in total reported sales to $611 million. However, underlying pricing dynamics demonstrate severe regulatory pressure: net selling prices fell 14% year-over-year, reflecting the mandatory impact of U.S. Medicare Part D price reductions effective in 2026 under the IRA alongside lower volume. Top-line revenue growth was artificially buffered because the 14% price decline and lower volume were offset by a 15% favorable change in estimated sales deductions and an 8% increase in wholesale inventory levels.

### Pipeline Reinvestment

To defend against patent expirations and rebuild its moat, Amgen increased GAAP Research & Development (R&D) expenses by 11% year-over-year to $2,111 million in Q3 2026 (representing 21.4% of product sales). Capital reinvestment is concentrated in high-prospect pipeline candidates, most notably MariTide (maridebart cafraglutide), an antibody-peptide conjugate targeting GLP-1 and GIPR. Management outlined extensive Phase 3 MARITIME trial rollouts across obesity, overweight, Type 2 diabetes (T2D), cardiovascular outcomes, heart failure, and obstructive sleep apnea.

## Intrinsic Value versus Price and Margin of Safety

Calculating intrinsic value requires weighing high-margin, commercial biopharmaceutical cash flows against structural patent drop-offs, clinical trial risks, and fixed debt liabilities.

### Margin of Safety Requirement

Applying Warren Buffett's framework requires a conservative margin of safety when evaluating biopharmaceutical businesses with substantial debt. Although Amgen generates dependable cash flows, the presence of $54.0 billion in total debt, ongoing pipeline clinical hold risks, and regulatory requests for market withdrawals (TAVNEOS) requires a significant valuation discount before declaring a prudent entry point.
