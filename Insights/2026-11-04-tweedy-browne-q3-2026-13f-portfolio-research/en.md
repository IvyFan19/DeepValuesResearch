### The Owner Earnings DCF Framework
Under Graham-and-Dodd value methodology, reported accounting net income rarely reflects true economic cash generation due to non-cash charges and variable capital expenditures. Tweedy, Browne utilizes an **Owner Earnings** model defined as:

$$\text{Owner Earnings} = \text{Reported Net Income} + \text{Depreciation \& Amortization} - \text{Maintenance Capital Expenditures}$$

Where *Maintenance Capital Expenditures* represent the capital investments required to maintain the company’s competitive position and physical asset capacity.

The Owner Earnings figure is then discounted using a conservative hurdle rate (typically a high single-digit or low double-digit discount rate) to account for risk, without relying on speculative terminal growth assumptions.

### Reverse DCF Mechanics
While standard DCF models attempt to project future cash flows to determine intrinsic value, a **Reverse DCF** model reverses this process. By taking the current market price and solving for the cash flow growth rate required to justify that price, value investors can determine market expectations:

$$\text{Current Market Price} = \sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{\text{Owner Earnings}_0 \times (1 + g)^t}{(1 + r)^t} + \frac{\text{Terminal Value}}{(1 + r)^n}$$

Where:
* $g$ = Implied Owner Earnings growth rate
* $r$ = Required discount rate
* $n$ = Valuation horizon

If the market's implied growth rate ($g$) is negative or zero while the business generates stable owner earnings, the security trades at an attractive margin of safety.
