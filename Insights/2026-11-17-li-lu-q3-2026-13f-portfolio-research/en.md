### Biographical Background & Key Milestones
Li Lu (age 58) was born on April 6, 1966, in Tangshan, China. His formative years were defined by remarkable hardship: he survived the devastating 1976 Tangshan earthquake, and during the Cultural Revolution of the 1960s, both of his parents were sent to government labor camps, forcing him to grow up moving through multiple orphanages and caretakers. He began his higher education at Nanjing University in 1985, studying physics before transferring to economics. In 1989, Li Lu became a key student organizer during the Tiananmen Square protests, helping coordinate student activities and taking part in a hunger strike. Following the government crackdown on the movement, he fled China to France before immigrating to New York City at age 23.

In 1990, Li Lu published his autobiography detailing his experiences in China, titled *Moving the Mountain: My Life in China*, which subsequently inspired a 1994 feature-film documentary. After settling in the United States, he completed an unprecedented academic path at Columbia University. Over a six-year period, he simultaneously earned three degrees in 1996: a Bachelor of Arts (BA) in economics, a Master of Business Administration (MBA), and a Juris Doctor (JD)—making him one of the first students in Columbia’s history to receive all three degrees concurrently. Maintaining a strong allegiance to his alma mater, Li Lu served as a Columbia University trustee from 2017 to 2024 and donated $15 million in 2025 to renovate Columbia Law School's library, which was renamed The Li Lu Law Library. In May 2021, he co-founded The Asian American Foundation (TAAF) to combat anti-Asian hate and empower AAPI communities, serving as its Inaugural Board Chair from 2020 to 2024 and remaining on its board. He also serves as a trustee of the California Institute of Technology (Caltech).

Munger met Li Lu on Thanksgiving in 2003, became an investor in Himalaya Capital, and served as a mentor and close friend. In 2010, Li Lu translated and published the Chinese edition of *Poor Charlie's Almanack: The Wit and Wisdom of Charles T. Munger*, contributing its foreword.
