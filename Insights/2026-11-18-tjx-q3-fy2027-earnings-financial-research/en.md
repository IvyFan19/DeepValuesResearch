# TJX Companies (TJX) Q3 FY2027 Intrinsic Value Analysis: A Long-Term Owner’s Evaluation

While short-term market reactions often fixate on modest quarterly guidance adjustments or Wall Street consensus beats and misses, long-term value investors must focus on underlying business productivity: TJX's flexible, off-price business model continues to capture global retail market share, generate high returns on invested capital, and convert revenue into substantial owner earnings. ## 1. Owner Earnings, Cash Flow, and Accounting Reality

Evaluating cash flow generation for the thirty-nine weeks ended October 31, 2026, reveals the underlying economic productivity of TJX's business model. For the nine-month period, TJX reported GAAP net income of $4,395 million and generated net cash provided by operating activities of $5,042 million, while property additions (capital expenditures) totaled $1,722 million (Exhibit 99.1, Condensed Statements of Cash Flows). 

A complete accounting picture requires analyzing why operating cash flow ($5,042 million) significantly exceeded GAAP net income ($4,395 million) despite a massive seasonal inventory build of $2,592 million (-$2,592 million cash impact for 9M FY27). This cash flow strength was enabled by substantial non-cash add-backs, primarily non-cash depreciation and amortization of $1,025 million, share-based compensation of $154 million, and a deferred income tax provision of $90 million. More importantly, operating cash flow benefited from favorable working capital expansion: accounts payable grew by $1,708 million as vendor credit effectively financed the holiday inventory accumulation, while accounts receivable and other assets provided a cash inflow of $354 million and accrued expenses/other liabilities added $152 million. 

From a value investing perspective, stock-based compensation ($154 million for the nine-month period ended October 31, 2026) must be treated as a real corporate cost that dilutes shareholder equity. Because stock-based compensation is a non-cash item already charged inside Net Income, it must **never** be subtracted again from Net Income. However, when converting GAAP Operating Cash Flow to true owner cash generation, stock-based compensation must be subtracted alongside capital expenditures because it represents a real economic outlay of equity value. Starting from operating cash flow of $5,042 million, deducting $1,722 million in capital expenditures and $154 million in stock-based compensation yields true owner cash generation of $3,166 million for the nine-month period.

## 2. Durability of the Economic Moat

TJX's economic moat relies on structural cost advantages, a global opportunistic sourcing network, and an agile store operational model that creates a dynamic "treasure hunt" consumer experience. As detailed in the FY2026 Form 10-K, TJX maintains a buying organization of over 1,400 buyers sourcing merchandise across more than 100 countries from a universe of approximately 21,000 vendors. This massive scale allows TJX to acquire closeouts, order cancellations, and manufacturer overruns at 20% to 60% below full-price retail rates, operating with lower inventory commitments and buying far closer to need than traditional retailers.

Divisional performance in Q3 FY2027 demonstrates broad structural strength across retail banners and geographic regions:

- **Marmaxx (U.S.):** Comparable store sales grew +2% (on top of +6% in Q3 FY2026), with net sales rising 3% to $9,329 million.
- **HomeGoods (U.S.):** Comparable store sales grew +5%, driving net sales up 8% to $2,737 million.
- **TJX Canada:** Comparable store sales increased +6%, pushing net sales up 8% (9% on a constant currency basis) to $1,607 million.
- **TJX International (Europe & Australia):** Comparable store sales expanded +8%, driving net sales growth of 11% (12% constant currency) to $2,279 million.
- **Consolidated TJX:** Total comparable store sales rose +4% against overall net sales growth of 6% to $15,952 million.

A structural pillar of TJX's moat is its flexible store layout. Unlike traditional department stores burdened by fixed departmental walls and rigid display fixtures, TJX stores feature open floor layouts without permanent partition walls. This enables store managers and merchants to rapidly expand high-demand inventory categories and contract underperforming departments in real time, maximizing sales density per square foot and turning inventory quickly.

## 9. What Would Change Our View

1. **Inventory Growth Outstripping Sales:** A persistent trend over multiple consecutive quarters where inventory grows significantly faster than net sales, signaling a loss of buying discipline or ineffective inventory management.
2. **Structural Gross Margin Contraction:** A sustained decline in normalized gross profit margins (excluding short-term freight or fuel volatility) below historical baselines, indicating erosion of buying power over vendor networks.
3. **International Underperformance:** Failure of international expansion units (TJX International or TJX Canada) to maintain return on invested capital comparable to domestic Marmaxx and HomeGoods banners.
4. **Capital Allocation Shift:** A departure from organic store reinvestment, debt paydown, and share buybacks in favor of large, overpriced, dilutive M&A acquisitions outside core off-price retail expertise.
