## 1. The Investor: Philosophy, Fund, and Lifetime Work

### Biography & Background
Cathie Wood, age 70 (born Catherine Duddy in 1955 in Los Angeles, California), is an American investor and the Chief Executive Officer and Chief Investment Officer of ARK Investment Management. Raised by Irish immigrant parents—her father serving as a radar systems engineer in the Irish Army and United States Air Force—she graduated from Notre Dame Academy in Los Angeles in 1974. In 1981, she graduated *summa cum laude* from the University of Southern California with a Bachelor of Science degree in finance and economics. During her time at USC, she studied under renowned economist Arthur Laffer, who became her mentor and helped launch her financial career.

Wood began her professional career in 1977 as an assistant economist at Capital Group. In 1980, she moved to New York City to join Jennison Associates, where she spent 18 years serving as chief economist, equity analyst, portfolio manager, and managing director. During the early 1980s, she famously debated economist Henry Kaufman on the trajectory of interest rates, arguing that rates had peaked. In 1998, she co-founded the NYC-based hedge fund Tupelo Capital Management alongside Lulu C. Wang. In 2001, she joined AllianceBernstein as CIO of Global Thematic Strategies, managing $5 billion in assets over a 12-year tenure.

### Establishment of ARK Invest
In 2014, after AllianceBernstein deemed her vision for actively managed exchange-traded funds (ETFs) focused on disruptive innovation too risky, Wood departed to establish ARK Investment Management in New York City. The firm was named after the Ark of the Covenant, inspired by Wood's reading of the One-Year Bible. ARK’s initial four active ETFs were seeded with capital from Bill Hwang. In November 2021, ARK Investment Management relocated its principal corporate headquarters from New York to St. Petersburg, Florida.

### Investment Philosophy & Style
DeepValues Research classifies ARK Investment Management’s style strictly as thematic growth. Wood’s investment framework centers on identifying and allocating capital to companies driving "disruptive innovation." These thematic investments span several technological convergence platforms, including genomics, autonomous vehicles, artificial intelligence, energy storage, and blockchain technology. 

Unlike traditional institutional asset managers, ARK maintains a policy of open research, publishing weekly market commentaries and annual *Big Ideas* research reports. The firm’s active ETF structure results in high gross portfolio turnover, with daily position weight adjustments driven by intraday market fluctuations and fund creation/redemption dynamics.

### Career Performance Rollercoaster
Wood’s career has been defined by extreme performance cycles. Her signature victory was an early high-conviction call on Tesla between 2017 and 2020, during which the stock appreciated from $40 to $400 on a split-adjusted basis. In 2020, ARK’s flagship fund, the ARK Innovation ETF (ARKK), surged 170%, becoming the top-performing global equity fund with over $1 billion in assets and earning Wood accolades as the top stock picker of 2020. 

However, the fund experienced severe drawdowns during the 2021–2022 central bank rate-tightening cycle. ARKK fell 24% in 2021 and dropped approximately two-thirds of its value across 2021–2022, becoming the worst-performing equity fund covered by Morningstar in Q1 2022. For the 10-year period ending December 31, 2023, Morningstar ranked ARK Invest as the top "wealth destroyer" fund family, calculating $14 billion in net dollar value destruction. Subsequently, ARK funds experienced notable performance rebounds across 2025 (where ARKK gained 23.4% through Q3) and into early 2026, with Morningstar identifying four ARK funds among the top five performing stock ETFs of 2025.

## 4. The Biggest Moves, Valued

To evaluate whether Cathie Wood’s major rebalancing decisions align with disciplined capital allocation, DeepValues Research subjected the top moves to our standardized ten-year Owner Earnings Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) and Reverse DCF framework.

*Owner Earnings* are calculated according to Warren Buffett’s classic valuation formula: **Owner Earnings = Operating Cash Flow - Maintenance Capex - Stock Compensation**, where Maintenance Capex represents the lesser of total capital expenditure and depreciation & amortization. Operating cash flow serves as the entry point, from which maintenance capex (the capital required to keep business operations constant) and non-cash stock compensation (already an operational cost) are subtracted to yield baseline owner earnings.

## 8. Limitations of 13F Filings & Regulatory Disclaimers

### Disclosures and Institutional Filings Limitations
Institutional holdings reports filed via Form 13F with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) carry inherent structural limitations:
1.  **Delayed Visibility:** Disclosures reflect positions held on the final day of the calendar quarter (September 30, 2026) and are submitted up to 45 days after quarter-end (filed November 13, 2026).
2.  **Partial Asset Scope:** Filings reveal only long US-listed equity positions. They completely omit short positions, cash reserves, foreign non-US equities, fixed-income securities, derivative contracts, and private investments.
3.  **No Intra-Quarter Tracking:** Transactions executed between quarterly reporting dates are invisible; reported holdings may have been modified or completely liquidated prior to the filing publication date.
