## The Investor: Philosophy, Fund and Lifetime Work

Bridgewater Associates was founded in 1975 by Ray Dalio (age 76). Operating today out of Westport, Connecticut, the firm evolved from a small institutional advisory practice into the world's largest hedge fund manager. In its formative years, Dalio advised commercial and agricultural clients—including cattle ranchers, grain producers, and corporate giants such as McDonald's—on hedging operational cash flows using commodity futures contracts. To disseminate its macroeconomic research, Bridgewater introduced its daily subscription publication, *Daily Observations*, which gained wide industry readership and built the firm's reputation for top-down economic modeling. This analytical rigor eventually attracted major institutional asset owners, including the corporate pension funds of Eastman Kodak and the World Bank.

### Systematic Macro Philosophy and the "D-Process"

Bridgewater's investment engine is built on systematic macro investing. The firm evaluates global financial markets by synthesizing top-down quantitative views on interest rates, inflation expectations, foreign exchange dynamics, real GDP growth cycles, and balance sheet deleveraging. A central foundation of Dalio’s economic framework is the distinction between ordinary business cycle recessions and structural debt deleveraging cycles. Dalio termed this balance sheet repair phase the "d-process" (deleveraging and deflationary process). Under the d-process model, when interest rates reach zero or structural debt burdens become unsustainable relative to incomes, standard monetary policy mechanisms fail, requiring coordinated monetary easing, debt restructuring, and fiscal intervention to prevent prolonged economic contraction.

To capture these multi-asset macroeconomic trends systematically, Bridgewater developed two flagship strategies:
* **Pure Alpha (Launched 1991):** Designed to generate tactical excess returns ("alpha") above broad market benchmarks across equity, fixed income, currency, and commodity markets.
* **All Weather (Launched 1996):** Pioneered the investment methodology known as risk parity. Rather than allocating capital evenly across asset classes, All Weather balances portfolio exposure by risk contribution, seeking consistent returns across shifting inflation and growth regimes.

### Historical Performance, Low-Rate Drag, and Ownership Transition

Bridgewater built its institutional legacy by preserving capital and generating positive absolute returns during major structural market disruptions. The firm delivered positive net performance during both the Black Monday crash of October 1987 and the 2008 global financial crisis, having warned of structural risks in the subprime mortgage sector as early as 2007. However, during the 2019–2024 period, Bridgewater's flagship Pure Alpha strategy experienced a relative performance drag. Prolonged ultra-low interest rates and global central bank quantitative easing compressed macroeconomic spreads, reducing systemic macro trading edges relative to a conventional 60/40 stock-bond benchmark.

To ensure institutional continuity, Dalio executed a multi-year governance transition. He stepped down as co-Chief Investment Officer (co-CIO) in September 2022, transferring operational control and voting rights to Bridgewater’s board of directors. Dalio completed his formal separation from Bridgewater in 2025 after selling his remaining equity stake and retiring from the firm's board. Following his departure, Dalio transitioned to managing his personal wealth through the Dalio Family Office (DFO), assuming the role of Chief Investment Officer. To manage DFO's global macro strategy, Dalio appointed former Bridgewater research head Steven Kryger as co-CIO alongside deputy CEO Alma DeMetropolis. Early 2026 disclosures revealed that the Dalio Family Office held approximately $503 million in U.S. equities, with over 75% of that capital allocated to gold-based exchange-traded funds.
