# Analyzing Duan Yongping’s November 2026 Strategy: Option Overlays, Buyback Discipline, and Valuation Reality

Explaining his rationale for holding back on open-market buying despite recent price momentum, he stated: "I haven't bought recently; I want to let the company buy back shares first, as I'm afraid driving up the price would force the company to spend more."

Crucially, this tactical operational pause does not represent a change in his long-term bullish stance on Pop Mart. Instead, it reflects strict alignment with Warren Buffett’s capital allocation principles regarding corporate share repurchases and owner-earnings efficiency. When a prominent investor aggressively accumulates shares on the secondary market, the resulting buying pressure can inflate the stock price. Higher market prices force corporate management to spend more cash per repurchased share, directly diminishing the net intrinsic value accretion generated for remaining long-term shareholders. By refraining from personal open-market accumulation—contrasting with his October 21, 2026 statement indicating he might continue buying—Duan allows corporate buybacks to execute at lower costs. From an analytical perspective, however, because his disclosure uses the word "recently" without stating an exact start date, specific purchasing cessation dates cannot be assumed.
