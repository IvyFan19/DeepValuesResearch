## 1. Owner Earnings and Cash Generation vs. Adjusted EPS

To evaluate Disney’s true economic cash generation capability, long-term investors must look beyond reported accounting earnings and dissect the divergence between GAAP Net Income, non-GAAP Adjusted EPS, Operating Cash Flow, and Free Cash Flow for the fiscal year ended October 3, 2026.

For FY2026, Disney reported GAAP diluted earnings per share of $5.63, reflecting an 18% decline compared to $6.85 in FY2025. Conversely, non-GAAP Adjusted EPS expanded 18% to $7.02, up from $5.93 in the prior year. This variance is explained by significant non-operating and non-cash accounting adjustments in both periods. In FY2026, reported GAAP net income was reduced by $1,456 million ($0.65 per share) in pre-tax restructuring and impairment charges—which included a $1,012 million impairment recorded on the company's 50% equity investment in A+E Global Media—as well as $1,284 million ($0.50 per share) in acquisition-related intangible amortization, and $422 million ($0.23 per share) in non-cash tax charges resulting from the Fubo and NFL transactions. Furthermore, results benefited from an extra operating week in Q4 (the 53rd week), which contributed an estimated $0.17 per share to full-year adjusted EPS ($1.6 billion in total revenue and $490 million in total segment operating income).

From a value investing perspective, Warren Buffett defines Owner Earnings as reported net income plus depreciation, depletion, and amortization, minus the capital expenditures required to maintain long-term competitive position. When calculating Owner Earnings directly from the cash flow statement, Stock-Based Compensation (SBC) requires meticulous accounting treatment. In FY2026, equity-based compensation rose to $1,645 million from $1,363 million in FY2025. While GAAP rules deduct SBC as an operating expense to arrive at Net Income, the Statement of Cash Flows adds back this non-cash expense to compute Operating Cash Flow ($19,270 million). Because stock compensation represents a real economic dilution cost to existing shareholders, SBC must be subtracted when deriving Owner Earnings directly from cash provided by operations.

To establish a true normalized cash generation baseline, investors must also account for extraordinary cash outflows. In FY2026, operating cash flows absorbed a $1,277 million cash drag for income taxes due to the payment of deferred U.S. federal and California state tax liabilities from fiscal 2024 and 2025 under California wildfire disaster tax relief. 

The step-down calculation from reported Operating Cash Flow to Normalized Owner Earnings is derived as follows:

$$\text{Unadjusted Owner Earnings} = \text{Operating Cash Flow } (\$19,270\text{M}) - \text{CapEx } (\$8,698\text{M}) - \text{SBC } (\$1,645\text{M}) = \mathbf{\$8,927\text{ million}}$$

$$\text{Normalized Owner Earnings} = \$8,927\text{M} + \text{Non-Recurring Tax Outflow } (\$1,277\text{M}) = \mathbf{\$10,204\text{ million}}$$

Contrasting this **$10,204 million ($10.20 billion)** normalized Owner Earnings figure against reported Free Cash Flow of **$10,572 million ($10.57 billion)** reveals that Disney’s underlying economic engine generated cash nearly identical to its headline free cash flow, even after fully burdening the business for stock dilution.

Forensic examination of cash flow line items reveals additional non-cash adjustments, led by $5,605 million in depreciation and amortization expense and $1,012 million in "Impairments of goodwill, produced and licensed content and other assets." Investors should note a crucial accounting distinction between this statement of cash flows line item and the restructuring charges on the income statement: the cash flow statement line reflects broader content and asset write-downs, whereas the $1,456 million pre-tax restructuring charge on the statement of operations includes a separate, specific $1,012 million impairment recognized on the A+E Global Media equity investment prior to its divestiture.

### Experiences Segment
The Experiences segment generated record full-year performance, delivering revenue of $39,566 million (up 9%) and operating income of $11,191 million (up 12%). Excluding the 53rd week (which contributed ~2 percentage points to revenue and operating income growth), the segment grew through guest volume expansion and domestic pricing power. In Q4 FY2026, per capita guest spending increased 4% across domestic parks and 5% across international parks. Domestic Parks & Experiences revenue rose 19% in Q4 (8% from the 53rd week), driving a 36% gain in domestic operating income to $1,252 million.

Growth was anchored by robust attendance at Walt Disney World and capacity expansion at Disney Cruise Line following the launches of the *Disney Destiny* (November 2025) and *Disney Adventure* (March 2026). International attendance expanded at Disneyland Paris (supported by *World of Frozen*), offsetting localized consumer softness and attendance declines at Shanghai Disney Resort and Hong Kong Disneyland.

### Entertainment & SVOD
The Entertainment segment achieved full-year revenue of $46,562 million (up 10%) and operating income of $5,443 million (up 16%). The primary engine of operating leverage was the scaling of direct-to-consumer streaming (Disney+ and Hulu). In Q4 FY2026, SVOD revenue surged 19% to $6,050 million, while SVOD operating income expanded to $824 million from $402 million in Q4 FY2025 (with the 53rd week contributing $160 million).

For the full year, SVOD operating margin reached 11.5%. Operating efficiency was driven by price increases, 10% subscriber growth, ad-tier scaling, and lower marketing costs. The business deepened engagement through international local originals (including *A Shop for Killers Season 2*, *Soy Luna: Let's Roll Again*, and Korean hit *The Remarried Empress*) and executed strategic app unification, bringing Hulu content, live TV, and creator video into Disney+.

### Sports (ESPN)
The Sports segment generated FY2026 revenue of $18,632 million (up 5%) and operating income of $3,094 million (up 7%). Q4 subscription and affiliate fee revenue rose 18% to $3,422 million, while advertising revenue grew 20% to $955 million. Viewership engagement reached multi-year highs across NFL *Monday Night Football* (up 20% aggregate viewership in Q4) and regular-season college football (Ohio State–Texas drew 14 million viewers on ABC).

On January 31, 2026, Disney consummated the NFL Transaction, acquiring NFL Network, NFL RedZone, and NFL Fantasy assets from NFL Enterprises LLC in exchange for a 10% non-controlling interest in ESPN (leaving Disney with an effective 72% interest and Hearst with 18%). The addition of RedZone and expanded College Football Playoff rights solidifies ESPN's live rights moat ahead of its inaugural broadcast of Super Bowl LXI in 2027.
