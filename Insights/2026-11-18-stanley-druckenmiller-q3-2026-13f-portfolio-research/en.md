## 4. The Biggest Moves, Valued

To evaluate how Druckenmiller’s portfolio maneuvers align with strict value discipline, DeepValues subjects each highlighted company to an Owner-Earnings Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) and Reverse DCF model built on 10-year SEC XBRL filings. 

Under the canonical Warren Buffett framework, owner earnings isolate the real distributable cash flow generated for equity owners:
$$\text{Owner Earnings} = \text{Operating Cash Flow} - \text{Maintenance Capex} - \text{Stock-Based Compensation}$$

*Maintenance capex* is rigorously modeled as $\min(\text{Capital Expenditures}, \text{Depreciation \& Amortization})$. Any capital expenditures exceeding D&A are classified as growth investments. All cash flows in the DCF models are discounted at a standardized **10.0% hurdle rate**, with 5 years of explicit forecast growth ($g$), a 5-year transition fade to 2.5%, and a 2.5% terminal growth rate.

## 6. What a Patient Investor Can Learn

Analyzing Duquesne Family Office's Q3 2026 trading activity reveals major strategic principles at the intersection of macro timing and fundamental equity analysis:

### The Analytical Collision: Static DCF Models vs. Macro Thematic Positioning
The primary tension in evaluating Druckenmiller's portfolio through a DeepValues lens is the direct clash between static Owner-Earnings DCF models and macro-thematic equity positioning. Standard DCF models are inherently backward-looking anchors; they extrapolate historical 3-to-10-year cash flow trends, enforcing a strict linear margin of safety. 

A macro investor like Druckenmiller operates under a fundamentally different paradigm. When sizing positions in cyclical or re-platforming tech companies like Seagate Technology (+180%) or Unity Software (+436%), Druckenmiller is explicitly pricing in non-linear macro inflections—such as a massive structural expansion in Total Addressable Market (TAM) driven by hyperscale AI hardware deployment or enterprise software re-platforming—that standard historical DCF models cannot project. Macro investors willingly accept negative static margins of safety because they are purchasing dynamic optionality on multi-year paradigm shifts.

### Capex Expansion as an Uncompromising Exit Trigger
Duquesne's full liquidation of Alphabet (GOOGL) demonstrates a clear rule regarding capital intensity. When wide-moat compounders transition from capital-light software monopolies into capital-intensive infrastructure operators—evidenced by Alphabet's TTM capex ballooning to $159.8B ($131.3B of which was growth capex above depreciation)—the cash flow conversion yield degrades. Druckenmiller's complete liquidation at fair value ($283.34 price vs $276.20 base intrinsic value) underlines a tactical refusal to lock up capital in mega-cap platforms whose return on invested capital (ROIC) is under pressure from massive infrastructure build-outs.

### Conviction Concentration and Aggressive Portfolio Pruning
Despite managing billions, Druckenmiller maintains extreme conviction concentration (31.5% in Natera, 68.4% in top 10 holdings) alongside rapid portfolio turnover (23.8% this quarter). Rather than holding underperforming positions, Duquesne demonstrates a relentless willingness to cut legacy theses (such as trimming Taiwan Semiconductor by 94% and selling out of United Airlines and Delta Air Lines) to free up capital for highest-conviction macro bets.
