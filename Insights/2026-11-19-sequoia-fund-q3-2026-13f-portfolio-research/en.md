## The biggest moves, valued

To determine whether Sequoia traded out of fundamental valuation discipline or strategic capital reallocation, we evaluate the economic cash flow generated by the fund's largest position moves using the DeepValues Owner-Earnings DCF framework.

In owner-earnings valuation, true economic cash flow is defined as:

*   **Owner Earnings = Operating Cash Flow - Maintenance Capital Expenditure - Stock-Based Compensation**

Where **Maintenance Capital Expenditure** is calculated as the lesser of total capital expenditure and depreciation & amortization. Spending above depreciation is classified as growth capital expenditure. While growth capex expands long-term earning power, treating it as maintenance upkeep understates true cash generation. However, if massive growth spending yields returns below the cost of capital, conservative valuation anchors become crucial.

### Structural Analysis of 13F Clone Performance Drag
The 13F clone strategy lagged the S&P 500 by 1.2 percentage points annually (+173.2% vs +199.3% cumulative total return). Value investors must understand the structural causes of this tracking divergence:

1.  **45-Day Regulatory Reporting Lag:** Form 13F disclosures are filed up to 45 days after quarter-end. A public clone buying 45 days late misses price movements that occurred during the operating quarter.
2.  **Exclusion of Non-US Holdings:** Form 13F filings cover only US-listed equities. Sequoia's actual portfolio includes international listings (such as European or Asian primary listings) that are omitted from 13F disclosures.
3.  **Exclusion of Cash Reserves & Interest Yields:** Institutional portfolios hold strategic cash balances. While cash creates drag during market rallies, it provides capital preservation during downturns. The 13F clone assumes 100% equity allocation, magnifying drawdowns (-34.7% max drawdown vs -23.9% for SPY).
4.  **Omission of Active Risk Hedges & Intra-Quarter Trades:** 13F filings capture static quarterly snapshots. They cannot reflect intra-quarter trading profits, fixed-income instruments, options, or short positions used for active portfolio hedging.
