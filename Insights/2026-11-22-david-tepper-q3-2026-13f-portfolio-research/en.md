## The Biggest Moves, Valued: Owner-Earnings DCF & Reverse DCF Analysis

DeepValues evaluates corporate equity using Warren Buffett's Owner Earnings framework combined with Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) and Reverse DCF modeling.

$$\text{Owner Earnings} = \text{Operating Cash Flow} - \text{Maintenance Capex} - \text{Stock-Based Compensation}$$

### Understanding the Owner Earnings Mechanics
1. **Stock-Based Compensation (SBC):** SBC is treated as a real economic expense. Although non-cash on the accounting cash flow statement, issuing shares dilutes existing owners. DeepValues subtracts SBC to reflect true economic cash available to shareholders.
2. **Maintenance Capex vs. Growth Capex:** Standard Free Cash Flow (FCF) penalizes a business by subtracting 100% of capital expenditures. Under Buffett's framework, **Maintenance Capex** is defined as the capital required to preserve existing competitive position and production capacity, capped at the lesser of total Capex or Depreciation & Amortization (D&A). Any Capex expended beyond D&A is categorized as **Growth Investment**—discretionary outlay designed to expand future earning power. Excluding growth investments from baseline maintenance charges prevents heavily investing technology companies from appearing artificially uncash-generative.
3. **Standardized Hurdle Rate:** A standard 10.0% discount rate is applied across all DCF models to establish a consistent hurdle rate across technology, cyclical semiconductor, and software enterprises.

## Closing Note: Limitations of Form 13F Filings

Form 13F reports are quarterly regulatory filings submitted to the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) by institutional investment managers managing over $100 million in qualifying assets. These filings are submitted up to 45 days following the end of each calendar quarter, presenting a delayed point-in-time snapshot of long US-listed equity holdings. 

Form 13F disclosures do not provide a complete representation of a fund's total balance sheet or net performance. They omit cash balances, short positions, non-US equity listings, corporate and sovereign debt, options strategies, and trades executed post quarter-end.
