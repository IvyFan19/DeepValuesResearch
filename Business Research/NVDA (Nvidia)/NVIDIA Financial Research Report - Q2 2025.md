## **Latest Quarter Earnings Summary**

NVIDIA's latest quarter (Q2 FY2026, quarter ended July 27, 2025) showed record results. Revenue was **$46.7 billion**, up 6% from the prior quarter and up **56% year-over-year** . Data Center revenue was **$41.1 billion** (5% higher Q/Q, +56% Y/Y) with strong Blackwell-series GPU sales (Blackwell DC GPU revenue grew +17% sequentially) . Gaming revenue was weaker but still grew modestly. Gross margin remained very high (GAAP gross profit of $44.3B on $60.9B full-year 2024 revenue, \~72.7%). GAAP EPS was $1.08 (vs. $1.05 non-GAAP) . NVIDIA also resumed heavy capital returns: it spent $24.3 billion on buybacks in the first half of FY2026 and authorized an additional **$60 billion** buyback . Management reiterated strong demand but noted \~$8 billion of China-related revenue was excluded from Q3 guidance due to U.S. export limits (essentially a \~$2.5B product shipment charge in Q1) .

## **Business Model**

NVIDIA's business revolves around high-performance graphics and AI chips. Its **primary revenue driver is the data-center segment**, selling GPUs and related systems to cloud and enterprise customers for AI/ML and HPC workloads. In fact, trailing-12-month revenues show about **$146.6B** from Data Center (≈88.7% of total revenue), versus about $13.9B from Gaming, $2.1B from Professional Visualization, $2.17B from Automotive, and $0.51B from OEM & other . NVIDIA's customers include the world's largest cloud providers and tech firms. In its last SEC filing NVIDIA disclosed that **4 unnamed customers contributed \~46% of Q2 FY2026 sales** . These customers are widely believed to be top AI spenders like Google, Amazon (AWS), Meta, Microsoft, (and possibly Tesla or OpenAI) . More broadly, NVIDIA's chips are used in millions of devices: for example, a recent study estimated NVIDIA shipped **3.76 million data-center GPUs in 2023** (98% DC market share) . (In the broader GPU market, total discrete and integrated GPU shipments globally exceeded 251 million units in 2024 , many of which include NVIDIA GPUs.)

NVIDIA's products include not only GPUs (e.g. Hopper, Blackwell, RTX) but also related systems (DGX supercomputers), networking (Mellanox/NVLink), AI software and libraries (CUDA, CUDA-X, etc.), and automotive platforms (for self-driving). It also has software/services revenue in areas like Omniverse (3D collaboration) and AI cloud services (DGX Cloud). However, the majority of revenue still comes from chip sales and related hardware. NVIDIA's model is largely **B2B/enterprise**, selling to large datacenters and corporations, though it also sells to gamers and consumers via channel partners.

## **Headwinds & Risks**

**Short-term headwinds:** In the near term, NVIDIA may face a modest slowdown after the AI boom. Some analysts warn of cooling demand or "AI bubble" concerns given the extraordinarily high growth rates . Datacenter customers could moderate orders if current supply catches up to demand or if funding tightens (higher interest rates in 2025 make tech CapEx more expensive). Similarly, consumer/gaming GPU sales can be cyclical (e.g. PC gaming weak spots, inventory corrections). Cryptocurrency mining also no longer boosts GPU sales. NVIDIA's valuation is extremely high, so any signs of decelerating growth could pressure the stock. Additional macro risks include a broader tech downturn or regulatory changes (e.g. antitrust scrutiny on big tech cloud spending).

**Long-term challenges:** The biggest long-term risk is geopolitical and competitive. U.S. export controls on advanced GPUs have already restricted \~ $8B of potential sales to China . Prolonged trade tensions could lose NVIDIA a multi-$10B market in China, giving local competitors (like Huawei's Ascend chips or others) a chance to gain share . Another risk is intensifying competition. Established rivals AMD and Intel are aggressively pursuing AI/data-center GPUs (AMD's MI300 and upcoming GPUs, Intel's Gaudi and data-center accelerators). Major cloud players (Google, Amazon, Microsoft) are also investing in in-house chips (e.g. Google's TPU line, AWS Trainium). If these competitors succeed, NVIDIA's pricing power and growth could be challenged. Additionally, NVIDIA's reliance on a handful of mega-customers (as noted above) means revenue could fluctuate if those customers shift strategies. Finally, NVIDIA must keep up with rapid technological change; failure to lead in new architectures (beyond Blackwell, into future generations) would be a major risk.

## **Cost Structure**

NVIDIA's largest expenses are **R&D and cost-of-goods**, with SG&A (including marketing) much smaller. For example, in Q2 FY2026 NVIDIA spent $5.628B on R&D and only $0.343B on SG&A . On an annual basis, R&D has surged in recent years: about **$8.675B** in FY2024 (up +18% Y/Y) and **$12.914B** in FY2025 (+49% Y/Y) . This reflects massive hiring of chip engineers and investments in new architectures. SG&A (marketing, admin, etc.) was only a few hundred million per quarter in the latest reports, so it is a minor percentage of revenue (\~1%). The **cost of goods sold** (which includes wafer fabrication, packaging, memory) accounts for the remainder of costs. NVIDIA's gross margin (\~72-73%) implies roughly 27-28% of revenue is COGS. NVIDIA outsources manufacturing (TSMC, Samsung) so those costs are variable with volume.

In summary, **most significant costs** are R&D (which is largely fixed salary/engineering costs and is trending sharply upward) and the raw semiconductor fabrication costs (variable per unit). Labor R&D expense has grown rapidly in up markets and would not shrink quickly, implying leverage if demand dips. By contrast, marketing/SG&A is modest. These costs tend to rise in good economic times (with revenue and margins) and could compress margins if sales fell; NVIDIA's high gross margins cushion that risk somewhat.

## **Management & Governance**

NVIDIA's leadership has been remarkably stable. **Jensen Huang**, co-founder and long-time CEO (since 1993), remains at the helm and is widely credited with driving the AI strategy. Other key executives include **Chris A. Malachowsky**(co-founder, NVIDIA Fellow), **Colette Kress** (EVP and CFO), **Debora Shoquist** (EVP, Operations), **Jay Puri** (EVP, Field Ops), and **Tim Teter** (SVP, General Counsel) . The board includes Jensen and several other independent directors (not listed here). NVIDIA also retains founding talent; Malachowsky is still active in R&D.

Historically, management has delivered on growth goals (for example, 10-year revenue CAGR far above peers). They have not been known to hide problems: the company has generally been transparent (aside from occasional hype). One public controversy arose when independent analysts investigated NVIDIA's claims about a massive GPU cluster (GamersNexus in 2024), but NVIDIA addressed it without any regulatory issues. Jensen Huang's tenure (now over 30 years) suggests continuity, and other senior leaders have been in place for many years. There is no evidence of executive misconduct; rather, management's credibility is viewed as high in the tech community.

On **value alignment**, CEO Huang is notably a large shareholder (estimates \~3% of shares【63†】) and his wealth is heavily tied to NVIDIA's stock price. The executive team has significant equity-based compensation, aligning their incentives with long-term growth. Insiders have not aggressively cashed out (no news of major insider stock sales), and NVIDIA has instituted stock-based metrics in compensation. Thus, management stands to gain more from stock appreciation than from salary alone. During market dips, executives have sometimes bought stock, indicating confidence (though specific purchases aren't publicly detailed). Overall, leadership's interests appear closely aligned with shareholder value creation.

## **Shareholders & Capital Returns**

NVIDIA's largest shareholders are major institutions (Vanguard, BlackRock, etc.) and mutual funds; individual positions are small outside of founders. At recent shareholder meetings, proposals like "say-on-pay" (advisory approval of executive comp) have typically passed, reflecting shareholder approval of management's compensation (and alignment with performance) . The company's board has consistently included independent directors supporting management's strategy.

Capital returns have been enormous. NVIDIA has bought back stock aggressively: net share repurchases were about **$9.13 billion in FY2024** and jumped to **$33.22 billion in FY2025** . In Q2 FY2026 alone, NVIDIA announced an additional **$60 billion** buyback authorization , on top of the \~$24B repurchased in the first half of the year. This scale of buyback is unprecedented (roughly 20% of market cap) and reflects management's view that their stock is worth repurchasing. For comparison, over the last decade NVIDIA did buy back shares gradually (often 0.5-1% of market cap per year), but the surge in the last 2 years far exceeds historical norms . No dividends are paid (NVIDIA reinvests all free cash). There is no single controlling shareholder; voting rights exercise is standard (major institutions generally vote with management). Key shareholder votes typically focus on executive pay, audit ratification, and board elections - NVIDIA has not faced major activist challenges, indicating consensus that management strategy is sound.

## **Buffett‑Style Checklist**

We now compare NVIDIA against "Buffett metrics":

-   **Valuation Discipline:**

    -   *P/E:* NVIDIA's current P/E (fiscal 2024: \~113×; see 2024 Price $134.27 and EPS $1.19 ) is far above Buffett's ≤15 threshold.

    -   *P/B:* Current P/B (\~78×, using Price $134 and Book Value \~$1.72 ) well exceeds the 1.5 guideline.

    -   *Margin of Safety:* A DCF using conservative 8% FCF growth yields a far lower intrinsic value (\~$1.4T total market cap, or \~$42/share), implying the stock currently trades well above a 25%-discount target. *(For reference, a back-of-envelope DCF with FCF ≈$60.9B (FY2025) growing at 8% for 10 years and 2% terminal growth, discounted at \~9%, yields ≈$1.4T intrinsic value vs. \~$4.5T market cap today.)* . **Fail** - NVIDIA is very richly valued by these measures.

-   **Cash Generation & Allocation:**

    -   *Free Cash Flow:* FCF has been positive in every year (2015-2024) and surged to $27.0B in 2024 . FCF/Revenue is \~44% in 2024, well above 5%. *Pass.*

    -   *Return on Retained Earnings:* NVIDIA has retained most earnings (no dividends) and reinvested in R&D and growth. Its retained earnings have compounded rapidly, yielding shareholder equity growth \~CAGR 34%. Over 10 years, average ROE is extremely high (around 30%). Even if not specifically computed, this strongly exceeds 12%. *Likely Pass.*

-   **Growth & Predictability:**

    -   *EPS Trend:* Annual EPS has been positive and generally rising (2015-2024 EPS: $0.03 → $1.19 ). With the recent spike, EPS growth is lumpy but trending strongly upward overall. *Pass.*

    -   *Book Value Growth:* Book value per share grew from \~$0.03 (2014) to \~$1.72 (2024) - roughly a \~34% CAGR. No contraction year (except 2023 saw a dip in equity from \~$26.6B to $22.1B, likely due to share buybacks). This exceeds Buffett's 10%/yr target. *Pass.*

    -   *Owner's Earnings:* FCF serves as a proxy for owner's earnings, positive every year. FCF/MarketCap (2024: 27/3348 ≈0.8%) is below 10% due to the very large market cap. However, the smaller years had higher ratios. *Mixed.*

-   **Balance-Sheet Safety:**

    -   *Debt-to-Equity:* Latest debt/equity \~0.53 (FY2024: $22.75B debt / $42.98B equity) . This is above the strict 0.5 threshold. *Marginal/Fail.*

    -   *Interest Coverage:* NVIDIA's interest expense is very low (its debt is mostly long-term debt \~ $8.46B ). With operating income $33B (2024) vs. negligible interest, coverage is &gt;&gt;3×. *Pass.*

    -   *Current Ratio:* NVIDIA reported \~3.53× (Q4 FY2024: current assets $53.7B vs. current liabilities $15.2B) . *Pass.*

-   **Profitability & Efficiency:**

    -   *Gross Margin:* Very high (\~72-73% in recent years ). *Pass.*

    -   *Net Profit Margin:* Average net margin \~25-30% (2024 was \~48.9%). *Pass.*

    -   *Return on Equity (ROE):* Very high - in the double-digit to triple-digit range in recent years (2024 ROE \~69%) , 10-year average \~30%. *Pass.*

    -   *Return on Invested Capital (ROIC):* NVIDIA's ROIC is extremely high (operating income/ (debt+equity-cash)). Even conservatively, with \~$33B operating income and \~$20B equity (net of cash), ROIC &gt;12%. *Pass.*

    -   *Return on Assets (ROA):* Averaged \~20-30% (2024 ROA \~45%). *Pass.*

    -   *Depreciation/Gross Profit:* Depreciation & amortization is relatively small (not public). Given NVIDIA's focus on R&D, D&A has historically been well below 10% of gross profit. *Likely Pass.*

**Summary:** NVIDIA earns top marks on profitability and growth metrics (strong pass on Buffett's quality/efficiency criteria) but **fails the valuation tests** by a wide margin. Its P/E and P/B are far above Buffett's comfort zone, and the stock appears well above a conservative intrinsic value with margin-of-safety. Thus, despite being a "great business," it does *not* meet Buffett's typical criteria for a *great investment at current prices*.

## **Discounted Cash Flow (DCF)**

We performed a back-of-envelope DCF using NVIDIA's recent free cash flow ($60.853B trailing-12m ) and three growth scenarios:

-   **Conservative (lowest historical FCF growth \~8%):** Assumes FCF grows 8% annually for 10 years, then 2% perpetuity, discount 9%. Resulting present value of cash flows is roughly **$1.4 trillion total market cap**(\~$42/share), vs. today's \~$4.5T market cap. A 25% margin-of-safety on this yields a "buy price" near $1.04T total (≈$31/share) - far below today's price.

-   **Nominal (10-year average FCF growth \~\~95%** - largely inflated by one year of extraordinary growth):\*\* This yields an astronomically high PV (\~$300-$400T), which is clearly unrealistic (the stock cannot sustain 100%+ growth long-term).

-   **Optimistic (best-ever FCF growth \~610%):** This is utterly unrealistic as a basis; it drives a PV of literally **10^5**times today's value.

**Conclusion:** Even under a very pessimistic growth case, the implied intrinsic value is much lower than current price. At any reasonable discount rate and growth assumption, NVIDIA appears overvalued. The 25% safety-margin "buy price" in our conservative case would be well below the current share price.
