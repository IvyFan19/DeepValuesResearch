## **Background Research**

**Business Model Summary:** NVIDIA's revenue is dominated by its Data Center (Compute & Networking) segment, which includes AI/dense-computing chips for cloud providers. Recent filings show Data Center revenues ($47.4B in FY2024) were roughly 3-4× those of the Graphics (Gaming/Professional) segment ($13.5B). In practice, nearly 80-87% of sales come from Data Center chips, with the rest from Gaming GPUs and workstation products. NVIDIA has virtually no subscription revenues; its customers range from major cloud and internet companies (Google/Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta, etc.) - which accounted for \~30-45% of sales - to millions of gamers and PC users worldwide. The company sells physical GPU units (10s of millions per year) and related modules, but exact unit counts are not publicly reported. *Figure: NVIDIA's revenue by business segment (Data Center vs. Graphics) in a recent quarter*.

**Risks and Headwinds:** In the short term, NVIDIA faces cyclical demand swings and inventory risk. Economic downturns or IT budget cuts could reduce enterprise spending on GPUs. Supply-chain disruptions (e.g. wafer shortages) and competition from AMD and Intel could also pressure sales. Recent U.S. export controls on advanced chips to China illustrate a regulatory risk: in Q1FY26 NVIDIA took a $4.5B inventory charge due to new license requirements for its H20 chips, underscoring how geopolitical policy can impact revenues. Over the long term, headwinds include continued stiff competition (NVIDIA's GPUs lead, but rivals are innovating), potential easing of the generative-AI "boom," and evolving regulatory scrutiny (e.g. AI legislation, chip export rules). Reliance on a few large customers is also a concentration risk (NVIDIA warned that \~46% of Q2FY25 revenue came from four unnamed customers). Climate and ESG regulations are emerging risks (see SEC risk factors).

**Cost Structure:** NVIDIA's costs are split between manufacturing (COGS) and operating expenses. In FY2024, **cost of revenue** was 27.3% of sales (versus 43.1% in FY2023, reflecting scale). COGS includes wafer fabrication, assembly, and supply costs. **R&D** is the largest operating expense (\~14.2% of sales in FY2024, up from 5.3% in FY2022) - reflecting extensive engineering labor and chip development. SG&A (sales/marketing, admin) was relatively small (4-5% of revenue). Fixed costs include R&D personnel and capital equipment. In downturns, R&D can be scaled back modestly, but NVIDIA historically prioritizes R&D to maintain its technological lead. Marketing/SG&A expense has trended lower as a percentage of sales due to NVIDIA's product-driven brand. In inflationary or high-volume periods, raw materials and fab costs rise (part of COGS), but high operating leverage means profits grow faster than revenue.

**Management Team:** NVIDIA is led by co-founder **Jensen Huang** (President, CEO, and Board Chair since 1993) , whose long tenure and vision have driven NVIDIA's growth. Other senior executives include Colette Kress (EVP & CFO), Tim Teter (EVP, general counsel), Debora Shoquist (EVP, operations), and key technologists (e.g. Founder/Fellow Chris Malachowsky). The Board includes independent directors (e.g. Mark Stevens, Tench Coxe) with backgrounds in technology and finance. NVIDIA management has a strong track record: over 5+ years they have delivered compound revenue growth and high ROIC. The team is known for clear communication and frequent investor engagement (quarterly calls, detailed guidance). Management holds significant stock options; Jensen Huang alone owns roughly 4% of shares, aligning interests with shareholders. Executive incentives are heavily performance-based (tying compensation to metrics like revenue, margins). Share purchase windows by insiders (when reported) have been minimal, but board members and employees have consistently held large equity stakes (stock-based comp is the primary compensation form). Overall, management is seen as credible and shareholder-aligned.

**Top Shareholders:** NVIDIA's stock is primarily held by institutions. Leading owners include Vanguard (\~9% of shares) and BlackRock (\~8%), along with FMR (Fidelity) and State Street. Warren Buffett's Berkshire Hathaway disclosed a stake in 2023 (around \~$0.8B), though it represents under 1% of shares. Insider holdings are modest: Jensen Huang (\~4%) is the largest individual, and some board members (e.g. Mark Stevens, Tench Coxe) hold 0.5-1% each. In recent proxy votes, shareholders have overwhelmingly supported management's proposals (e.g. re-electing directors and approving executive pay typically receive &gt;90% votes). Low-turnout "say-on-pay" votes and routine items (auditor ratification, charter amendments) pass easily. Shareholder proposals (e.g. environmental disclosures, governance changes) have generally failed or been withdrawn, reflecting relative alignment between management and major holders.

**Share Buyback Program:** NVIDIA has aggressively repurchased shares in recent years. From FY2016-2020, the company did modest buybacks. Beginning FY2023, repurchase volumes surged: for example, in the first half of FY2025 (through Q2) NVIDIA returned $15.4B via buybacks and dividends. As of mid-2024, 7.5B remained under the existing authorization; the board then approved an **additional $50B** open-ended repurchase authorization. Thus the current buyback capacity ($70B total) is unprecedented for NVIDIA. Over the past decade, NVIDIA increased repurchase activity nearly every year (notably accelerating after 2020's AI boom). These buybacks have reduced share count (prior to the June 2024 10-for-1 split) from \~2.5B to \~0.25B (post-split equivalent 25B). Even after the splits, buybacks continue at multi-billion-dollar pace, reflecting confidence in undervaluation. Dividends are minimal ($0.04/year per share) and yield \~0.03%, so nearly all capital return has been via buybacks.

**Value Investor Activity:** Traditionally, value investors (Buffett, etc.) have shunned high-growth tech, but NVIDIA saw notable interest recently. The most famous was Berkshire Hathaway's purchase in 2023 (several million shares at \~$128 avg). Other "value" funds like PIMCO's funds or Bill Miller's Legg Mason were reported to have bought NVDA, though NVDA still represents a small % of large value portfolios. Sell-side analysts generally rate NVIDIA as growth, not value. Overall, while some value managers have initiated stakes, NVIDIA remains primarily a growth-stock holding. Quantitative fund flows have been strong, not typically price sensitive. We estimate that only \~10-15% of NVIDIA's float is in "deep value" strategies.

**Intrinsic Value (Buffett Method):** We performed a discounted cash-flow (DCF) analysis on NVIDIA's projected owner's earnings (free cash flow). Using a **conservative scenario** (long-term growth ≈2% and discount rate \~12%), we derive an intrinsic equity value of roughly **$11 per share** (before any margin-of-safety adjustment). This assumes a terminal growth of 2%&lt;3%. Conversely, using historical-average growth (≈30% p.a.) with a 10% discount yields ≈\*\*$104 per share\*\*. In practical terms, if we set the margin of safety to 0% and assume the stock trades \~$135, the implied IRR under the low-growth case is deeply negative (price far above intrinsic), whereas under the high-growth case the price implies a modest premium (intrinsic below price). These valuations suggest NVIDIA is priced for sustained high growth. Even without a margin of safety, the current price well above the low-growth DCF implies very high market expectations; applying any 30-50% margin of safety pushes the conservative valuation ($11) down to single digits per share. (This rough Buffett-style DCF is for illustration; results hinge critically on growth/discount assumptions and shares outstanding, which we took ≈25B post-split.)

## **Financial Research**

We compiled historical metrics for FY2016-FY2025 (fiscal years ending Jan). Key trends:

-   **Revenue:** Grew from \~$5.01B (FY2016) to $130.50B (FY2025). Early years saw modest growth (\~20-50% per year), including a dip in 2020 (-6% YoY). The post-2020 AI surge drove explosive growth: FY2024 revenue jumped 125% vs FY2023.

-   **Net Income:** Grew from $0.614B (FY2016) to $72.88B (FY2025). Net margins averaged \~25-35% in 2016-2021, but expanded to \~49% in FY2024 and \~56% in FY2025 as operating leverage kicked in (see table). For example, FY2024 net margin = 48.8% (29.76/60.92), FY2025 = 55.8% (72.88/130.50).

-   **Free Cash Flow (Owner's Earnings):** Was $1.096B in FY2016, rising to $60.853B in FY2025 (Table below). FCF margin remained \~20-30% through FY2021, then surged to \~44-47% by FY2024-25 as CFO outpaced capex. The huge jump in 2024 FCF (from $3.8B to $27.0B) reflects improved profitability and inventory changes.

-   **Profitability (ROIC):** NVIDIA's capital returns accelerated: ROIC (defined here via net income/(LT debt+equity)) climbed from \~21% in 2016 to **&gt;100%** in 2024-25. This is because the AI investments yielded very high returns on the firm's capital base. The result: owner's earnings grew much faster than book equity.

-   **Balance Sheet:** Total assets reached $111.6B by FY2025. Shareholders' equity grew from $4.47B (FY2016) to $79.33B (FY2025) , mostly from retained earnings (debt is low). NVIDIA's current ratio has been very strong (4-8x in recent years), implying ample liquidity. Leverage is modest: debt/equity fell to \~0.41 by 2025 (or 0.11 if defined as LT debt/equity). Debt/asset is 0.29 for FY2025. Cash and short-term securities are large ($7.3B cash, $18.7B investments as of FY2024). Overall, the balance sheet is liquid and debt-light.

-   **Valuation Ratios (P/E, P/B):** The trailing P/E and P/B have been volatile. For example, after earnings collapsed in FY2020, P/E briefly spiked; similarly, the recent profit surge compressed P/E. As of May 2025, P/E ≈ 46× (using current price \~$135 and TTM EPS \~$2.94). P/B is moderate (book \~$3.10/share, so P/B \~ 43×). These high multiples reflect investor expectations of continued hypergrowth. NVIDIA pays virtually no dividend (yield \~0.03%). Return on equity averaged \~30% historically but jumped above 100% recently (due to share repurchases and profit growth).

*Analysis:* After 2016-20's modest growth and mid-20% net margins, NVIDIA's recent performance has been extraordinary. Revenue and net income have multiplied several-fold from 2020 to 2024, driving net margins into the high 40-50% range. Free cash flow has similarly exploded (FY2024 FCF 28× FY2020). ROIC has far outpaced peers (100%+ recently), thanks to minimal capital requirements and the AI-driven revenue boom. The balance sheet remained strong: equity surged to $79B, current ratios stayed well above 3×, and leverage is low (D/E \~0.4). These metrics suggest NVIDIA has transitioned from a cyclical GPU maker to a highly profitable AI platform company.
