# **Financial Analysis - PayPal Holdings (PYPL)**

*Disclaimer:* This analysis is for informational purposes only and does not constitute investment advice. It reflects data and sources as of mid-2025; always consult up-to-date filings and professional advisors before making investment decisions.

## **Business Model and Revenue Mix**

PayPal is a global digital payments platform (including PayPal, Venmo, Braintree, Zettle, etc.) that enables online consumer and merchant transactions. Its revenues come primarily from fees on payment transactions and value-added services. In 2024 PayPal's net revenue was **$31.80 B** . Of this, transaction fees (on payments, P2P transfers, merchant services, etc.) constituted about **90-91%** of revenue, with "other value-added services" (e.g. interest on loans, currency conversions, subscription services) making up roughly **9-10%** . For example, 2024 transaction revenue was $28.84 B (≈90.7%) vs. $2.96 B (9.3%) from other services . (Similarly, FY2023 saw 90.21% vs. 9.79% .)

In FY2024 PayPal had about **426 million active accounts** worldwide (391 M consumer, 35 M merchant) across \~200 markets . Total Payment Volume (TPV) was $1.65 T in 2024, with \~25.5 billion transactions. (On a trailing-12-month basis, payment transactions per active account were \~60 .) The business does not sell physical "units" or subscriptions in the traditional sense; its customer base is measured by active account count and transaction volumes. By end-2023 PayPal employed about **27,200** people , reflecting investments in technology, compliance, and customer support.

## **Short- and Long-Term Risks**

-   **Regulatory & Political:** PayPal is heavily regulated worldwide. It faces risks from financial regulations (anti-money-laundering, consumer protection, cryptocurrency law, BNPL regulation) and privacy laws (GDPR, etc.) . New fintech rules (e.g. stablecoin or digital wallet regulation) could affect its products. Political risks include sanctions (limiting operations in certain countries) and economic policy shifts (trade tensions affecting cross-border payments).

-   **Economic Cyclicality:** As a payments processor, PayPal's volume correlates with global commerce. Recession or inflation-driven consumer pullback could slow TPV. Conversely, high interest rates raise its funding costs (credit losses on BNPL loans) and reduce consumer spending. PayPal's profitability dipped in 2022 partly due to risk reserves and tougher credit conditions . Fluctuating currency rates also impact earnings (about 42% of revenues are international).

-   **Competitive:** The digital-payments space is highly competitive. Key competitors include Visa, Mastercard, Stripe, Square/Block, Apple/Google Pay, emerging crypto payment platforms, and traditional banks. PayPal acknowledges "substantial and increasingly intense competition worldwide" from both established networks and new fintechs . Merchant loyalty can shift to lower-cost or more convenient alternatives, and large networks could seek to capture parts of PayPal's market (e.g. Visa's Venmo upgrade).

-   **Tech/Cybersecurity:** PayPal is subject to cyber threats, fraud, and outages. Its 10-K warns that breaches or system failures could result in loss of customer trust, legal liability, and financial loss. Reliability and security are critical: any prolonged downtime or data breach could damage PayPal's brand and incur remediation costs.

-   **Operational & Partner Risks:** PayPal relies on third-party financial networks (banks, credit card networks) and partners (Merchants, Venmo rails, etc.). Interruptions or fee increases in these relationships (e.g. Visa/Mastercard raising interchange fees) could raise PayPal's costs or limit offerings . Inadequate customer support or service issues also pose a reputational risk.

## **Cost Structure**

PayPal's largest operating costs are **payment transaction expenses** (e.g. interchange fees paid to banks and card networks, fraud and rewards costs). In 2023 these totaled \~$14.39 B - about 58% of revenue. These costs are largely **variable** with transaction volume and average transaction size (higher TPV leads to higher fees paid to Visa/Mastercard). "Transaction and credit losses" (loan loss provisions for BNPL programs) were \~$1.68 B in 2023.

Other significant expenses include:

-   **Sales & Marketing:** $1.81 B (6% of 2023 revenue), down from prior years. Marketing spend was reduced \~20% in 2023 versus 2022 as growth strategies matured. It is partly fixed (brand campaigns) and partly variable (performance marketing).

-   **Technology & Development:** $2.97 B (10% of rev), funding platform upgrades, new features and cybersecurity. This is mostly fixed R&D/IT staff and infrastructure costs.

-   **General & Admin:** $2.06 B (7% of rev), including legal, compliance, and corporate overhead (also largely fixed).

-   **Customer Service/Operations:** $1.92 B (6% of rev), which scaled down in 2023 as support became more automated.

Cost trends: In 2023 PayPal reduced staffing ($27.2K employees globally ) and cut marketing/R&D spending, partly offsetting rising transaction costs. The operating expense mix shows PayPal investing more in operations and less in marketing, targeting higher efficiency in a slower growth environment.

## **Management and Governance**

In mid-2023 long-time CEO **Dan Schulman** retired, and **Alex Chriss** (former Intuit President) became President & CEO in September 2023 . **Jamie Miller** joined as CFO in November 2023 (replacing John Rainey). Other key leaders include Michelle Gill (SMB/Commerce), Diego Scotti (Consumer/Marketing), Isabel Cruz (HR) and Suzan Kereere (Global Markets) . PayPal's Board comprises experienced finance and tech executives, including CEO Chriss and ex-CEO Schulman (Executive Chair until 2023) .

Stock ownership guidelines are strict. Executives and directors are required to hold multiple years' salary in stock . As of 2024 the management team held relatively small personal stakes: CEO Alex Chriss owned only \~8,100 shares (&lt;0.01%), while former CEO Schulman retained \~646,800 shares . In total all directors/officers held \~0.1% of shares . PayPal prohibits hedging or pledging of insider stock . Overall, leadership's track record is strong in tech/finance (Schulman drove PayPal's spin-off growth from 2014-2023) and the new team emphasizes cost discipline and growth in "funded" products (loans).

## **Top Shareholders and Governance**

Institutional investors dominate PayPal's cap table. As of early 2024, **Vanguard** owned \~8.35% and **BlackRock** \~6.7% of outstanding stock . Other large holders include State Street and Fidelity (\~3-5% each). These funds typically vote with management. For example, the 2023 Annual Meeting saw virtually all director nominees reelected, and shareholder proposals mostly defeated . Notably, say-on-pay received only \~78% support in 2023 , indicating some investor concern over executive compensation, though still above the level that would force a policy change. Overall, major holders have not pursued activist campaigns, and generally support the board's governance slate.

## **Share Buyback Programs (2014-2023)**

PayPal has consistently repurchased stock since becoming independent. The key programs: in **July 2018** the Board authorized up to **$10 B** in repurchases (no expiration) . By 2021 about $5.1 B remained under that 2018 plan , and the program was effectively completed by early 2023. In **June 2022** a new **$15 B** authorization was approved . Under these plans, PayPal repurchased \~$4.2 B in 2022 and \~$5.0 B in 2023 . At end-2023 roughly **$10.9 B** remained available under the 2022 plan . In total PayPal has returned on the order of $19-20 B to shareholders via buybacks from 2015-2024 . The aggressive buybacks in 2022-23 (driven by lower share price and excess cash) have reduced share count significantly (\~27% cut since 2015).

## **Notable Investors and Trading Activity**

Among "value" investors, PayPal has not been a consensus pick. No household-value funds (e.g. Berkshire Hathaway, Oakmark) have reported large positions. Recently, investment manager **Pat Dorsey** (former Morningstar star, founder of Dorsey Asset Management) modestly *trimmed* his PayPal holding in Q1 2025 . Conversely, contrarian investor **Mohnish Pabrai** publicly noted building a position in late 2024, citing value from the pullback (though exact filing data is scarce). Overall, large institutions (Vanguard, BlackRock, Fidelity, etc.) drive position changes through passive index or quant strategies, not classic "value" trading. We found no evidence of major activist or event-driven buys/sells in 2024 beyond routine rebalancing.

## **Intrinsic Value Estimate (Buffett Method)**

Applying a Buffett-style DCF with generous assumptions suggests PayPal is fairly valued. Using owner's earnings (FCF) as the base: trailing-12-month free cash flow is about **$6.77 B** . Assuming terminal growth ≲3% and a discount rate of 10-15%, the intrinsic equity value ranges widely. For example, with *r*≈12% and *g*≈3%, value = 6.77\*(1+0.03)/(0.12-0.03) ≈ **$73 B**. This is roughly the current market capitalization (\~$72.6 B ), implying an implied required return of 12% to justify today's price. Using a higher 15% discount yields a lower fair value ($55 B), suggesting the stock is not deeply undervalued. Conversely, at 10% the value exceeds $90 B (30% above the current price). In short, PayPal's present value per Buffett's method is roughly in line with its market value, assuming mid-range discounting. With a 30-50% margin of safety, one would require the intrinsic value to be well below today's price, implying either significantly higher discount rates or slower growth than consensus. (By comparison, current consensus FCF growth is modest - long-term analysts project \~5-6% growth.)

## **Recent Quarterly Results (Q1 2024)**

The latest reported quarter (Q1 2024) continued modest growth and margin expansion. Net revenue was **$7.70 B**, +9% year-over-year (+10% ex-FX) . GAAP EPS was **$0.83** (+18% YoY), and Non-GAAP EPS **$1.08** (+27% YoY, reflecting a new presentation that now includes stock comp expense) . Total payment volume rose 14% YoY to $403.9 B , and TPV per account grew by double-digits. Active accounts ticked down 1% to 427 M (sequentially flat) . Operating margins expanded to \~15% GAAP (18.2% non-GAAP) , as disciplined spending kept expense growth below revenue growth. Free cash flow was \~$1.8 B for Q1 , and PayPal returned $1.5 B to shareholders via repurchases (25 M shares) that quarter . CEO Alex Chriss noted that 2024 is a "transition year," focusing on executing strategic initiatives and cost efficiencies . Overall the quarter was in line with guidance, demonstrating continued positive cash generation and steady growth in the core payment business.

## **Ten-Year Financial Trends and Ratios**

Over the last decade PayPal has grown from a $6.7 B revenue company in 2013 to \~$31.8 B in 2024 (≈+18% CAGR). Net income rose from \~$0.42 B (2014) to \~$4.15 B (2024) , with sharp volatility (losses in Q4'22 due to FinCrime provisions). Gross profit has roughly tripled (from \~$4.22 B to \~$12.89 B in 2024 ), and operating income has more than quadrupled . Free cash flow averaged \~$4-5 B in recent years , highlighting strong cash conversion (FCF margins have ranged \~14-25% ). For example, FCF jumped 58.7% in 2024 to $6.77 B as operating cash rose and capital spending stayed modest.

**Valuation & Profitability:** At a \~$72.6 B market cap , PayPal trades near \~17-18× trailing earnings (2024 EPS ≈$4.26) and 3.5-4× book value ($20.4 B equity in 2024 ). Return on equity has been unusually high (\~20%+) in recent years (2024 ROE ≈20.3%). Return on invested capital (ROIC) likewise exceeds 15% typically. These figures suggest PayPal is a profitable, capital-light business, but its valuation multiples are modestly above historical peers.

**Liquidity & Leverage:** PayPal carries no significant short-term liquidity issues - its cash and marketable securities (\~$18 B) comfortably exceed near-term debt. Total debt is moderate: long-term debt grew to \~$9.9 B by 2024 (leveraging the balance sheet for financing). Debt/Equity is about 48% in 2024 ($9.88B debt vs. $20.42B equity) . Debt/Assets is \~12% (debt/$81.6B assets ). The current ratio (Current Assets/Current Liabilities) has remained healthy (well above 1.5).

**Growth Rates:** Over 10 years, revenue CAGR ≈16% (2014-2024) while net income CAGR is higher but distorted by 2022. In 2022 PayPal deliberately cut guidance and took charges, so comparing 2021-2024 shows net income essentially flat. Longer-term growth depends on funding initiatives like BNPL/credit and new payment innovations. Owner's equity grew modestly (from \~$8.2B in 2014 to \~$20.4B in 2024 ), supporting the returns above.

In summary, the financials reveal a firm with strong cash generation, high ROE/ROIC, and solid margins (net margin \~13-19% in recent years ). Revenue growth has decelerated from double-digits in early 2020s to mid-single-digits more recently, reflecting market maturity. The company's metrics - P/E \~15-20×, P/B \~3.5-4×, FCF yield \~5-6% - suggest a moderate valuation given its quality.
