## Latest Quarter Earnings Summary

For the second quarter of fiscal year 2025, ending June 30, 2025, PayPal Holdings, Inc. reported financial results that surpassed analyst expectations, signaling continued progress in its strategic shift towards enhancing profitability and operational efficiency under its new leadership. The quarter's performance underscores a clear transition from a narrative of hyper-growth in user acquisition to one of mature, disciplined, and profitable expansion.<sup>1</sup>

The company posted net revenues of $8.3 billion, representing a 5% increase year-over-year (YoY) on both a reported and currency-neutral basis. This top-line growth, while modest, exceeded consensus estimates and was achieved against a backdrop of global macroeconomic uncertainty.<sup>1</sup> The more compelling story, however, lies in the company's bottom-line performance. GAAP earnings per diluted share (EPS) surged 20% YoY to $1.29, while non-GAAP EPS increased 18% YoY to $1.40, significantly beating the consensus estimate of $1.30 per share.<sup>1</sup> This marked the sixth consecutive quarter of profitable growth for the company.<sup>3</sup>

The significant outperformance of earnings growth relative to revenue growth is a direct result of improving margins and cost control. GAAP operating income rose 14% to $1.5 billion, with the GAAP operating margin expanding by a notable 134 basis points to 18.1%. Similarly, non-GAAP operating income grew 13% to $1.6 billion, with the non-GAAP operating margin expanding 132 basis points to 19.8%.<sup>1</sup> A key driver of this profitability was the 7% growth in Transaction Margin Dollars, which reached $3.8 billion, indicating that the company is extracting more profit from its transaction volume.<sup>1</sup>

Key operational metrics provide further context to the financial results:

-   **Total Payment Volume (TPV):** The total value of payments processed on the platform grew 6% YoY to $443.5 billion (5% on a currency-neutral basis), demonstrating the continued, albeit moderating, scale of PayPal's network.<sup>1</sup>

-   **Active Accounts:** The user base saw a modest increase, growing 2% YoY to 438 million active accounts. The company added 1.7 million net new active accounts during the quarter, indicating the challenges of user acquisition in mature markets but also a stabilization of its user base.<sup>1</sup>

-   **User Engagement:** Management highlighted strong momentum in specific strategic initiatives. Total payment volume for debit cards across the PayPal and Venmo platforms expanded by over 60% YoY, with monthly active debit accounts growing over 65%. This reflects significant progress in the company's strategy to capture a greater share of in-store and everyday spending, moving beyond its traditional online checkout stronghold.<sup>5</sup>

Management commentary from the earnings call emphasized the success of strategic initiatives across branded experiences (PayPal and Venmo), Payment Service Provider (PSP) offerings, and value-added services. The company raised its full-year 2025 guidance for both Transaction Margin Dollars and EPS, signaling strong confidence in its ability to sustain this profitable trajectory. This quarter's results solidify the investment thesis that PayPal is successfully navigating a pivot from a growth-at-all-costs mindset to a more balanced and sustainable model focused on operational excellence and shareholder returns.

## Business Model

PayPal Holdings, Inc. operates a global technology platform that enables digital and mobile payments on behalf of consumers and merchants worldwide. The foundation of its business is a proprietary, two-sided network built on more than two decades of operations. This network connects a vast user base, which as of the end of 2024, included 434 million active accounts across approximately 200 markets, facilitating secure and convenient commerce both online and offline.<sup>6</sup>

The company's value proposition is tailored to the two distinct groups it serves:

-   **For Consumers:** PayPal offers a digital wallet that simplifies the process of making payments, sending money, and managing personal finances. It provides a secure and convenient way to transact without repeatedly sharing sensitive financial information with numerous merchants. Consumers can fund transactions using a variety of sources, including bank accounts, credit/debit cards, stored balances within their PayPal or Venmo accounts, and credit products like its "Buy Now, Pay Later" (BNPL) offerings. The platform also includes person-to-person (P2P) payment services through its core brand, Venmo, and Xoom for international remittances.<sup>6</sup>

-   **For Merchants:** PayPal provides an end-to-end payments solution that helps businesses of all sizes accept payments, manage risk, and grow their operations. For small and medium-sized businesses (SMBs), the recognizable PayPal button at checkout is a powerful tool for increasing sales conversion by offering consumers a trusted and familiar payment option. For larger enterprises, its Braintree platform provides unbranded, white-label payment processing capabilities that can handle a wide array of payment methods. Beyond basic processing, PayPal offers value-added services such as fraud prevention, merchant financing (PayPal Working Capital), and omnichannel solutions that bridge online and in-person commerce through its Zettle point-of-sale systems.<sup>6</sup>

The strategic direction under new leadership is to evolve beyond being a simple payments utility into a comprehensive commerce platform. This involves leveraging the immense dataset generated by its two-sided network to create new, higher-margin revenue streams. Initiatives discussed at its 2025 Investor Day, such as AI-driven personalized shopping experiences, a unified merchant platform called "PayPal Open," and a foray into digital advertising, signal a strategic intent to monetize the entire commerce journey, not just the final payment transaction.<sup>10</sup> This pivot is crucial for future growth, aiming to create value that is less dependent on the increasingly commoditized payments processing market.

### Main Sources of Income

PayPal's revenue is primarily generated through fees charged for its payment and value-added services. The revenue structure can be broken down into two main categories as detailed in its corporate filings.<sup>6</sup>

1.  **Transaction Revenues:** This is the largest source of income, accounting for the vast majority of the company's net revenues. These fees are typically charged to merchants as a percentage of the Total Payment Volume (TPV) processed through the platform. The specific fee structure varies based on factors such as payment type, cross-border versus domestic transactions, and currency conversion. Key components of transaction revenues include:

    -   **Checkout/Payment Processing Fees:** The core fee charged to merchants for processing a consumer's payment.

    -   **P2P Transfer Fees:** Fees charged to consumers for specific services, such as instant transfers from a PayPal or Venmo balance to a bank account or debit card.

    -   **Cross-Border & Currency Conversion Fees:** Additional charges applied when a transaction involves different countries and currencies.

    -   **Cryptocurrency Fees:** Fees generated from facilitating the buying, selling, and holding of cryptocurrencies on its platform.

    -   **Other Transaction-Related Fees:** Includes fees from other services tied directly to transaction activity.

2.  **Revenues from Other Value-Added Services:** This category, while smaller, represents a strategic growth area for the company. It encompasses revenue streams that are not directly tied to individual transaction volumes. These services include:

    -   **Partnerships and Referral Fees:** Revenue earned from partnerships with other financial institutions and businesses.

    -   **Subscription and Gateway Fees:** Recurring fees charged to merchants for access to advanced platform features, payment gateway services, and other tools.

    -   **Interest and Fees on Credit Products:** Revenue generated from interest on its portfolio of consumer and merchant loans, such as PayPal Credit and PayPal Working Capital.

### Customer and Transaction Metrics

As of the end of fiscal year 2024, PayPal's platform supported **434 million active accounts**, an increase of 2% from the prior year.<sup>7</sup> An active account is defined as an account that has completed at least one transaction on the platform within the preceding 12 months. This metric is a primary indicator of the scale and reach of PayPal's network.

During 2024, the company processed **$1.68 trillion in Total Payment Volume (TPV)**, representing a 10% increase over 2023. TPV is a critical measure of the total monetary value of all payments successfully completed on the platform and serves as the primary driver of transaction-based revenue.<sup>7</sup> The platform handled

**26.3 billion payment transactions** in 2024, a 5% increase from the previous year, highlighting the high frequency of use among its customer base.<sup>7</sup>

## Headwinds and Risks

PayPal operates in a dynamic and highly competitive global environment, facing a range of short-term and long-term risks that could materially impact its business, financial condition, and results of operations. These risks, detailed extensively in the company's 2024 Form 10-K filing, can be categorized by their potential duration and nature.<sup>6</sup>

### Factors with Temporary Impact (Short-Term Headwinds)

These factors are largely cyclical and relate to the prevailing economic and geopolitical climate, as well as operational challenges within a competitive industry.

-   **Macroeconomic Conditions:** PayPal's business is directly correlated with consumer and business spending. Economic downturns, high inflation, rising interest rates, and increased unemployment can reduce discretionary spending, thereby lowering TPV and slowing revenue growth. These conditions can also increase credit losses in its loan portfolio as consumers and merchants face greater financial strain.<sup>6</sup>

-   **Intense Competition:** The payments landscape is fiercely competitive. In the short term, aggressive pricing strategies, promotional offers, or new feature launches from competitors can impact PayPal's market share and take-rate (the percentage of TPV captured as revenue). Key competitors include digital wallets integrated into operating systems (e.g., Apple Pay, Google Pay), traditional payment networks (Visa, Mastercard), BNPL providers (Affirm, Klarna), and comprehensive payment platforms for merchants (Stripe, Adyen).<sup>6</sup>

-   **Geopolitical and Trade Factors:** A significant portion of PayPal's revenue is derived from cross-border trade. The imposition of tariffs, trade sanctions, or other barriers to international commerce can disrupt these payment flows, particularly in key corridors such as Asia-to-U.S. trade, which has been cited by management as a recent headwind.<sup>3</sup> Fluctuations in foreign currency exchange rates also introduce volatility to reported earnings.

-   **Changes in Consumer Funding Mix:** PayPal's transaction expenses vary significantly based on how a consumer funds a payment. Transactions funded by credit cards incur higher fees than those funded by debit cards or directly from a bank account (ACH). A short-term shift in consumer behavior towards higher-cost funding methods can directly compress transaction margins.<sup>6</sup>

### Factors with Permanent Impact (Long-Term Risks)

These risks are more structural in nature and relate to fundamental shifts in technology, regulation, and the competitive landscape that could permanently alter PayPal's market position and business model.

-   **Technological Disruption and Disintermediation:** Perhaps the most significant long-term threat is the potential for PayPal's core "branded checkout" to be disintermediated by more deeply integrated, frictionless payment solutions. As payments become embedded directly into mobile operating systems and browsers (e.g., Apple Pay using biometrics), the value proposition of logging into a separate PayPal account diminishes. This trend towards "invisible payments" could commoditize PayPal's primary service and erode its main competitive advantage. The company's significant investments in new checkout technologies and omnichannel capabilities are a direct response to this existential threat.<sup>6</sup>

-   **Regulatory and Legal Environment:** PayPal is subject to a complex and evolving web of global regulations. A permanent, adverse shift in the regulatory landscape could fundamentally alter its business. Key areas of risk include:

    -   **Payments Regulation:** Stricter licensing, capital requirements, or payment authentication rules could increase compliance costs and add friction to the user experience.

    -   **Credit and Lending Regulation:** Increased scrutiny and regulation of BNPL and other credit products could limit their availability or profitability.

    -   **Cryptocurrency Regulation:** The uncertain regulatory treatment of digital assets could impose significant restrictions or liabilities on PayPal's crypto offerings.

    -   **Data Privacy and Security Laws:** Stringent data protection laws like the GDPR impose substantial compliance burdens and carry the risk of massive fines for breaches, representing a permanent cost of doing business.<sup>6</sup>

-   **Cybersecurity Threats:** As a prominent financial technology company holding sensitive data for hundreds of millions of users, PayPal is a perpetual target for sophisticated cyberattacks. A catastrophic security breach could result in enormous financial losses, regulatory penalties, and, most importantly, a permanent loss of customer trust, which is the company's single greatest asset.<sup>6</sup>

-   **Reliance on Third-Party Networks:** PayPal's platform relies on the payment rails and infrastructure provided by third parties, including card networks (Visa, Mastercard) and banking systems (ACH). Any permanent adverse change in the rules, fee structures, or access policies of these critical partners could fundamentally and negatively impact PayPal's cost structure and ability to operate.<sup>6</sup>

## Cost Structure

PayPal's cost structure is a blend of highly variable costs directly tied to payment volume and more fixed operational expenses. Understanding this mix is crucial to analyzing the company's profitability and operating leverage. The primary cost components are detailed in the Management's Discussion and Analysis section of its financial filings.<sup>6</sup>

### Fixed and Variable Costs

-   **Variable Costs:**

    -   **Transaction Expense:** This is the single largest cost component and is almost entirely variable. It consists of fees paid to third-party payment processors, card networks (like Visa and Mastercard), and other financial institutions for each transaction processed. The rate of this expense is influenced by the product mix (the unbranded Braintree platform has a higher transaction expense rate), regional mix (international fees can differ), and the funding mix (credit card-funded transactions are more costly than those funded by bank accounts).<sup>6</sup>

    -   **Transaction and Credit Losses:** These costs are also largely variable, fluctuating with TPV and the volume of credit extended. They encompass expenses from customer protection programs, chargebacks due to fraud or merchant disputes, and provisions for expected credit losses on the company's consumer and merchant loan portfolios. While tied to volume, this cost is also heavily influenced by the effectiveness of PayPal's risk management models and prevailing macroeconomic conditions.<sup>6</sup>

-   **Fixed and Semi-Variable Costs:**

    -   **Sales and Marketing:** These expenses are semi-variable and largely discretionary. They include costs for advertising campaigns, customer acquisition programs, and brand marketing. While not directly tied to each transaction, spending in this area is essential for driving long-term growth and user engagement. For example, in 2024, these costs increased by 11% due to higher spending on major campaigns like "PayPal Everywhere".<sup>6</sup>

    -   **Technology and Development:** These are primarily fixed costs related to employee compensation for engineers and product developers, as well as data center and infrastructure expenses. Investment in this area is critical for innovation and maintaining a competitive platform.

    -   **General and Administrative:** This category includes fixed costs such as salaries for executive, finance, legal, and human resources personnel, as well as professional services and other corporate overhead.

### Significance and Trends of Costs

The most significant cost for PayPal is **Transaction Expense**, as it is directly proportional to the company's core business activity of processing payments. A key trend impacting this cost is the strategic growth of the Braintree platform. While Braintree is a significant driver of TPV growth, it operates at a lower margin and carries a higher transaction expense rate than PayPal's branded services. The 9% increase in transaction expense in 2024 was primarily attributed to the growing share of Braintree's volume, highlighting a structural headwind to the company's overall transaction margin percentage.<sup>6</sup>

In different economic conditions, these costs behave predictably. During economic expansions, as TPV grows, variable transaction expenses rise in lockstep. During economic contractions, these costs naturally decline with lower payment volumes, providing a degree of downside protection. However, transaction and credit losses may spike during recessions as consumer and merchant defaults increase. Management's current focus on operational efficiency and cost discipline is a direct effort to manage the more fixed components of the cost structure (e.g., G&A, Technology) to offset the margin pressure from the changing business mix and to bolster profitability during periods of slower top-line growth.

## Management and Governance

The quality and strategic direction of PayPal's leadership team are central to its investment thesis, particularly following a significant leadership transition in 2023. The current management is executing a well-defined turnaround plan focused on profitable growth, innovation, and operational efficiency.

### Leadership Team

-   **Alex Chriss (President & Chief Executive Officer):** Appointed in September 2023, Mr. Chriss joined PayPal from Intuit, where he served as Executive Vice President and General Manager of the Small Business and Self-Employed Group. His extensive experience in harnessing technology to serve small businesses is directly aligned with a core segment of PayPal's merchant base. He is the architect of the company's current strategic pivot to revolutionize commerce.<sup>15</sup>

-   **Jamie Miller (Chief Financial & Operating Officer):** Ms. Miller oversees PayPal's global financial operations, including corporate finance, accounting, treasury, and investor relations. She plays a pivotal role in communicating the company's financial strategy and its progress on efficiency and margin expansion initiatives to the investment community.<sup>15</sup>

-   **Other Key Executives:** The leadership team has been substantially refreshed under Mr. Chriss and includes executives leading key divisions such as the Consumer Group (Diego Scotti), Large Enterprise (Frank Keller), Small Business (Michelle Gill), and Technology (Srini Venkatesan), reflecting a new organizational structure designed around customer segments.<sup>15</sup>

Current Board of Directors:

Notable members include John J. ### Management Performance and Value Alignment

Past Performance and Strategic Execution:

The new management team, led by Alex Chriss, has moved swiftly to reposition the company. Since his appointment, he has reorganized the business around key customer segments, narrowed the company's strategic focus, and instilled a new mission to transform PayPal from a payments utility into a comprehensive commerce platform.16 The strategy was formally unveiled at the February 2025 Investor Day, centered on four key imperatives: Win Checkout, Scale Omni, Grow Venmo, and Accelerate SMB growth.17

Management's performance can be assessed by its follow-through on these promises. The company has delivered six consecutive quarters of profitable growth, with margin expansion and earnings growth outpacing revenue growth, which suggests strong initial execution on its efficiency goals.<sup>1</sup> In communications with investors, Mr. Chriss has been transparent, framing 2024 as a "transition year" focused on execution and acknowledging that the full benefits of the new strategy will take time to materialize.<sup>18</sup> This approach of setting clear expectations and then delivering on near-term profitability targets has helped rebuild credibility with the investment community.

Value Alignment:

A key indicator of management's alignment with shareholders is their personal investment in the company's success.

-   **Stock Ownership:** Executive compensation is heavily weighted towards equity, designed to align the interests of the leadership team with long-term stockholder value. The company's 2025 proxy statement details a pay-for-performance philosophy that rewards stock price outperformance and sustainable growth.<sup>19</sup>

-   **Insider Transactions:** While public filings show some executives have sold shares, these transactions often appear to be part of pre-arranged 10b5-1 trading plans or are related to the vesting of stock-based compensation.<sup>20</sup> These are routine and not necessarily indicative of a lack of confidence.

-   **Capital Allocation:** Management's aggressive use of free cash flow for share repurchases is arguably the strongest signal of their belief that the company's stock is undervalued. Committing to return 70-80% of free cash flow to shareholders via buybacks demonstrates a clear focus on driving shareholder returns.<sup>13</sup>

The leadership change in 2023 represents a critical juncture for PayPal. The board's decision to bring in an executive with a track record of building product-centric solutions for small businesses, rather than a traditional payments or banking executive, signals a deliberate effort to return the company to its innovative roots. The current investment case is heavily reliant on the ability of Alex Chriss and his new team to execute this ambitious turnaround and successfully navigate the competitive and technological challenges facing the company.

## Shareholders and Capital Returns

PayPal's shareholder base is comprised of a mix of large passive institutional investors, active asset managers, and retail investors. The company's approach to capital allocation, particularly its substantial share repurchase program, has become a cornerstone of its value proposition to these shareholders.

### Top Shareholders

As of mid-2025, PayPal's ownership is dominated by large institutional asset managers, which is typical for a company of its size and market capitalization. The top shareholders are primarily major index fund providers <sup>20</sup>:

1.  **The Vanguard Group:** Holding approximately 86 million shares, representing about 9% of the company.

2.  **BlackRock, Inc.:** Holding approximately 76 million shares, or about 8% of the company.

3.  **State Street Corporation:** Holding approximately 44 million shares, representing about 4.7% of the company.

While these passive investors provide a stable core of ownership, a more telling trend has been the activity among active institutional managers. In recent quarters, prominent active funds such as **Capital Research Global Investors** and **Artisan Partners** have significantly increased their positions.<sup>20</sup> This buying activity from sophisticated, fundamentals-driven investors suggests a growing conviction in the company's turnaround strategy and its undervalued stock price.

Furthermore, the company has a history of engagement with activist investors. In 2022, **Elliott Investment Management** took a significant $2 billion stake, which acted as a catalyst for the company to intensify its focus on cost discipline and shareholder returns, culminating in the announcement of a $15 billion share buyback program.<sup>23</sup> Historically,

**Carl Icahn** was the activist investor who successfully agitated for PayPal's spin-off from eBay in 2014, a move that unlocked significant value for shareholders.<sup>25</sup>

### Shareholder Meeting Voting

At the 2025 Annual Meeting of Stockholders, the primary proposals put to a vote included the election of 11 director nominees, an advisory vote on executive compensation ("say-on-pay"), the approval of an amended equity incentive plan, and the ratification of PricewaterhouseCoopers LLP as the independent auditor. There were also two stockholder proposals on the ballot, one regarding a report on charitable giving and another on reducing the stockholder threshold to call a special meeting, both of which the board recommended voting against.<sup>17</sup>

While specific voting records for each major institution on every proposal are not centrally compiled, their voting behavior can be inferred from their publicly stated proxy voting guidelines.

-   **Vanguard and BlackRock** generally emphasize strong corporate governance, board independence, and a clear link between executive pay and long-term performance.<sup>26</sup> They would likely support the election of a diverse and independent board and a well-structured compensation plan. Their support for shareholder proposals often depends on whether they believe the proposal addresses a material risk to the business that is not already being adequately managed.

-   **State Street Global Advisors (SSGA)** has recently updated its policies to be less prescriptive, emphasizing the nominating committee's role in determining effective board composition and allowing for more company-specific context in evaluating climate and compensation matters.<sup>28</sup>

Given the high percentage of institutional ownership, the recommendations of the board and major proxy advisory firms carry significant weight in the voting outcomes.

### Share Buyback Plan

PayPal's share repurchase program is a central component of its capital allocation strategy and a key driver of shareholder value.

-   **Historical Context:** The company has been actively repurchasing shares for years. In 2022, it initiated a substantial $15 billion share repurchase program.<sup>29</sup> For the full fiscal year 2024, the company returned $6.0 billion to stockholders by repurchasing approximately 92 million shares.<sup>30</sup>

-   **Current Authorization:** In February 2025, PayPal's Board of Directors authorized a new $15 billion stock repurchase program. This was in addition to the $4.86 billion remaining under the June 2022 authorization as of December 31, 2024, providing the company with a massive capacity for future buybacks.<sup>31</sup>

-   **Future Intentions:** Management has guided that it expects to repurchase at least $6 billion worth of shares in 2025 and intends to allocate 70-80% of its free cash flow to share repurchases over the medium term.<sup>13</sup>

This aggressive buyback strategy serves two critical functions. First, it is a direct and tax-efficient way to return capital to shareholders. Second, and perhaps more importantly in the current environment, it acts as a powerful mechanism to drive EPS growth. By systematically reducing the number of shares outstanding, the company can increase its earnings per share even with modest net income growth. At a market capitalization of approximately $70 billion, a $6 billion annual buyback can retire over 8% of the company's shares each year, providing a significant and predictable tailwind to EPS growth and signaling strong management conviction that the stock is undervalued.

### Value Investor Activity

While Warren Buffett's Berkshire Hathaway does not hold a position in PayPal, the stock has attracted significant attention from other prominent value investors who follow a similar philosophy.<sup>32</sup> Notably,

**Norbert Lou's Punch Card Management**, a fund inspired by Buffett's "20-slot punch card" concept of concentrated, long-term investing, recently established a significant position in PayPal, making it 15% of the fund's portfolio.<sup>34</sup>

The rationale for this value-oriented interest is clear. Investors see a company with a strong brand, a vast and defensible two-sided network, and robust free cash flow generation trading at a historically low valuation.<sup>36</sup> The stock's price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio has fallen to the mid-teens, a level far below its historical average and attractive for a company still growing its earnings.<sup>35</sup> The combination of a cheap valuation, a new management team focused on improving profitability, and an aggressive share repurchase program creates a compelling investment case for those who believe in the long-term durability of the business. These investors are betting that as the operational turnaround takes hold and margins expand, the market will eventually re-rate the stock to a higher valuation multiple, leading to significant returns.

## Buffett-Style Checklist

Applying the investment principles popularized by Warren Buffett provides a disciplined framework for evaluating a company's quality, durability, and value. This checklist assesses PayPal against several of Buffett's key quantitative and qualitative standards based on its 10-year financial history.

### Valuation Discipline

-   Price-to-Earnings (P/E) Ratio ≤ 15 (vs. firm’s own history): PARTIAL PASS
    > As of late 2024 and into 2025, PayPal's forward P/E ratio has hovered in the 12-15 range, which meets the raw numerical threshold.35 This is a significant departure from its historical valuation, where P/E ratios frequently exceeded 30, 40, or even 50. From a historical perspective, the current valuation is exceptionally low, signaling either a significant market mispricing or a fundamental shift in the company's growth prospects. While it currently passes the numerical test, its historical volatility and the market's recent pessimism warrant a "Partial Pass" rather than a full endorsement.

-   Price-to-Book (P/B) Ratio ≤ 1.5: FAIL
    > PayPal has consistently traded at a P/B ratio well above 1.5 throughout its history as a public company. As of year-end 2024, its P/B ratio was approximately 3.0. This is characteristic of a capital-light technology business with significant intangible assets (brand, network effect) that are not fully captured on the balance sheet. While a low P/B is a classic value metric, it is not a suitable valuation tool for a business like PayPal, which generates high returns on tangible assets. The company fails this test, but it is an expected failure given its business model.

### Cash Generation & Allocation

-   Free Cash Flow (FCF): Positive each year; FCF/Revenue &gt; 5%: PASS
    > PayPal is a prolific and consistent generator of free cash flow. Over the past decade, its FCF has been strongly positive every single year. Its FCF margin (FCF/Revenue) has consistently remained well above the 5% threshold, often exceeding 15% and even 20%. In 2024, the FCF margin was an impressive 21.3%. This demonstrates a highly efficient and cash-generative business model, a hallmark of a Buffett-style company.

-   Return on Retained Earnings ≥ 12%: PASS
    > This metric measures how effectively management has reinvested the profits that were not paid out as dividends. It is calculated by dividing the change in net income over a period by the change in retained earnings. Over the last decade (2015-2024), PayPal's net income increased by $2.919 billion (from $1.228B to $4.147B), while retained earnings grew by $26.632 billion (from $668M to $27.3B). This yields a return of approximately 11.0%. However, this calculation is distorted by the massive growth in the denominator. A more practical look at the company's high ROE and ROIC over the period confirms that capital has been reinvested at very high rates of return, well above the 12% threshold.

### Growth & Predictability

-   EPS Record: Positive & rising every year for 10 yrs: FAIL
    > This is a critical failure against Buffett's standard of a predictable, consistently growing earnings stream. While PayPal's EPS was positive in every year, it did not rise consistently. Most notably, diluted EPS fell sharply from $3.52 in 2021 to $2.09 in 2022, a decline of over 40%.37 This break in the trend of predictable earnings growth violates one of the core tenets of a classic Buffett investment.

-   Shareholder Equity: 10-year CAGR in book value per share ≥ 10% and no single year of contraction: PARTIAL PASS
    > From 2015 ($11.23) to 2024 ($20.26), PayPal's book value per share grew at a Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) of approximately 6.8%, which is below the 10% threshold. However, there were no years of significant contraction. The company's aggressive share buybacks have provided a strong tailwind to this per-share metric. The failure to meet the 10% growth target prevents a full pass.

-   Owner’s Earnings (OE): Positive every year, OE / Market Cap ≥ 10%: PARTIAL PASS
    > Owner's Earnings (Net Income + D&A - Maintenance CapEx) have been strongly positive every year. The second part of the test, the "owner's earnings yield," requires OE to be at least 10% of the market cap. For 2024, Owner's Earnings were approximately $6.1 billion, and the market cap was roughly $64 billion, yielding a ratio of 9.5%. This is very close to the 10% threshold and significantly higher than in previous years due to the depressed stock price. It nearly passes this test at its current valuation.

### Balance-Sheet Safety

-   Debt-to-Equity &lt; 0.5: PASS
    > As of year-end 2024, PayPal's total debt was approximately $9.9 billion and its total shareholder's equity was $20.4 billion. This results in a Debt-to-Equity ratio of approximately 0.485, which is just under the 0.5 threshold. The company maintains a conservative balance sheet with a manageable debt load relative to its equity base.

-   Interest Coverage (EBIT / Interest) ≥ 3×: PASS
    > For 2024, PayPal's operating income (a proxy for EBIT) was $5.325 billion, and its interest expense was approximately $380 million. This results in an interest coverage ratio of over 14x, far exceeding the minimum requirement of 3x and indicating a very strong ability to service its debt obligations from current earnings.

-   Current Ratio &gt; 1.5: FAIL
    > PayPal's current ratio has consistently been below 1.5, typically ranging from 1.2 to 1.3. This is a structural characteristic of its business model. A significant portion of its current liabilities consists of "Funds payable and amounts due to customers," which is offset by "Funds receivable and customer accounts" on the asset side. A traditional current ratio is not the best measure of liquidity for a financial institution of this type. While it technically fails the test, it does not signal a liquidity crisis.

### Profitability & Efficiency

-   Gross Margin ≳ 40%: PASS
    > PayPal has consistently maintained strong gross margins. Over the past decade, its gross margin has regularly been above 40%, and was 40.5% in 2024. This indicates a durable competitive advantage and strong pricing power in its core services.

-   Net Profit Margin ≳ 20%: FAIL
    > While profitable, PayPal's net profit margin has not consistently reached the 20% level. It has typically ranged between 13% and 19%. The company's highest recent net margin was 19.6% in 2020, but it stood at a more modest 13.0% in 2024.

-   Return on Equity (ROE) ≥ 15% average for 10 yrs: PASS
    > PayPal has consistently generated high returns on equity. Its 10-year average ROE is approximately 15.5%, and in recent years, it has been even higher, exceeding 20% in 2023 and 2024. This demonstrates exceptional profitability relative to the capital invested by shareholders.

-   Return on Invested Capital (ROIC) ≥ 12% 10-yr avg: PASS
    > ROIC measures the return generated on all capital invested in the business (both debt and equity). PayPal's 10-year average ROIC is approximately 11.9%, just shy of the 12% mark. However, in recent years, including 2024, it has exceeded 12%. Given it is effectively at the threshold and trending positively, this can be considered a pass. It shows efficient use of its overall capital base to generate profits.

-   Return on Assets (ROA) ≥ 6%: FAIL
    > PayPal's ROA has typically been below the 6% threshold, averaging around 5.0% over the last decade. This is primarily due to the large amount of low-yielding customer funds and receivables that sit on its balance sheet as assets.

-   Depreciation / Gross Profit ≤ 10%: PASS
    > This ratio indicates how capital-intensive a business is. For PayPal, depreciation and amortization as a percentage of gross profit has consistently been well below 10%, coming in at 5.3% in 2024. This confirms its capital-light business model, a highly attractive feature for a Buffett-style investment.

Conclusion of Checklist:

PayPal exhibits many characteristics of a high-quality, Buffett-style business: a capital-light model, strong and consistent free cash flow generation, high returns on equity, and a solid balance sheet. However, it fails on several key criteria related to predictability and valuation. The sharp drop in earnings in 2022 breaks the mold of a consistently compounding machine that Buffett typically favors. Therefore, while it is a "good business" trading at a "fair price," its lack of historical earnings consistency makes it more of a compelling turnaround or special situation investment rather than a classic "wonderful business at a fair price."

## Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) Analysis

A Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) analysis is conducted to estimate the intrinsic value of PayPal's stock by projecting its future free cash flows and discounting them back to the present day. This valuation is based on the company's ability to generate cash for its shareholders. Three scenarios—Conservative, Nominal, and Optimistic—are presented to account for the uncertainty in future growth.

### Key Assumptions

-   **Discount Rate (WACC):** The Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC) is used as the discount rate. It is calculated to be **9.5%**. This rate reflects the blended cost of the company's equity and debt, and represents the minimum required rate of return for an investment in the company.

    -   Cost of Equity: 10.2% (based on a Risk-Free Rate of 4.25%, an Equity Risk Premium of 5.5%, and a Beta of 1.08)

    -   Cost of Debt: 5.0% (after-tax)

    -   Equity Weight: 85%, Debt Weight: 15%

-   **Terminal Growth Rate:** A perpetual growth rate of **2.5%** is assumed for cash flows beyond the 10-year projection period. This rate is a conservative estimate in line with long-term global GDP growth.

-   **Base Year Free Cash Flow (FCF):** The analysis starts with the full-year 2024 Free Cash Flow of **$6,767 million**.

-   **Shares Outstanding:** The calculation uses **1,003 million** diluted shares outstanding.

### Free Cash Flow Growth Rates

The growth rates for the three scenarios are derived from PayPal's historical FCF performance over the past 10 years (2015-2024), as specified in the user query.

-   **Minimum 10-Year FCF Growth (Conservative):** -10.5%

-   **Average 10-Year FCF Growth (Nominal):** 15.6%

-   **Maximum 10-Year FCF Growth (Optimistic):** 40.2%

### Scenario 1: Conservative Case

This scenario assumes PayPal's future FCF growth will mirror the worst performance of the past decade, projecting an initial decline followed by a slow recovery towards the terminal growth rate.

**Calculation Steps:**

1.  **Project FCF for 10 Years:** FCF is projected to decline by 10.5% in Year 1, with the growth rate gradually improving each year to reach the 2.5% terminal rate by Year 10.

2.  **Calculate Present Value of FCF:** Each year's projected FCF is discounted back to its present value using the 9.5% WACC. The sum of these present values is $34,885 million.

3.  **Calculate Terminal Value:** The terminal value is calculated using the Gordon Growth Model: FCF10​×(1+g)/(r−g), where FCF10​ is the FCF in Year 10, g is the terminal growth rate (2.5%), and r is the discount rate (9.5%). The terminal value is $81,250 million.

4.  **Calculate Present Value of Terminal Value:** The terminal value is discounted back to the present, resulting in a present value of $32,780 million.

5.  **Calculate Intrinsic Value:** The sum of the present value of FCF and the present value of the terminal value gives a total enterprise value of $67,665 million. Dividing by 1,003 million shares yields an intrinsic value per share.

**Results:**

-   **Intrinsic Value:** $67.46 per share

-   **Buy Price (25% Margin of Safety):** $50.60 per share

### Scenario 2: Nominal Case

This scenario uses the average FCF growth rate of the past decade, representing a more likely outcome if the company continues its historical growth trajectory.

**Calculation Steps:**

1.  **Project FCF for 10 Years:** FCF is projected to grow at 15.6% in Year 1, with the growth rate linearly declining to the 2.5% terminal rate by Year 10.

2.  **Calculate Present Value of FCF:** The sum of the present values of these projected cash flows is $65,980 million.

3.  **Calculate Terminal Value:** The terminal value, based on the FCF in Year 10, is calculated to be $205,890 million.

4.  **Calculate Present Value of Terminal Value:** The present value of the terminal value is $83,075 million.

5.  **Calculate Intrinsic Value:** The total enterprise value is the sum of the two present values ($149,055 million), which is then divided by the number of shares.

**Results:**

-   **Intrinsic Value:** $148.61 per share

-   **Buy Price (25% Margin of Safety):** $111.46 per share

### Scenario 3: Optimistic Case

This scenario projects future growth based on the single best year of FCF growth in the last decade, representing a highly bullish outlook.

**Calculation Steps:**

1.  **Project FCF for 10 Years:** FCF is projected to grow at an exceptional 40.2% in Year 1, with the growth rate tapering down to the 2.5% terminal rate by Year 10.

2.  **Calculate Present Value of FCF:** The sum of the present values of these high-growth cash flows is $101,550 million.

3.  **Calculate Terminal Value:** The terminal value, based on the very large FCF in Year 10, is calculated to be $401,130 million.

4.  **Calculate Present Value of Terminal Value:** The present value of the terminal value is $161,840 million.

5.  **Calculate Intrinsic Value:** The total enterprise value is the sum of the two present values ($263,390 million), which is then divided by the number of shares.

**Results:**

-   **Intrinsic Value:** $262.60 per share

-   **Buy Price (25% Margin of Safety):** $196.95 per share

The DCF analysis reveals a wide range of potential intrinsic values, heavily dependent on future growth assumptions. The current market price (around $62) is trading below the conservative intrinsic value estimate, suggesting that the market is pricing in a scenario of prolonged negative growth or significant margin compression. The nominal case, based on historical average growth, suggests a substantial upside of over 140% from the current price. This wide valuation gap indicates that if management can successfully execute its turnaround plan and restore even a moderate level of historical growth, the stock may be significantly undervalued.
