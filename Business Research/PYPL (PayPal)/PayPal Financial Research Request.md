# EQUITY RESEARCH REPORT: PAYPAL HOLDINGS, INC. (PYPL)

## Executive Summary: The Strategic Pivot to Transaction Margin Expansion

As of the third quarter of 2025, PayPal Holdings, Inc. (PYPL) finds itself at a defining inflection point in its post-eBay corporate lifecycle. Following a period of severe valuation compression-where the price-to-earnings multiple contracted from pandemic highs of over 50x to a value-territory 12x-the company is executing a rigorous strategic transformation under the leadership of CEO Alex Chriss. The narrative has shifted decisively from "growth at all costs," characterized by the pursuit of active account quantity and total payment volume (TPV), to a disciplined focus on "profitable growth," specifically targeting transaction margin dollar expansion and operational efficiency.

The fiscal landscape of 2025 validates this pivot. While top-line revenue growth has moderated to the mid-single digits (GDP-plus growth), the quality of that revenue has improved. The stabilization of transaction margins, historically pressured by the mix shift toward unbranded Braintree processing, suggests that the new management's initiatives-including the "PayPal Everywhere" omnichannel strategy and the enhanced monetization of Venmo-are gaining traction. Furthermore, the company's capital allocation strategy has become aggressively shareholder-friendly, with a fortress balance sheet enabling massive share repurchases that provide a structural floor to earnings per share (EPS) growth.

This report offers an exhaustive analysis of PayPal's financial standing, competitive moat, and intrinsic value. It integrates a decade of financial data (2015-2025), examines the nuance of the "transaction margin dollar" metric, and subjects the company to rigorous valuation methodologies, including Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) and Buffett-style quality filters.

## 1. Latest Quarter Earnings Analysis: Q3 2025

The third quarter of 2025 serves as a critical scorecard for the operational changes implemented throughout late 2023 and 2024. The results indicate a company that has successfully stabilized its core business while beginning to harvest efficiencies from a leaner cost structure.

### Operational Performance and Revenue Quality

For the quarter ended September 30, 2025, PayPal reported net revenues of $8.42 billion, representing a 7.3% increase year-over-year on a reported basis and 6% on a foreign-exchange-neutral (FXN) basis. This performance exceeded the Zacks Consensus Estimate of $8.26 billion by approximately 1.95%. While the headline revenue growth is solid, the composition of that growth is paramount. Transaction revenues, which constitute the backbone of the business (approx. 89.4% of total net revenues), grew 6.4% to $7.52 billion.

However, the "North Star" metric for the current management team is Transaction Margin Dollars (TM$). In Q3 2025, TM$ grew 6% to 3.87 billion. More significantly, when excluding the interest earned on customer balances-a high-margin but interest-rate-sensitive revenue stream-TM still grew by 7%. This creates a compelling narrative: the underlying payments business is expanding its profitability independently of the macroeconomic interest rate environment. This counters the bearish thesis that PayPal's recent earnings stability was solely an artifact of high interest rates masking a deteriorating core business.

### Earnings, Margins, and Cost Discipline

Profitability metrics for the quarter demonstrated significant operating leverage. Non-GAAP earnings per share (EPS) reached $1.34, surpassing the consensus estimate of $1.19 and marking a 12% year-over-year increase. The GAAP operating margin expanded by 33 basis points to 18.1%, although the non-GAAP operating margin contracted slightly by 19 basis points to 18.6%. This slight non-GAAP contraction is attributed to deliberate reinvestment in marketing initiatives like the "PayPal Everywhere" reward enhancements, which are designed to drive daily active usage.

A critical component of this profitability is the containment of transaction expenses. The transaction expense rate improved to 0.89% of Total Payment Volume (TPV), down from 0.91% in the prior year. This improvement suggests that PayPal is successfully managing its funding mix, encouraging users to utilize lower-cost funding sources (like bank transfers or debit) or that loss rates are improving. Speaking of losses, transaction and credit losses were $483 million, up from $352 million in the prior year, a reflection of volume growth and a normalization of credit environments, yet remaining within manageable risk tolerances.

### Volume Dynamics and Engagement

Total Payment Volume (TPV) reached $458.1 billion, an 8% increase year-over-year. While the total number of active accounts grew a modest 1% to 438 million, the engagement of those accounts is shifting. Payment transactions per active account (TPA) on a trailing 12-month basis decreased by 6% to 57.6. However, this headline decline is misleading. It is driven by the strategic churning of low-value, inactive accounts. When excluding Payment Service Provider (PSP) transactions (largely unbranded processing), TPA actually increased by 5%, indicating that the core, loyal user base is transacting more frequently.

### Forward Guidance and Strategic Implications

Management raised its full-year 2025 guidance, projecting non-GAAP EPS between $5.35 and $5.39, representing 15-16% growth. They also forecasted Transaction Margin Dollar growth of 5-6% for the full year. This upward revision signals management's confidence in the fourth-quarter holiday spending season and the continued scaling of value-added services (VAS), which grew 14.7% in the quarter. The guidance effectively communicates that the "transitional year" of 2024 has successfully laid the groundwork for durable compounding in 2025 and beyond.

## 2. Background Research

### 2.1 Business Model Analysis

PayPal operates a global, two-sided network that connects merchants and consumers, a structural advantage that differentiates it from pure-play gateways or acquirers.

**Income Sources:** PayPal's revenue model is bifurcated into Transaction Revenues and Value-Added Services (VAS).

-   **Transaction Revenues:** Accounting for roughly 90% of the top line, these fees are generated on a volume basis (Take Rate). This segment includes the high-margin "PayPal Button" (Branded Checkout), the lower-margin but high-volume Braintree platform (Unbranded processing for enterprises like Uber and Airbnb), and Venmo. The take rate is a blended average; Branded checkout commands premium pricing due to its high conversion rates, while Unbranded (Braintree) competes on price and reliability with rivals like Adyen and Stripe.

-   **Value-Added Services (VAS):** This segment includes revenue from partnerships, referral fees, subscription fees, gateway fees, and significantly, interest earned on customer balances. In Q3 2025, VAS revenue grew 14.7% to $895 million, acting as a high-margin growth vector.

-   **Credit:** PayPal also generates income through its credit products (PayPal Credit, Pay in 4), earning interest and fees, though it has moved to an "asset-light" model by partnering with external lenders like KKR and Blue Owl to offload receivables, thereby reducing balance sheet risk.

**Customers and The Two-Sided Network:** The platform serves approximately 438 million active accounts, consisting of both consumers and merchants. This two-sided nature creates a powerful network effect: merchants accept PayPal because it converts customers at higher rates (consumers trust the button), and consumers use PayPal because it is ubiquitously accepted. The strategic focus has shifted from expanding this number to deepening the "share of wallet" of existing users through products like the PayPal Debit Card and improved rewards programs.

**Competitive Moat:** PayPal's moat is historically founded on "Trust" and "Ubiquity."

-   **Trust:** In an era of increasing cyber fraud, consumers are hesitant to enter credit card details into unknown merchant forms. The PayPal login acts as a trusted intermediary.

-   **Data Advantage:** Because PayPal sees both the issuing (consumer) and acquiring (merchant) side of the transaction, it possesses a unique "closed-loop" data set. This allows for superior fraud detection (higher authorization rates) and creates the potential for a targeted advertising business, where PayPal can serve personalized offers to consumers based on purchase history-a key pillar of Alex Chriss's new commerce strategy.

### 2.2 Headwinds and Risks

Despite the turnaround, PayPal faces substantial headwinds that justify the market's cautious valuation.

**Short-Term Risks:**

-   **Macroeconomic Sensitivity:** As a transaction-based model, PayPal is a proxy for consumer discretionary spending. Inflationary pressures or a recession in 2025/2026 would directly impact TPV.

-   **Execution Risk on Innovation:** The company is rolling out "Fastlane," a guest checkout solution designed to capture volume from users *not* logged into PayPal. The success of this product is critical to countering the "commoditization" narrative. Failure to gain merchant adoption would be a significant strategic setback.

**Long-Term Risks:**

-   **Apple Pay and Digital Wallets:** The most significant existential threat is Apple Pay. Integrated at the operating system level on iPhones, Apple Pay offers a friction-less experience that challenges PayPal's historical convenience advantage. PayPal must compete by offering superior rewards or ubiquity across both Android and iOS ecosystems ("PayPal Everywhere").

-   **Commoditization of Unbranded Processing:** The Braintree segment competes with Adyen and Stripe. This is a price-sensitive market. While Braintree drives huge volume, it dilutes the overall corporate margin. The risk is that Branded volume (high margin) slows while Unbranded (low margin) grows, compressing the blended take rate.

-   **Regulatory Scrutiny:** The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) has increased scrutiny on "junk fees" and the regulation of digital wallets as banking-like entities. Additionally, the company faces reputational risks regarding account freezes and "de-banking" allegations, which have spurred shareholder proposals demanding reports on civil liberties and potential viewpoint discrimination.

### 2.3 Cost Structure Analysis

PayPal's cost structure demonstrates a high degree of scalability, though it requires constant discipline to maintain margins.

-   **Transaction Expense (Variable):** This is the cost of revenue, primarily interchange fees paid to card networks (Visa/Mastercard) and bank routing fees. In Q3 2025, this expense was 0.89% of TPV. This cost is strictly variable with volume. Management actively optimizes this by encouraging users to fund transactions via bank accounts or stored balances (low cost) rather than credit cards (high cost).

-   **Transaction and Loan Losses (Variable):** This represents fraud losses and credit defaults. The increase to $483 million in Q3 2025 highlights the perpetual arms race against cyber fraud. PayPal utilizes sophisticated AI models to keep this loss rate low relative to total volume.

-   **Non-Transaction Operating Expenses (Fixed/Semi-Fixed):** This includes Customer Support, Technology & Development, Sales & Marketing, and General & Administrative (G&A) costs. Management has successfully decoupled these costs from revenue growth. While revenue grew 7% in Q3 2025, non-transaction operating expenses grew only \~3%. This operating leverage is achieved through workforce consolidation and the deployment of AI in customer service operations to handle routine queries.

### 2.4 Management Team

The leadership team has undergone a complete overhaul in 2023-2024, replacing the "growth-era" executives with operators focused on efficiency and product velocity.

-   **Alex Chriss (President & CEO):** Formerly of Intuit, Chriss was appointed in September 2023. His "Intuit Playbook" involves focusing on small business solutions and rigorous capital allocation. His compensation is heavily performance-weighted, including a significant sign-on equity grant that aligns his wealth with shareholder returns.

-   **Jamie Miller (CFO):** Joining in late 2023, Miller brings experience from EY and GE. Her tenure has been marked by increased transparency in guidance (specifically the introduction of the Transaction Margin Dollar metric) and a commitment to share buybacks.

-   **Isabel Cruz (Chief People Officer):** Tasked with rightsizing the organization and aligning the workforce with the new strategic priorities.

-   **Board of Directors:** The board has been refreshed with members like Joy Chik (Microsoft), enhancing oversight of the company's AI and security initiatives. The board is actively engaged in risk oversight, particularly concerning cybersecurity and regulatory compliance.

### 2.5 Top Shareholders and Voting Stands

PayPal's ownership structure is dominated by large institutional passive investors, ensuring stability but also requiring management to strictly adhere to governance norms.

-   **Institutional Concentration:** The top shareholders are The Vanguard Group (\~9%), BlackRock Inc. (\~7%), and State Street Corp. These managers generally vote in line with management recommendations but have become increasingly vocal on ESG and human capital management issues.

-   **Activist History:** While Elliott Management took a stake in 2022 to push for cost cuts, they have largely exited. Currently, the shareholder base is devoid of a loud activist, though "anti-ESG" or "civil liberties" activist groups (like the American Family Association) have submitted proposals regarding political bias in account monitoring, which the company successfully omitted or defeated.

### 2.6 Share Buyback Plans

Capital return is a central pillar of the investment thesis. PayPal generates excess free cash flow far beyond its capital expenditure needs, allowing for aggressive share repurchases.

-   **Execution:** In Q3 2025 alone, the company repurchased approximately 21 million shares for $1.5 billion. Over the trailing 12 months, PayPal returned $5.4 billion to shareholders via buybacks.

-   **Authorization:** In early 2025, the Board authorized a new $15 billion stock repurchase program. At the current market capitalization of \~$60 billion, this authorization represents nearly 25% of the company's float, providing a massive structural tailwind to EPS. This signals management's view that the stock is significantly undervalued.

### 2.7 Value Investors' Buy/Sell History

Analysis of 13F filings for Q3 2025 reveals a divergence between insider actions and deep-value institutional accumulation.

-   **Insiders:** Recent months have seen selling activity from executives such as Alex Chriss and Aaron Webster. While often part of 10b5-1 plans (automatic selling for tax/diversification), the lack of open-market *buying* by insiders is a point of caution.

-   **Value Funds:** Conversely, deep value funds have been accumulating. Edgestream Partners increased its position by 350% in Q2 2025. The stock frequently appears in "Magic Formula" or high-quality value screens due to its combination of high ROIC and low P/E. "Superinvestors" tracked by Dataroma show a mix of holding and trimming, with no massive capitulation, suggesting the "smart money" sees the value proposition at these levels.

### 3.2 Buffett-Style Standard Analysis

Applying the investment philosophy of Warren Buffett reveals a high-quality business trading at a discount, albeit one facing "moat erosion" concerns.

-   **Valuation (PASS):** Buffett seeks a "margin of safety." With a current P/E of \~12.3x and a Price-to-Free-Cash-Flow ratio of roughly 10x, PayPal is priced for zero growth. Given the 15%+ EPS growth guidance, the market is offering a substantial discount for uncertainty. The PEG ratio (Price/Earnings to Growth) is well below 1.0 (approx. 0.65-0.84), suggesting deep undervaluation.

-   **Cash Generation (PASS):** PayPal is a capital-light business. It does not require heavy factories or inventory. In 2024, it generated $6.8 billion in Free Cash Flow on $31.8 billion in revenue, a conversion ratio of over 21%. The "Owner Earnings" (Net Income + D&A - Maintenance CapEx) are substantial.

-   **Growth (NEUTRAL/PASS):** While the days of 20% top-line growth are likely over, the company has stabilized into a reliable mid-single-digit revenue grower with double-digit bottom-line growth. This fits the profile of a mature "compounder" rather than a speculative growth stock.

-   **Balance Sheet (PASS):** The company holds a net cash position (Cash & Investments &gt; Debt). With $14.4 billion in liquidity and robust interest coverage (EBIT covers interest expense &gt;13x), PayPal is financially impregnable.

-   **Profitability (PASS):** The company delivers high returns on capital. ROE is 24.3% and ROIC is 14.6%, both significantly exceeding the cost of capital. This indicates that every dollar retained by the business creates value for shareholders.

### 3.3 DCF Analysis (Discounted Cash Flow)

To ascertain the intrinsic value of PayPal, we employ a 5-year DCF model.

**Assumptions:**

-   **Discount Rate (WACC):** 10.0%. (We use a higher rate than the market beta suggests to account for execution risks and competitive threats).

-   **Terminal Growth Rate:** 3.0% (Aligned with long-term global GDP/Inflation).

-   **Base FCF:** TTM Adjusted FCF of \~$5.6 billion.

**Scenario 1: Conservative (Stagnation Case)**

-   *Narrative:* PayPal loses branded market share to Apple Pay; Braintree margins compress further. Revenue grows at inflation (3%). FCF grows only 2% annually.

-   *FCF Projection:* $5.7B → $5.8B → $5.9B → $6.0B → $6.1B.

-   *Terminal Value:* $6.1B \* (1.02) / (0.10 - 0.02) = $77.8B.

-   *PV of Flows + Terminal:* \~$48B + Net Cash ($3B) = $51B.

-   *Implied Share Price:* **\~$52.00**.

-   *Verdict:* The stock is currently trading slightly above this "worst-case" stagnation level ($61.24).

**Scenario 2: Nominal (Base Case - Management Execution)**

-   *Narrative:* Management hits its targets. Revenue grows 6-7% annually. Margins stabilize. Buybacks reduce share count, concentrating FCF per share. FCF grows 8% annually driven by efficiency.

-   *FCF Projection:* $6.0B → $6.5B → $7.0B → $7.6B → $8.2B.
