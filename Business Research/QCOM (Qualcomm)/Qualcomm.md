In practice, QCT includes sales of Snapdragon and other chips for smartphones, IoT devices and automotive, while QTL includes royalties on smartphones and other devices using Qualcomm's wireless patents. # **Industry & Company Risks**

Supply-chain issues, interest rate pressures, or currency swings also pose short-term risks. **Long-term/persistent risks:** Qualcomm faces intense competition (from MediaTek, Samsung and others), and customers increasingly integrate their own modems/ASICs (as Apple did for 5G modems), which could erode future chipset sales. The patent/licensing business is vulnerable to legal challenges and potential patent exhaustion; some analysts worry that smartphone 3G/4G license royalties may decline over time. Geopolitical factors (e.g. U.S.-China tech tensions) and regulatory actions (antitrust or export controls) could also impact Qualcomm's markets and licensing. For example, Qualcomm's profitability depends on licensing key wireless patents, and competition from unlicensed/alternative standards could reduce licensing revenues. In its 10‑K, Qualcomm specifically warns that its business "may suffer as a result of our customers vertically integrating (i.e., developing their own chips)".
