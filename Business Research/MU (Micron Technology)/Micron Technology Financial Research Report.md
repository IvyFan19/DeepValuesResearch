## **Latest Quarter Earnings Summary**

Micron's most recent quarter (Fiscal Q4 2025, ended Aug 28, 2025) was exceptionally strong. Revenue hit **$11.32 billion**(up 46% year-over-year from $7.75 b in Q4 2024) . GAAP net income was **$3.20 billion** ($2.83 per share) and non-GAAP net income was $3.47 billion ($3.03 per share). The company generated **$5.73 billion** of operating cash flow, up from $4.61 b in the prior quarter . CEO Sanjay Mehrotra highlighted record data-center (AI-driven) demand as the key driver: demand from cloud/AI customers lifted DRAM sales to record levels and drove gross margins above 44% . Micron's guidance was similarly optimistic - forecasting another quarter of record revenue with gross margins above 50% .

## **Business Model**

Micron designs and manufactures **semiconductor memory and storage products** (primarily DRAM and NAND flash chips) for computers, networking equipment, servers (data centers), mobile and automotive devices. Its revenues come mainly from two product lines: **DRAM chips** (used in PCs, servers, smartphones, etc.) and **NAND flash** (used in SSDs, memory cards, eMMC/UFS storage, etc.). In fiscal 2024, DRAM accounted for roughly 70% of sales (about $17.6 b of $25.1 b total revenue ) and NAND about 28% ($7.23 b ). In the latest quarter, DRAM's share has climbed to \~80% and NAND \~20% . Other products (like NOR/legacy memory, 3D XPoint/QuantX) are minor.

*Figure: Quarterly revenues from DRAM (yellow) versus NAND (blue) . DRAM sales consistently dominate Micron's income.*

-   **Income sources:** \~80% DRAM chips, \~20% NAND flash (recent data) . DRAM revenue is driven by data-center/cloud servers and high-performance PC graphics; NAND is driven by client SSDs, mobile devices, automotive storage, etc.

-   **Customers:** Micron sells to a large global customer base of electronics OEMs, cloud providers, auto and industrial manufacturers, and retailers. No single customer dominates (the largest customer was \~10% of 2024 sales ). Its customer roster includes major PC/phone makers and hyperscale cloud companies (e.g. Microsoft Azure, AWS, Google, etc.). Nearly half of Micron's sales are to customers headquartered outside the U.S., and \~80% of products ship internationally , reflecting a global market.

-   **Sales volume:** The company ships billions of memory chips (gigabits of DRAM and NAND). Exact unit counts are not publicly given; Micron instead reports sales by bit-equivalent volumes. In Q4 2025, DRAM bits sold jumped \~50% year-over-year , illustrating strong demand.

-   **Competitive moat:** Micron's competitive strengths include advanced process technology (it was first with 232-layer and 1γ DRAM), a broad product portfolio (including new High-Bandwidth Memory for AI), and one of only three global players in memory (with Samsung and SK Hynix). Uniquely, Micron is the **only U.S.-based memory manufacturer**, which may provide strategic advantages (e.g. U.S. government support under the CHIPS Act and appeal to domestic buyers) . However, the memory market is highly cyclical and commoditized, so any moat is limited. It competes on technology, scale and reliability rather than brand power.

## **Headwinds & Risks**

**Short-term (cyclical) headwinds:** Memory is notoriously cyclical. Micron is sensitive to **market oversupply** and pricing fluctuations: when fabs (including Micron's) expand capacity faster than demand (e.g. in consumer smartphone or PC downturns), prices can plunge. In the near term, a slowing global economy or tech spending cuts could reduce demand for PCs, phones, and servers, pressuring sales and margins. Supply chain disruptions (pandemics, component shortages, shipping issues) could also impact manufacturing throughput or costs. Foreign exchange fluctuations (e.g. a stronger dollar) can affect revenue in local currencies. Quarterly sales can swing dramatically (as seen in 2022-2023) .

**Long-term (structural) risks:** The biggest risks are strategic/geopolitical. Trade tensions and export controls (especially U.S. restrictions on selling advanced chips to Chinese customers) could permanently limit part of Micron's market . For example, China's recent cybersecurity review and restrictions on Micron products may force the loss of certain Chinese customers. Conversely, China is heavily subsidizing its own memory industry (e.g. Yangtze Memory) - if those efforts succeed, it could erode Micron's market share globally. There is also risk of technological disruption: entirely new memory technologies (e.g. MRAM, PCRAM) could emerge, although none are close to mass replacing DRAM/NAND today. Environmental regulations are another longer-term factor: memory manufacturing uses large amounts of water, chemicals, and energy, so tighter regulations (or water scarcity) could raise costs or cap production.

**Other risks:** Intense competition (from Samsung, SK Hynix, and possible new entrants) and cyclicality mean Micron's results can be volatile. The 10-K warns of many industry risks, e.g. volatility in average selling prices, dependency on a few key suppliers and customers, and geopolitical export issues . Micron's capital-intensive business also means heavy fixed costs; in downturns, underutilization can lead to inventory write-downs (as happened in 2023).

## **Cost Structure**

Micron's cost structure is dominated by **semiconductor fabrication and R&D**:

-   **Fixed costs:** Building and operating fabs is extremely capital-intensive. The company's fixed costs include depreciation on large fabrication facilities and equipment. (For 2024, accumulated PP&E was $39.7 billion with depreciation \~$7.7 billion per year .) R&D expenses are also substantial and relatively fixed; for FY2024 R&D spending was **$3.43 billion** (14% of revenue) to develop next-gen memory processes. Salaries of specialized engineers and technicians similarly represent largely fixed overhead once fabs are built.

-   **Variable costs:** These include raw materials (silicon wafers, chemicals) and fab operating costs (power, utilities) which scale with production volume. Manufacturing yields and mix also affect per-unit cost (advanced processes tend to be costlier initially). As memory prices rise, gross profit per bit increases; when prices fall, costs (fixed and variable) eat into margins.

-   **Other expenses:** SG&A and marketing are relatively small; SG&A was about 4% of revenue in 2024 ($1.13 b ). Marketing for Micron products is modest (Crucial/consumer brand sales aside). Distribution is largely direct to OEMs, so marketing spend is low compared to consumer brands.

-   **Cost trends:** In down-cycles, high fixed costs can pressure margins. For example, 2023 saw inventory write-downs and lower gross margins . In up-cycles (like FY2025), higher selling prices have driven record gross margins (FY2025 \~40% ). Over time, economies of scale and improved yields can reduce per-unit costs, but ramping new nodes raises short-term costs. During economic downturns, Micron has periodically reduced capex (e.g. FY2023 capex cut \~50% vs prior ) to preserve cash.

## **Buffett‑Style Checklist**

Examining Micron against Buffett's metrics (with historical context):

-   **Valuation Discipline:** *Buffett* likes low P/E and P/B. Micron's P/E has varied wildly with cycles. In the late 2010s memory bust, P/E was depressed; post-2023 rebound it has risen sharply. As of late 2025, P/E is well above 15 (even though earnings grew, the stock ran up on AI hype). Book value per share is rising \~few percent per year (equity \~$45B on 1.11B shares ⇒ book \~$40) vs price \~$80, so P/B \~2.0 (&gt;1.5). Thus it **fails** strict Buffett valuation rules (P/E&lt;15, P/B&lt;1.5). There is no clear "margin of safety" now - one would have to assume much higher future cash flows.

-   **Cash Generation & Allocation:** Buffett demands consistently positive free cash flow (FCF) and good return on reinvestment. Micron's FCF was positive in good years (FY2022 FCF \~$3.2B and FY2024 \~$4.3B) but was negative in the down-cycle year (FY2023). On average, FCF/Revenue is healthy: e.g. \~10% in FY2025 (FCF \~$3.7B on $37B sales ). So on FCF/Revenue (&gt;5%) it **passes**. However, consistency is lacking. The free cash flow has grown on average but with big swings, so the "positive each year" criterion is borderline.

-   **Growth & Predictability:** Buffett likes steadily rising EPS and book value. Micron's EPS has been *volatile*, with a GAAP loss in FY2023 but record profit in FY2025 . Over 10 years, EPS is not "rising every year" due to cyclic peaks and troughs . Book value per share has grown modestly (Buffett would like ≥10% CAGR, Micron is lower). Owner's earnings (FCF) have been positive in most recent up-cycle, but were negative in the down year. OE/Market Cap is sometimes above 10% (e.g. FY2022-2025 profits were large vs \~$60-90B market cap), but again not consistently all years. Thus Micron **fails** the "predictable growth every year" test: it is a cyclical business, not a steady compounder.

-   **Balance Sheet Safety:**

    -   *Debt-to-Equity:* FY2024 total debt (\~$13.4B) to equity ($45.1B) ≈ 0.30 , well under 0.5 - **passes**.

    -   *Interest Coverage:* FY2024 operating income \~$1.3B vs interest expense $0.56B gives \~2.3× coverage, below Buffett's 3× target - **fails**.

    -   *Interest/Operating Income:* \~43% of operating income went to interest , above Buffett's &lt;15% guideline - **fails**.

    -   *Current Ratio:* FY2024 current assets ($24.4B) vs current liabilities ($9.2B) ≈ 2.65 , comfortably above 1.5 - **passes**.

-   **Profitability & Efficiency:**

    -   *Gross Margin:* Historically Micron's gross margins fluctuate with cycle. It was \~45% in FY2022 , fell as low as \~23% in FY2024 , and has rebounded to \~40% in FY2025 . It meets \~40% long-term on average - roughly **passes** (recently around 40%).

    -   *Net Margin:* FY2025 net margin \~23% (8.54/37.38) , exceeding Buffett's \~20% benchmark - **passes**. Over 10 years, average net margin is lower (volatile), but on average it meets this standard.

    -   *ROE:* FY2025 ROE = 8.54/45.13 ≈ 18.9% , above 15% - **passes**. (Buffett likes ≥15%.) FY2022 ROE was also \~19% .

    -   *ROIC:* (Net income)/(Equity+Debt) ≈ 8.54/(45.13+13.4)≈15% - **passes** (above 12%).

    -   *ROA:* ≈8.54/69.4 ≈ 12.3% - **passes** (above 6%).

    -   *Depreciation/Gross Profit:* This is very high: FY2024 depreciation $7.70B vs gross profit $5.61B , so depreciation ≈137% of gross profit - **fails badly** Buffett's rule of ≤10%. This high number reflects heavy capex from new fabs; some analysts adjust by considering "owner earnings" rather than GAAP profit.

In summary, Micron meets many profitability and balance-sheet criteria (strong gross/net margins, ROE, ROA, low debt), but fails on valuation (P/E, P/B) and predictability (volatile earnings, low depreciation efficiency). It also fails interest coverage tests due to significant debt servicing.

## **Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) Analysis**

*(A full DCF would require projecting 10-year cash flows and discount rates. Limited historical FCF data prevents a highly reliable calculation. However, we sketch three scenarios based on past FCF growth extremes.)*

-   **Free Cash Flow History:** Micron's free cash flow (FCF = operating cash flow minus capex) has varied. For example, FY2022 FCF was about $3.2 b , FY2024 roughly \~$4.3 b, and FY2025 \~$3.7 b (2023 was a down year with FCF likely negative or small). Thus the worst FCF growth in the past decade was the crash in 2023, and the best was the surge into 2024-25.

-   **Growth assumptions:**

    -   *Conservative case:* use the lowest observed FCF growth rate (e.g. \~-90% seen in 2022→2023). Assuming FCF plunges or stays flat, intrinsic value would be very low.

    -   *Nominal case:* use the average growth (\~midpoint of historic swings). If we average a decline and rebound, we might estimate modest growth (e.g. \~30% annual).

    -   *Optimistic case:* use peak growth (e.g. \~+100% as seen into 2024-25).

-   **Projections:** Starting from FY2025 FCF \~$3.72 b (our best estimate from press releases), we project 10 years of FCF under each case, then discount at, say, 8% (typical WACC for a tech firm). (Detailed math is omitted due to data limits, but roughly:

    -   *Conservative:* FCF flat or declining, terminal value low ⇒ very low intrinsic price (≪ current price).

    -   *Nominal:* FCF grows \~30% for a few years then stabilizes ⇒ moderate valuation (maybe slightly above current price).

    -   *Optimistic:* FCF doubles for several years then high growth ⇒ high intrinsic value (well above current price).

-   **25% Safety Price:** Buffett prefers paying 25% below intrinsic. Under optimistic assumptions, that target might still be above current (i.e. no immediate bargain). Under conservative assumptions, current price may be far above a stress-test "intrinsic" value.

**Conclusion:** Because Micron's cash flows are so volatile, any DCF is highly sensitive to the growth assumption. The official filings and analysts' estimates should be used instead for precision. Based on our sketch: conservative DCF yields a much lower fair value, nominal is roughly around the current market level, and optimistic suggests potential upside if a multi-year boom continues.
