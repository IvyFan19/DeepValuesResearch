# II. Circle of Competence & Business "First Principles"

## 2.1 The Physics of Memory: From Sand to Silicon Stacks

To invest with a margin of safety, one must understand the atomic realities of the business. Micron operates in the semiconductor memory sector, primarily manufacturing Dynamic Random Access Memory (DRAM) and NAND Flash storage. While both are built on silicon wafers, their physics and economics differ radically.

### The DRAM Imperative

DRAM is the "working memory" of a computing system. It is volatile, meaning it requires a constant electrical charge to retain data. In the context of AI, DRAM is the bottleneck. A GPU (like Nvidia's Blackwell) can process calculations faster than it can receive data. If the memory cannot feed the GPU fast enough, the expensive GPU sits idle. This is the "Memory Wall" problem.

To solve this, the industry developed High Bandwidth Memory (HBM). Unlike standard DRAM, which sits flat on a motherboard, HBM stacks multiple DRAM dies vertically, connected by microscopic channels called Through-Silicon Vias (TSVs). This architecture allows for exponentially higher bandwidth and lower power consumption. However, it introduces extreme manufacturing complexity. Drilling thousands of holes through a silicon wafer without cracking it or destroying the transistors requires a level of precision that decimates yields. This is the physical root of the investment thesis: **HBM is hard to make.**

### The NAND Data Lake

NAND is non-volatile storage; it retains data without power. It is the digital warehouse. While HBM grabs the headlines, the AI revolution is generating oceans of data that must be stored. Training an LLM requires datasets measured in petabytes. Micron’s leadership in high-capacity Enterprise SSDs (e.g., 60TB drives using QLC technology) addresses this need. The physics of NAND involves stacking memory cells vertically (currently 232+ layers). The challenge here is "aspect ratio"—drilling a hole through 200+ layers of material is like dropping a penny from the Empire State Building and hitting a cup of coffee on the sidewalk.

## 2.2 Unit Economics: The Margin Transformation

The shift in product mix is driving a permanent alteration in Micron's unit economics.

**The "Bit Cross" Phenomenon:** Historically, cost reductions per bit (driven by node transitions) outpaced price declines, allowing for margin stability. In the AI era, the cost per bit for HBM is significantly higher due to packaging and yield loss. However, the price per bit is 5-7x higher.

-   **DRAM vs. NAND Split:** As of Q1 FY26, DRAM accounts for \~79% of revenue ($10.8 billion), while NAND contributes \~20% ($2.7 billion). This heavy skew toward DRAM is favorable for investors because the DRAM industry is a consolidated oligopoly with rational pricing behavior, whereas NAND remains more fragmented and prone to price wars.

-   **Gross Margin Expansion:** Micron reported a non-GAAP gross margin of 56.8% in Q1 FY26, an 11 percentage point increase sequentially. This is elite manufacturing performance. For context, in the 2023 downturn, gross margins turned negative. The rapid expansion to nearly 60% demonstrates the immense operating leverage inherent in the fab model: once fixed costs (depreciation, R&D) are covered, incremental revenue flows almost entirely to profit.

## 2.3 Moat Analysis: The Oligopoly Fortress

Micron operates within a classic oligopoly, protected by insurmountable barriers to entry.

**The "Big Three" Structure:**

1.  **Samsung Electronics:** The volume leader (South Korea). Historically the aggressor in price wars, Samsung has stumbled in the HBM transition, struggling to qualify its HBM3E chips with Nvidia. This "fumble" has opened a massive window of opportunity for Micron and SK Hynix.

2.  **SK Hynix:** The technology leader (South Korea). They were the first to supply HBM to Nvidia. However, they are capacity-constrained and lack the balance sheet depth of Samsung.

3.  **Micron Technology:** The efficiency leader (USA). Micron has historically been the "fast follower," but with its 1-beta and 1-gamma nodes, it has arguably taken the process technology lead.

**Barriers to Entry (The Moat):**

-   **Capital Intensity:** A modern EUV-equipped fab costs $15-20 billion and takes 3-4 years to build. This prevents new entrants from disrupting the market. Even nation-states struggle to replicate this infrastructure.

-   **Intellectual Property:** The patent thicket surrounding DRAM and 3D NAND architecture is dense (50,000+ patents). Cross-licensing agreements protect the incumbents.

-   **The "Cleanroom" Constraint:** There is a global shortage of qualified cleanroom space and the specialized construction crews required to build them. You cannot simply "spin up" capacity.

**Anti-Moat: The China Threat (CXMT/YMTC):** The primary threat to the moat comes from China. ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) is aggressively expanding capacity in legacy DRAM (DDR4). While US export controls prevent them from acquiring the EUV tools needed for HBM or leading-edge DDR5, they can flood the market with older chips.

-   *Strategic Implication:* Micron must abandon the low-end commodity market to avoid the "Red Ocean" of Chinese competition. The shift to HBM and high-end data center products is not just a growth strategy; it is a survival strategy.

## 2.4 The Capital Cycle: Why This Time Is Different

The "Capital Cycle" theory posits that high returns attract capital, leading to oversupply. In semiconductors, this is usually driven by massive CapEx spending.

**Forensic Analysis of CapEx Trends:** Micron has raised its FY26 CapEx guidance to \~$20 billion , up significantly from \~$13.8 billion in FY25. A surface-level analysis would scream "Sell!"—rising CapEx usually marks the top of the cycle.

-   *However,* we must look at **Capital Efficiency**. In 2018, $1 billion of CapEx produced a certain amount of bit growth. Today, due to the complexity of EUV and 3D packaging, $1 billion produces *fewer* incremental bits. The "Capital Intensity" (CapEx/Revenue) is rising not because they are flooding the market, but because the technology is getting harder.

-   *Conclusion:* The "Supply Response" is dampened. The industry is spending more to produce less growth. This keeps supply tight even as spending rises, breaking the traditional boom-bust signal.

**2. Receivables (Days Sales Outstanding - DSO):**

-   *Data:* DSO is stable at roughly 57 days.

-   *Interpretation:* Receivables are growing in line with revenue. There is no evidence of "channel stuffing" (shipping product early to meet quarterly targets) or extended credit terms being used to artificially boost sales.

**3. Goodwill & Intangibles:**

-   *Analysis:* Micron grows primarily through organic R&D and CapEx, not acquisitions. Goodwill on the balance sheet is negligible. This eliminates the risk of massive write-downs that often plague serial acquirers in the tech space.

## 5.1 The "AI Winter" Scenario

The entire bull case rests on the exponential growth of AI infrastructure. What if AI is a bubble?

-   **Probability:** Moderate. While AI usage is growing, the monetization gap is real.

## 5.2 The "China Flood" (Commoditization 2.0)

-   **Scenario:** China's CXMT and YMTC, fueled by unlimited state subsidies, aggressively expand capacity in legacy nodes (DDR4/LPDDR4).

-   **Impact:** They flood the global market with memory priced below cost. While Micron focuses on HBM, the "floor" of the market collapses. This forces Micron to abandon the consumer/mobile markets entirely, becoming a niche supplier to data centers. If the data center market sneezes, Micron catches pneumonia because it no longer has the consumer market as a buffer.

## 5.3 The Technological Displacement (Anti-Moat)

-   **CXL (Compute Express Link):** This new interconnect standard allows servers to "pool" memory. Currently, memory is stranded—if one server has too much and another has too little, you can't share. CXL allows sharing. This increases *efficiency*, which implies *less* total memory might be needed per cluster. Efficiency is the enemy of volume.

-   **Compute-In-Memory (CIM):** Radical architectures that process data *on* the memory chip itself, bypassing the need for massive bandwidth. If this technology matures, it could render Micron's HBM roadmap obsolete.

## 5.4 Balance Sheet Suicide

-   **Scenario:** Micron commits to $20B+ annual CapEx for FY26, FY27, and FY28 based on linear projections of AI growth. The cycle turns in 2027.

-   **Result:** They are caught with massive new fabs coming online just as demand crashes. Depreciation charges soar, margins turn negative, and cash burn accelerates. The "fortress balance sheet" is drained, forcing a dilutive equity raise at the bottom.
