# **Micron Technology, Inc. (MU) - Comprehensive Financial & Strategic Analysis**

## **Background Research**

### **Business Model & Revenue Sources**

Micron Technology is a leading **memory and storage semiconductor** company, producing **DRAM** (dynamic RAM) and **NAND flash** products that power data centers, personal devices, smartphones, automotive systems, and more. DRAM has historically contributed roughly **70% of revenue** and NAND \~30%. The company operates four primary business units (segments):

-   **Compute & Networking (CNBU)** - memory for data center, PC, graphics, networking (\~38% of FY2024 revenue)

-   **Mobile (MBU)** - memory for smartphones and mobile devices (\~25%)

-   **Embedded (EBU)** - automotive, industrial, and consumer electronics (\~18%)

-   **Storage (SBU)** - SSDs and component NAND for enterprise, cloud, client (\~18%)

*FY2024 Revenue by Business Segment (CNBU, MBU, EBU, SBU) - CNBU is the largest segment, reflecting Micron's heavy focus on data center and networking memory.*

Micron's **business model** centers on high-volume manufacturing of memory chips, with revenues driven by the balance of bit shipments (volume) and average selling prices (ASPs). It sells primarily to **OEMs and cloud providers**, with no single customer consistently over 10% of sales (in FY2023 no customer was &gt;10%; in FY2024, two customers reached \~12% and 11%). This diversified client base includes tech giants (for data center and mobile memory) and numerous device manufacturers, ensuring broad end-market exposure. Micron shipped **43,000** PBs of memory bits in FY2024 (approximate, derived from bit growth data) - a figure illustrating its massive scale (exact unit volumes are not disclosed, but bit shipment growth was strong in FY2024). The company's revenue is cyclical, swinging with global **demand for memory**: in the recent cycle, annual revenue plunged from **$30.8 B** in 2022 to **$15.5 B** in 2023 (amid a memory glut) and then rebounded to **$25.1 B** in 2024. Micron also earns a small portion of revenue from NOR flash and other memory (under 1%). It sells under **Micron® and Crucial®** brands as well as through distributors, reaching both enterprise and consumer channels.

### **Short-Term vs Long-Term Risks**

**Short-term risks** for Micron are largely tied to the **memory market cycle** and macroeconomics. The company faces extreme volatility in memory chip prices - **DRAM and NAND ASPs** can swing widely quarter-to-quarter. In FY2023, a sharp **downturn** occurred as weak consumer demand and customer inventory corrections led to oversupply; Micron's revenue fell \~50% and it posted a $5.8 B net loss. Such down-cycles compress margins (Micron had a **-9% gross margin**in 2023), and the company even wrote down $1.83 B of inventory that year. **Geopolitical factors** also pose immediate risks: in 2023, China's cybersecurity regulator (CAC) effectively **banned** certain Micron products for Chinese critical infrastructure, hurting Micron's sales in China. China accounted for \~16% of Micron's revenue prior to this, so the ban created near-term headwinds. Additionally, **export controls** and U.S.-China tech tensions could limit Micron's access to Chinese customers or equipment in the short run. Economic slowdowns (higher interest rates, recession) can quickly dampen electronics demand, reducing memory shipments. In summary, near-term Micron performance is highly sensitive to **memory pricing, customer inventory cycles, and geopolitical restrictions**.

**Long-term risks** center on industry and structural factors. Memory is a **commoditized, highly cyclical industry** with few players (Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron dominate DRAM). Competitive behavior - such as aggressive capacity expansions by competitors - can lead to prolonged oversupply and price crashes. While Micron has invested in leading-edge technology (e.g. 1β DRAM, 232-layer NAND), failing to execute on **R&D and node transitions** would erode its cost competitiveness. The company must also manage **massive capital expenditures** to stay technologically relevant: \~$7-8 B capex annually in recent years. These heavy fixed costs are a risk if returns on investment suffer during downturns. Geopolitically, Micron's long-term prospects could be constrained if China develops domestic memory rivals (e.g. CXMT or YMTC) or if trade policies fragment the global market. Additionally, reliance on **Taiwanese and other Asian supply chains** for equipment and materials could be a vulnerability if geopolitical conflict arises in those regions. Long-term secular changes, such as the possibility of **new memory technologies** (e.g. MRAM, new non-volatile memories) supplanting DRAM/NAND, are low probability but present a risk if Micron fails to adapt. Finally, **environmental and regulatory pressures** (for instance, export bans on advanced chip tools or stricter environmental rules on chip fabs) could incrementally raise costs or limit growth. In sum, Micron's long-run challenges include managing the **boom-bust memory cycle**, out-investing rivals in technology, and navigating geopolitical shifts in the semiconductor landscape.

### **Cost Structure: Fixed vs Variable Costs**

Micron's **cost structure** is dominated by high fixed costs due to its capital-intensive manufacturing. The **Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)** includes depreciation of fabrication equipment, maintenance of cleanroom fabs, and other overhead that are largely fixed in the short term. In FY2023, Micron's COGS actually exceeded revenue (109% of sales) as prices collapsed. Historically, however, Micron's cost of revenue averages \~60% of sales in normal conditions, implying a gross margin around 40% during upcycles. Major cost components:

-   **Manufacturing & Depreciation:** Billions invested in fabs/equipment lead to high depreciation expenses regardless of output. (Micron's depreciation was $7.8 B in 2024, nearly 31% of revenue.) When utilization falls, these fixed costs cause gross margins to drop sharply (Micron incurred $382 M of under-utilization cost in 2023). Conversely, during high demand, the fixed cost per chip drops, boosting margins.

-   **Raw Materials & Utilities:** Variable costs like silicon wafers, chemicals, electricity, and gases scale with output. Micron continuously works to **lower cost per bit** produced by transitioning to advanced process nodes (e.g. more bits per wafer). Each new generation (e.g. 1α to 1β DRAM, or more layers in NAND) typically reduces per-bit cost, helping offset price declines.

-   **Labor:** Skilled labor in fabs is partly fixed (staff needed to run facilities 24/7). While some labor can be flexed or shifted, Micron cannot drastically cut its 48,000 workforce without harming long-term capacity. Labor is thus a semi-fixed cost, with yearly increases due to wage inflation.

-   **R&D:** Technically an operating expense, R&D is crucial for cost reduction and stays high even in downturns. Micron spent **$3.43 B on R&D in FY2024** (14% of revenue) and maintained \~$(3.1-3.4) B per year through the cycle. This reflects a commitment to innovation (e.g. EUV lithography adoption, new memory architectures) regardless of short-term earnings.

-   **Sales, General & Admin (SG&A):** Smaller relative cost (\~$1.1 B in 2024, 4% of sales), more variable, but Micron generally keeps SG&A lean.

Under different economic conditions, Micron's cost structure has the following trends: In **boom times**, high factory utilization and better yields mean unit costs drop, and fixed costs are spread over more units - leading to strong gross margins (45% in FY2022). In **downturns**, the opposite occurs: unit costs rise as fabs run below capacity or inventories build up. Micron then takes actions like **slashing production output** (to reduce oversupply) and cutting variable expenses (e.g. slowing hiring, reducing discretionary spend). Notably, Micron and its peers curbed DRAM and NAND output sharply in 2023 to stabilize prices. Despite volume cuts, certain costs (depreciation, R&D) persisted, causing Micron to report a -$1.4 B gross loss in 2023. Micron's **cost-per-bit** has a long-term downward trajectory (\~15-20% cost reduction per year historically) through technology advances, which is vital in an industry where ASP per bit also declines over time.

In summary, Micron's **fixed costs (fabs, equipment depreciation, core staff, R&D)** are very high, making profitability extremely sensitive to volume and price. Variable costs (materials, some labor) scale with production but are outweighed by the fixed overhead. This operating leverage means that **small changes in memory prices lead to large swings in profit**. For example, when DRAM prices rose and volumes surged in 2021, Micron's net income jumped to $5.9 B (21% net margin); a year later in the bust, it swung to a -$5.8 B loss. Managing this cost structure entails maintaining a strong balance sheet to survive downturns and investing steadily in cost-reduction tech so that Micron remains profitable on the *average* of cycles.

### **Estimating Intrinsic Value (Warren Buffett Method)**

To gauge Micron's intrinsic value from a value investing perspective, we can use a **discounted cash flow (DCF)** focusing on owner earnings and conservative assumptions - akin to Warren Buffett's approach. Key inputs and assumptions:

-   **Owner's Earnings:** Micron's normalized free cash flow (FCF). We take a conservative view given cyclicality. Over the past decade, FCF averaged around $1-2 B/year (with huge swings). FY2022's FCF was $4.0 B, but FY2023 was -$6.1 B and FY2024 roughly breakeven. For a baseline, assume Micron can generate \~$4 B in FCF in a mid-cycle year - roughly corresponding to a moderate upcycle (this is about $3.50 per share in free cash). This aligns with Micron's FY2021-22 average operating cash flows and capex needs.

-   **Growth Rate:** Use the **lowest growth rate in last 10 years** to be safe. Micron's revenue CAGR over 2014-2024 is low-single-digits (memory demand grows, but cycles reset). Let's assume **2% annual growth** in FCF, which is very conservative (below GDP and below long-term data demand growth, but prudent for margin of safety). Terminal growth beyond 10 years we cap at **&lt;3%** (say 2%).

-   **Discount Rate:** Buffett often uses around **10%** as a hurdle for stable businesses, but given Micron's volatility, some might demand **12-15%**. However, we'll illustrate with 10% and also consider 15% for extra margin. Buffett also insists on a **margin of safety** of 30-50% off the intrinsic value.

-   **Shares Outstanding:** \~1.13 B.

Using these assumptions: Project $4 B FCF growing at 2% for 10 years, then 2% terminally. Discount at 10%. The DCF sum ≈ **$51 B** present value for equity (after debt) - which per share is about **$45-$46**【78†】. At a 15% discount rate (more demanding), the intrinsic value per share would be much lower (likely in the $20s). Even at 10%, $45 is well below Micron's current market price around $90. To incorporate a margin of safety, a value investor might require buying at **30-50% below $45**, i.e. around **$22-$32** per share. Clearly, Micron's actual trading price is far above that, reflecting optimism about a strong cycle upswing (e.g. AI-driven memory demand) that could justify higher cash flows than our conservative base.

Alternatively, we can reverse-engineer the **expected return implied by the current stock price**. If Micron at \~$90 is fairly valued, what discount rate makes the DCF equal \~$90? Solving for r, we find it's around **6-7%** expected annual return (given our $4B, 2% growth assumptions) - essentially a bond-like return【81†】. Many value investors would consider that inadequate, hence they are not buyers at current levels. They were buyers when expected returns were high (e.g. when Micron was $40, the implied return if it normalized to $45 intrinsic was &gt;15% plus optionality on cycle upside).

However, one can argue our $4B FCF is too low if Micron's future includes a sustained AI boom. Analysts project Micron's FCF could exceed $8-10 B at the next peak (Micron itself generated over $8.5 B operating cash in FY2024 as business recovered). If we assume $6 B owner earnings and 3% growth, the DCF value at 10% discount would roughly double to \~$90 B (about $80/share). With a margin of safety (50%), one would seek entry around $40. Micron indeed traded in the $50s in 2022-2023's trough, which some might have seen as close to a value buy zone.

In Buffett's terms, **Micron's intrinsic value is hard to pin down precisely due to cyclical swings**, but we can conclude: it is likely significantly *below* the current market capitalization if using conservative inputs. Thus, at \~$90, the stock **prices in a robust future** - not leaving the classic 30-50% margin of safety. If one demanded a 15% discount rate to compensate for uncertainty, Micron's current price would only make sense if we foresee extraordinary FCF growth (which would rely on sustained high memory demand and Micron holding strong margins - possible, but not guaranteed).

So, a value investor today might say: *"Micron is a great company long-term, but not a bargain at $90. I'd revisit if it drops to the $40s or lower, where a margin of safety exists."* The **current expected return** at $90 (with our baseline FCF) is only \~6-7%, which is lower than many would target【81†】. In summary, **Micron's intrinsic value (DCF-based) is perhaps around $45-$60/share under cautious assumptions**, and the current price is above that range, indicating limited margin of safety at present.

## **Financial Research**

### **10-Year Financial Metrics Overview**

Micron's financial performance over the last decade reflects its cyclical but upward-trending nature. We organize key metrics into **Valuation, Cash Flow, Balance Sheet,** and **Growth** categories:

-   **Valuation Metrics:**

    -   *Market Price (year-end):* Rose from \~$14 in 2015 to \~$84 in 2024, with big swings (e.g. fell 60% in 2015, +88% in 2017, -46% in 2022). **Share price volatility** is very high - Micron often trades at low multiples in peaks and high multiples in troughs.

    -   *Market Cap:* Grew from \~$15 B in 2015 to \~$94 B in 2024. Notably, market cap hit \~$110 B in early 2022 before the crash, then recovered to \~$93 B by Aug 2024. The share count reduction (\~7% over decade) provided a slight boost.

    -   *Book Value (Shareholders' Equity):* Increased dramatically from $11.6 B in 2014 to $45.1 B in 2024. Micron's book value quadrupled mainly due to retention of earnings during upcycles (especially 2017-2018 and 2021-2022). Equity dipped in 2023 (net loss) but remained high relative to earlier years.

    -   *P/E (Price/Earnings):* Extremely variable. At cycle peaks, P/E has been **as low as \~3-5×** (e.g. FY2018 EPS $11.51, stock \~$31 =&gt; P/E \~2.7; FY2022 EPS $7.75, stock \~$49 =&gt; P/E \~6.3). At cycle troughs, P/E is not meaningful (losses or very high values - e.g. FY2020 P/E \~40×, FY2024 P/E \~120× due to tiny earnings). The **5-year average P/E** is \~10× if we exclude loss years, underscoring Micron's tendency to trade at low multiples when earnings are strong (investors "don't believe" peak earnings are sustainable).

    -   *P/B (Price/Book):* Ranged roughly **1.1× to 2.4×** over the decade. It was \~1.2× in 2015, peaked at \~2.4× in 2017 (stock up, equity still low) and 2021, then back to \~1.1× in late 2022 (stock crashed, book high)【85†】. By 2024, P/B \~2.1× (84/40 book per share) - near the upper end of Micron's historical range. The **mean P/B** \~1.6×. Micron's P/B tends to bottom near 1× in severe downturns (2016, 2022)【85†】, implying the market value approaching liquidation book value - a sign of pessimism that proved opportunistic for investors.

    -   *Return on Equity (ROE):* Averages are misleading due to swings. Micron's ROE soared to **\~42% in FY2018**(net $14.1B on \~$33B equity), was \~17-18% in 2021-2022, and deeply negative in bust years (e.g. -12.5% in 2023). Over 10 years, ROE has been positive \~7 years and negative \~3. The **normalized ROE** might be \~10-15% across cycles (Micron's FY2015-2024 average ROE is \~10%, counting losses as negative).

-   **Cash Flow Metrics:**

    -   *Revenue:* Highly cyclical growth. Grew from $16.2 B in 2015 to $30.4 B in 2018 (+88% in 3 years), then dropped to $23.4 B in 2019 (-23%). After a COVID dip in 2020 ($21.4 B), revenue hit a new high $30.8 B in 2022, then halved to $15.5 B in 2023 (-49%). FY2024 rebounded 62% to $25.1 B. Over 10 years, revenue CAGR is \~4.3%. The **pattern** is boom (record highs every \~3-4 years) followed by bust - but note each bust trough tends to be higher than the prior one (e.g. 2016 trough $12.4B, 2023 trough $15.5B).

    -   *Gross Profit & Margin:* Gross profit spiked to **$17.9 B in 2018 (59% margin)**. In down years, GP collapses or goes negative: 2016 GP $2.5 B (20% margin), 2019 $10.7 B (46%), 2023 **- $1.4 B** (gross *loss*; -9% margin). FY2024 GP recovered to $5.6 B (22% margin). Over the cycle, **gross margin** averages \~30-40%, but with wide variance. Notably, Micron had to write down inventory by $1.8 B in 2023 hitting COGS, showing how quickly margins can erode.

    -   *Operating Profit & Margin:* Similar story. Operating income in 2018 was $14.99 B (49% op margin); 2022 was $9.70 B (31% margin). Losses: 2016 barely broke even ($168 M), 2023 op loss -$5.75 B. FY2024 returned to $1.3 B op income (5.2% margin). Micron's **operating margin** can be extraordinarily high in booms (\~50%) and sharply negative in busts.

    -   *Net Income & Net Margin:* Net margin peaked at **46.5% in 2018**, one of the highest of any large tech that year, and was 28% in 2022. In loss years, net margin \~ -20% to -40% (2023 was -37.5%). Over 10 years, average net margin \~10% (skewed by big positives). Net Income ranged from -$5.83 B (2023) to +$14.14 B (2018). Few companies see such **swings from +$14B to -$6B** in a short span.

    -   *Free Cash Flow (FCF):* Perhaps the best "owner earnings" measure. Micron's FCF mirrors earnings volatility. Big peaks: **$8.52 B in 2018**; $4.0 B in 2022. Near-breakeven or negative in weak times: -$2.65 B in 2016 (heavy capex, low profit); -$6.12 B in 2023 (operating cash collapsed while capex remained). Over 10 years (2015-2024), cumulative FCF is \~$15 B, averaging \~$1.5 B/year, but that obscures the pattern of large inflows during booms and outflows during busts. Micron's **FCF margin** ranged from +28% (2018) to -40% (2023)【89†】.

-   **Balance Sheet Metrics:**

    -   *Total Assets:* Grown steadily (aside from inventory fluctuations). $22.4 B in 2014 to $69.4 B in 2024. Each cycle's profit was reinvested in PP&E and building cash. By 2024, assets nearly 3× 2014 levels, reflecting new fabs and technology investments (also some inflation in replacement cost).

    -   *Total Debt:* Micron uses moderate debt. Long-term debt was $4.96 B in 2014, rose to \~$9-10 B by 2017 (Micron took on debt to acquire Intel's interest in joint ventures etc.), then **fell** to $3.8 B by 2018 as Micron paid down debt aggressively with windfall profits. However, in 2022-2023, Micron raised debt again to buffer the downturn - LT debt jumped to **$13.05 B in 2023**. By 2024 it was $12.97 B. Additionally, Micron carries short-term debt and leases; total liabilities in 2024 were $24.3 B.

    -   *Debt/Equity:* Using **total liabilities to equity**, Micron was conservatively financed for most of the decade. D/E was \~0.33× (33%) in 2014-2015, spiked to 113% in 2016 (when equity plunged on losses), then improved to \~30-38% in 2018-2021. With debt raise and equity drop, D/E rose to **46% in 2023** and \~54% in 2024. Even at 54%, it's not high leverage by corporate standards (and much of liabilities are current, like payables). Using just interest-bearing debt to equity, D/E is \~0.29× in 2024.

    -   *Debt/Assets:* Similarly, low. It oscillated between \~25% and 35% most years. It hit \~23% in 2018 (low debt, high cash) and rose to \~35% in 2024. This means \~1/3 of Micron's assets are funded by liabilities, implying a solid equity cushion.

    -   *Cash & Equivalents:* Micron's cash war chest grew in good times. From \~$5 B in mid-2010s to over **$9 B by 2021**, then the company used some cash in 2022-23 (but also raised debt) to end 2024 with \~$8.3 B cash, plus \~$0.8 B marketable investments. Liquidity was ample enough that Micron kept investing through the downturn.

    -   *Shareholder Equity:* Already noted under book value - climbed impressively from $12-13 B in mid-2010s to nearly $50 B at peak 2022. It dipped to $44.1 B in 2023 then slightly up to $45.1 B in 2024. Micron's **equity base quadrupled** in 10 years, indicating that a large portion of its earnings were retained for growth and resilience (and reflecting a much larger asset base).

-   **Growth Metrics:**

    -   *Revenue Growth:* Highly non-linear. There were periods of &gt;40% YoY growth - e.g. +49.7% in 2014 (Elpida acquisition), +64% in 2017, +50% in 2018. Followed by sharp declines: -13% in 2016, -23% in 2019, -49% in 2023. The **3-year stacked growth** often nets out: 2016-18 big up; 2019-21 flat-to-up; etc. Over 10 years, total revenue growth \~55% (from $16.4B to $25.1B), which is modest \~4.5% CAGR. But this masks the amplitude of cycles.

    -   *Net Income Growth:* Even more extreme. 2013 to 2018 saw NI go from $1.19 B to $14.14 B (an almost 12× increase in 5 years). Then it fell 55% in 2019, swung to -$276 M in 2016 and -$5.83 B in 2023. On a full decade basis, net income grew from $3.05 B in 2014 to $0.78 B in 2024 - not a useful comparison given cyclical timing. A more apples-to-apples might be comparing peak-to-peak: 2018 vs 2022 net ($14.1B vs $8.7B) - here 2022 was lower, illustrating how the 2018 cycle was exceptionally strong due to industry consolidation and high ASPs. If AI demand makes the next peak bigger, we could see new highs later.

    -   *FCF Growth:* Similarly volatile. There's no steady trend; it's deeply negative some years, massively positive others. However, Micron's *cumulative FCF* over 2015-2024, \~$15 B, shows that in net it did create cash despite the down years. If we measure trough-to-trough, FCF improved: 2016 was -$2.65B, 2023 -$6.1B (worse); peak-to-peak, 2018 $8.5B vs 2022 $4.0B (peak FCF was lower in latest cycle due to heavy capex on future tech). On a qualitative note, Micron's cash generation in good times is plenty to fund technology upgrades and to return some cash to shareholders.

**Analysis of the Metrics:** The metrics above highlight **Micron's cyclicality** as the dominant theme. During upcycles, Micron's valuation multiples (P/E, EV/EBITDA not shown but similar) become very low because earnings temporarily explode. The market anticipates the inevitable downturn, so it doesn't award a high multiple - this is why Micron's P/E might be 4× at peak EPS, reflecting skepticism that those earnings are sustainable. Conversely, in downturns, Micron shows losses or minimal earnings, so P/E is meaningless or extremely high - yet those are often the best buying moments (P/B \~1 and strong balance sheet provides margin of safety). A value investor strategy with Micron has historically been to **buy when P/B \~1 and P/E is high/negative (blood in the streets), and sell when P/E is low (euphoria)**.

Micron's **profitability ratios** like ROE and ROIC underscore the same: ROIC was &gt;30% in super-cycle years, meaning Micron was generating tremendous returns on its invested capital then; but it dropped to negative ROIC in bad years (FY2023 ROIC \~ -10%). The average ROIC over the cycle might be in the high single digits. If Micron can sustain positive ROIC through an entire cycle (which it did over 2017-2022), it creates value for shareholders. The concern is always the **troughs eroding cumulative returns**, which Micron combats via prudent capital management (e.g. strong current ratio ensures no distress in downturns).

Micron's **liquidity** (current ratio \~3-4 most years) and **moderate leverage** (debt/assets \~25-35%) are strengths. The current ratio spiked to 4.5× in 2023 as Micron held large inventories and cash relative to current liabilities, then normalized to 2.6× in 2024 as inventories sold down. This high liquidity helped Micron avoid issuing equity or risking default during the 2023 trough. Debt to equity climbed in the latest downturn but remains quite low (0.5:1). **Micron's financial position is solid**, giving it staying power to weather cycles - a crucial factor for long-term value.

From a **growth perspective**, while end demand for memory (bytes) has a secular \~20%+ CAGR (especially with AI, 5G, IoT trends), Micron's financial growth in USD is much lower due to price per bit declines. So Micron's strategy is to outrun price erosion with volume and cost improvements. The metrics show that revenue and net income ten-year CAGRs are modest, but that belies the increasing *intrinsic* scale of the business (Micron ships far more bits today than 10 years ago, just at lower unit prices). Investors expect Micron's revenue to eventually trend upward long-term as the world consumes more memory (the AI boom is one such catalyst - Micron expects memory content in AI servers to be multiples of traditional servers). Indeed, Micron's FY2025 outlook indicates a likely new record revenue. Thus, the **growth story for Micron is not linear** but stepping up with each cycle higher than the last.

### **Conservative DCF Valuation (Lowest Growth Scenario)**

As previously discussed in the intrinsic value section, we perform a conservative **Discounted Cash Flow analysis** using the lowest growth rate observed in the past decade for Micron's cash flows. Micron had years of revenue decline (the lowest being -49% in 2023), but for long-term valuation we'll assume a low positive growth to be conservative. We use:

-   **FCF Base:** Approx. $4 B (mid-cycle estimate). This is cautious given Micron's 2018 FCF was $8.5 B, but we choose a number closer to the cycle average.

-   **FCF Growth:** **2% per year** for next 10 years - representing a low terminal-like growth (well below the expected memory industry demand growth, hence conservative).

-   **Terminal Growth:** **2%** (no stepping down, effectively already at a low rate &lt; long-term GDP).

-   **Discount Rate:** **10%** (a moderate required return - some might argue for higher due to risk, but we'll start with 10% to estimate an optimistic intrinsic value).

-   **Shares:** \~1.13 B.

Under these inputs, the DCF yields an equity value of roughly **$51 B**, or about **$45 per share**【78†】. We would then apply a **margin of safety**. If requiring a 30% margin, a buy price would be \~$31. If 50%, \~$23. Micron's actual market price in the last couple of years did dip into the $40s (but not $30s) during the worst of 2022-23, which was near the upper bound of that range. Thus, a strict value investor might say Micron never quite hit a 50% MOS, but came within a 30% MOS of the \~$45 intrinsic (stock reached \~$50, which is \~10% above $45).

However, one could critique that 10% discount is low for Micron's risk. If we use a **15% discount rate** to reflect higher required return, the implied intrinsic value would be much lower (\~$30/share or less), suggesting Micron was very overvalued at most points unless bought at rock-bottom. Conversely, if one believes Micron's future cash flows will **grow faster than 2%** (which is likely given secular trends), intrinsic value would be higher. For example, if we assume FCF grows 5% annually for 10 years (with the same $4B base), at 10% discount the DCF per share would be much higher (roughly $70+). So the intrinsic value is *extremely sensitive* to growth and discount assumptions - reflecting the uncertainty in forecasting for a cyclical firm.

In practice, many analysts do a scenario-based DCF: an optimistic case (assuming Micron achieves say $10 B FCF at peak and averages $6 B), and a pessimistic case (prolonged slump). The midpoint might align with the current price. At present, the market at \~$90 likely anticipates that memory demand (especially from AI) will drive strong growth in the coming years, effectively raising the "g" in our model above 2%.

Nonetheless, our **"Buffett-style" conservative DCF suggests Micron's current price is above the level a classic value investor would pay**. The 2% growth, 10% discount model indicating \~$45 intrinsic vs $90 market means the market is pricing in far better outcomes (or accepting a lower return). If we adjust the discount rate to solve for current price, as earlier, we got around a **6-7% implied return**【81†】 - not attractive to value investors seeking 10%+. Therefore, while Micron may well be a great company to hold through cycles (and many investors do, counting on long-term industry growth), from a pure valuation standpoint the **margin of safety is not present at the moment**. The time to buy, per this model, was when Micron was unloved (e.g. mid-2022 or early 2023 under $60). Indeed, Buffett's deputies at Berkshire reportedly considered Micron in 2020 but did not follow through - possibly due to uncertainties or because it ran up too quickly.

To summarize: using **conservative DCF with minimal growth, Micron's estimated intrinsic value (\~$40-$50)** is below the current market price. A **30-50% margin of safety** on that intrinsic value yields a target buy range in the $20s-$30s, which Micron hasn't seen since 2020. Thus, by Buffett's standard, Micron today is more a **hold or sell** than a screaming buy. That said, many would argue the conservative assumptions are too pessimistic given technological tailwinds. If one is confident that Micron's earnings will be materially higher in a few years (say due to AI causing a super-cycle), one might justify paying up.
