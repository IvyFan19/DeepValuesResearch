## **Latest Quarter Earnings**

-   **Q2 2025 results:** Net revenues were **RMB 356.7 bn (≈US$49.8 bn), up 22.4 % YoY** . Service revenue grew faster than merchandise revenue thanks to the **JD Food Delivery** business; the company fulfilled more than **2.2 billion orders** during its "618" promotion .

-   **Operating expenses:** Cost of revenues remains the largest expense (approx. **87 % of revenues**). Fulfilment expenses were **RMB 16.6 bn (4.6 % of revenue)**, marketing expenses **RMB 13.9 bn (3.9 % of revenue)**, R&D expenses **RMB 5.1 bn (1.4 %)** and G&A expenses **RMB 1.9 bn (0.5 %)** . Fulfilment and marketing expenses rose due to investment in new businesses.

-   **Profitability:** Operating loss was **RMB 1.7 bn** because of heavy investment in JD Food Delivery and other new services . Net income attributable to ordinary shareholders was **RMB 6.2 bn**; diluted EPS **RMB 4.15** . Free cash flow for the quarter was **RMB 22.0 bn** and cash & cash equivalents were **RMB 223.4 bn (≈US$31.2 bn)** .

-   **Share repurchase:** JD has a **US$5 bn share repurchase program** effective Sep 2024-Aug 2027 and had repurchased **80.7 M Class A shares (\~40.4 M ADSs)** for **US$1.5 bn** by 30 Jun 2025 .

## **Business Model**

-   **Revenue segments (2024):** JD's business is divided into four segments: **electronics & home appliances** (≈51 % of revenue), **general merchandise** (18 %), **marketplace & marketing services** (5 %) and **logistics & other services**(26 %) . The pie chart below illustrates the mix.

-   **Customers:** JD focuses on **mass‑market consumers** in China. It reported **588.3 million annual active customer accounts** for the twelve months ended Sep 30 2022 . Shopping frequency rose over **40 % YoY** in Q2 2025 , reflecting higher engagement.

-   **Scale & network:** JD operates China's second‑largest e‑commerce platform with integrated **self‑owned warehouses**, **last‑mile delivery fleet** and **cross‑border logistics**. The ecosystem employs about **900 000 people**and had **over 60 000 suppliers** as of 2024 . JD Food Delivery added **300 000 riders**.

### **Sources of income**

1.  **Direct sales (electronics & general merchandise):** JD sells products bought from suppliers and earns gross profit on the spread between sale price and procurement cost.

2.  **Marketplace & marketing:** Third‑party merchants list products on JD's platform and pay commission and advertising fees.

3.  **Logistics & other services:** JD Logistics provides warehousing and delivery services to JD and third‑party clients, charging service fees.

4.  **New businesses:** On‑demand delivery (food delivery and groceries) and international cross‑border commerce.

## **Headwinds & Risks**

-   **Short‑term:** Weak consumer demand in China amid economic slowdown; intense price competition from rivals (Alibaba, Pinduoduo, Douyin); higher fulfilment and marketing costs to build JD Food Delivery and overseas businesses; regulatory inspections on livestreaming and discounting.

-   **Long‑term:** Structural decline in China's population and consumption; regulatory scrutiny on platform economy and data security; geopolitical tensions restricting cross‑border trade; supply‑chain disruptions and reliance on 60 000+ suppliers ; currency risk from RMB-USD translation; capital intensive logistics network requiring continual investment.

-   **Environmental/Social risks:** High headcount and heavy reliance on riders pose labour‑cost and compliance risks; carbon‑emission goals may require costly upgrades.

## **Cost Structure**

-   **Fixed costs:** Warehouses, logistics hubs, technology infrastructure and cloud computing; depreciation & amortisation; interest expense (≈US$397 M in 2024 ).

-   **Variable costs:** Cost of goods sold (≈87 % of revenue), fulfilment (warehousing & delivery), marketing, R&D, payment processing and customer service. Marketing expenses were **RMB 37.8 bn (3.6 % of revenue) in 2022**, **RMB 40.1 bn (3.7 %) in 2023** and **RMB 48.0 bn (4.1 %) in 2024** . R&D expenses were around RMB 16-17 bn per year .

-   **Largest costs:** Procurement (COGS) and fulfilment; marketing has been increasing as JD promotes new services.

-   **Trends:** Gross margin has remained \~13 % since 2015, while operating margin improved to \~3 % in 2024; marketing spend as a percent of revenue is slowly rising . During recessions, JD tends to slash marketing and capex.

## **Management & Governance**

-   **Executive team:** Founder **Richard Qiangdong Liu** chairs the board and remains involved. **Sandy Ran Xu**, former CFO, became CEO in May 2023; **Ian Su Shan** became CFO in May 2023 . Other executives include **Pang Zhang** (CHRO) and **Chengfeng He** (Chief Compliance Officer) .

-   **Board:** Composed of Richard Liu, CEO Sandy Xu and several independent directors such as finance professor **Ming Huang** and former New Oriental president **Louis T. Hsieh** .

-   **Past performance:** Management delivered strong revenue growth and improved margins, but profits remain volatile due to heavy investment cycles. The company promptly discloses results and holds earnings calls; there are no major accounting scandals reported. However, founder Richard Liu faced a high‑profile personal allegation in 2018; the case was settled and he remains Chairman.

-   **Value alignment:** Richard Liu owns **≈11.4 % of shares** and thus benefits from long‑term value creation. Other executives receive stock‑based compensation. JD regularly repurchases shares, which aligns incentives.

## **Shareholders & Capital Returns**
