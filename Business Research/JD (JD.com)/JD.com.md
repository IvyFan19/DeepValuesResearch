### **1. Business Model Overview**

JD.com is one of China's largest e-commerce companies, operating primarily as a direct online retailer with an extensive logistics network (often likened to an "Amazon of China"). **Revenue Streams:** JD's **main income sources** are (a) direct online **product sales** (first-party retail of electronics, home appliances, general merchandise, etc.), and (b) **services**including marketplace commissions, advertising/marketing, and logistics services for third parties. In 2024, direct product sales (e.g. electronics and general merchandise) contributed roughly **80%** of revenue, while services (logistics, marketplace, ads) made up about **20%**. A **pie chart** of JD's 2024 revenue breakdown illustrates these proportions, with \~48% from electronics/home appliances, \~31% from general merchandise, \~12% from logistics/other services, and \~8% from marketplace advertising. JD's platform had approximately **588-600 million annual active customers** as of late 2023/early 2024. These users generate billions of orders; for instance, JD Logistics delivered orders at such scale that **cost per delivery hit an all-time low in Q1 2020** due to volume gains. The company also operates **JD Plus**, a paid membership loyalty program (akin to Amazon Prime) with over **30 million** subscribers by 2022, reflecting JD's push into subscription services.

### **2. Headwinds and Risks**

**Economic & Industry:** JD faces macroeconomic headwinds from **slowing consumer spending in China** and a post-pandemic economic recovery that remains uneven. Any downturn in China's economy can soften online retail demand, posing a short-term risk. Industry competition is intense: rival platforms like Alibaba and Pinduoduo engage in price wars and heavy promotions, pressuring JD's margins. Notably, JD launched a RMB¥10 billion subsidy campaign in 2023 to compete on price, indicating margin sacrifice to defend market share (short-term pain for long-term user retention). **Regulatory & Political:** China's regulatory climate for tech firms introduces uncertainty - from **antitrust actions** to data security laws. JD (like peers) operates under the government's watch; potential new regulations or a tech "crackdown" could limit certain business practices. U.S.-China geopolitical tensions also present risk (e.g. ADR delisting threats or restrictions on cross-border capital flows), though JD has mitigated some by dual-listing in Hong Kong. **Operational & Long-Term:** Maintaining JD's massive logistics infrastructure has high fixed costs; if order volume growth stalls, **operating leverage could work against JD**, compressing margins. Rapid expansion into new arenas (e.g. JD's new food delivery service) is a double-edged sword - while it opens growth avenues, it incurs **significant upfront losses** that "could potentially offset profits from JD's core operations in the near term". Executing successfully in highly competitive new sectors (food delivery, on-demand retail) will be challenging. Additionally, **technological disruption** or shifting consumer preferences (e.g. rise of video-centric shopping or social commerce) require JD to continuously adapt or risk losing relevance. In summary, **short-term risks** include margin pressure from competition and economic softness, while **long-term risks** center on regulatory changes, evolving competitive landscapes (new entrants or formats), and the need for ongoing innovation. Overall, analysts note JD's position remains robust, but these threats must be monitored.

However, as volume increases, JD achieves economies of scale - indeed, during a surge of orders in early 2020, **cost per delivery hit record lows** due to volume efficiencies offsetting added costs. This demonstrates JD's high operating leverage: greater sales volume can spread fixed logistics costs and improve per-unit profitability. **Marketing:** Selling and marketing is more discretionary; JD ramped marketing spend by +19.5% in 2024 (to ¥48.0B) to support growth initiatives, equal to \~4.1% of revenue (up from 3.7% in 2023). Overall, JD's economies of scale and tech-driven efficiencies have allowed it to **gradually increase margins** despite its low-margin retail model (operating margin rose to 3.3% in 2024 from 2.4% in 2023), highlighting improved cost leverage.

Using a Buffett-style intrinsic value approach (discounting owner earnings), we estimate JD.com's **intrinsic value** and margin of safety. We project JD's **owner earnings** (free cash flow available to shareholders) over the next decade and discount to present using a **10%-15% required return** (discount rate), then apply a **30%-50% margin of safety**. Key assumptions: JD's current annual owner earnings (free cash flow) is around $5.5-6.0 billion (approximately JD's 2024 free cash flow). Given JD's growth prospects and improving margins, we use a conservative growth rate of \~5% for owner earnings for 5 years, then taper to &lt;3% terminal growth (to reflect a low perpetual growth beyond year 10).

**Valuation Calculation:** Discounting these cash flows at 10% gives an intrinsic value estimate of roughly **$80-90 billion**for the equity (for 5 years at 5% growth, then 2% terminal) - equivalently, about **$52-$58 per share** (with \~1.54B shares). This scenario already builds in caution (low growth, modest terminal rate). If we assume a slightly higher growth (e.g. 8% for 10 years, then 2% terminal), the DCF value rises to about $110B+ (≈$70+/share). Taking a midpoint, JD's intrinsic value might be around **$90-$100B ($60+/share)** under reasonable assumptions. Now applying a **Margin of Safety**: requiring a 30-50% discount, a value investor would target a buy price \~$30-$42 per share. JD's current market cap is about $55B (share price \~$35), which is **\~50% of the $110B intrinsic estimate** in the optimistic case - i.e. about a 50% margin of safety. Even under the more conservative $80B intrinsic value, the current price offers \~30% discount. Thus, **JD at $35 appears to trade with a margin of safety of \~30-50%** relative to intrinsic value range.

Another way to frame this is to find the **implied expected return** at the current price assuming no margin of safety: If we adjust the discount rate until the DCF equals the current $55B market cap, we get an implied required return of \~15% (i.e. the market is pricing JD such that investors would earn \~15% annually if our cash flow forecasts materialize). This expected return is quite high, reflecting the market's cautious sentiment. If one were to **set margin of safety to 0%** (pay fair value), JD's price would need to roughly double from current levels to \~$70 (eliminating the discount).

In Buffett terms, JD offers a favorable risk/reward: a **double-digit expected return** if bought at \~$35 (due to the earnings yield plus growth) and a significant margin of safety built in, assuming our conservative forecasts hold. We used a **15% discount rate** (high hurdle) and &lt;3% terminal growth - at JD's current price, even a 0% margin of safety implies \~15-17% annual return potential to investors, indicating the stock is undervalued. However, prudent investors may still demand a margin of safety given execution and regulatory risks. By requiring a 15% discount rate and adding (say) 30% safety, they might only buy below \~$40 - which, conveniently, is near the stock's current level. In conclusion, **our intrinsic value estimate (Buffett method) suggests JD's stock is undervalued**, with the current price embedding a substantial margin of safety.
