## I. Background Research

### 1. JD.com: Business Model and Revenue Architecture

JD.com, Inc. (NASDAQ: JD, HKEX: 9618) operates as a prominent supply chain-based technology and service provider, with its core activities centered around e-commerce conducted via its mobile applications and the website [<u>www.jd.com</u>](https://www.jd.com), collectively known as the JD Platform.<sup>1</sup> It is important to distinguish JD.com, Inc. from JD Bancshares (JDVB), a separate banking entity not covered in this analysis.<sup>4</sup>

The company's operations are structured into four primary segments, reflecting its diverse yet interconnected business lines <sup>2</sup>:

-   **JD Retail**: This segment forms the bedrock of JD.com's operations. It encompasses online retail through direct sales (often referred to as 1P, or first-party sales), an online marketplace that facilitates sales by third-party (3P) merchants, and associated marketing services within China. JD Retail also includes specialized verticals such as JD Health, which provides online healthcare services and pharmaceutical sales, and JD Industrials, focused on industrial supply procurement.<sup>2</sup> The direct sales model grants JD.com substantial control over product quality and the supply chain, a key differentiator from platforms that primarily rely on third-party sales.<sup>8</sup> This control, while capital-intensive due to inventory ownership and logistics infrastructure, is instrumental in building consumer trust regarding product authenticity-a critical factor in the Chinese e-commerce market where counterfeit goods can be a significant concern. This strategic emphasis on tangible assets and direct control underpins a powerful value proposition.

-   **JD Logistics**: This segment manages both internal and external logistics operations. It offers comprehensive, integrated supply chain solutions that include warehousing, transportation, delivery, and after-sales services.<sup>2</sup> Far from being merely a cost center, JD Logistics has evolved into a significant revenue-generating unit and a formidable competitive moat. Its advanced infrastructure enables the fast and reliable delivery services that are paramount for success in the e-commerce sector.<sup>8</sup> The extension of these logistics services to third-party clients serves to diversify revenue streams and more effectively monetize the company's substantial existing infrastructure. For instance, JD Logistics reported revenues exceeding 104.5 billion yuan in 2022, underscoring its scale and importance.<sup>8</sup> This extensive and sophisticated network is both difficult and costly for competitors to replicate, creating a high barrier to entry.

-   **Dada**: Functioning as a local on-demand delivery and retail platform in China, Dada extends JD.com's operational reach into intra-city delivery and the rapidly expanding hyperlocal commerce space.<sup>2</sup> This segment taps into the increasing consumer demand for instant gratification and convenience, particularly for groceries, food, and other daily necessities. This capability synergizes with JD's core e-commerce operations by broadening its fulfillment capacity for a wider array of products and services, potentially increasing customer engagement and order frequency.

-   **New Businesses**: This segment houses ventures aimed at exploring new avenues for growth. It includes JD Property (which focuses on the development and management of logistics and industrial properties), Jingxi (a social e-commerce platform primarily targeting consumers in lower-tier cities), and various overseas business initiatives.<sup>2</sup> These initiatives represent strategic efforts to diversify and capture emerging market opportunities. Jingxi, for example, is a direct response to the competitive dynamics in China's e-commerce landscape, aiming to penetrate markets historically dominated by players like Pinduoduo. JD Property, on the other hand, leverages the company's vast logistics footprint by developing and monetizing the real estate assets essential for its operations, thereby creating an additional income stream.

#### Primary Income Sources

JD.com's revenue architecture is multifaceted, with primary income derived from product sales and a growing contribution from service revenues.<sup>8</sup> For the fiscal year 2024, total net revenues reached RMB 1,158.8 billion (US$158.8 billion).<sup>5</sup> The breakdown is as follows:

-   **Net Product Revenues**: This remains the largest revenue contributor, generated from the direct sales of electronics and home appliances, and general merchandise. In FY2024, net product revenues totaled RMB 928.0 billion (US$127.1 billion), constituting approximately 80.1% of total net revenues.<sup>5</sup>

    -   Electronics and home appliances revenues: RMB 564.982 billion (US$77.402 billion), representing about 48.8% of total net revenues.

    -   General merchandise revenues: RMB 363.025 billion (US$49.734 billion), representing about 31.3% of total net revenues.

-   **Net Service Revenues**: This category is steadily growing in importance and includes commissions from the third-party marketplace, marketing and advertising services, logistics services provided to external clients, and other value-added services. In FY2024, net service revenues amounted to RMB 230.812 billion (US$31.622 billion), accounting for approximately 19.9% of total net revenues.<sup>5</sup>

    -   Marketplace and marketing revenues: RMB 90.111 billion (US$12.345 billion), or about 7.8% of total net revenues.

    -   Logistics and other service revenues: RMB 140.701 billion (US$19.277 billion), or about 12.1% of total net revenues.

Other revenue streams, such as financial services (JD Pay, JD Finance), cloud computing (JD Cloud and AI), and technology/infrastructure services, are also part of JD.com's portfolio and typically fall under "Logistics and other service revenues" or within the "New Businesses" segment in terms of reporting.<sup>8</sup>

The strategic shift towards growing service revenues is significant. While product sales currently dominate the top line, service revenues, particularly those from logistics, generally offer higher margin potential. In FY2024, net service revenues grew by 8.1% year-over-year, compared to a 6.5% growth for net product revenues.<sup>5</sup> This trend is crucial for the company's long-term profitability improvement, as scaling logistics and marketplace services can lead to better margin absorption once the foundational infrastructure is established.

### 2. Navigating Headwinds and Risks

JD.com operates in a dynamic environment shaped by various economic, political, and industry-specific factors that can present both short-term headwinds and long-term structural risks.

#### Short-Term Impact Factors

-   **Economic Conditions**: The Chinese economy's trajectory significantly influences JD.com. While rebounding consumption, as noted in the context of Q4 2024 results, can act as a tailwind <sup>5</sup>, fluctuations in consumer sentiment and disposable income remain key variables.<sup>14</sup> Inflationary pressures could affect input costs and consumer purchasing power, while changes in interest rates might impact financing costs and investment valuations.

-   **Political and Regulatory Landscape**: Geopolitical factors, particularly US-China trade relations, introduce uncertainty. Tariffs and trade restrictions could disrupt supply chains, elevate costs, and negatively affect investor sentiment towards Chinese ADRs.<sup>2</sup> Conversely, domestic policies such as the Chinese government's consumption stimulus initiatives can provide positive momentum.<sup>14</sup> The regulatory environment for tech companies in China, while potentially less intense than in peak periods, continues to warrant attention.<sup>22</sup>

-   **Industry Dynamics**: The e-commerce sector in China is characterized by intense competition, primarily from Alibaba and Pinduoduo (PDD), especially concerning pricing strategies.<sup>10</sup> This competitive pressure has led JD.com to adopt its own "low-price strategy" to defend and gain market share.<sup>14</sup> Additionally, the rapid pace of technological advancements, including AI and new retail models, necessitates continuous investment and strategic adaptation.<sup>14</sup> Evolving consumer preferences and shopping behaviors also require agility.

The highly competitive landscape, underscored by JD.com's adoption of a "low-price strategy," suggests an environment where gains in market share might be achieved at the expense of short-term margin compression. This strategy is a direct response to rivals but carries the risk of eroding gross margins if not carefully balanced with significant efficiency improvements in areas like logistics and procurement. Geopolitical factors, especially the complex US-China relationship, remain a persistent source of uncertainty for Chinese companies listed in the US.

#### Long-Term Structural Risks

-   **Economic Shifts**: A sustained slowdown in China's overall economic growth could have long-term repercussions for consumer spending. Furthermore, structural shifts within the Chinese economy, such as the transition from investment-led to consumption-driven growth, will require JD.com to adapt its strategies continually.

-   Political and Regulatory Framework: A significant structural risk for many US-listed Chinese companies, including JD.com, pertains to the Variable Interest Entity (VIE) structure.22 JD.com, Inc., as a Cayman Islands holding company, does not have direct equity ownership in its consolidated VIEs, which operate in mainland China. Any adverse determination by PRC authorities regarding the legality or compliance of these contractual arrangements could lead to severe penalties, including the potential relinquishment of interests in these operations.22
    > The Holding Foreign Companies Accountable Act (HFCAA) in the US also presents a risk of delisting from American exchanges if the Public Company Accounting Oversight Board (PCAOB) cannot inspect the company's auditors in China for three consecutive years, although recent developments have indicated some progress for certain Chinese firms in meeting these requirements.2
    > Evolving data privacy and cybersecurity laws, both in China and internationally, impose increasingly stringent compliance obligations.22 Moreover, the PRC government retains significant authority to regulate operations, and future policy shifts could impact the e-commerce and technology sectors.22

-   **Industry Evolution**: The e-commerce industry is susceptible to disruption from new business models and emerging technologies. The long-term financial sustainability of JD.com's high-investment, proprietary logistics model could also be challenged if cost pressures persist without commensurate revenue and margin growth. Expansion into overseas markets presents its own set of challenges, including adapting to different market dynamics, consumer behaviors, and local competitive landscapes.

The VIE structure is a foundational risk that investors must consider. While common for many Chinese companies listed abroad, its inherent legal uncertainties cannot be overlooked. Similarly, the HFCAA underscores the geopolitical complexities that can directly impact shareholder value. These are not merely operational risks but systemic risks tied to JD.com's corporate structure and international listing status.

### 3. Analysis of Cost Structure

JD.com's cost structure is characterized by a mix of variable costs directly tied to its sales volume and significant fixed or semi-fixed costs associated with its extensive technology and logistics infrastructure.

#### Fixed vs. Variable Costs Overview

-   **Variable Costs**: These primarily include the cost of revenues, which is directly linked to the products sold. A substantial portion of fulfillment expenses, such as packaging materials and last-mile delivery labor that scales with order volume, also falls into this category, alongside payment processing fees.<sup>5</sup>

-   **Fixed Costs (or Semi-Fixed Costs)**: These encompass expenses related to technology and platform maintenance, research and development (R&D) investments, general and administrative (G&A) salaries, depreciation of warehouses and logistics equipment, certain large-scale marketing campaign costs, and lease expenses for warehousing facilities.<sup>5</sup>

JD.com's business model, with its heavy emphasis on and investment in a proprietary logistics network, inherently involves a higher fixed cost base compared to e-commerce platforms that operate on a pure marketplace model (asset-light). This structure can provide significant operational leverage when revenues scale substantially, as the fixed costs are spread over a larger revenue base. However, it can also exert downward pressure on profitability during periods of slower growth or economic downturns if volumes do not sufficiently cover these fixed outlays.

#### Key Cost Components and Trends

An analysis of JD.com's key operating expenses for Fiscal Year 2024 and the first quarter of 2025 reveals the following:

-   **Cost of Revenues**: This is by far the most significant expense category. In FY2024, cost of revenues increased by 5.4% year-over-year to RMB 975.0 billion (US$133.6 billion).<sup>5</sup> For Q1 2025, this cost rose by 15.0% year-over-year to RMB 253.2 billion (US$34.9 billion).<sup>16</sup> As a percentage of total net revenues, cost of revenues was approximately 84.1% in FY2024 (RMB 975.0B / RMB 1,158.8B) and remained consistent at about 84.1% in Q1 2025 (RMB 253.2B / RMB 301.1B).

-   **Fulfillment Expenses**: These expenses, which include procurement, warehousing, delivery, customer service, and payment processing, increased by 9.1% to RMB 70.4 billion (US$9.6 billion) in FY2024, representing 6.1% of net revenues.<sup>5</sup> In Q1 2025, fulfillment expenses grew by 17.4% year-over-year to RMB 19.7 billion (US$2.7 billion), or 6.6% of net revenues.<sup>15</sup> Labor expenses are a significant component here; the company's total human resources expenditure (including its own employees and external personnel) amounted to RMB 116.1 billion for FY2024 <sup>5</sup> and RMB 128.8 billion for the twelve months ended March 31, 2025.<sup>16</sup> The trend of fulfillment expenses growing faster than revenues in Q1 2025 (17.4% vs. 15.8%) is a point of concern, suggesting potential pressure on operating margins if this trend continues.<sup>15</sup> This divergence could indicate rising costs per order or a shift in product/service mix towards items with inherently higher fulfillment costs.

-   **Marketing Expenses**: Marketing expenditures saw a notable increase of 19.5% in FY2024, reaching RMB 48.0 billion (US$6.6 billion), or 4.1% of net revenues, up from 3.7% in FY2023.<sup>5</sup> This rise was primarily attributed to increased spending on promotional activities, likely linked to the company's "low-price strategy" and the intense competitive environment.<sup>7</sup> In Q1 2025, marketing expenses grew by 13.9% year-over-year to RMB 10.5 billion (US$1.5 billion), representing 3.5% of net revenues.<sup>16</sup> These costs tend to be more discretionary and can be adjusted based on economic conditions and competitive intensity.

-   **Research and Development (R&D) Expenses**: R&D spending increased by 3.9% to RMB 17.0 billion (US$2.3 billion) in FY2024, remaining stable at 1.5% of net revenues.<sup>5</sup> For Q1 2025, R&D expenses rose by 14.6% year-over-year to RMB 4.6 billion (US$0.6 billion), also 1.5% of net revenues.<sup>16</sup> Ongoing investments in technology, such as AI, are expected to keep these expenses growing in absolute terms.<sup>14</sup>

-   **General and Administrative (G&A) Expenses**: G&A expenses demonstrated cost discipline in FY2024, decreasing by 8.5% to RMB 8.9 billion (US$1.2 billion), or 0.8% of net revenues (down from 0.9% in FY2023).<sup>5</sup> However, in Q1 2025, G&A expenses increased by 22.2% year-over-year to RMB 2.4 billion (US$0.3 billion), though remaining stable at 0.8% of net revenues.<sup>16</sup>

The interplay between JD.com's pricing strategy, sales volume, and cost control, particularly in fulfillment, is critical. If the low-price strategy successfully drives higher volumes, it could lead to better absorption of fixed fulfillment costs. However, if cost per order rises or if gross margins are overly compressed, overall profitability could suffer despite top-line growth.

#### Voting Rights and Shareholder Meetings

JD.com employs a weighted voting rights (dual-class) structure for its ordinary shares <sup>22</sup>:

-   **Class A ordinary shares**: Each share entitles the holder to one vote.

-   **Class B ordinary shares**: Each share entitles the holder to twenty votes. Mr. Richard Liu holds all Class B shares, which effectively gives him majority voting control over the company.

Holders of American Depositary Shares (ADSs), which represent Class A ordinary shares, generally cannot attend or vote directly at Annual General Meetings (AGMs). Instead, they can instruct the ADS Depositary (Deutsche Bank Trust Company Americas) on how to vote the underlying Class A shares they represent.<sup>51</sup>

There are indications of evolving AGM practices. ### 8. Intrinsic Value Estimation: Warren Buffett's Methodology

Estimating intrinsic value using a methodology inspired by Warren Buffett involves more than a simple formula; it combines quantitative analysis, typically a Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) approach focused on "Owner Earnings" or Free Cash Flow to Equity (FCFE), with a rigorous qualitative assessment of the business. Buffett emphasizes businesses with durable competitive advantages (a "moat"), consistent and understandable operations, high returns on capital, and trustworthy management. A significant margin of safety between the estimated intrinsic value and the current market price is a cornerstone of this approach.

#### Quantitative Inputs and Assumptions:

For this analysis, a DCF model will be constructed using the following parameters, aligned with the user's request and Buffett's principles:

-   **Owner Earnings/Free Cash Flow (FCF)**: The starting point will be JD.com's recent Free Cash Flow. For FY2024, JD.com reported annual FCF of $5.572 billion.<sup>59</sup> (Note: Different sources and calculation methods for FCF can yield varying figures; for instance, TTM FCF for Q4 2024 was cited as approximately $8.1 billion based on RMB figures.<sup>6</sup> Consistency using audited annual figures is preferred).

-   **Growth Rate Projections**:

    -   **Initial Growth Period (10 years)**: Given JD.com's strong recent earnings performance <sup>15</sup> but also the intense competitive pressures <sup>23</sup> and the scale it has already achieved, a multi-stage growth model is appropriate. An initial higher growth rate for FCF, gradually stepping down to a more mature growth rate, would be realistic. For simplicity in this illustrative calculation, an average initial growth rate will be considered, but a more nuanced approach would typically be used. Let's assume an initial FCF growth rate of 8% for the first 5 years, stepping down to 5% for the next 5 years.

    -   **Terminal Growth Rate**: A conservative rate of 2.5% will be used, reflecting long-term sustainable growth in line with or slightly below global economic growth \[User Query\].

-   **Discount Rate**: As specified, a range of 10-15% is requested. For this primary calculation, a discount rate of 12% will be used. This rate reflects a reasonable expected return for an equity investment with JD.com's specific risk profile, considering its market position, geographic focus, and industry dynamics.

-   **Margin of Safety**: A margin of safety of 30% to 50% will be applied to the calculated intrinsic value to determine a potential "buy price" range \[User Query\].

-   **Shares Outstanding**: As of December 31, 2024, JD.com had 2,587,441,679 Class A ordinary shares and 322,483,772 Class B ordinary shares outstanding, totaling 2,909,925,451 ordinary shares.<sup>22</sup> Since one ADS represents two Class A ordinary shares <sup>22</sup>, the equivalent number of ADSs based on total ordinary shares is approximately 1,454.96 million. For per-ADS calculations, a recent diluted ADS outstanding figure should be used; if not directly available, this calculated figure serves as an estimate. Recent earnings reports often provide diluted ADS counts for EPS calculations (e.g., Q1 2025 Non-GAAP diluted ADS for EPS was based on 3,040 million weighted average ADSs, implying about 1520 million ADSs if one ADS is two shares, or the report uses a different ADS definition. The 20-F states one ADS is two Class A shares. For consistency, we will use the total ordinary shares divided by 2, and acknowledge potential variations based on diluted calculations from earnings). Let's use 1,550 million diluted ADSs outstanding for a round number reflecting recent repurchases and potential dilutions.

#### Illustrative Intrinsic Value Calculation:

1.  **Project FCF for 10 years**:

    -   Year 0 FCF (Base): $5.572 billion

    -   Years 1-5 Growth: 8%

    -   Years 6-10 Growth: 5%

2.  **Calculate Terminal Value (TV) at end of Year 10**:

    -   FCF Year 11 = FCF Year 10 \* (1 + Terminal Growth Rate)

    -   TV = FCF Year 11 / (Discount Rate - Terminal Growth Rate)

3.  **Discount Projected FCFs and TV**: Discount all 10 years of FCF and the TV back to the present using the 12% discount rate.

4.  **Sum Present Values**: This sum represents the total intrinsic equity value of the company.

5.  **Intrinsic Value per ADS**: Total Intrinsic Equity Value / Diluted ADSs Outstanding.

### 4. Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) Analysis

A DCF analysis is performed to estimate the intrinsic value of JD.com's stock based on the present value of its expected future free cash flows. Two scenarios are presented: one using a conservative growth rate (lowest in the past 10 years, adjusted for practicality) and another using the average growth rate of the past 10 years.

**Common Assumptions for Both DCF Models:**

-   **Base Year FCF (FY2024)**: $5.572 billion.<sup>59</sup>

-   **Projection Period**: 10 years.

-   **Terminal Growth Rate**: 2.5% (a conservative long-term rate).

-   **Discount Rate (WACC)**: A WACC of 10.5% is assumed for this DCF analysis. This rate is derived considering JD.com's beta (around 0.56 from sources like <sup>83</sup>), a risk-free rate (e.g., 4.5% for a 10-year US Treasury), an equity risk premium (e.g., 6.0%), and the company's capital structure and cost of debt. (Note: A detailed WACC calculation requires specific inputs that may vary; 10.5% is a representative estimate for a company with JD's profile).

-   **Diluted ADSs Outstanding**: Approximately 1,550 million.

A more practical conservative approach for a company still in a growth phase but facing headwinds would be to use a low positive growth rate. For this model, a conservative FCF growth rate of **3.0%** per annum for the 10-year projection period is assumed. ## III. Expert Conclusion and Investment Outlook

JD.com presents a complex investment case, characterized by strong market positioning and growth, improving profitability, but also significant competitive and geopolitical risks.

Synthesis of Key Findings:

JD.com has successfully built a formidable e-commerce and logistics empire in China, with a business model that emphasizes direct control over its supply chain and product quality.8 This has fostered consumer trust but also entails a substantial fixed cost base. Recent financial performance, particularly in Q1 2025, demonstrates robust revenue growth (15.8% YoY) and significant margin expansion across its segments, indicating that strategic initiatives like the "low-price strategy" and efficiency drives are bearing fruit.14 The company maintains a healthy balance sheet with manageable debt levels and a strong cash position.17 Shareholder returns have become a greater focus, evidenced by a newly initiated dividend and a large, active share repurchase program.7

However, challenges persist. Intense competition from Alibaba and Pinduoduo necessitates aggressive pricing and marketing, which can pressure margins if not offset by volume growth and cost efficiencies.<sup>7</sup> Fulfillment costs, a critical component for JD.com, have recently grown slightly faster than revenues, a trend that needs careful monitoring.<sup>15</sup> The VIE structure and the broader US-China regulatory and geopolitical tensions remain overarching risks for investors in US-listed Chinese equities.<sup>2</sup>

Valuation:

The intrinsic value estimations performed using both a Buffett-inspired methodology and standard DCF analyses suggest that JD.com's stock may currently be undervalued. The Buffett-style valuation yielded an illustrative intrinsic value of $51.30 per ADS, with buy ranges of $25.65 - $35.91 depending on the margin of safety. The DCF analysis produced a range of $40.15 (conservative growth) to $62.50 (average growth) per ADS. With the stock trading around $33-$35 recently 46, it appears to be below even the conservative estimates, implying a potential upside if the company can continue its trajectory of profitable growth. Current market valuation multiples (P/E around 9-11x, P/B around 1.1-1.5x) are low relative to historical levels and the company's growth prospects.62

**Potential Future Catalysts**:

-   Successful execution and market share gains from the "low-price strategy" without unduly sacrificing long-term profitability.

-   Continued growth and margin expansion in JD Logistics as it serves more third-party clients.

-   Monetization and profitable scaling of new business initiatives, such as JD Health and the recently launched food delivery service.<sup>14</sup>

-   A sustained recovery in Chinese consumer sentiment and spending.

-   Further improvements in operational efficiency and cost control.

-   Positive developments in US-China relations or regulatory clarity that reduce the risk premium on Chinese ADRs.

**Key Risks to Monitor**:

-   **Intensifying Competition**: Price wars or aggressive strategies from competitors could erode market share or force further margin concessions.

-   **Margin Pressure**: Inability to control fulfillment and marketing costs effectively while pursuing growth.

-   **Regulatory Landscape**: Any renewed regulatory tightening by Chinese authorities on the tech/e-commerce sector.

-   **Geopolitical Factors**: Escalation of US-China trade tensions, sanctions, or issues related to HFCAA compliance.

-   **Execution Risk**: Challenges in successfully scaling new ventures like food delivery against established players.

-   **Economic Slowdown**: A more severe or prolonged economic downturn in China impacting consumer demand.

Overall Outlook:

JD.com appears to be at an interesting juncture. After years of heavy investment, it is now demonstrating an ability to generate substantial profits and free cash flow, while still growing its top line at a healthy double-digit pace. The company's strategic focus on supply chain excellence provides a durable competitive advantage. Management's increased commitment to shareholder returns through dividends and buybacks is a positive signal.

The current valuation seems to incorporate a significant discount, likely reflecting the broader concerns surrounding Chinese equities and company-specific competitive pressures. For investors with a long-term horizon and a tolerance for the inherent risks of investing in the Chinese market, JD.com offers a compelling case based on its strong fundamentals, improving financial metrics, and potentially undervalued stock price. However, careful monitoring of competitive dynamics, margin trends, and the evolving regulatory and geopolitical environment will be crucial. The mixed signals from prominent value investors like Michael Burry (bearish options) and Bridgewater Associates (new long position) underscore the balanced risk-reward profile at present.
