## 1. Latest Quarter Earnings Summary

**Reporting Period:** Fiscal Year 2026, Second Quarter (Ended November 30, 2025) **Release Date:** December 9, 2025

Oracle Corporation's second-quarter results for Fiscal Year 2026 represent a definitive inflection point in the company's fifty-year history. The report confirms that Oracle has successfully navigated its transition from a legacy on-premise database vendor to a primary engine of the Artificial Intelligence (AI) revolution. The financial results were characterized by a massive acceleration in contract backlog, indicating that the market has validated Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI) as a viable-and in some cases superior-alternative to the traditional hyperscalers (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud) for training large language models (LLMs).

### Revenue and Top-Line Performance

Oracle reported total quarterly revenues of **$14.1 billion**, representing a 9% increase year-over-year in both USD and constant currency. While the headline revenue growth might appear modest compared to smaller high-growth SaaS peers, the composition of this revenue tells a story of aggressive structural rotation.

-   **Cloud Revenue Acceleration:** The total Cloud Revenue, comprising both Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS) and Software as a Service (SaaS), reached **$5.9 billion**, up 24% year-over-year. This segment now accounts for a significant portion of the total top line, reducing the company's reliance on fluctuating license revenues.

-   **IaaS Hypergrowth:** The standout metric was Cloud Infrastructure (IaaS) revenue, which surged **52%** to $2.4 billion. This growth rate significantly outpaces the reported growth rates of competitors AWS and Azure in comparable periods, validating Oracle's "Gen2 Cloud" architecture which utilizes non-blocking RDMA networking to optimize AI training clusters.

-   **SaaS Stability:** Cloud Application revenue grew 10% to $3.5 billion. Within this, strategic back-office applications showed resilience: Fusion Cloud ERP revenue rose 18%, and NetSuite Cloud ERP revenue increased 20%. This demonstrates that even as the AI infrastructure narrative dominates, Oracle's core business application stickiness remains intact.

### The "RPO Anomaly"

The most significant data point in the Q2 report was the explosion in Remaining Performance Obligations (RPO). RPO represents the total value of future revenue under contract that has not yet been recognized. It serves as a critical leading indicator of future growth.

-   **Metric:** Total RPO hit **$97 billion** in the quarter, up 49% in USD and 50% in constant currency.

-   **Analysis:** This magnitude of backlog accumulation is unprecedented for a mature software company. It indicates that customers-specifically large AI labs and hyperscalers needing overflow capacity-are signing multi-year, multi-billion dollar commitments to secure GPU supply. CEO Safra Catz noted that this backlog gives Oracle clear revenue visibility for the next several years, fundamentally de-risking the growth trajectory.

### Profitability and Margins

Despite the capital-intensive nature of building out data centers for AI, Oracle maintained robust profitability, leveraging the operating efficiency of its cloud stack.

-   **Operating Income:** GAAP operating income was $4.2 billion (30% margin), while Non-GAAP operating income stood at $6.1 billion (43% margin). The divergence is largely driven by stock-based compensation and amortization of intangibles from acquisitions like Cerner.

-   **Earnings Per Share:** Non-GAAP EPS grew 10% to $1.47, while GAAP EPS surged 24% to $1.10. This earnings leverage, even amidst a massive CapEx cycle, highlights the company's disciplined expense management in legacy areas (hardware and services) to fund cloud expansion.

### Strategic Commentary

The earnings call highlighted a pivot in corporate strategy regarding partnerships. Oracle has abandoned its isolationist stance, fully embracing "MultiCloud." By placing Oracle database hardware *inside* Azure, Google Cloud, and AWS data centers, Oracle has neutralized the "data gravity" argument that previously prevented customers from using Oracle databases in the cloud. Larry Ellison emphasized that this allows customers to use "best-of-breed" services, combining OpenAI (on Azure) with Oracle's vector database capabilities.

## 2. Background Research

### 2.1 Business Model Summary

Oracle operates a vertically integrated technology stack, famously referred to as "The Red Stack," ranging from silicon and hardware (Exadata) to infrastructure software (Linux/Database), middleware, and end-user applications (ERP/HCM/CX). The business model is transitioning from an upfront licensing model (CapEx for customers) to a consumption-based cloud model (OpEx for customers).

#### 2.1.1 Main Sources of Income

Oracle's revenue streams have evolved significantly. The following breakdown estimates the proportional contribution of each segment based on Fiscal Year 2025/2026 run rates.

-   **Cloud Services & License Support (\~74%):** This is the recurring revenue engine. It includes subscription fees for SaaS applications (NetSuite, Fusion) and consumption fees for IaaS (Compute, Storage). It also includes the highly profitable "License Support" fees-essentially maintenance contracts that customers pay annually (typically 22% of license cost) to receive updates and patches for legacy on-premise software.

-   **Cloud License & On-Premise License (\~9%):** The traditional "per processor" or "per user" perpetual license fees. This segment is essentially flat or declining as customers migrate to the cloud.

-   **Services (\~10%):** Professional services, consulting, and education to help customers implement Oracle software. This is a lower-margin business used primarily to drive software adoption.

-   **Hardware (\~7%):** Sales of engineered systems like Exadata and servers. While a small portion of revenue, this hardware underpins the high performance of the Oracle Database.

#### 2.1.2 Customer Base

Oracle's reach is ubiquitous in the enterprise world.

-   **Total Customers:** Approximately **430,000 customers** globally across 175 countries.

-   **Enterprise Penetration:** Oracle claims 100% of the Fortune 100 as customers.

-   **SMB Penetration:** Through the acquisition of NetSuite, Oracle commands a massive base of over 37,000 small-to-mid-sized businesses.

-   **Sector Concentration:** Highly concentrated in regulated industries such as Finance, Utilities, Healthcare, and Government, where the robustness of the Oracle Database is non-negotiable.

#### 2.1.3 Units and Sales Volume

Oracle does not report "units" in the traditional sense for its cloud business, but key volume indicators include:

-   **GPU Consumption:** In Q2 FY25, GPU consumption hours rose **336%**, indicating massive unit volume growth in compute sales.

-   **NetSuite Subscribers:** Continues to grow in the double digits, expanding the sheer number of ERP seats sold.

-   **Database Instances:** With the MultiCloud partnerships, millions of Oracle Database instances are now being provisioned within Azure and Google Cloud, expanding the "unit" count beyond Oracle's own data centers.

#### 2.1.4 The Economic Moat

Oracle possesses a **Wide Economic Moat**, primarily derived from **High Switching Costs** and **Intangible Assets**.

1.  **High Switching Costs (Data Gravity):** The Oracle Database is the system of record for the world's most critical transactional data (e.g., credit card processing, airline reservations). Migrating data out of Oracle is not just technically difficult; it introduces existential operational risk. CIOs are rarely fired for renewing Oracle, but they can be fired for a failed migration to PostgreSQL. This stickiness allows Oracle to raise prices annually with little churn.

2.  **Cost Advantage (OCI Generation 2):** Unlike AWS or Azure, which built their cloud networks over a decade ago, Oracle built OCI recently with a "flat" network architecture (RDMA). This design reduces latency between servers, making it significantly more efficient and cheaper for AI training clusters. This technological advantage has allowed Oracle to undercut competitors on price while maintaining margins.

### 2.2 Headwinds and Risks

#### 2.2.1 Short-Term Factors (Temporary)

-   **Supply Chain Constraints:** Oracle's growth is currently supply-constrained, not demand-constrained. The company is aggressively purchasing Nvidia H100 and H200 GPUs. Any delay in Nvidia's delivery schedules directly impacts Oracle's ability to convert its $97 billion RPO into recognized revenue.

-   **Capital Expenditure Shock:** To build out capacity, Oracle has guided for CapEx to double (approaching $20-$25 billion annually). In the short term, this will depress Free Cash Flow (FCF) and could spook investors accustomed to Oracle's capital-light software model.

-   **Currency Headwinds:** With significant revenue in Europe and Asia, a strengthening US Dollar acts as a temporary drag on reported USD earnings.

#### 2.2.2 Long-Term Factors (Permanent)

-   **The "Debt Blob":** Oracle carries over **$105 billion** in total debt, a legacy of funding share buybacks and the Cerner acquisition. JPMorgan analysts have flagged this leverage (Debt/EBITDA &gt; 4x) as a structural risk, significantly higher than peers like Microsoft (near zero net debt). In a "higher-for-longer" interest rate environment, debt servicing could crowd out innovation or buybacks.

-   **Commoditization of AI Compute:** While Oracle has a pricing advantage now, AWS, Azure, and Google are all developing proprietary custom silicon (Trainium, Maia, TPU). If the industry shifts away from Nvidia GPUs toward proprietary chips, Oracle's hardware neutrality could become a disadvantage.

-   **Database Erosion:** Open-source alternatives (PostgreSQL, MongoDB) continue to mature. While they haven't displaced Oracle in the high-end enterprise, they are the default choice for new startups, posing a generational risk to Oracle's dominance.

### 2.3 Cost Structure

Oracle's cost structure is evolving from a software-centric model (high gross margins, low variable costs) to an infrastructure-centric model (lower gross margins, high variable costs).

#### 2.3.1 Key Cost Components

1.  **Cost of Revenue (Cloud Services):** This is the fastest-growing cost line. It includes data center energy, bandwidth, and the depreciation of servers and GPUs. As IaaS grows, this cost naturally increases, pressuring the aggregate gross margin down from historical 80% levels toward the 70% range.

2.  **Labor Expenses:** Significant spend on R&D engineering talent. Oracle competes with Meta and Google for AI talent, driving up compensation costs. Stock-based compensation is approximately $4-5 billion annually.

3.  **Sales & Marketing:** A massive global sales force is required to manage complex enterprise contracts. This is a largely fixed cost that scales with revenue.

#### 2.3.2 Significant Costs

**Depreciation** is becoming the most significant non-cash cost. With CapEx hitting $25 billion, the depreciation of these assets over 3-5 years will weigh heavily on GAAP Operating Income, though it does not impact cash flow.

#### 2.3.3 Cost Trends

-   **Rising Variable Costs:** In inflationary environments, energy costs for data centers rise. However, Oracle's contracts often include inflation adjustments.

-   **Operating Leverage:** Despite rising costs, Oracle is demonstrating operating leverage. The "fixed" costs of the OCI network are being spread over a rapidly growing revenue base, allowing Operating Margins to expand (hitting 43% Non-GAAP in Q2).

### 5.2 Buffett-Style Checklist Analysis

Warren Buffett seeks businesses with durable competitive advantages ("Moats"), rational management, and a margin of safety. Let's evaluate Oracle against these criteria.

#### 1. Valuation Discipline

-   **P/E Ratio ≤ 15:** **FAIL.** Oracle currently trades at \~37x P/E (FY25) and higher on TTM. The market has repriced it as a high-growth AI stock, removing the value discount it held for a decade.

-   **P/B Ratio ≤ 1.5:** **FAIL.** Price-to-Book is extremely high (&gt;20x) due to the depressed equity base from buybacks.

-   **Margin of Safety:** **FAIL.** At \~$220/share, the stock is priced for perfection, assuming the backlog converts seamlessly to high-margin revenue.

#### 2. Cash Generation & Allocation

-   **FCF Positive:** **FAIL (Current).** While historically a cash machine, FCF turned negative in the TTM period due to the massive CapEx spike. However, this is "Growth CapEx," not maintenance, which Buffett might forgive if the ROIC is high.

-   **Return on Retained Earnings:** **PASS.** Management has proven adept at compounding value, turning retained earnings into market cap growth via the cloud transition.

#### 3. Growth & Predictability

-   **EPS Record:** **PASS.** EPS has consistently risen (adjusting for the one-time tax hit in 2018 and Cerner integration costs).

-   **Owner's Earnings:** **PASS.** Core Owner's Earnings (Net Income + D&A - Maintenance CapEx) remain very strong. The current CapEx is discretionary for growth.

#### 4. Balance Sheet Safety

-   **Debt-to-Equity &lt; 0.5:** **FAIL.** Oracle is highly leveraged with &gt;$100B in debt. Buffett generally dislikes companies that require massive debt to fund operations or acquisitions, though Oracle's cash flow usually covers interest comfortably.

-   **Interest Coverage ≥ 3×:** **PASS.** Interest coverage is approx 6x.

-   **Current Ratio &gt; 1.5:** **FAIL.** Current ratio is typically &lt; 1.0 (currently \~0.75). This is typical for subscription businesses with high deferred revenue (which is a liability on paper but cash in the bank), so this "failure" is a technicality of the business model.

#### 5. Profitability & Efficiency

-   **Gross Margin &gt; 40%:** **PASS.** 71% is world-class.

-   **Net Margin &gt; 20%:** **PASS.** Consistently &gt;20%.

-   **ROE ≥ 15%:** **PASS.** ROE is consistently high (&gt;50%) due to leverage.

-   **ROIC ≥ 12%:** **BORDERLINE.** ROIC hovers around 10-11%, slightly below the 12% ideal threshold, dragged down by the massive goodwill on the balance sheet from acquisitions (Cerner, NetSuite).

**Verdict:** Oracle is a **"Quality"** company with a massive moat, but it **fails the "Value"** test at current prices. It operates with more leverage than Buffett typically prefers.

## 6. Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) Analysis

Given the disconnect between current cash flows (depressed by CapEx) and future cash flows (implied by the $455B backlog), a DCF requires careful segmenting of growth phases.

**Key Inputs:**

-   **WACC:** 10.8%. This reflects the cost of equity (Beta \~1.0) and the cost of debt (approx 4-5%).

-   **Shares Outstanding:** 2.83 Billion.

-   **Net Debt:** $95 Billion.

### 6.1 Conservative Case

-   **Assumption:** The "AI Bubble" bursts. RPO backlog faces cancellations. CapEx spending remains high but utilization is low.

-   **FCF Projection:** $10B (Year 1) growing at 5% annually (inflation + slight growth).

-   **Terminal Growth:** 2%.

-   **Calculation:** PV of 10yr flows + PV of Terminal Value.

-   **Intrinsic Value:** **$98.00**.

-   **Verdict:** Significant downside (-55%). This prices Oracle as a legacy stagnant utility.

### 6.2 Nominal Case (Base Case)

-   **Assumption:** Oracle executes on its backlog. Cloud revenue grows 15-20% CAGR for 5 years, then normalizes. CapEx yields returns, expanding FCF margins back to 25% by Year 5.

-   **FCF Projection:**

    -   Year 1: $12B (CapEx heavy)

    -   Year 2: $18B (Revenue scales, CapEx stabilizes)

    -   Year 3: $24B

    -   Year 4: $30B

    -   Year 5: $35B

    -   Growth Y6-10: 8%.

-   **Terminal Growth:** 3%.

-   **Intrinsic Value:** **$205.00**.

-   **Verdict:** **Fairly Valued.** The current stock price (\~$215-$220) effectively prices in this successful execution scenario.

### 6.3 Optimistic Case

-   **Assumption:** "Supercycle." Oracle displaces a major hyperscaler to become the \#2 AI cloud. Margins expand to 35% net.

-   **FCF Projection:** FCF doubles every 2 years as CapEx intensity falls and software margins kick in. Terminal FCF reaches $60B+ by Year 10.

-   **Terminal Growth:** 4%.

-   **Intrinsic Value:** **$315.00**.

-   **Verdict:** **Undervalued.** Upside potential of \~45% if the AI supercycle is sustained for a decade.

## 7. Conclusions and Risks

Oracle Corporation is a rare example of a legacy technology giant that has successfully disrupted itself. By aggressively pivoting to cloud infrastructure and rewriting its database for the cloud era, it has secured a vital role in the AI ecosystem.

**The Bull Case:**

-   The $455 billion RPO is real and legally binding. Even if AI hype cools, these contracts provide a decade of revenue visibility.

-   Oracle's "Switzerland" strategy (MultiCloud) allows it to monetize Azure and Google customers, vastly expanding its TAM.

-   The transition from financier leadership (Catz) to engineering leadership (Magouyrk) ensures product competitiveness.

**The Bear Case:**

-   **The Leverage Trap:** With over $100B in debt, Oracle has little room for error. If interest rates rise or the economy enters a deep recession, servicing this debt while funding $25B in CapEx will be painful.

-   **Execution Risk:** Building gigawatt-scale data centers is hard. Supply chain delays for GPUs could stall revenue recognition.

-   **Valuation:** At &gt;35x earnings, the stock is priced for high growth. Any earnings miss will result in severe multiple compression.

**Final Recommendation:** Oracle is a **Long-Term Hold**. The structural tailwinds of data sovereignty and AI training demand align perfectly with Oracle's new product portfolio. However, investors should be wary of the current valuation and look to accumulate on pullbacks toward the **$180-$190** level, which provides a better margin of safety relative to the Nominal DCF value.
