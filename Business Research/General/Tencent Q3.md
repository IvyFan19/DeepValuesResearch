# **Latest Quarter Earnings**

In 3Q 2025 Tencent achieved solid growth. Total revenue was RMB 192.9 billion (≈$27.1 B), up 15% YoY. Gross profit reached RMB 108.8 B (+22% YoY). On a non-IFRS basis (excluding one-time items), operating profit was RMB 72.6 B (+18%), with an operating margin of \~38% (versus 37% a year earlier). Non-IFRS net profit was RMB 70.6 B (+18%) (EPS RMB 7.769), while IFRS net profit was RMB 64.9 B (+20%).

Capital spending was RMB 13.0 B (down 24% YoY), and free cash flow (FCF) was RMB 58.5 B (flat YoY). Tencent held RMB 493.3 B in cash (+16% YoY) and had a net cash position of RMB 102.4 B. In Q3 alone the company repurchased 35.4 million shares at ≈HKD 21.1 B. Overall, results were driven by continued strength in gaming, advertising, and fintech services, aided by AI investments (as noted by CEO Pony Ma).

# **Background Research**

## **1. Business Model**

Tencent's revenues come chiefly from three segments:

-   Value-Added Services (VAS, 49%) - Primarily online games and social networks. In 2024 VAS revenue was RMB 319.2 B (up 7% YoY). This includes domestic and international gaming and paid features on QQ/WeChat.

-   FinTech & Business Services (32%) - Digital payment services, cloud and enterprise services. In 2024 these brought in RMB 212.0 B (+4% YoY), driven by wealth management, payment fees, and cloud.

-   Marketing Services (18%) - Online advertising (WeChat Moments, Weixin Search, Mini Programs, video accounts). In 2024 this segment was RMB 121.4 B (+20% YoY).

("Others" is just \~1% in 2024.) Tencent's user base is enormous: as of end-2024 it had \~1.385 billion monthly users on Weixin/WeChat and 524 million on mobile QQ. About 262 million of these were paying subscribers for value-added services (e.g. game subscriptions). This scale creates cross-selling opportunities: for example, the WeChat "super-app" integrates messaging, payments, shopping, and more, which greatly entrenches users.

Tencent's moat stems from these network effects. Analysts note that WeChat is "the beating heart of Tencent's moat", cementing user loyalty and enabling monetization via ads, payments, cloud, etc.. The company's world-leading gaming franchise (with hits like PUBG Mobile and League of Legends), plus its ecosystem (QQ, video, music), reinforces lock-in. In short, Tencent leverages its massive social and gaming platforms to earn recurring revenue from users and advertisers.

## **2. Headwinds and Risk Factors**

Short-term risks include:

-   Regulatory uncertainty in China - The government's stance on gaming approvals, data privacy, or anti-trust could tighten again. This might delay game launches or restrict ad/fintech operations.

-   Tech and supply constraints - U.S.-China tensions have led to export restrictions on AI chips. Reuters reports that chip shortages are limiting Tencent's cloud growth, since it has to prioritize chips for internal AI projects. If chip supply remains tight, cloud and AI-driven services could grow slower.

-   Economic conditions - A slowing Chinese economy or reduced consumer spending could dent revenue. Analysts warn that if China hits a rough patch, spending on games and online ads may slow and businesses might cut cloud/fintech budgets. This is a cyclical risk in the near term.

-   Intense competition - Competitors (ByteDance/TikTok, Alibaba Cloud, NetEase, Kuaishou, etc.) are battling for users, ad dollars and cloud business. Margin pressures (e.g. in advertising or cloud) are possible as rivals invest heavily.

Long-term risks include:

-   Geopolitical tensions - Ongoing US-China tech friction could have lasting effects (e.g. bans on WeChat overseas, limits on foreign listings, or sanctions). Such shocks could fundamentally disrupt parts of Tencent's business.

-   Structural shifts - Demographic or behavioral changes (slower population growth, shifts away from gaming) could permanently slow Tencent's addressable market. Similarly, if Web3/AI drastically change the internet landscape, Tencent must adapt or risk obsolescence.

-   Regulatory regime - Even if current strictness eases, future Chinese policy (on data security, antitrust, fintech licensing, crypto, etc.) could reshape the industry long-term.

-   Valuation & market risk - Finally, because Tencent trades at premium multiples (see below), a broad market downturn or a tightening of global capital flows into Chinese stocks could permanently depress its valuation.

In summary, Tencent must navigate Chinese regulatory cycles, global tech warfare, macroeconomic conditions, and stiff competition. Many of these risks are already well-known and priced in, but sharp policy shifts or geopolitical escalations could still have major impact. Analysts highlight that "regulatory uncertainty and geopolitical shocks deserve close watch" for Tencent in the coming years.

## **3. Costs Structure**

Tencent's cost base is dominated by R&D, content, and employee expenses. Key figures (2024 vs 2023) are:

-   R&D/Tech investment - Tencent incurred RMB 70.7 B in R&D spending in 2024 (up from RMB 64.1 B in 2023), primarily staff costs. These are expensed as incurred (no major capitalized software). R&D mainly covers new products (games, AI, cloud) and improving its platforms.

-   Employee benefits - Total personnel costs were RMB 112.8 B (2024). This includes engineering, operations, and sales staffs (including the R&D staff above). Salaries and share-based compensation together are the largest single cost line.

-   Content costs - At RMB 68.6 B (2024), these mainly cover game development costs (some paid to third-party developers), media licensing (video/music content), and server infrastructure for content delivery. This is largely variable, tied to user activity and licensing deals.

-   Depreciation - RMB 27.3 B (2024) of non-cash depreciation on data centers, equipment, and intangible amortization.

-   Bandwidth/server fees - RMB 26.4 B (2024) for data center operations and network costs.

-   Marketing & promotion - RMB 25.1 B (2024) on advertising, user acquisition, and branding. This is variable depending on growth strategies (it rose \~10% YoY from 2023).

The largest costs are employee/R&D and content. In combination they form the bulk of Tencent's expense (total OPEX in 2024 was RMB 349.2 B). These costs have trended upward (5-10% YoY) reflecting Tencent's expansion and higher headcount. During economic downturns, Tencent can cut marketing spend or moderate hiring, but much R&D and content spend is ongoing. In summary, Tencent has a high fixed-cost base (tech talent and data centers) plus variable costs tied to revenue (content royalties, ads spend).

## **4. Management Team**

-   Executive Leadership: Tencent's CEO and co-founder, Ma Huateng (Pony Ma), has led the company since 1999 (he is Chairman and CEO) . Other co-founders/longtime execs include Daniel Xu (Co-founder and CIO) and Zhang Zhidong (Tony Zhang, Advisor Emeritus). Key current executives: Martin Lau (President, joined 2005), Mark Ren (COO and Group President of gaming/content), Allen Zhang (Head of Weixin/WeChat), James Mitchell (Chief Strategy Officer) , Dowson Tong (Cloud/AI group head), and John Lo (CFO). These leaders have been with Tencent for many years, reflecting continuity.

-   Board of Directors: The board is a mix of executives and independent/affiliate directors. Chairman Ma serves on the board, as do representatives of Tencent's largest shareholder (Naspers/Prosus). For example Koos Bekker and Charles Searle (Naspers veterans) are non-executive directors . Independent directors include industry figures like Li Dongsheng (TCL CEO) , Ian Stone (telecom executive) , Yang Siu Shun (former PwC partner) , and academics like Ke Yang and Zhang Xiulan. Former co-founders Chen Yidan and Jason Zeng remain as Advisor Emeritus.

-   Track Record: Management has generally executed on long-term goals (growing WeChat, global gaming, and fintech) and has operated transparently. Tencent publishes detailed financials each quarter/annual, and management answers analyst questions publicly (e.g. conference calls). There have been no material scandals; analysts note Tencent's prudent capital allocation (net cash position) and steady dividends. Management tends to invest its own funds: \~31.8% of Tencent's shares are held by insiders/promoters, aligning their interests with shareholders. The board has authorized large share buybacks (discussed below) indicating confidence.

-   Value Alignment: Senior executives hold significant equity (Ma \~8.8% of stock) and Tencent has instituted share ownership schemes. The management's wealth is largely tied to Tencent's performance. They have bought back shares aggressively when the stock dipped (e.g. HKD 112 B in buybacks in 2024 ), which suggests they value long-term share growth over short-term earnings.

## **5. Top Shareholders and Voting**

The largest shareholder by far is Prosus N.V. (formerly Naspers) with about 23% of Tencent's stock. Founder Pony Ma holds roughly 8.8%. Other institutional holders (mutual funds, banks, etc.) own small stakes. Since Tencent's shares (HK-listed) carry equal votes, a holder's share (%) equals its voting power.

At the 2025 AGM (May 14, 2025), all ordinary resolutions passed overwhelmingly. Key outcomes (with "For" votes) were: final dividend approval (≈99.99% yes), directors re-elections (98-99% yes for Pon Ma's slate; one independent director, Li Dongsheng, received \~83% support). Notably, the proposal to empower the board to issue new shares received only 69.1% in favor (≈30.9% opposed), reflecting some shareholder skepticism about dilution. The share repurchase mandate passed with \~99.8% support, indicating strong backing for buybacks. In sum, institutional and retail shareholders have largely approved management's agenda, except for some resistance on new-share issuance.

## **6. Share Buyback Plan (2024-2025)**

Tencent has been a "share buyback king" in Hong Kong. In 2024, it bought back about HKD 112 billion of stock (the largest annual repurchase in its history). For 2025, Tencent announced it intends to repurchase at least HKD 80 B of shares . These programs (started in 2020) are unprecedented compared to the prior decade, during which buybacks were rare or small. The 2025 plan is somewhat below 2024's level but still massive (≈$10 B USD). Historically, management indicated these buybacks are opportunistic; they have continued even as the share price recovered. The consistent use of capital returns (buybacks + dividends) suggests management favors returning cash to shareholders rather than over-investing.

## **7. Value Investor Activity**

Tencent has attracted various institutional investors over time. The most notable case was Berkshire Hathaway (Warren Buffett), which accumulated Tencent shares in 2019-2020 and then gradually exited by late 2022 (reports show Buffett sold essentially all of it). Other "deep value" investors (e.g. certain mutual funds) have held smaller stakes, often selling or reducing positions after Tencent's strong rally. We lack exact average buy/sell prices, but broadly: early 2020 purchases averaged below the 2020-21 price surge, so early buyers profited; later investors faced higher entry. As of late 2025, no major value guru is publicly known to be a significant holder (insiders and Prosus dominate).

Notable trends: Tencent's market price and market cap have grown dramatically (reflecting broad tech rally and Chinese tech re-rating). Balance-sheet metrics show extraordinary strength: Tencent has virtually no net debt (net cash HKD 102 B in Q3 2025) and high liquidity (current assets far exceed current liabilities, giving a current ratio well above 1.5). Its interest coverage is enormous (operating profit vastly exceeds any interest expense, effectively infinite since debt is near zero). Leverage ratios (debt/equity, debt/assets) are very low (&lt;0.05).

Profitability is high: 2025 trailing net margin is around 30%, and gross margins in core segments are well above 50%. Tencent's ROE (\~20%) and ROA (\~7.6%) have been excellent, reflecting efficient use of capital. Its R&D and capex have raised depreciation, but depreciation/​gross-profit is still moderate (\~8-9%). Free cash flow (after capex) has been positive every year; 2024 FCF was roughly RMB 142 B (≈$20 B) on RMB 660 B revenue (FCF margin \~21.5%).

## **Metrics vs. Deep-Value Criteria**

Evaluating Tencent against classic "deep value" screen standards:

-   Valuation Discipline: Tencent fails typical value thresholds. Its current P/E (\~26×) is well above 15. P/B is also above 1.5. PEG is &gt;1 given mid-teens growth. Thus it is not "cheap" relative to its own history or peers.

-   Cash Generation & Allocation: Tencent excels here. FCF has been positive and substantial annually (FCF/Revenue &gt; 20%). The company spends a modest share of revenue on capex (\~18%) , leaving strong free cash. Return on retained earnings (net income relative to book equity) is high (we estimate &gt;15%).

-   Growth & Predictability: Tencent has delivered consistent growth, especially in recent years. EPS and book value per share have generally trended upward (no major dilution except share issuance for acquisitions). Owners' earnings (cash net of capex) have been positive each year, though OE/MarketCap is around \~10% (depending on year).

-   Balance Sheet Safety: Very strong. Debt-to-equity is near 0, interest coverage is enormous, and current ratio likely &gt;2. Tencent carries very little financial risk.

-   Profitability & Efficiency: Tencent's profitability far exceeds the suggested cutoffs: gross margins \~50-60%, net margins \~30%. Its 10-year average ROE (\~20%) and ROIC are well above 15% and 12% respectively. Depreciation is modest relative to gross profit (around 8-9%).

In summary, Tencent passes the quality and safety screens (profit margins, ROE, liquidity) but fails the valuation screens (P/E, P/B, PEG). By "deep value" criteria of buying at large discounts, Tencent would not be a buy right now. It is a high-quality compounder more than a cheap value stock.

## **Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) Analysis**

To estimate intrinsic value, we model Tencent's Free Cash Flow (FCF) under three scenarios: No-growth, Conservative growth, and Nominal growth. Based on 2024 FCF ≈RMB 142 B, we assume:

-   No-growth: FCF remains flat at RMB 142 B indefinitely.

-   Conservative: FCF grows at the lowest 10-year CAGR (for illustration, assume \~5%).

-   Nominal: FCF grows at the 10-year average CAGR (approx 15%).

Assuming a discount rate (\~weighted cost of equity ≈10%) and a terminal growth of 2% for the growing cases, we compute present values. (Details: cash flows are projected for 5 years, then terminal value.) The results (illustrative) might be:

-   No-growth: PV of cash flows \~ RMB 1.42 T. With \~9.18 B shares outstanding, intrinsic price ≈ RMB 154 per share (≈$22 USD).

-   Conservative (5% FCF growth): PV \~ RMB 1.90 T; price ≈ RMB 207 (≈$30 USD).

-   Nominal (15% FCF growth): PV \~ RMB 3.2 T; price ≈ RMB 348 (≈$50 USD).

Current market price (≈HKD 598 ≈ RMB 526 as of Nov 2025) lies near or above even the nominal-case valuation. A 30% margin of safety would require prices roughly RMB 109, 145, and 244 for the three cases. (These calculations hinge on growth/dsicount assumptions; we use conservative tax/CapEx assumptions and 10% discount.) Thus, under no-growth or conservative growth assumptions, Tencent appears significantly overvalued versus these DCF-derived "intrinsic" estimates. Only the high-growth case (15% CAGR) approaches today's price, implying limited margin of safety.
