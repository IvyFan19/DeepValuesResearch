## Latest Quarter Earnings Summary

The Walt Disney Company's fiscal first quarter of 2026, which concluded on December 27, 2025, represents a pivotal inflection point in the company's post-pandemic recovery and digital transformation. Total revenues for the period increased by 5% year-over-year to $26.0 billion, up from $24.7 billion in the first quarter of fiscal 2025.<sup>1</sup> This top-line growth was characterized by resilience in the Experiences segment and a continued trajectory toward scale in the Direct-to-Consumer (DTC) operations. However, the period also highlighted the inherent volatility of the modern media landscape, as total segment operating income faced a 9% contraction, falling to $4.6 billion from $5.1 billion.<sup>1</sup> This decline was largely anticipated by the market, driven by the absence of significant political advertising revenue that had bolstered the prior-year results and substantial pre-opening expenses associated with the aggressive expansion of the Disney Cruise Line fleet.<sup>1</sup>

Income before income taxes remained comparable to the prior year at $3.7 billion, but diluted earnings per share (EPS) saw a slight reduction to $1.34 from $1.40. Adjusted EPS, which excludes specific non-recurring items and accounting adjustments, decreased by 7% to $1.63.<sup>1</sup> Despite these short-term pressures, the Entertainment segment's revenue rose by 7% to $11.6 billion, though its operating income plummeted by 35% to $1.1 billion.<sup>1</sup> This margin compression in the Entertainment segment was primarily the result of higher programming and production costs, alongside a difficult year-over-year comparison in the theatrical slate.<sup>1</sup> Specifically, the prior-year quarter had benefited from the massive success of *Inside Out 2* and *Deadpool & Wolverine*, creating a high bar for current theatrical revenue.<sup>3</sup>

The Direct-to-Consumer (DTC) business remains the most scrutinized element of Disney's portfolio. In Q1 2026, SVOD (Subscription Video on Demand) revenue increased by 11% compared to Q1 2025, reaching a milestone in profitability with operating income rising $189 million to $450 million.<sup>1</sup> This resulted in an SVOD operating margin of 8.4%, a significant improvement that reflects Disney's shift from aggressive subscriber acquisition to disciplined margin expansion through price increases and the successful implementation of an ad-supported tier.<sup>1</sup> By the end of the 2025 fiscal year, the combined Disney+ and Hulu subscription count reached 196 million, with Disney+ alone accounting for 132 million subscribers.<sup>2</sup>

The Sports segment, anchored by ESPN, reported revenues of $4.9 billion, a modest 1% increase.<sup>1</sup> However, its operating income fell to $191 million from $247 million, a 23% decline.<sup>1</sup> This segment was impacted by the temporary suspension of YouTube TV carriage, which incurred an adverse impact of approximately $110 million.<sup>1</sup> Additionally, higher programming and production costs for marquee sports rights more than offset a 10% growth in sports-related advertising revenue.<sup>1</sup> In contrast, the Experiences segment continued to serve as the company's primary cash generator, posting record quarterly revenue of $10.0 billion.<sup>1</sup> Domestic Parks and Experiences saw operating income growth of 8%, even as international visitation headwinds and pre-opening expenses for new cruise ships like the *Disney Treasure* created localized margin pressure.<sup>1</sup>

## Business Model

The Walt Disney Company operates through a unique, synergistic business model often referred to as the "Disney Flywheel." At its core, the company is a storyteller that leverages high-value intellectual property (IP) across a vertically integrated ecosystem of content creation, distribution, and physical experiences.<sup>8</sup> This model allows Disney to extract multiple layers of value from a single creative asset-for instance, a character introduced in a Marvel film can simultaneously drive box office revenue, streaming subscriptions on Disney+, theme park attendance through themed lands (e.g., Avengers Campus), and high-margin consumer product sales.<sup>8</sup>

### Main Sources of Income

Disney's revenue is fundamentally divided into three primary segments as of the latest reporting structure:

1.  **Entertainment**: This is the largest segment by revenue, encompassing the company's linear television networks (ABC, Disney Channel, FX, National Geographic), its direct-to-consumer streaming services (Disney+, Hulu, Disney+ Hotstar), and the content sales and licensing arm (Walt Disney Studios, Pixar, Marvel, Lucasfilm, 20th Century Studios).<sup>3</sup> This segment is the "engine" of the company's IP creation.

2.  **Experiences**: This segment includes Disney's six global resort destinations (Disneyland Resort, Walt Disney World, Disneyland Paris, Hong Kong Disneyland, Shanghai Disney Resort, and Tokyo Disney Resort), the Disney Cruise Line, and the Consumer Products business, which licenses Disney's IP to third-party manufacturers.<sup>3</sup>

3.  **Sports**: This segment is comprised of ESPN and its associated platforms (ESPN+, international sports channels). It operates as a distinct pillar due to the unique economics of live sports rights and advertising.<sup>3</sup>

### Customer Base and Unit Sales

As a global consumer giant, Disney's "customer" base is multifaceted, spanning hundreds of millions of individuals across various touchpoints. In the digital realm, the company tracks "paid subscriptions," which reached a combined 196 million for Disney+ and Hulu by the end of fiscal 2025.<sup>2</sup> Specifically, Disney+ has 132 million subscribers, while Hulu accounts for the remainder of the SVOD total.<sup>2</sup> The company also serves millions of "guests" annually at its theme parks; while exact global attendance figures are rarely disclosed in quarterly earnings, the Experiences segment revenue of $36.16 billion implies a massive throughput of high-spending visitors.<sup>3</sup>

In terms of unit sales, Disney's "units" vary by segment:

-   **Theatrical**: Tens of millions of movie tickets sold globally for blockbuster releases.

-   **Parks**: Millions of individual park admissions, hotel room nights, and "guest spend" units (food, beverage, and merchandise).<sup>14</sup>

-   **DTC**: Monthly or annual subscription units.

-   **Consumer Products**: Billions of dollars in wholesale merchandise value licensed to partners.<sup>14</sup>

### The Moat: Structural Competitive Advantages

The Walt Disney Company possesses one of the most substantial economic moats in the global economy, primarily built on **Intangible Assets** and **Network Effects**.

-   **Irreplaceable IP Portfolio**: Disney owns the most valuable collection of character-driven franchises in history. The library of Disney Animation, Pixar, Marvel, Star Wars, and Avatar creates a "share of mind" among children and parents that competitors like Netflix or Warner Bros. Discovery struggle to replicate.<sup>8</sup> Warren Buffett famously noted that Disney's brand is comparable to Coca-Cola's in its global recognition and that "owning the mouse" is a unique advantage because the characters do not require agents or salary renegotiations.<sup>15</sup>

-   **The Synergistic Flywheel**: Disney's ability to cross-promote is its greatest structural advantage. A hit movie acts as a low-cost marketing campaign for a high-margin theme park ride, which in turn sells toys and clothing, which in turn drives interest in a streaming sequel.<sup>8</sup>

-   **High Barriers to Entry in Experiences**: The capital required to build a world-class theme park is astronomical. Disney's decades of experience in "Imagineering," combined with vast real estate holdings and existing infrastructure, makes it nearly impossible for a new entrant to compete at the same scale and quality.<sup>9</sup>

## Headwinds & Risks

The Walt Disney Company operates in an industry undergoing seismic structural changes, exposing it to both transitory and permanent risks.

### Short-Term Headwinds (Temporary)

-   **Theatrical Slate Lumpy-ness**: Earnings are highly dependent on the "hit" nature of the theatrical cycle. A quarter without a $1 billion blockbuster can result in unfavorable year-over-year comparisons, as seen in early FY2026 following the record-breaking 2024 slate.<sup>1</sup>

-   **Macroeconomic Sensitivity in Parks**: Consumer discretionary spending at theme parks is sensitive to inflation and economic slowdowns. Temporary visitation headwinds in international markets or domestic parks can fluctuate based on travel trends and disposable income.<sup>1</sup>

-   **Weather and External Events**: As a physical experience provider, Disney is subject to localized disruptions. For example, Hurricanes Milton and Helene in late 2024 caused a $120 million headwind to park operating income.<sup>7</sup>

-   **Expansion Pre-Opening Costs**: Disney is currently in a high-CAPEX cycle, spending $9 billion annually on cruise ships and park attractions.<sup>4</sup> Pre-opening expenses for new cruise ships (e.g., $90 million for the *Disney Destiny*) depress margins temporarily before the assets become operational.<sup>2</sup>

### Long-Term Risks (Permanent)

-   **Linear Television Decay**: The "cord-cutting" phenomenon is a permanent industry shift. Disney's domestic linear networks (ABC, Disney Channel) face a structural decline in affiliate fees and advertising as audiences move to streaming.<sup>3</sup> While Disney+ is intended to capture this audience, the margins of a direct-to-consumer model are currently lower than the historic "double-dip" revenue model of cable TV.<sup>10</sup>

-   **Content Cost Inflation**: To remain competitive with tech giants like Amazon, Apple, and Netflix, Disney must spend approximately $24 billion annually on content.<sup>2</sup> This "content arms race" creates a high floor for fixed costs that can permanently compress margins if subscriber growth or ARPU (Average Revenue Per User) does not scale accordingly.<sup>8</sup>

-   **Technological Disruption and AI**: The rise of AI-generated content and immersive platforms like Roblox or Fortnite poses a permanent challenge to traditional passive storytelling. Disney must adapt its IP to interactive and virtual environments (e.g., via the "Disney Movies VR" initiatives) to maintain relevance with younger generations.<sup>8</sup>

-   **Geopolitical Exposure**: With significant operations in China (Shanghai Disney) and India (Star India joint venture), Disney is permanently exposed to geopolitical tensions, trade tariffs, and regulatory changes in foreign markets.<sup>1</sup>

## Cost Structure

Disney's financial model is characterized by high fixed costs associated with content production and physical infrastructure, balanced by variable costs in its consumer-facing operations.

### Fixed vs. Variable Costs

-   **Fixed Costs**: These remain constant regardless of attendance or subscription levels. The most significant fixed costs are **content amortization** (the $24 billion annual investment in films and shows), **depreciation and maintenance** for parks and ships ($5.3 billion annually), and **technology infrastructure** for streaming platforms.<sup>2</sup> The "development, licensing, and maintenance" of the Disney+ platform alone is estimated at $10 billion annually.<sup>22</sup>

-   **Variable Costs**: These fluctuate with activity. In the Experiences segment, these include food, beverage, and merchandise costs, which rose 36% to $5.4 billion in a recent fiscal year.<sup>22</sup> In the DTC segment, variable costs include content delivery (CDNs) and customer support, estimated at approximately $2 per subscriber per month.<sup>22</sup>

### Significant Cost Categories

1.  **Programming and Production (Content)**: This is Disney's most significant expense. The company must continuously reinvest in its library to prevent churn in streaming and drive theatrical revenue.<sup>2</sup>

2.  **Labor Expenses**: Disney is one of the world's largest employers, with 231,000 employees as of late 2025.<sup>13</sup> Labor is a major driver of park operating expenses, where salaries and benefits are a primary component of the "general operations" cost base.<sup>22</sup>

3.  **Advertising & Marketing**: To support its massive theatrical releases and streaming growth, Disney's SG&A (Selling, General, and Administrative) costs reached $16.5 billion in FY2025.<sup>23</sup> This includes over $100 million in direct U.S. advertising spend across digital and TV properties.<sup>27</sup>

### Cost Trends and Economic Conditions

Disney's costs generally trend upward over time due to inflation in labor and content production. During economic downturns, Disney has shown an ability to reduce "discretionary" spending (e.g., reducing marketing for older titles) but cannot easily cut the fixed costs of its theme parks or long-term sports rights contracts.<sup>2</sup> During the COVID-19 pandemic, the company successfully reduced CAPEX to "maintenance levels" but still incurred significant cash outflows for interest, taxes, and critical maintenance.<sup>29</sup>

## Management & Governance

The leadership of The Walt Disney Company has undergone significant upheaval and restructuring over the past three years, centered on the return of Bob Iger and a renewed focus on board-level accountability.

### Executive Leadership Team

-   **Robert A. Iger (Chief Executive Officer)**: Iger, who previously served as CEO from 2005 to 2020, returned in November 2022. His current mandate is to lead a "company-wide transformation" that prioritizes creativity and positions the streaming business for sustained profitability.<sup>9</sup> Iger is 74 years old and is expected to step down as CEO later in 2026 once a successor is named.<sup>30</sup>

-   **Hugh Johnston (Senior EVP & CFO)**: Joined Disney in late 2023 from PepsiCo, where he served as Vice Chairman and CFO. He oversees all financial operations, strategic planning, and investor relations.<sup>9</sup>

-   **James Pitaro (Chairman, ESPN)**: Heads Disney's sports strategy, including the transition of ESPN to a primary direct-to-consumer offering.<sup>9</sup>

-   **Josh D'Amaro (Chairman, Disney Experiences)**: Leads the theme parks, cruise line, and consumer products segment.<sup>9</sup>

-   **Dana Walden & Alan Bergman (Co-Chairmen, Disney Entertainment)**: Oversight of the film studios and television divisions.<sup>9</sup>

### Board of Directors

The board is currently chaired by **James P. Gorman**, the former Executive Chairman of Morgan Stanley, who joined the board in 2024 and was named Chairman as of January 2, 2025.<sup>12</sup> Gorman's appointment was seen as a move to bring seasoned financial and succession-planning expertise to the leadership transition. Other notable directors include **Mary T. Barra** (Chair & CEO of General Motors), **Amy L. Chang** (former Cisco EVP), and **Sir Jeremy Darroch** (former CEO of Sky).<sup>12</sup>

### Historical Context and Integrity

The company has faced criticism regarding its succession planning, particularly following the short and controversial tenure of Bob Chapek (2020-2022).<sup>9</sup> Chapek's management style and handling of creative talent were questioned by investors and insiders, eventually leading to his replacement by Iger.<sup>21</sup> While Iger is widely respected for his past acquisitions (Pixar, Marvel, Lucasfilm, Fox), activist investors like Nelson Peltz have historically questioned the board's "obliviousness" to declining free cash flow and mounting streaming losses during the 2022 period.<sup>34</sup> However, as of late 2025, management has regained significant institutional trust, as evidenced by the 2025 proxy battle victory.<sup>21</sup>

### Value Alignment

Disney's executive compensation is heavily weighted toward performance-based equity.

-   **Ownership Thresholds**: The CEO is required to hold equity valued at five times his salary within five years. As of January 15, 2026, Bob Iger holds equity valued at **over 28 times** his salary, representing a significant portion of his wealth invested in the firm.<sup>35</sup>

-   **Incentives**: In fiscal 2025, the Compensation Committee introduced "targeted enhancements" to align pay more directly with shareholder returns, focusing on EPS growth and DTC profitability.<sup>18</sup>

### Major Shareholder Meetings and Voted Topics

At the most recent annual meetings (2024 and 2025), several high-stakes proposals were decided:

-   **Board Elections**: Shareholders overwhelmingly re-elected Disney's slate of directors in 2024, defeating nominees from activists Trian (Nelson Peltz) and Blackwells.<sup>21</sup>

-   **Executive Compensation**: The "Say on Pay" advisory vote passed in 2025, though 20% of shareholders voted against it in 2024, citing Iger's $31.6 million package relative to the stock's underperformance.<sup>20</sup>

-   **ESG and Social Issues**: A proposal to withdraw from the Human Rights Campaign's Corporate Equality Index (LGBTQ+ ranking) was soundly defeated, with only 1% of shareholders voting in favor.<sup>38</sup> Conversely, a proposal for a climate risk report for retirement plan beneficiaries received the most support among defeated shareholder proposals in 2025.<sup>20</sup>

-   **Cumulative Voting**: A shareholder proposal to adopt cumulative voting for board elections (to empower minority shareholders) has been a recurring topic, with the board consistently recommending a "Vote Against".<sup>18</sup>

### Share Buybacks and Dividends

Disney has significantly pivoted its capital return policy in fiscal 2025 and 2026:

-   **Buyback Plan**: Management has set a **$7 billion** share repurchase target for fiscal 2026, doubling the $3.5 billion repurchased in 2025.<sup>4</sup> This is an aggressive acceleration compared to the prior decade, during which buybacks were often secondary to massive acquisitions and pandemic liquidity conservation.<sup>19</sup>

-   **Dividends**: The Board declared a cash dividend of $1.50 per share for FY2026, a **50% increase** over the $1 paid in 2025.<sup>18</sup>

## Buffett-Style Checklist (Deep Value Standards)

Evaluating The Walt Disney Company against rigorous value investing standards reveals a business that possesses a "Wonderful" brand but currently faces "Fair" valuation and historical earnings volatility.

### 1. Valuation Discipline

-   **Price to Earning ratio (P/E) ≤ 15**: **Fail**. The TTM P/E of 16.5 is slightly above the threshold, although it has dropped significantly from the pandemic highs.<sup>26</sup>

-   **PEG Ratio &lt; 1**: **Fail**. Peter Lynch's PEG ratio is currently around 2.8, indicating that the market is paying a premium for Disney's turnaround prospects.<sup>47</sup>

-   **Price to Book ratio (P/B) ≤ 1.5**: **Fail**. Disney currently trades at 1.79x book value, which is reasonable for a media company with massive intangible IP but exceeds the strict 1.5x value limit.<sup>26</sup>

-   **Margin of Safety (Intrinsic Value)**: **Fail**. With current market prices near $112 and conservative DCF intrinsic values ranging from $84 to $109, there is no significant margin of safety at present.<sup>8</sup>

### 2. Cash Generation & Allocation

-   **Free-Cash-Flow (FCF)**: **Pass**. FCF has been positive every year except for the peak pandemic disruption and the immediate aftermath of the Fox acquisition. The FCF/Revenue margin of 10.67% easily exceeds the 5% standard.<sup>11</sup>

-   **Return on Retained Earnings ≥ 12%**: **Fail**. The average over the last five years has been depressed due to the $71B acquisition of Fox and the subsequent pandemic lockdowns.<sup>49</sup>

### 3. Growth & Predictability

-   **EPS Record (10 Years)**: **Fail**. Disney reported a net loss in 2020 and had inconsistent EPS growth during the streaming build-out phase.<sup>10</sup>

-   **Shareholder-equity (BVPS CAGR ≥ 10%)**: **Pass/Fail**. While BVPS has grown from $28.50 in 2016 to $62.94 in 2025 (a CAGR of \~9.2%), there was a contraction in equity during the 2023 period.<sup>26</sup>

-   **Owner's-earnings (OE)**: **Fail**. OE/Market Cap of 5.58% is below the 10% target for deep-value investors.<sup>26</sup>

### 4. Balance-Sheet Safety

-   **Debt-to-Equity &lt; 0.5**: **Pass**. The current ratio of 0.31-0.37 indicates conservative leverage.<sup>26</sup>

-   **Interest Coverage ≥ 3x**: **Pass**. Disney's interest coverage is approximately 13x, indicating a very safe debt profile.<sup>26</sup>

-   **Interest Expense / Operating Income ≤ 15%**: **Pass**. Currently stands at 7.7%, well below the 15% risk threshold.<sup>23</sup>

-   **Current Ratio &gt; 1.5**: **Fail**. The ratio of 0.71 suggests tight short-term liquidity, though common in subscription models.<sup>26</sup>

### 5. Profitability & Efficiency

-   **Gross Margin ≳ 40%**: **Fail**. Currently 37.76%.<sup>26</sup>

-   **Net Profit Margin ≳ 20%**: **Fail**. Currently 13.14%.<sup>26</sup>

-   **Return on Equity (ROE) ≥ 15% (10-yr avg)**: **Fail**. The 10-year average is skewed by the pandemic and Fox integration losses.<sup>43</sup>

-   **Return on Assets (ROA) ≥ 6%**: **Pass**. Currently 6.80%.<sup>26</sup>

-   **Depreciation / Gross Profit ≤ 10%**: **Fail**. Currently 14.8%, reflecting high CAPEX requirements for theme parks.<sup>26</sup>

## Conclusion and Strategic Outlook

The Walt Disney Company remains the world's preeminent entertainment franchise, but its transition from a legacy media giant to a digitally native conglomerate is not without costs. Financially, the company has successfully de-leveraged following the 21st Century Fox acquisition and achieved profitability in its streaming segment. However, the high capital intensity of its physical park expansion and the rapid erosion of its highly profitable linear networks segment create a "headwind-versus-growth" struggle that keeps the stock at a fair valuation rather than a deep value discount. For a value investor, Disney offers a high-quality moat but currently lacks the 25-30% margin of safety required for a "Buffett-style" entry. The nominal intrinsic value of $123.57 suggests the stock is currently trading within its fair value range, with the 2026 CEO succession serving as the next major catalyst for share re-rating.
