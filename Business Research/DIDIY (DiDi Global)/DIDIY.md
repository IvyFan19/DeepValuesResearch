# **Background Research**

**Business model:** Didi Global is a mobility platform offering ride-hailing, taxi and chauffeur services, carpooling, bike/scooter sharing and other mobility options, primarily in China but also in markets like Brazil and Mexico . In addition, Didi has "other initiatives" such as in-car infotainment, vehicle services (e.g. charging, leasing), food delivery, logistics and financial products. The vast majority of revenue comes from its core ride-hailing business: in FY2023, total revenue was RMB192.4 billion (≈USD26.3 billion) , of which \~RMB175.0b (≈91%) was China Mobility services and \~RMB7.8b (≈4%) was International (Latin America, etc) . -   **Revenue sources:** Didi's platform revenue ("platform sales") is primarily commissions and fees on completed rides and deliveries . (It also earns modest fees from advertising, membership programs, and driver financing.) In Q4'24, *platform sales* (ride commissions) were RMB19.06b, up 23.1% YoY , while total revenue was RMB52.91b (7.1% YoY growth) . The vast majority of revenue is variable with ride volume, though fixed fees and subscriptions (e.g. vehicle leasing programs for drivers) contribute little.

-   **Customers and usage:** Didi serves hundreds of millions of riders and drivers. In 2024 Didi processed ≈16.0 billion rides (up \~19% YoY) . It reportedly has over 550 million annual users and tens of millions of drivers (reported in media sources ), though exact current counts are not public. (For context, the China Mobility segment alone logged 12.4 billion transactions in 2024 .)

-   **Units/subscriptions sold:** Didi does not sell "units" like hardware; it sells ride services. The nearest proxy is ride volume (billions of transactions) or possibly active driver/subscriber counts (not published).

**Risks and headwinds:**

-   **Regulatory/Political (short-term):** Chinese regulators have heavily scrutinized Didi since 2021. The 2021 data-security crackdown forced Didi to suspend app downloads and tighten data controls, culminating in a $1.2 billion fine in July 2022 . The lingering effects include slower user growth and compliance costs. In the U.S., Didi's 2021-22 IPO also triggered an SEC probe (later settled) . Geopolitical tensions (U.S.-China tech tensions) and China's evolving gig-economy regulations (e.g. proposed labor protections for drivers) pose ongoing regulatory risk.

-   **Economic cyclical (short-term):** Demand for ride-hailing depends on consumer mobility and spending. Didi's growth may slow if China's economy weakens (it has shown signs of sluggish growth ). Fuel prices and city lockdowns (as seen during COVID) can also temporarily reduce ridership.

-   **Competitive (structural):** Didi dominates China's market, but competition is intensifying. Super-apps (Alibaba, Meituan) integrate ride services and could lure users away . In international markets (Latin America), Didi competes with Uber and local players, which may pressure take-rates. Globally, autonomous vehicle technology and alternative mobility (e-scooters, improved public transit) represent long-run competitive threats.

-   **Structural costs and margins:** The ride-hailing business traditionally operates on thin margins due to heavy subsidies/incentives. Didi's Q4'24 cost of revenue was RMB43.3b on RMB52.9b revenue (\~82% of sales) . Sales & marketing, R&D and G&A are each \~4-6% of revenue (2023: S&M 5.4%, R&D 4.6%, G&A 4.4% ). Advertising and incentive costs are therefore significant and volatile. Over time, as Didi has scaled, these ratios have improved - for example, Didi's marketing spend dropped to 5.4% of revenues in 2023 from 6.9% in 2022 . Still, any future crackdowns or driver-population rules could raise costs permanently.

**Cost structure:**

-   **Fixed costs:** Core platform maintenance (IT infrastructure, data centers), office staff (engineering, admin) and R&D are largely fixed on a quarter basis. For example, R&D expense was RMB8.9b in 2023 (4.6% of revenue) and G&A (mostly SG&A staff and overhead) RMB8.4b (4.4% of revenue) . Before 2022, G&A was inflated by one-time items (e.g. the RMB8.0b fine) . Moving forward, these fixed costs tend to grow with the company's scale but at sub-linear rate (operating leverage).

-   **Variable costs:** The largest single cost is the *Cost of Revenue* (driver payments and incentives). In 2023 this was RMB162.9b, \~84.7% of revenue . This includes driver commissions and heavy passenger discounts. Sales & marketing also contains variable incentives (coupons, promotions) which fell to 5.4% of revenue . Labor costs for corporate employees are moderate (personnel costs were cited as a factor in Q4'24 G&A increase ). Advertising is significant but declining as a percentage. The cost-per-ride has fallen over time with improved efficiency; for instance, Didi's net take-rate (1 - cost/revenue) improved as GTV grew .

-   **Cost trends:** As shown in Didi's financial reviews, cost/revenue ratios have generally improved. In 2023, cost of revenue was 84.7% of sales, up slightly from 82.2% in 2022 (due to ramped-up driver subsidies in a competitive market). However, S&M, R&D and G&A as % of sales each fell in 2023 vs. 2022 . In soft economic times, Didi has moderated incentives; in 2024 Q4 it reported flat or reduced marketing spend year-over-year . Conversely, in growth phases or to capture market share, Didi has raised marketing support (e.g. 23.3% YoY jump in platform sales revenue in China in Q4'24 at the expense of higher subsidies).

**Management and governance:**

-   **Leadership:** Founder and CEO Will Wei Cheng (Cheng Wei) runs the company; he has been CEO since inception and remains the largest single voting influence . Co-founder Jean Qing Liu (Jean Liu) was long-time President/COO and on the board, instrumental in strategy and funding, but stepped down from the President role in May 2024 (becoming a "permanent partner" focusing on people/talent) . Yue Zhuo is CFO (since 2021). Other long-time founders (e.g. Bo Zhang in tech) remain on the leadership team . The board is a mix of company insiders (Cheng Wei as Chairman, Jean Liu until 2024) and outside investors' appointees. Notable board members include corporate executives (e.g. Yi Zhang, Alibaba's legal chief, is a director ) and representatives of major investors like SoftBank and Tencent (data on exact names is limited, but SoftBank is a key shareholder).

-   **Track record and integrity:** Management has delivered strong growth pre-crackdown: Didi grew revenues \~37% YoY in 2023 after a modest COVID dip. The team navigated the 2016 Uber China merger and expanded internationally. However, the 2021-22 regulatory issues did raise governance concerns. The company has since complied with data rules and has largely regained market access. The 2023 annual report noted an improvement to profitability (net profit RMB0.5b in 2023 vs. loss in 2022) . There have been no public allegations of fraud or insider malfeasance. Insider trading history: founders Cheng and Liu control most voting power through dual-class shares . Their ownership is locked (e.g. Cheng's 13G filing shows no selling in 2023 ). Reportedly, founder stock sales have been minimal since IPO. Compensation: Cheng's CEO pay is undisclosed (likely modest by U.S. standards); generally Chinese tech CEOs earn far less than Western peers. Founders have significant "skin in the game": Cheng's trust owns large equity blocks . We find no evidence of egregious insider selling or misalignment; in fact, the major shareholders (founders and investors) have largely held their stakes even through the share-price decline.

**Major shareholders and voting:** Didi's share structure is 1:4 ADS to Class A ordinary share . The largest holders are the founders and parent VIE trusts. Notable institutional shareholders (in the ADR) are far smaller - for example, SoftBank's Vision Fund held \~20% pre-crackdown (now worth a fraction), and global funds like Prosus/Tencent (through VIE) owned low double-digit percentages. Public filings show only \~3% of ADRs are held by institutions (TradingEconomics). Voting is heavily concentrated: according to SEC filings, Will Cheng and Jean Liu (via affiliated trusts) control \~31% of voting power ; SoftBank and Tencent may each have single-digit voting percentages. In practice, shareholder votes (annual meetings or special meetings) have focused on corporate governance (e.g. ratifying the VIE structure, approving buybacks). We found no contentious votes reported; the board's slate is controlled by major holders, so major proposals (governance, business plans) generally pass. Key enacted governance topics include approving the share repurchase program (Nov 2023).

**Share buybacks:** Didi had no public buybacks while private or in early IPO years. In November 2023 the board authorized a **$1 billion** share repurchase (over 24 months) . By Q4 2024, about $218 million (45.8m ADS) had been repurchased . In fact, by early 2025 the plan was expanded: filings indicate up to **$2 billion** authorized , with \~$847.5m remaining (after the initial tranche). This is Didi's first buyback effort, reflecting management's belief shares are undervalued. There is no buyback activity in prior years to compare. In summary, the current buyback is sizeable (5-10% of market cap), whereas the last 10 years saw essentially none (Didi was private until 2021).

**Major investor trading:** Because most of Didi's stock is held by founders and locked-up investors, there is limited "buy/sell" data on value investors. SoftBank's Vision Fund famously wrote down its Didi stake from \~$14 billion pre-IPO to \~$1.8 billion by 2024 (a massive sell or markdown). Some hedge funds and mutual funds (e.g. Davis/New York Venture) hold ADRs, but in small amounts. We found no published records of major "value investor" purchases/sales (no Berkshire/Aumann/Akris known). In sum, the market is dominated by founder/VIE shares; public float is modest and institutional holdings are low.

**Intrinsic value (Buffett method):** Using a Buffett-style discounted cash flow, we estimate intrinsic value per share as follows. First, approximate "owner's earnings" \~FCF. As noted by Gurufocus, Didi's run-rate owner earnings are roughly **$2.2 billion/year** (\~0.50 USD per ADR annually) . (This is after backing out \~4% of GTV as new investment expense .) With shares outstanding ≈4.4 billion ADS (implied by the 0.50 per-ADR figure), that yields \~$0.50 annual EPS. We then discount using 12% (midpoint of 10-15%) and assume terminal growth 3%.

-   *Conservative DCF (lowest growth):* Assume 0% nominal growth for 10 years. The present value of 10 years of $2.2b plus a 3%-growing perpetuity at year 10 is roughly **$41.8 billion** . Dividing by 4.4B ADS gives about **$9.5 per ADR**. With a 50% margin of safety, intrinsic ≈ $4.75. At current OTC price ($5), the 0%-MOS expected return would be \~$(9.5/5 - 1)≈90%.

-   *Moderate DCF (average growth):* If we instead use \~5% annual growth (approx. historic average), the PV is higher (≈$63.2b) and per-ADS value \~**$14.4**. (50% MOS value \~$7.2.) The 0%-MOS return at $5 price would be \~$(14.4/5 - 1)≈188%.

These are illustrative; actual results depend on growth and discount inputs. But even the conservative model suggests Didi's ADS may be significantly undervalued by Buffett criteria, especially if growth resumes.

**Recent quarterly results:** In Q4 2024 (reported Mar. 18, 2025) Didi showed continued revenue growth but swung to a net loss. Q4 revenues rose 7.1% YoY to RMB52.9b (≈$7.3b) as ride volume rebounded. China Mobility volume was up \~10.8% vs prior year , and International bookings grew \~29.8% . Despite higher sales, Didi reported a Q4 net loss of RMB1.3b (vs. a small profit in Q4'23). This loss was largely due to one-time and non-operating items (e.g. $0.1B investment loss) , while core operating profit was roughly flat. On a full-year basis, revenue reached RMB206.8b in 2024 (up 7.5% YoY) and net profit was RMB1.3b (vs. RMB0.5b in 2023). Cash and equivalents remained strong (\~RMB53-55b) . In summary, Q4'24 showed solid top-line growth and record transaction volumes, but profitability was hit by accounting losses; management reiterated focus on driving efficiency and shareholder returns (noting the recent buyback) .

**10-year historicals:** Didi's full consolidated IFRS financials are available from IPO filings onward. Key trends (USD millions, fiscal years ended Dec. 31):

-   **Valuation metrics:** Shares trade OTC; in 2024 (OTC ticker DIDIY) price averaged \~$5. Full-year market cap was roughly $4.5-5.0 billion. Book value (equity) \~RMB25b (≈$3.6b) in 2024. P/E is not meaningful (2024 net profit $186m vs cap \~$4.5b → P/E ≈24, but 2022 had a huge loss). Price/Book \~1.4x. Return on equity (ROE) and ROIC turned positive in 2023-24 (ROE \~3-5% in 2023, 2024), but were negative in 2021-22 due to losses.

-   **Cash flow/income:** Revenue (USD) was roughly $26.4b in 2023 and $28.2b in 2024. Gross profit was thin (net take-rate \~15-20% of GTV). Operating (pre-tax) profit was small ($1.9b in FY2024 ), after heavy opex and one-time losses. In 2024 Didi swung to modest net profit ($186m, 0.9% net margin) from break-even in 2023. Free cash flow (FCF) turned positive in 2023-24; management reports cash from operations of \~RMB1-2b in H1'24 .

-   **Balance sheet:** Didi carries negligible debt (virtually zero net debt; 2023 total liabilities \~RMB160b mostly payables, vs. \~RMB65b cash ). Current ratio is high (\~above 2x). Debt-to-equity is low (since equity is small and nearly no debt). Shareholders' equity (USD) grew from negative (due to prior losses) to \~$3.5b by end-2024.

-   **Growth:** FY 2024 revenue grew \~7.5% YoY (USD: \~$26.4b→$28.2b). Net income grew strongly (from \~$74m to $186m). Over 2021-24, revenue CAGR ≈30% (from \~$12b in 2021 to $28b in 2024). Cash flow has also grown from negative to positive.

**Qualitative evaluation:** Didi's finances show a maturing ride-hailing business: top-line growth has slowed from pandemic-era highs but remains positive in 2024. Profitability was depressed by one-time charges (e.g. FV losses, fines), but the underlying business is improving (positive adjusted EBITDA, narrowing losses). Cash generation is recovering; Didi holds ample liquid resources. The balance sheet is very strong with minimal debt, enhancing financial flexibility. Valuation multiples appear low, but must be weighed against regulatory and competitive risks. Overall, Didi's financial profile is stabilizing after turmoil, with healthy growth, improving margins, and a safe balance sheet - a condition Buffett would likely view as favorable if the regulatory cloud can clear.
