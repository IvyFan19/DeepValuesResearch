# **Microsoft (MSFT) Financial Research Report**

## **Latest Quarter Earnings Summary**

Microsoft delivered **record results** in the most recent reporting period. For the fiscal year 2025 (ended June 30, 2025), revenue reached **$281.7 billion**, a 15% increase year-over-year, with operating income up 17% to $128.5 billion . Notably, Azure cloud revenue surpassed $75 billion for the first time, growing 34% annually . This momentum has carried into the latest quarter. In the quarter ended September 30, 2025, sales continued to rise at double-digit rates (approx. **18%** year-over-year growth) , reflecting strong demand across business segments. These results underscore Microsoft's ability to achieve **high growth at massive scale**, driven by its cloud services (Azure, Office 365) and improving margins. Net profit also climbed robustly - TTM (trailing 12-month) net income reached **$104.9 billion** , benefiting from operating leverage and disciplined cost management. Overall, Microsoft's latest earnings illustrate a company firing on all cylinders, with **revenue, profit, and cash flow at record highs**, buoyed by cloud and AI-related demand.

## **Business Model**

Microsoft's business model is built on a diverse portfolio of software and cloud services that enjoy wide moats and recurring revenue. The company operates three main segments: **Productivity & Business Processes** (Office 365, LinkedIn, Dynamics), **Intelligent Cloud** (Azure, Windows Server, GitHub), and **More Personal Computing** (Windows OS, Surface devices, Xbox, search ads). This mix provides multiple revenue streams. High-margin software (like Office and Windows) generates steady cash flow, while Azure and cloud services drive growth . Microsoft enjoys powerful network effects and switching costs - e.g. Office 365 and Azure are deeply embedded in enterprise IT operations, making customers unlikely to leave. Its subscription-based offerings (Office 365, Azure, etc.) yield recurring revenue and long-term customer relationships. The company's emphasis on cloud and AI has transformed the business model toward consumption-based revenue: Azure usage grows as clients transition more workloads to the cloud. This scalable cloud model helped push gross margins near 69% . Additionally, Microsoft's ecosystem (Windows, Office, Azure, GitHub, LinkedIn, Xbox) allows cross-selling and integration, reinforcing its competitive advantage. Overall, Microsoft's model is that of a diversified **tech conglomerate**: a balanced engine of mature, cash-cow products and high-growth cloud/AI services. This provides both **stability and growth**, with durable competitive advantages in operating systems, productivity software, and cloud infrastructure. These qualities have translated into exceptional profitability and cash generation, as seen by consistently high margins and returns on capital.

## **Headwinds & Risks**

Despite its strengths, Microsoft faces several headwinds and risks. **Valuation risk** is notable - the stock trades at a high earnings multiple (P/E \~33x) after a sustained rally, which could lead to volatility if growth expectations are not met. **Regulatory risk** is increasing: Microsoft's size and market dominance (e.g. Windows &gt;75% desktop OS share, Office 365 in productivity software) invite antitrust scrutiny. Ongoing regulatory challenges (such as the Activision Blizzard acquisition review, or potential antitrust actions in app stores and cloud) could constrain certain business practices. **Currency fluctuations** are a headwind too - as a global company, a strong U.S. dollar recently has dampened reported growth (FX headwinds in overseas revenue). In terms of operations, Microsoft's reliance on enterprise IT spending and cloud adoption means it is exposed to **macroeconomic cycles**; a slowdown in corporate IT budgets or cloud optimizations by customers could moderate Azure growth. Competitively, Microsoft faces **intense competition** in cloud (AWS, Google Cloud) and enterprise software (Slack/Zoom vs. Teams, Google Workspace vs. Office). While it currently competes well, rapid innovation by rivals or disruptive new technologies (e.g. open-source AI platforms) are risks. Cybersecurity is another area of focus - high-profile hacks (e.g. the recent Azure cloud breach) highlight risks in safeguarding customer data; any security lapse can erode customer trust. Finally, Microsoft's **acquisition strategy** (e.g. LinkedIn, gaming studios) entails integration risk and significant capital outlay. Overall, while Microsoft's business is robust, investors should monitor these risks: regulatory developments, competitive moves, macroeconomic signals, and execution in high-growth areas like AI - any of which could pose challenges to Microsoft's growth trajectory.

## **Cost Structure**

Microsoft benefits from a highly scalable cost structure with significant operating leverage. Its cost of revenue mainly includes infrastructure costs for Azure/data centers, manufacturing costs for hardware (Surface, Xbox), and royalties for content (gaming). Notably, Microsoft's **gross margin is very high (\~69%)** , reflecting the heavy mix of software and cloud services in revenue. Software products (Windows, Office) have low marginal costs, boosting gross profitability. Even Azure, while more cost-intensive, has seen margin improvement as it scales (cloud gross margin \~69% including Azure, per management ). Operating expenses consist of R&D, Sales & Marketing, and G&A. R&D is sizeable (over $24 billion in FY2024) as Microsoft invests in AI, cloud, and software development - but this is an essential spend to maintain its tech lead. Sales and marketing expense supports enterprise sales teams and consumer marketing (e.g. for Xbox), but Microsoft's strong brand and product ubiquity also mean marketing needs are efficient relative to revenue. The result is excellent operating leverage: operating income margins have been in the mid-40% range recently . In FY2024, operating margin was 44.6% - for every additional dollar of revenue, a large portion falls to profit. This is evident in the last decade: revenue grew \~2.6× while net income grew \~7.2× , indicating widening margins over time. A risk in the cost structure is the rising **capital expenditure** needed for data centers and AI infrastructure. Capex jumped to $44.5B in FY2024 (from $5.9B in 2015) , as Microsoft pours investment into Azure and AI hardware (GPUs, servers). While these investments pressure free cash flow in the short term, they are necessary to support long-term growth. Importantly, Microsoft's strong cash generation (over $78B in free cash flow TTM ) easily funds its capex and dividends. Overall, Microsoft's cost structure - high fixed-cost base but low variable costs - enables **tremendous scalability**, positioning the company to expand operating profits faster than revenue as it grows.

## **Management & Governance**

Microsoft's management is widely regarded as capable and shareholder-friendly. CEO Satya Nadella (at the helm since 2014) has led a successful cultural and strategic transformation, pivoting Microsoft to a cloud-first, AI-focused strategy. Under Nadella, the company broke down silos and embraced partnerships (even with competitors), a shift from the more insular style of prior eras. This cultural change is often credited with reinvigorating innovation and growth at Microsoft. Governance practices are strong: Microsoft has an independent board of directors overseeing management. The board includes tech and industry veterans and is chaired by CEO Nadella following John Thompson (former chair). Microsoft's executive compensation is tied to performance metrics like revenue growth, operating income, and total shareholder return, helping align management incentives with shareholders' interests. Importantly, Microsoft has emphasized **compliance and ethics**, especially after past antitrust episodes - today it generally engages proactively with regulators (e.g. offering concessions in the Activision acquisition). In terms of capital allocation, management has been **prudent and returns-focused**. They pursue acquisitions strategically (LinkedIn in 2016 expanded Microsoft's reach in professional networking; recent gaming acquisitions aim to bolster Xbox content) while maintaining a solid balance sheet. Microsoft carries moderate debt ($120B debt vs $102B cash/investments) and a high credit rating, reflecting conservative financial management. Under Nadella, R&D investment has grown (to over 12% of revenue) but with clear focus on key priorities (cloud, AI, security). Overall, Microsoft's leadership is considered a strength - exemplified by its successful cloud transition - and governance frameworks are robust. Any change in this trusted leadership (for example, if Nadella were to depart unexpectedly) would be a notable risk, but currently management quality and stability give confidence in Microsoft's strategic direction.

## **Shareholders & Capital Returns**

Microsoft's ownership is dispersed, with no single majority owner - its largest shareholders are institutional investors. Vanguard, BlackRock, State Street and other big asset managers together hold a significant portion, reflecting Microsoft's status as a mega-cap widely held in index funds. Notably, co-founder Bill Gates has been steadily selling his stake (now &lt;1.5%). CEO Satya Nadella also owns only a modest amount of shares (under 1%), meaning Microsoft is essentially **ownerless** in the sense of not being family-controlled - governance relies on the board and shareholder voting. This broad ownership can be a strength (no dominant insider pushing self-interest) but also means management is relatively insulated from any one shareholder's influence.

Microsoft has been very **shareholder-friendly in capital returns**. It pays a steady and growing dividend (current annual dividend \~$3.64 per share) and has increased the dividend for 19 consecutive years. In the last 12 months, dividends totaled **$24.1B** . The dividend payout ratio is around 30% of earnings, which is conservative given Microsoft's cash flow. Additionally, Microsoft returns cash via large share buybacks. Over the past decade, it repurchased hundreds of millions of shares - for example, $19.3B was spent on buybacks in the last year . These buybacks have reduced the share count (basic shares down from \~8.0 billion in 2015 to \~7.4 billion in 2024 ), boosting EPS growth. The company's **free cash flow generation (&gt;$70B/year) comfortably covers dividends and buybacks**, and Microsoft still retains cash for strategic uses. As a result of these returns, Microsoft's total shareholder return has been exceptional (stock up \~10x over the past decade). Looking forward, one could expect Microsoft to continue similar capital return policies - moderate dividend growth (high single digits) and sizable buybacks - as long as cash flows remain strong. The only constraint might be if Microsoft chooses to finance major acquisitions with cash; even then, its balance sheet and cash flow provide ample flexibility. Overall, **shareholders have benefited greatly** from Microsoft's disciplined capital allocation and generous cash return program, which complement the stock's appreciation driven by earnings growth.

## **Buffett‑Style "Deep Value" Checklist**

We now evaluate Microsoft against a set of **Deep Value Investing criteria** often inspired by Benjamin Graham and Warren Buffett's approach. These benchmarks assess company quality and whether the stock offers a **"margin of safety."**Below is each criterion and whether Microsoft meets the standard:

**1. Valuation Discipline**

-   **P/E ≤ 15 (vs its own history):** *Fail.* Microsoft's current P/E is **\~33.7** , well above 15. Even looking at its 10-year history, P/E has generally been &gt;20 for the past several years (never dropping to 15). Microsoft's earnings multiple expanded as investors priced in high growth and quality - great for shareholders, but it means the stock is not cheap by this standard.

-   **PEG Ratio &lt; 1:** *Fail.* Microsoft's PEG (Price/Earnings to Growth) is **well over 1**. Using FY2024 EPS growth (\~21.9%) and a P/E \~36, the PEG is \~1.6-1.7. Stockanalysis shows a PEG of **3.04 for FY2025** (likely based on slower growth expectations ahead). Either way, it far exceeds 1.

-   **P/B ≤ 1.5:** *Fail.* Microsoft's Price-to-Book is about \*\*9.7× (current)】 and was \~12× at FY2024. This high P/B reflects Microsoft's immense intangible asset value (brand, software, patents) not captured on the balance sheet. Nonetheless, by the ≤1.5 deep-value rule, it fails.

-   **Margin of Safety (Buy ≥25% below intrinsic value):** *Fail.* Our discounted cash flow analysis (see next section) suggests intrinsic value per share well *below* the current market price. For example, even a generous growth scenario yielded an intrinsic value around $300/share, versus the market price \~$470 - meaning the stock is trading **above** intrinsic value, not below it. Microsoft does not offer a 25% discount to a conservative intrinsic value; rather, it's priced for high growth. There is *no margin of safety* at the current valuation.

**2. Cash Generation & Allocation**

-   **Positive Free Cash Flow each year; FCF/Revenue &gt; 5%:** *Pass.* Microsoft has generated positive free cash flow every year for decades. In the past 10 years, FCF margin ranged \~25%-33% , easily above 5%. Even during investment-heavy years, FCF remained robust (e.g. FY2023 FCF $59.5B on $211.9B revenue = 28% margin). This criterion is comfortably met - Microsoft is a cash machine.

-   **Return on Retained Earnings ≥ 12%:** *Pass.* This metric examines how effectively retained profits are turned into additional earnings. Microsoft clearly excels - over 2015-2024, it retained roughly $161B cumulative (increase in retained earnings) and grew annual net income by $\~76B. Rough estimation of return on that retention is over **45%**(i.e. $76B gain on $161B retained) - well above 12%. Even on an annual basis, Microsoft's reinvested earnings yield high returns: its ROE averages \~30% and it pays out only \~1/3 of earnings as dividends, implying retained portions are earning \~20%+ returns (since net income kept leads to growth in net income) . Thus, Microsoft's management has deployed retained capital extremely effectively (largely into R&D, acquisitions, and buybacks), far surpassing the 12% threshold.

**3. Growth & Predictability**

-   **EPS positive & rising every year for 10 yrs:** *Mostly Pass (minor exceptions).* Microsoft had positive EPS each year and a strongly upward trend. EPS roughly quadrupled from 2015 to 2024. However, it was not *literally* up every single year: FY2018 EPS dipped \~35% due to a one-time tax charge, and FY2023 EPS was roughly flat (slight decline) due to investment write-downs. Excluding unusual items, the underlying earnings power rose consistently. On an adjusted basis, Microsoft's EPS grew in at least 8 or 9 of the last 10 years, which is close to the ideal. The brief EPS declines were due to non-operational factors. Overall, Microsoft demonstrates excellent earnings stability and growth - albeit not a perfect 10/10 years increase, it's *very close*.

-   **Book Value per Share 10-yr CAGR ≥ 10% & no year of decline:** *CAGR: Pass; No declines: Fail.* Book value per share (BVPS) grew from about $10 in 2015 to $36 in 2024, roughly a **14% CAGR** (above 10%). However, there were one or two years where BVPS dipped. For example, massive share buybacks and the 2018 tax charge caused *shareholders' equity* to dip in FY2018, and while the share count also decreased, there was a slight BVPS contraction around that time. Also in FY2022-FY2023, dividend payouts and buybacks outpaced retained earnings, so equity per share stagnated. Thus, Microsoft fails the "no single year contraction" rule. That said, the overall **trend in BVPS is strongly upward**, and declines were small and driven by deliberate capital return (arguably not a negative).

-   **Owner's Earnings positive every year; Owner's Earnings/Market Cap ≥ 10%:** *Positive: Yes; Yield 10%: No.*Owner's earnings (a Buffett measure approximated by FCF or net income + depreciation - maintenance capex) have been positive each year - Microsoft always generates substantial owner earnings. However, **Owner's Earnings yield** is nowhere near 10%. For instance, taking free cash flow (\~$74B) as a proxy for owner's earnings, and current market cap \~$3.5T, gives \~2.1% yield. Even at the lows a decade ago, OE/Market Cap might have been \~5-6%. Microsoft's earnings yield (E/P \~3%) and FCF yield (\~2-3%) are far below 10% because of the high valuation. So while quality is superb, the stock fails the deep-value yield test.

**4. Balance-Sheet Safety**

-   **Debt-to-Equity &lt; 0.5:** *Pass.* Microsoft's D/E is about **0.33** (33%) as of latest quarter . Throughout the last decade, D/E ranged \~0.3-0.5. The company has modest leverage and plenty of equity. It easily meets the &lt;0.5 criterion.

-   **Interest Coverage (EBIT/Interest ≥ 3×):** *Pass.* Microsoft's interest coverage is extremely high. In FY2024, operating income was $109.4B and interest expense was about $2.98B . That's a coverage of \~37×. Even in earlier years, coverage never fell anywhere close to 3× (it's generally &gt;20×). Microsoft could pay its interest many times over - no issues here.

-   **Interest Expense / Operating Income ≤ 15%:** *Pass.* The flip side of coverage, Microsoft's interest expense is only \~2.7% of operating income . This easily beats the ≤15% rule. Microsoft's operating profits vastly exceed its interest obligations.

-   **Current Ratio &gt; 1.5:** *Fail.* Microsoft's current ratio (current assets / current liabilities) is **about 1.35** (as of Sep 2025) . It has dipped in recent years because the company doesn't hoard excess cash - they return a lot via buybacks/dividends. While 1.3+ is still healthy for a company with Microsoft's cash generation, it falls short of the conservative &gt;1.5 benchmark. Earlier in the decade, Microsoft often had a current ratio above 2 (lots of cash on hand), but as of the latest quarter: $191B current assets vs $141B current liabilities . So this criterion is not met strictly, though the quality of Microsoft's current assets (a large portion is cash/investments) mitigates concerns.

**5. Profitability & Efficiency**

-   **Gross Margin ≳ 40%:** *Pass.* Microsoft's gross margins are \~69% , far above 40%. This reflects its software/cloud economics. It easily passes this high-bar criterion.

-   **Net Profit Margin ≳ 20%:** *Pass.* Microsoft's net margin has been \~20-36% in recent years , and was 36.0% in FY2024. It consistently exceeded 20% after 2016. Even including the tax-hit year FY2018, that was \~15% net - one off. Microsoft comfortably meets a 20% net margin standard in most years, currently \~35%.

-   **ROE ≥ 15% (10-yr avg):** *Pass.* Microsoft's ROE averaged \~30%+ over the past decade. For example, in FY2024 ROE = 88.1/268.5 = **33%**, and it was similar or higher in prior years (except FY2018 anomaly). The 10-year average ROE is well above 15% . No year was ROE below 15% (even 2018's one-time hit still gave ROE \~18%). This indicates strong efficiency in deploying equity capital.

-   **ROIC (or ROTC) ≥ 12% (10-yr avg):** *Pass.* Return on invested capital (taking into account debt and equity) is also very high for Microsoft. As a proxy, ROIC \~ (Net Operating Profit After Tax) / (Debt + Equity). Microsoft's NOPAT in FY2024 was \~$109B minus taxes \~$18B → \~$91B. Invested capital (debt + equity) \~ $97.9 + $268.5 = $366.4B. ROIC ≈ 25%. Even averaging over years, Microsoft's ROIC is well above 12%. This signals a strong competitive moat.

-   **ROA ≥ 6%:** *Pass.* Microsoft's ROA (Net Income/Total Assets) is currently around 17-18% , and even at its lowest in the decade it stayed above \~6%. For instance, FY2020 ROA = 44.3/301.3 = 14.7%; FY2018 (depressed net) still \~6.4%. So Microsoft clears 6% handily.

-   **Depreciation / Gross Profit ≤ 10%:** *Almost Pass (slightly high).* This is a measure of capital intensity. In FY2024, depreciation+amortization was about $20.0B , while gross profit was $171.0B . D&A was \~11.7% of gross profit, a tad above the ≤10% mark. A decade ago this ratio was much lower (\~5-8%), but as Microsoft built out massive cloud data centers, depreciation rose. In FY2022 it was $14.6B D&A on $135.6B GP (\~10.8%) . The average might hover around 10-11%, so technically just above the cutoff. This is a **minor miss** - Microsoft is still not very capital-intensive relative to peers (for context, many industrial firms have D/A &gt; 20% of gross profit). Given Microsoft's high-margin business, depreciation is under good control, but strictly speaking, it's slightly above the arbitrary 10% threshold now due to cloud investments.

**Summary:** Microsoft **excels in quality and profitability** - it meets almost all the business-performance criteria (profit margins, ROE/ROA, FCF generation, low debt, interest coverage). However, it **fails all the strict value criteria** (P/E, P/B, PEG, margin of safety, yield). This is expected for a premium company whose stock is priced for growth. In other words, Microsoft is a **great company** by Buffett's quality standards (wide moat, high returns on capital, consistent growth), but it's **not available at a bargain price**. A deep value investor seeking a 50-cent dollar would not find it here at current valuations. Microsoft's stock resembles a "wonderful company at a fair (or expensive) price" rather than a cigar-butt cheap stock. Long-term value investors might still hold it for compounding, but by Graham-style deep value metrics, Microsoft does **not** pass (except on the business quality fronts).

## **DCF Valuation (Intrinsic Value Analysis)**

To estimate Microsoft's **intrinsic value**, we perform a Discounted Cash Flow analysis under three scenarios: **No-Growth**, **Conservative**, and **Nominal Growth**. These cases use Microsoft's recent free cash flow and different growth assumptions derived from its 10-year history. We then determine a fair value per share and a suggested "buy price" (with a 30% margin of safety) for each scenario.

**Assumptions:** We use Microsoft's trailing 12-month **free cash flow** of \~$78.0 billion as the starting point. We assume a discount rate (required return) of **10%** - reflecting a blend of Microsoft's low risk (beta \~0.9) and market return expectations, plus a small margin. For perpetuity growth beyond the forecast, we assume a modest **terminal growth rate of 2%** (roughly inflation-level, prudent for a mature giant). Shares outstanding \~7.43 billion. All values in USD.

**1. No-Growth Scenario (0% FCF growth):** This "zero-growth" case assumes Microsoft's free cash flow will *flatline* at the current \~$78B level indefinitely, with no growth. This is extremely conservative given Microsoft's history - essentially imagining it cannot increase earnings further (or that any growth is offset by declines elsewhere). In a DCF, a no-growth perpetuity value = FCF / discount rate. Using $78.0B and 10% gives an enterprise value of about **$780 billion**. After dividing by shares, intrinsic value per share ≈ **$105**. The 30% margin-of-safety price would be **$73**. **Result:***Intrinsic value ≈ $105/share*. Microsoft's current stock ($470+) is far above this, reflecting that the market expects significant growth (which we ignored here). In reality, Microsoft will almost certainly grow, but this scenario provides a floor. It suggests that if Microsoft's growth completely stopped, the stock is dramatically overpriced. (Note: Even at $105, the stock wouldn't be "cheap" as that implies a 10% FCF yield; many mature no-growth companies trade around that level.)

**2. Conservative Scenario (low growth):** Here we assume Microsoft grows FCF at **the lowest rate it has seen in the past decade**. Microsoft's weakest annual FCF growth was about +3% (e.g. FY2018) - excluding the one anomalous decline of -8.7% in FY2023 (which was due to timing of working capital and investments). We'll use **3%** annual FCF growth for the next 10 years, then 2% terminal. This mimics a scenario where Microsoft grows very slowly (perhaps due to saturation or economic stagnation).

-   **Forecast:** Year 1 FCF = $78.0B \*1.03 = $80.3B, and so on growing 3% to Year 10 = \~$105B. Terminal value at year 10 = $105B \* (1.02) / (0.10-0.02) = $105B \* 1.02 / 0.08 ≈ $1338B.

-   **Valuation:** Discounting all 10 years of cash flows and the terminal value at 10% yields a present value of approximately **$1.07 trillion** for the enterprise. Divide by 7.43B shares = **$144 per share** intrinsic value. The **buy price with 30% margin** = \~$101/share.

**Result:** *Intrinsic value ≈ $140-145 per share.* This scenario is still **well below** the current trading price. It indicates that if Microsoft's growth slows to low-single-digits, the stock is overvalued. Microsoft would need to be around $140 to offer a fair 10% return under sluggish growth. At $470, the market is clearly pricing in far more optimism than 3% growth.

**3. Nominal Growth (average case):** In this scenario, we use Microsoft's **average FCF growth rate** over the past 10 years as the projection. The 10-year FCF CAGR was about **13-14%** annually (despite ups and downs) . We'll use **13%**growth for the next 10 years, then revert to 2% terminal growth. This reflects a continuation of Microsoft's strong performance (though notably 13% is lower than recent EPS growth \~ high teens, being a bit conservative).

-   **Forecast:** Year 1 FCF = $78B *1.13 = $88.1B; Year 10 FCF = \~$78B*(1.13^10) ≈ $265B. Terminal value in year 10 = $265B \*1.02/(0.10-0.02) ≈ $3376B.

-   **Valuation:** Summing the PV of years 1-10 cash flows and terminal value (discounted at 10%) yields roughly **$2.29 trillion**. On a per-share basis, intrinsic value ≈ **$308 per share**. The **30% MOS buy price** would be about **$216**.

**Result:** *Intrinsic value ≈ $300-310 per share.* This suggests that even assuming Microsoft can maintain its historical average FCF growth (\~13%) for another decade (a bullish assumption given its large size), the stock's fair value might be around $300. The current market price \~$470 is significantly higher, implying the market expects either faster growth, a longer high-growth period, or is accepting a lower return (e.g. using a 7% discount rate would produce a higher DCF value). Our $308 intrinsic would imply a P/FCF \~30×, which is already rich. The stock's price is \~50% above this DCF value, signaling no margin of safety - quite the opposite, the stock is baking in a lot of future success.

**DCF Summary:** All three scenarios indicate that Microsoft's stock is *overvalued relative to conservative intrinsic value estimates.* The no-growth case yields \~$105, the slow-growth \~$144, and even an optimistic average-growth case \~$308 - all below the current \~$470. The margin-of-safety prices ($73, $101, $216 respectively) are far below the market price. This doesn't mean Microsoft isn't a great business - it is - but it means investors are paying a hefty premium, presumably for Microsoft's competitive advantages and future growth (e.g. AI opportunities). In DCF terms, the market is effectively assuming **high-teens growth for many years** or using a lower discount rate (reflecting low interest rates or lower risk). If one requires a 10% return and a margin of safety, Microsoft would need to drop considerably (to around $200 or less) to be a compelling **value buy**. In conclusion, by DCF analysis Microsoft appears **overvalued** - a wonderful company, but not at a bargain price today.

*(Note: DCF models are sensitive to assumptions. If one uses a 8% discount rate, the nominal case value would shoot up, arguably justifying a higher price.
