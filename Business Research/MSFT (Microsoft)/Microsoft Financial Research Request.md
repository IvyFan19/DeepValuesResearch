## Latest Quarter Earnings Review: Fiscal Year 2026 Q1

The earnings report for the first quarter of Fiscal Year 2026 serves as a definitive validation of the company's "AI-First" strategy, while simultaneously highlighting the massive costs associated with maintaining its leadership position.

Revenue and Top-Line Performance

Microsoft delivered a resounding beat on top-line expectations, reporting revenue of $77.7 billion, representing an 18% increase year-over-year (17% in constant currency).1 This performance was driven by broad-based strength across all segments, but the divergence in growth rates underscores the strategic importance of the cloud. The "Intelligent Cloud" segment, which houses the Azure platform, grew by approximately 20% to $24.1 billion, outpacing the company average.4 This suggests that despite the massive scale Microsoft has already achieved, the addressable market for cloud infrastructure remains far from saturated.

Profitability and Margins

Operating income surged 24%, outpacing revenue growth and demonstrating the inherent operating leverage in Microsoft's software-dominated business model.1 Gross margin dollars increased 18%, although the gross margin percentage dipped slightly to 68%.4 This compression is a direct result of the scaling of AI infrastructure; the depreciation of new GPU clusters and the higher energy costs associated with training large language models are structurally lower-margin activities compared to traditional SaaS software delivery. Net income was $27.7 billion, translating to a diluted earnings per share (EPS) of $3.73.6 This bottom-line figure included a negative impact of $0.41 per share driven by equity losses, primarily attributed to the company's deepening investment in OpenAI.4

Guidance and Outlook

Management's guidance for the upcoming quarters remains bullish but reflects the supply-constrained nature of the current AI boom. Revenue for the "Productivity and Business Processes" segment is forecast between $33.3 and $33.6 billion.1 Critically, management indicated that Azure growth is currently limited not by demand, but by the company's ability to bring capacity online, a bottleneck that justifies the aggressive capital expenditure strategy.7

## Background Research

### 1. Business Model Analysis

Microsoft operates as a diversified technology conglomerate, effectively functioning as a tax on global corporate productivity. Its business model has successfully transitioned from a transactional license-based model to a recurring revenue subscription and consumption model.

#### Sources of Income

#### Customer Base and Reach

Microsoft's client base is arguably the most extensive of any enterprise company in existence.

-   **Commercial Seats:** The company boasts over 400 million paid commercial seats for Microsoft 365.<sup>9</sup> This figure represents a vast majority of the global knowledge workforce.

-   **Enterprise Penetration:** Azure is utilized by 85% of the Fortune 500, serving as the digital backbone for the world's largest corporations.<sup>10</sup>

-   **Small and Medium Business (SMB):** There are over 3.7 million organizations globally using Microsoft 365, although this represents only a fraction of the total businesses worldwide, indicating room for expansion.<sup>11</sup>

-   **Consumer Reach:** Windows 11 has been installed on over 31% of all Windows devices globally, and the Xbox ecosystem engages hundreds of millions of gamers.<sup>9</sup>

#### Units and Subscriptions

The recurring nature of Microsoft's revenue is supported by massive subscriber numbers.

-   **Microsoft 365 Consumer:** The subscriber base grew to 89.0 million in FY2025, driven by the migration of legacy users to the cloud.<sup>12</sup>

-   **Gaming:** Xbox Game Pass subscribers have reached approximately 37 million.<sup>13</sup> This subscription service is central to the company's strategy to become the "Netflix of Gaming."

-   **AI Adoption:** Microsoft Copilot, the company's flagship AI product, has seen rapid adoption with active users estimated between 20 to 30 million.<sup>14</sup>

#### Economic Moat

Microsoft possesses a **Wide Economic Moat**, reinforced by three primary pillars:

1.  **High Switching Costs:** For an enterprise deeply integrated into the Microsoft ecosystem-using Windows for endpoints, Azure for infrastructure, Teams for communication, and Office for documentation-the operational friction and financial cost of switching to a competitor like Google or AWS are prohibitive. The suite creates a "walled garden" that is exceptionally difficult to leave.<sup>15</sup>

2.  **Network Effects:** Platforms like LinkedIn (professional networking) and GitHub (developer collaboration) gain value as more users join. Similarly, the ubiquity of Office file formats (.docx,.xlsx) creates a standard that forces external stakeholders to adopt the same tools.<sup>15</sup>

3.  **Cost Advantage (Scale):** The barrier to entry in the AI era is capital. With quarterly capital expenditures exceeding $30 billion, Microsoft is one of the few entities on the planet capable of building the necessary infrastructure to train frontier AI models. This financial fortress effectively prevents smaller competitors from challenging its dominance in the "Intelligent Cloud".<sup>7</sup>

### 2. Headwinds and Risks

#### Short-Term Headwinds (Temporary Factors)

1.  **Capacity Constraints:** The most immediate risk is the company's inability to build data centers fast enough. Management has admitted that Azure growth is capped by available capacity.<sup>7</sup> This forces potential revenue to be deferred or lost to competitors who may have spare capacity.

2.  **Hardware Cyclicality:** The "More Personal Computing" segment remains tethered to the global PC market. Softness in PC sales, driven by macroeconomic uncertainty, drags down Windows OEM revenue and device sales.<sup>4</sup>

3.  **Margin Compression:** The shift toward AI-driven revenue introduces a structural change in margins. AI queries are computationally expensive compared to traditional search or software tasks. As AI becomes a larger portion of the revenue mix, gross margins may face continued pressure in the near term.<sup>4</sup>

#### Long-Term Risks (Permanent Factors)

1.  **The "AI Capex Wall" and ROI Uncertainty:** Investors are increasingly concerned about the return on investment for the massive capital outlays. If Generative AI proves to be a sustaining innovation rather than a disruptive platform shift, the depreciation costs of billions in GPU infrastructure could weigh on earnings for a decade. The risk is a scenario similar to the telecom fiber boom of the late 90s-massive overcapacity leading to asset write-downs.<sup>17</sup>

2.  **Antitrust and Regulatory Action:** Microsoft is under intense scrutiny from global regulators (FTC, EU, CMA). The integration of Teams into Office, the acquisition of Activision Blizzard, and the partnership with OpenAI are all vectors for antitrust litigation. A forced breakup or significant behavioral remedies could dismantle the integration strategies that form the core of its moat.<sup>19</sup>

3.  **Data Sovereignty and Copyright:** The legal foundation of Generative AI is being challenged. Shareholder proposals have already demanded reports on "AI Data Sourcing Accountability".<sup>21</sup> If courts rule that training models on copyrighted data constitutes infringement, Microsoft could face billions in liabilities and a fundamental restructuring of its AI cost base.

### 3. Cost Structure Analysis

Microsoft's cost structure is evolving from a software-centric model (high fixed R&D, low variable costs) to a utility-centric model (high fixed infrastructure, moderate variable energy costs).

#### Fixed and Variable Costs

1.  **Capital Expenditures (Fixed/Allocated):** This is the most significant cost driver. Cash capital expenditures hit $34.9 billion in the latest quarter.<sup>2</sup> These costs are capitalized and depreciate over time, creating a long-tail drag on profitability.

2.  **Labor Expenses (Fixed):** Research and Development (R&D) expenses reached $33.1 billion for the trailing twelve months.<sup>22</sup> This represents the cost of human capital-the engineers and scientists required to maintain software leadership.

3.  **Cost of Revenue (Variable):** This line item increased by 20% in the recent quarter.<sup>4</sup> It includes the electricity to run data centers and the hardware depreciation. As AI usage scales, the variable cost per query is significantly higher than traditional software interactions.

#### Significant Costs and Trends

The most significant trend is the decoupling of revenue growth from capital intensity. While revenue grew 18%, capital expenditures grew significantly faster. In different economic conditions, Microsoft has high **operating leverage**. In a downturn, it can slow hiring and pause new data center builds (reducing future capex), while its subscription revenue remains sticky. However, the current "AI Arms Race" effectively forces the company to spend aggressively regardless of the immediate economic cycle to prevent ceding ground to Google or Amazon.<sup>23</sup>

### 2. Deep Value Investing Standards Check

We apply a rigorous "Graham-and-Dodd" style checklist to determine if MSFT meets the criteria of a high-quality value investment. This methodology prioritizes safety of principal and reasonable valuation over speculative growth.

#### 1. Valuation Discipline: **FAIL**

-   **Price to Earning ratio (P/E) ≤ 15:** **FAIL.** The current P/E of \~36.8x is more than double the traditional value threshold of 15. It is also trading at the upper end of its own 10-year range, suggesting significant multiple expansion rather than earnings compression.

-   **PEG Ratio &lt; 1:** **FAIL.** With a P/E of 36.8 and a growth rate of roughly 15-18%, the PEG ratio sits between 2.0 and 2.5. This indicates the stock is expensive relative to its growth, failing Peter Lynch's criterion.

-   **Price to Book ratio (P/B) ≤ 1.5:** **FAIL.** At \~12x Book Value, Microsoft trades at a massive premium to its net assets. However, for a software company with immense intangible value (IP, brand, ecosystem), Book Value is often a poor metric of intrinsic worth.

-   **Margin of Safety:** **FAIL.** There is no 25% discount to a conservative intrinsic value (see DCF section). The market is pricing the stock for perfection.

#### 2. Cash Generation & Allocation: **PASS**

-   **Free-Cash-Flow (FCF) Positive:** **PASS.** Microsoft is one of the most prolific cash generators in corporate history, consistently producing over $60-$70 billion annually.<sup>38</sup>

-   **FCF/Revenue &gt; 5%:** **PASS.** The FCF margin is approximately 24% ($70.5B / $293.8B), demonstrating elite efficiency in converting sales to cash, even amidst heavy capital spending.<sup>38</sup>

-   **Return on Retained Earnings:** **PASS.** Management has historically demonstrated an ability to reinvest capital at high rates of return, evident in the successful growth of Azure and the commercial cloud business.

#### 3. Growth & Predictability: **PASS**

-   **EPS Record:** **PASS.** Earnings per share have been positive and generally rising for the last decade. The only significant dip (2018) was due to a one-time tax charge related to the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA), not operational weakness.<sup>40</sup>

-   **Shareholder Equity CAGR ≥ 10%:** **PASS.** Equity has grown from \~$72 billion in 2016 to over $363 billion in 2025, a Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) of nearly 20%.<sup>36</sup>

-   **Owner's Earnings:** **PASS.** Owner's earnings (Operating Cash Flow minus Maintenance Capex) are robustly positive.

#### 4. Balance Sheet Safety: **PASS (EXEMPLARY)**

-   **Debt-to-Equity &lt; 0.5:** **PASS.** The ratio is currently \~0.12, reflecting a fortress balance sheet with significantly more cash than debt.<sup>41</sup>

-   **Interest Coverage ≥ 3x:** **PASS.** Operating income ($135.9B) covers interest expense hundreds of times over. In fact, due to its massive cash pile, Microsoft often earns more interest income than it pays in interest expense.<sup>41</sup>

-   **Current Ratio &gt; 1.5:** **PASS.** Liquidity is robust, ensuring the company can meet short-term obligations without stress.

#### 5. Profitability & Efficiency: **PASS**

-   **Gross Margin ≳ 40%:** **PASS.** Consistently maintains \~68% gross margins.<sup>39</sup>

-   **Return on Equity (ROE) ≥ 15%:** **PASS.** Consistently above 30%, indicating highly efficient use of shareholder capital.<sup>37</sup>

**Summary Verdict:** Microsoft is the archetype of a "Wonderful Company" (Buffett's qualitative standard) but fails the "Deep Value" (Graham's quantitative standard) test due to its rich valuation. It passes all safety and quality metrics with flying colors but fails all price metrics.

### 3. Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) Analysis

To determine the intrinsic value of Microsoft, we employ a Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) model across three scenarios.

-   **Discount Rate (WACC):** We assume a discount rate of **8.5%**, reflecting a risk-free rate of \~4.5% plus an equity risk premium, adjusted for Microsoft's low beta.

-   **Shares Outstanding:** **7.43 Billion** shares.<sup>37</sup>

-   **Base Free Cash Flow (TTM):** **$70.5 Billion**.<sup>38</sup>

#### Scenario A: No-Growth (Stagnation)

-   **Assumption:** Microsoft fails to grow its Free Cash Flow from this point forward. This implies AI investments yield zero incremental return and legacy businesses plateau.

-   **Calculation:** Perpetuity Value = FCF / Discount Rate = $70.5B / 0.085 = $829.4 Billion.

-   **Intrinsic Value per Share:** $829.4B / 7.43B shares = **$111.63**

-   **Buy Price (30% Margin of Safety):** $78.14

#### Scenario B: Conservative (Historical Low Growth)

-   **Assumption:** FCF grows at **10%** for the next 10 years (aligned with the lower end of historical performance during the pre-cloud transition) and 3% terminal growth.

-   **Step-by-Step Calculation:**

    -   Years 1-10 FCF Sum (Discounted): \~$980 Billion.

    -   Year 10 FCF: \~$182 Billion.

    -   Terminal Value: $182B \* (1.03) / (0.085 - 0.03) = $3.4 Trillion.

    -   Present Value of Terminal Value: \~$1.50 Trillion.

    -   Total Enterprise Value: $980B + $1.50T = $2.48 Trillion.

    -   Add Net Cash (\~$50B): $2.53 Trillion.

-   **Intrinsic Value per Share:** $2.53T / 7.43B shares = **$340.51**

-   **Buy Price (30% Margin of Safety):** $238.36

#### Scenario C: Nominal (AI Success)

-   **Assumption:** FCF grows at **15%** for the next 5 years (AI boom), fading to **10%** for years 6-10, with a **3.5%** terminal growth rate.

-   **Step-by-Step Calculation:**

    -   Years 1-5 FCF (Discounted): \~$450 Billion.

    -   Years 6-10 FCF (Discounted): \~$680 Billion.

    -   Terminal Value (Year 10 FCF = \~$260B): $260B \* (1.035) / (0.085 - 0.035) = $5.38 Trillion.

    -   Present Value of Terminal Value: \~$2.38 Trillion.

    -   Total Enterprise Value: $450B + $680B + $2.38T = $3.51 Trillion.

    -   Add Net Cash (\~$50B): $3.56 Trillion.

-   **Intrinsic Value per Share:** $3.56T / 7.43B shares = **$479.13**

-   **Buy Price (30% Margin of Safety):** $335.39

DCF Conclusion:

The stock is currently trading around $474 (November 2025). This price aligns almost perfectly with the Nominal scenario, suggesting the market has fully priced in a successful AI transition and sustained double-digit growth for a decade. There is currently zero margin of safety for deep value investors. The stock is priced for perfection; any stumble in AI monetization or growth would likely result in multiple compression.
