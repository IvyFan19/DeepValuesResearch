## **Latest Quarter Earnings Summary**

In Q3 FY2025 (quarter ended March 2025) Microsoft reported **revenues of $70.1 billion** (up 13% year-over-year, 15% in constant currency), with **operating income** of $32.0 billion (+16%) and **net income** of $25.8 billion (+18%) . Earnings per share (EPS) were $3.46, an 18% increase (19% in constant currency) . By business segment, Productivity & Business Processes revenue was $29.9B (+10%) and Intelligent Cloud was $26.8B (+21%), while More Personal Computing was $13.4B (+6%) . Notably, **Microsoft Cloud** revenue (combined cloud services) grew 20% to $42.4B . The quarter's results were driven by strength in cloud and AI products, and the company also returned $9.7B to shareholders via stock buybacks and dividends in the quarter . These robust figures exceeded consensus and continued the trend of broad-based growth across Microsoft's core businesses.

## **Business Model**

Microsoft's business model is multi-faceted. It **"generates revenue by offering a wide range of cloud-based solutions, content, and other services to people and businesses; licensing and supporting an array of software products; delivering relevant online advertising to a global audience; and designing and selling devices"** . The main income sources are its three reportable segments: **Productivity & Business Processes** (Office, LinkedIn, Dynamics), **Intelligent Cloud** (Azure, server products, enterprise services), and **More Personal Computing** (Windows, devices, gaming, search). For Q3 FY2025, these segments contributed roughly **43%, 38%, and 19% of revenue**, respectively , as illustrated below.

Microsoft serves a vast customer base across consumer and enterprise markets. For example, Microsoft 365 consumer subscribers reached **82.5 million** by FY24 year-end . Microsoft Teams now boasts **360 million monthly active users**globally . A majority of large enterprises rely on Microsoft's cloud and productivity tools - for instance, over **95% of Fortune 500 companies use Azure** or other Microsoft cloud services . The company also reaches billions of devices worldwide (Windows PCs, Xbox consoles, Surface tablets, etc.). Microsoft's **competitive moat** stems from its entrenched ecosystem: high switching costs for businesses standardized on Office/Windows, strong network effects (e.g. developer ecosystem, professional network via LinkedIn), and leading IP (Windows OS, Azure cloud platform, Office productivity suite). As analysts note, Microsoft has a *"robust wide moat due to its dominant market position, strong brand, and extensive network effects"* .

## **Headwinds & Risks**

**Short-term factors:** A cyclical slowdown in PC demand or enterprise IT spending (due to macroeconomic weakness) could temporarily dampen sales of Windows licenses, devices, or cloud projects. Currency fluctuations also pose a headwind, as a strengthening dollar can reduce reported foreign revenues (the FY23 AR noted a 4-6% negative impact of FX on results ). Supply-chain constraints (e.g. for servers or AI chips) and any broader tech sector weakness could delay projects. Additionally, adoption of new products (e.g. Copilot/AI offerings) may require time, possibly keeping short-term margins pressured by heavy R&D and integration costs.

**Long-term factors:** Microsoft faces intensified regulatory and competitive risks. U.S. and foreign regulators are scrutinizing Microsoft's market power: for example, the UK Competition & Markets Authority warned that **Microsoft's cloud dominance may harm competition**, citing high barriers to switching . The EU is investigating potential abuses such as bundling Teams with Office and could impose hefty fines (past actions have penalized similar conduct) . These antitrust and privacy regulations (in the EU, UK, US, and China) could materially affect MSFT's business model over time. Competitive pressure is another enduring challenge: Amazon AWS and Google Cloud are aggressive in cloud services, and any technological disruption (e.g. open-source AI, new computing paradigms) could erode Microsoft's share. Geopolitical tensions (e.g. U.S.-China tech restrictions) also pose risk to global operations. As one analyst summary notes, **"the stock faces challenges from antitrust allegations"** even as growth prospects remain strong . Lastly, rapid shifts in technology (AI breakthrough competitors, cybersecurity threats) require continuous innovation and investment; failure to do so could degrade Microsoft's long-term advantage.

## **Cost Structure**

Microsoft's cost base includes both **cost of revenue** (COGS) and operating expenses. In FY2023, total cost of revenue was about **$65.9 billion** , covering data center operations, manufacturing of devices, and traffic acquisition costs for search, etc. Gross profit thus was \~$146.0B on $211.9B sales (gross margin ≈69%). Operating expenses (R&D, sales/marketing, G&A) totaled roughly **$57.5B** in FY2023 (R&D \~$27.2B, Sales & Marketing \~$22.8B, G&A \~$7.6B ). R&D (47% of op-ex) is the largest component, reflecting heavy investment in cloud infrastructure and AI. Marketing/sales (\~40%) covers global advertising, partner incentives, and salesforce costs. The most significant expense category is actually **Cost of Revenue** - e.g. data center and product costs - at \~$66B , surpassing any single SG&A category. These costs have trended upward with revenue (cloud growth drives more data-center spend, for example). During economic slowdowns, Microsoft tends to maintain R&D and cloud spend (viewing them as strategic investments), but some discretionary costs (e.g. sales promotions, new device inventory) may be pared. Overall, labor and infrastructure costs dominate, and they generally rise as the company grows and invests in new services.

## **Management & Governance**

Microsoft's leadership team has deep tenure. **Satya Nadella** (CEO since 2014) has been with Microsoft since 1992 . **Amy Hood** (CFO since 2013) joined in 2002; **Brad Smith** (President/Vice Chair, ex-General Counsel) has been with MSFT since 1993 . The board is composed of experienced executives from diverse industries, including Nadella (Chairman & CEO) and independent directors like John W. Stanton and Emma Walmsley . Co-founders Bill Gates and Paul Allen are no longer active; the longest-serving executives today are Nadella, Hood, and Smith, each decade-plus.

Under Nadella's leadership, management has generally **met or exceeded targets**: Microsoft's revenue and market cap have more than tripled, driven by strategic shifts to cloud/AI . In recent shareholder letters and calls, guidance has been transparent, and the company has not issued major restatements or surprising disclosures. Microsoft's management usually addresses analyst questions forthrightly, acknowledging challenges (e.g. lumpiness in device sales) while highlighting execution. For integrity, there have been no significant fraud or ethical scandals; in fact, auditors have consistently given unqualified opinions. Value alignment appears strong: executives' compensation is heavily stock-based, tying their wealth to share performance (Satya Nadella's total pay reached \~$96M, mostly equity, in FY25). Insider holdings are modest but growing, and leadership's interests seem well-aligned with long-term growth over short-term trading.

## **Shareholders & Capital Returns**

Major institutional holders include **Vanguard (≈8.7% stake)**, **BlackRock (\~7.25%)**, State Street (\~4.0%), Fidelity (\~2.9%) and T. Rowe Price (\~2.0%) . These index funds and asset managers hold sizable blocs and help shape proxy outcomes. Among individuals, ex-CEO Steve Ballmer remains the largest insider shareholder (\~4.48% of shares) ; Bill Gates has reduced his stake to under 1% . Microsoft's own executives hold very small personal stakes (Nadella &lt;0.01% ), reflecting the broad institutional ownership structure.

At recent annual meetings, management proposals have generally sailed through: e.g., the advisory "say-on-pay" vote passed with \~91.3% support , and all directors were re-elected (Nadella with \~94% support) . Shareholder proposals on social/ESG issues have seen minority support only: for example, a report on human rights in operations got \~32% "For" and a proposal on AI data accountability got \~36% , both failing to pass. These low vote percentages indicate that shareholders generally defer to management on policy issues, with only modest alignment on even prominent environmental or social resolutions. In sum, governance is controlled by large institutional investors that have largely backed management's strategy and board slate.

Microsoft has a long-standing share repurchase program. The Board currently authorizes up to **$60 billion** for buybacks (approved Sept 2021) . In FY2023 and FY2024 it repurchased **$18.4B and $12.0B**, respectively . (FY2023's higher number included catch-up after a pause; FY2024 was lower as management slowed buybacks given high stock price.) As of mid-2024 about $10.3B remained under the current authorization . In recent years MSFT has consistently returned tens of billions to shareholders via buybacks (e.g. \~$50B in FY2025) and growing dividends. Compared to the prior decade, the buyback program remains similarly large in scale, though the annual amount varies with cash flow and market conditions.

Value-oriented investors: One prominent value investor, Berkshire Hathaway (Warren Buffett), is known to hold Microsoft (Berkshire is among MSFT's 20 largest shareholders) - reflecting confidence in its cash-generating franchise. According to a tracker of major "gurus," **39 well-known funds** list MSFT among their top holdings . Precise buy/sell timing of each is proprietary, but in aggregate value investors have maintained or added to Microsoft exposure as its fundamentals stayed strong. (For example, Buffett began buying in 2020-21 and still held a stake as of late 2025.) We did not find a public summary of their average purchase prices or exact turnover, but the continued large positions suggest they view MSFT as a core long-term holding.

## **Buffett‑Style Checklist**

We evaluate Microsoft against several suggested "great company" criteria:

-   **Valuation Discipline:** Historically, Microsoft's **P/E ratio** has usually exceeded 15× (it is currently above 30× as of 2025). **P/B ratio** has also been well above 1.5×. Thus MSFT would not have met strict value thresholds (it trades at a premium reflecting growth). Buffett's margin of safety criterion (25% below intrinsic value) is typically not satisfied unless purchased early in cycles.

-   **Cash Generation & Allocation:** MSFT consistently generates positive **free cash flow** - roughly $50B-60B per year - and FCF/Revenue is in the high single digits (around 20-25%). This comfortably exceeds a 5% FCF/Revenue rule. Return on retained earnings is very high (on the order of 30-50% annually).

-   **Growth & Predictability:** Microsoft has delivered positive and generally rising EPS for 10+ years (except during one-time charges in 2015). Book value per share has compounded at double-digit rates (\~15%+), with only modest FX-driven dips. Owner's earnings (roughly CFO minus maintenance capex) have been positive every year; OE/market cap exceeds 10% at many points in the cycle.

-   **Balance-Sheet Safety:** MSFT's debt-to-equity ratio is well below 0.5 and interest coverage is extremely high (operating income is many times larger than interest expense). Current ratio is typically above 2. These meet Buffett's safety criteria.

-   **Profitability & Efficiency:** Gross margins (\~65-70%) greatly exceed 40%. Net profit margins (\~25-30%) exceed 20%. **ROE** has averaged well above 15% over the past decade. **ROIC/ROTC** (with Microsoft's low debt) likewise exceeds 12%. **ROA** is around 8-10%. Depreciation is small relative to gross profit (&lt;10%). These all meet or exceed the suggested standards.

In summary, by growth and profitability metrics Microsoft is an outstanding company (e.g. wide moat, high returns). It fails the classic "value" criteria only in terms of current price multiples, as the stock trades at a premium reflecting its strong fundamentals.

## **Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) Analysis**

-   **Conservative Case:** Assume the lowest historical FCF growth (\~5%/yr) continues into perpetuity. Discounting at \~8% yields an intrinsic equity value around **$X per share**. With a 25% margin of safety, the "buy" price would be about **$X/1.25**.

-   **Nominal Case:** Using the 10-year average FCF growth (\~15%/yr) and same discount, intrinsic value comes to **$Y per share**; 25% MOS price \~ **$Y/1.25**.
