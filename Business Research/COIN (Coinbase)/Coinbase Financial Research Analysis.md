## Latest Quarter Earnings Summary

Coinbase Global, Inc. (NASDAQ: COIN) delivered a robust financial performance in the third quarter of 2025, significantly surpassing analyst expectations and signaling a strong recovery in the digital asset market. The results were characterized by surging transaction revenues, continued growth in the subscription and services segment, and a substantial increase in profitability, reaffirming the company's highly cyclical nature and its sensitivity to broader crypto market sentiment.

The company reported total revenue of $1.9 billion, marking a 25% increase quarter-over-quarter (Q/Q) and a substantial 55.1% increase year-over-year (Y/Y). This top-line growth translated directly to the bottom line, with net income surging to $433 million, a dramatic 477% increase from the $75 million reported in the same period last year. On a per-share basis, the company posted earnings of $1.44, beating the Zacks Consensus Estimate by a wide margin of 39.8%. Adjusted EBITDA, a key measure of profitability, reached $801 million, a 78.5% improvement from the prior year's quarter.

A deeper analysis of the revenue drivers reveals that the outperformance was primarily fueled by a resurgence in trading activity. Total transaction revenue grew to $1.0 billion, up 37% sequentially and 82.7% year-over-year. This was underpinned by a 59.4% Y/Y increase in total trading volume to $295 billion.

-   **Consumer Trading Volume** was particularly strong, growing 37% Q/Q to $59 billion, a rate that outpaced the broader U.S. spot markets.

-   **Institutional Trading Volume** rose 22% Q/Q to $236 billion. The associated revenue from this segment experienced a remarkable 122% Q/Q surge to $135 million. However, this headline figure is heavily influenced by the recent acquisition of the derivatives platform Deribit, which closed on August 14 and contributed $52 million in revenue in its first partial quarter with Coinbase. While this demonstrates successful execution of the company's acquisitive growth strategy, it also suggests that nearly half of the quarter's institutional transaction revenue came from this new source, potentially masking a more modest organic growth rate in the core institutional trading business.

The **Subscription and Services** segment continued its steady growth trajectory, with revenue climbing to $747 million, up 14% Q/Q and 34.3% Y/Y. This growth was driven by all-time highs in key underlying metrics, including average USDC balances held in Coinbase products (over $15 billion), institutional loan balances, and Assets on Platform, which grew 95% Y/Y to $516 billion. Despite the strategic emphasis on this more stable revenue stream, its 14% sequential growth was less than half the 37% growth seen in the more volatile transaction segment. This disparity underscores that during periods of high market activity, the company's financial performance remains disproportionately tied to trading volumes, reinforcing its position as a high-beta investment proxy for the broader crypto market.

On the cost side, total operating expenses increased 29.2% Y/Y to $1.4 billion, driven by costs associated with recent acquisitions and a 12% Q/Q increase in headcount to 4,795 full-time employees. Looking ahead to Q4 2025, management guided for Subscription and Services revenue to be in the range of $710-$790 million, reflecting continued growth in the USDC ecosystem, partially offset by the headwind of expected market interest rate cuts. Technology, Development, and G&A expenses are projected to increase to a range of $925-$975 million, largely due to the full-quarter impact of the Deribit and Echo acquisitions.

## Business Model

Coinbase Global, Inc. operates a comprehensive digital platform that serves as a primary gateway to the cryptoeconomy for millions of users worldwide. Founded in 2012 with the initial goal of making Bitcoin accessible, the company has since evolved into a multifaceted financial infrastructure provider for both retail and institutional clients. The company's overarching strategy is to build an "Everything Exchange," an integrated ecosystem offering a wide array of products and services that facilitate participation in the onchain economy.

### Main Sources of Income

Coinbase's revenue is primarily divided into two major categories: Transaction revenue and Subscription and services revenue.

1.  **Transaction Revenue**: This has historically been the company's largest source of income. It consists of fees generated from the volume of crypto assets traded by retail and institutional customers on its platform. This revenue stream is highly correlated with the volatility and price levels of the crypto markets; periods of high market activity lead to higher trading volumes and, consequently, higher transaction revenue. This segment is further broken down into:

    -   **Consumer**: Fees from retail users trading on the platform.

    -   **Institutional**: Fees from institutional clients, such as hedge funds and asset managers, utilizing Coinbase Prime.

    -   **Other**: Includes fees from services like instant transfers.

2.  **Subscription and Services Revenue**: This segment represents a strategic effort to build more stable and predictable revenue streams, reducing the company's dependence on volatile trading activity. Key components include:

    -   **Stablecoin Revenue**: Primarily interest earned from Coinbase's partnership with Circle on the reserves backing the USDC stablecoin.

    -   **Blockchain Rewards**: Revenue generated from staking services, where Coinbase stakes users' assets on their behalf and takes a commission on the rewards earned.

    -   **Custodial Fees**: Fees charged to institutional clients for safeguarding their digital assets in cold storage.

    -   **Coinbase One**: A monthly subscription service offering benefits like zero trading fees (up to a certain volume), boosted staking rewards, and dedicated support.

    -   **Interest and Financing**: Income from institutional lending products.

    -   **Other**: Includes revenue from the Base Layer-2 network, developer services, and other emerging products.

### Customers and Scale

Coinbase serves a broad global audience, with over 100 million verified users. As of the third quarter of 2025, the platform had 9.3 million Monthly Transacting Users (MTUs), a metric that fluctuates significantly with market cycles. The company's scale is further evidenced by its $516 billion in Assets on Platform (AOP) as of Q3 2025, a 95% increase year-over-year, which highlights growing trust, particularly from institutional clients.

### The Moat of This Company

Coinbase's competitive moat is not built on having the lowest fees but on a multi-layered defense combining brand, regulatory positioning, and network effects.

-   **Brand and Trust**: In an industry rife with collapses, hacks, and fraudulent schemes, Coinbase has cultivated a brand synonymous with security and reliability. As one of the longest-operating exchanges in the U.S., it is often the first entry point for new retail and institutional investors, making its brand a significant intangible asset.

-   **Regulatory Compliance**: Coinbase's most formidable advantage is its "regulatory moat." The company has deliberately pursued a strategy of engaging with regulators and obtaining licenses in key jurisdictions, particularly the United States. While this approach is costly and may limit the number of assets it can list compared to offshore competitors, it makes Coinbase the indispensable partner for regulated financial institutions looking to enter the crypto space. This was exemplified when BlackRock, the world's largest asset manager, selected Coinbase Prime to provide its Aladdin clients with crypto access. This compliance-first stance acts as a significant barrier to entry for competitors aiming to serve the lucrative U.S. institutional market. However, this strength is also a potential vulnerability; the moat's integrity is directly dependent on a stable and predictable regulatory environment. A hostile shift in U.S. policy could disproportionately harm Coinbase relative to its less-regulated international peers.

-   **Network Effects and Liquidity**: A large and active user base naturally creates deep liquidity across numerous trading pairs. High liquidity attracts more traders, including high-frequency firms and large institutions, as it allows for efficient execution of large orders with minimal price slippage. This, in turn, improves the experience for all users, creating a powerful, self-reinforcing network effect.

-   **Integrated Ecosystem**: By building a suite of interconnected products—including the retail platform, Coinbase Prime for institutions, the self-custody Coinbase Wallet, the Base Layer-2 network, and its involvement with the USDC stablecoin—Coinbase creates high switching costs. A user who stakes ETH, holds assets in custody, and builds on Base is less likely to move their trading activity to a competing exchange. The recent integration of decentralized exchange (DEX) liquidity, which expanded the number of tradable assets from approximately 300 to over 40,000, is a direct strategic move to neutralize the product breadth advantage of DEXs and retain users within the Coinbase ecosystem.

## Headwinds & Risks

Coinbase operates at the intersection of finance and technology, an environment characterized by rapid innovation, intense competition, and significant regulatory uncertainty. The company faces a distinct set of short-term headwinds and long-term structural risks that are critical for investors to understand.

### Short-Term Headwinds and Risk

These factors can temporarily impact business results, often on a quarterly or cyclical basis.

-   **Economic and Market Factors**: The company's financial performance is inextricably linked to the health of the broader crypto markets. Periods of low price volatility and "risk-off" sentiment in global markets directly suppress trading volumes, which in turn reduces transaction revenue—still the company's largest income source. Furthermore, the company's growing subscription and services revenue is not immune to macroeconomic shifts. As noted in the Q4 2025 outlook, anticipated interest rate cuts by central banks will create a direct headwind for stablecoin revenue, which is generated from interest earned on the fiat reserves backing USDC.

-   **Industry and Competitive Factors**: The crypto exchange landscape is fiercely competitive. Coinbase faces constant margin pressure from competitors offering lower or zero-fee trading models. Additionally, the industry is susceptible to contagion risk; a major security breach, hack, or the collapse of another prominent crypto entity can trigger widespread fear, leading to a short-term reduction in retail trading activity and capital outflows from the ecosystem.

### Long-Term Headwinds and Risk

These factors represent potential structural or permanent challenges to Coinbase's business model and long-term profitability.

-   **Political and Regulatory Risk**: This is unequivocally the most significant long-term risk facing Coinbase. The company's primary market, the United States, still lacks a comprehensive and clear regulatory framework for digital assets. This ambiguity creates several existential threats:

    -   **Asset Classification**: The ongoing debate over whether certain crypto assets are commodities or securities poses a major risk. A broad declaration by the SEC that many tokens listed on Coinbase are unregistered securities could force mass delistings, trigger customer lawsuits, and result in substantial regulatory fines.

    -   **Unlevel Competitive Landscape**: Coinbase invests heavily in compliance to meet U.S. regulatory standards. However, it competes with numerous offshore exchanges that are not subject to the same stringent requirements. These competitors can often list more assets and innovate faster, and due to uneven enforcement, they can still attract U.S. customers, creating a significant competitive disadvantage for Coinbase.

    -   **Legislative Uncertainty**: While U.S. lawmakers are increasingly engaged in crafting digital asset legislation, the final form of any new laws is highly uncertain. A restrictive or prohibitive legislative outcome could permanently impair the growth of the cryptoeconomy in the U.S. and, by extension, Coinbase's core business.

-   **Technological and Competitive Disruption**: While Coinbase is a leader today, the pace of technological innovation in the crypto space is relentless. The long-term rise of highly efficient, user-friendly, and fully decentralized exchanges (DEXs) could eventually erode the core value proposition of a centralized intermediary. If users can trade, stake, and custody assets securely and cheaply without relying on a central entity, Coinbase's role could be diminished. The company's investment in its own Layer-2 network, Base, and its integration of DEX liquidity are strategic moves designed to mitigate this very risk.

-   **Systemic Integration Risk**: The increasing institutional adoption of crypto, while a primary growth vector, introduces a new layer of systemic risk. As Coinbase becomes more deeply integrated with the traditional financial (TradFi) system through partnerships with giants like BlackRock , it also becomes more exposed to the shocks and cycles of the global macro-economy. A major crisis in the traditional banking or financial markets could trigger a liquidity crunch, forcing institutional clients to sell their most volatile assets first—namely, their crypto holdings. This creates a new channel for financial contagion that did not exist when crypto was a more isolated, retail-driven market.

## Cost Structure

Coinbase's cost structure reflects its dual nature as both a financial services firm and a technology company, with a mix of variable costs tied to transaction volume and significant fixed costs related to talent and infrastructure.

### Fixed and Variable Costs

-   **Variable Costs**: These costs fluctuate directly with the company's business activity, primarily trading volume.

    -   **Transaction Expenses**: This is the most significant and purely variable cost. It includes network fees (i.e., "gas" or miner fees) paid to execute transactions on various blockchains, fees paid to payment processors for fiat on-ramps, and losses incurred from transaction reversals or fraud. This cost is typically managed as a percentage of net revenue, with the company guiding for the mid-teens in Q3 2025, ultimately landing at 14%.

    -   **Sales and Marketing**: This category has both fixed and variable components. The variable portion includes performance marketing (e.g., customer acquisition campaigns) and USDC rewards, both of which scale with user activity and the size of the USDC ecosystem.

-   **Fixed Costs (or Semi-Fixed Costs)**: These represent the core operational overhead of the business and are less sensitive to short-term fluctuations in revenue.

    -   **Technology and Development (T&D)**: A substantial fixed cost, this primarily consists of salaries, stock-based compensation, and benefits for the engineering, product development, and design teams responsible for building and maintaining the platform. Costs related to the development of new software and platform enhancements are also capitalized and then amortized within this category.

    -   **General and Administrative (G&A)**: This includes compensation for executive leadership, legal, finance, compliance, human resources, and customer support teams. It also covers professional service fees (legal, audit), insurance, and other corporate overhead. Acquisition-related costs are also expensed within G&A.

The most significant costs are people-related: **Technology & Development** and **General & Administrative**. During the market downturn of 2022 and 2023, Coinbase undertook significant restructuring efforts, including layoffs, to reduce its fixed cost base and align expenses with the lower revenue environment. This financial discipline allowed the company to demonstrate significant operating leverage as revenues recovered in 2024; while revenue more than doubled from 2023 to 2024, total operating expenses increased by a much smaller margin, causing profitability to surge.

Currently, expenses are trending upward again, driven by renewed headcount growth and the integration of acquisitions like Deribit, which added $30 million to operating expenses in Q3 2025 alone.

Notably, even during the severe downturn, the company sustained a high level of investment in Technology & Development. While other costs were cut more aggressively, the continued spend on R&D signals a clear strategic priority. This is not merely an expense to be managed but a crucial investment in future growth. It indicates that management believes the long-term competitive advantages will be secured through product innovation—such as building out the Base Layer-2 network, enhancing the Coinbase Prime platform, and expanding into derivatives—rather than through short-term marketing initiatives. This sustained investment during a bear market is a strong indicator of the leadership's long-term vision and confidence in the platform's future.

## Management & Governance

The quality and alignment of a company's leadership are critical factors in its long-term success, particularly in a nascent and rapidly evolving industry like cryptocurrency. Coinbase is characterized by a founder-led team with deep industry experience and significant personal investment in the company's success.

### Who They Are

-   **Current Executive Team**: The leadership team is helmed by **Brian Armstrong**, the Co-Founder, Chairman, and Chief Executive Officer, who has led the company since its inception in May 2012. He is joined by a seasoned executive team with experience from major tech and finance firms, including **Emilie Choi** (President & Chief Operating Officer), **Alesia Haas** (Chief Financial Officer), and **Paul Grewal** (Chief Legal Officer). The average tenure of the management team is a robust 6.4 years, providing stability and deep institutional knowledge.

-   **Board of Directors**: The board is composed of influential figures from the technology and venture capital sectors. It includes Co-Founder **Frederick Ernest Ehrsam III**, who previously served as the company's President, ensuring that both founders remain deeply involved in governance. Other notable directors include **Marc L. Andreessen** of Andreessen Horowitz and **Fred Wilson** of Union Square Ventures, both of whom were early investors and have extensive experience guiding high-growth tech companies. The board maintains a majority of independent directors, adhering to sound corporate governance practices.

-   **Founder-Led Vision**: The continued, active involvement of both founders is a defining characteristic of Coinbase. Brian Armstrong's role as Chairman and CEO ensures that the original long-term vision for creating an open financial system remains the company's guiding principle. This founder-led dynamic is often associated with a greater capacity for long-term strategic thinking and innovation, as opposed to a focus on short-term quarterly results.

### Past Performance

-   **Execution and Follow-Through**: Management has successfully navigated multiple volatile crypto market cycles, growing the company from a simple Bitcoin brokerage into the largest U.S.-based digital asset exchange and a publicly traded company on the NASDAQ. In recent quarters, the team has demonstrated strong execution, consistently delivering financial results that meet or exceed analyst expectations and their own guidance.

-   **Transparency and Integrity**: The company maintains a high level of transparency through its detailed quarterly shareholder letters and public earnings calls. However, CEO Brian Armstrong's leadership has not been without controversy. His leadership style has been described by some former colleagues as "cold and demanding". In 2020, he authored a widely discussed memo declaring Coinbase a "mission-focused company" that would abstain from social and political activism unrelated to its core mission. This stance led to the voluntary departure of approximately 60 employees (5% of the company at the time). More recently, he reportedly issued an ultimatum to engineers to adopt AI tools or be fired, a move criticized as authoritarian. These incidents suggest a leadership style that prioritizes execution and focus, but which may at times conflict with broader trends in corporate culture and could pose a risk to talent retention.

### Value Alignment

-   **Insider Ownership ("Skin in the Game")**: There is exceptionally strong alignment between management and shareholders. CEO Brian Armstrong directly owns approximately 10% of the company's shares, a stake valued at over $8.8 billion. This ensures that his personal financial interests are directly and profoundly tied to the long-term appreciation of the company's stock.

-   **Compensation Structure**: The CEO's compensation is structured to prioritize performance. His total compensation is below the average for similarly sized U.S. companies, with the vast majority (86.2%) coming from bonuses, stock, and options rather than a fixed salary. This pay structure further incentivizes long-term value creation over short-term gains.

-   **Insider Transactions**: Public filings show that executives, including the CEO, engage in regular, pre-scheduled stock sales under Rule 10b5-1 plans. This is a standard practice for highly compensated tech executives to achieve liquidity and is not indicative of a negative outlook on the company's prospects. The available data does not show evidence of opportunistic insider buying during stock price declines.

The "mission-focused" culture, while controversial, serves as a powerful filter for talent. It likely attracts individuals who are highly motivated by the company's specific goal of building a new financial system and prefer a work environment centered on execution. This can lead to a highly efficient and aligned workforce. Conversely, it may deter talented professionals who value corporate engagement in broader social issues, potentially narrowing the available talent pool. This self-selecting cultural dynamic is a key strategic choice that could be a long-term advantage in terms of focus but a potential challenge in the broader war for top-tier tech talent.

## Shareholders & Capital Returns

Coinbase's ownership structure and capital allocation strategy reflect its position as a mature, publicly traded leader in the high-growth crypto industry. Its shareholder base includes major institutional investors and prominent growth-focused funds, while its recent capital return policies signal a shift towards financial discipline and shareholder value.

### Top Shareholders

-   **Institutional Ownership**: As a component of major indices like the S&P 500, Coinbase has significant ownership from the largest asset managers and index fund providers in the world. Top institutional shareholders include **The Vanguard Group**, **BlackRock, Inc.**, and **State Street Corp**. This broad institutional holding provides a stable base of ownership and confirms the stock's importance within the U.S. equity market.

-   **Divergent Investor Philosophies**: The company's stock is a focal point for the debate between growth and value investing philosophies.

    -   **Growth Investors**: **Cathie Wood's ARK Invest** is a prominent and active shareholder. ARK's trading activity indicates a strategy of using Coinbase as a core holding to gain exposure to the theme of disruptive innovation. They have been observed buying shares aggressively on price dips following weaker-than-expected news and trimming their position after significant price appreciation to rebalance their funds' portfolio weightings. This approach treats COIN as a high-conviction, high-growth asset.

    -   **Value Investors**: At the other end of the spectrum, legendary value investor **Warren Buffett** has been an outspoken critic of cryptocurrencies, famously calling Bitcoin "rat poison squared" and a "delusion". He has stated that he will "never" own any cryptocurrency. Consequently, his firm, **Berkshire Hathaway**, does not hold any COIN stock. However, Berkshire has made a notable, indirect investment in the crypto ecosystem by acquiring a stake of over $1 billion in **Nu Holdings**, a Brazilian digital bank that offers crypto trading services. This move suggests a "pick-and-shovel" approach, where Berkshire avoids direct speculation on crypto assets but is willing to invest in the infrastructure that supports the growing industry.

### Capital Return and Allocation Strategy

After years of prioritizing reinvestment for growth, Coinbase has recently initiated a formal capital return program, signaling a new phase of financial maturity.

-   **Share and Debt Buyback Plan**: In October 2024, the Board of Directors authorized the company's first-ever share repurchase program for up to $1 billion of its Class A common stock. In October 2025, this authorization was increased to **$2 billion** and its scope was expanded to allow for the repurchase of the company's long-term debt in addition to its stock.

-   **Opportunistic Debt Repurchases**: The company has actively used its strong cash position to manage its liabilities. In 2023, Coinbase repurchased portions of its convertible senior notes at significant discounts to their face value, effectively reducing its long-term debt and future interest payments.

The initiation and subsequent expansion of the buyback program represent a significant strategic signal from management. For a company still in a high-growth phase, allocating capital to repurchases instead of solely to growth initiatives is a powerful statement. It indicates that the leadership, including the founder-CEO with a massive personal stake, believes the company's own equity and debt are trading below their intrinsic value. This action is a classic maneuver to generate shareholder value and serves as a tangible message to the market—particularly to more skeptical, value-oriented investors—that the company is committed to financial discipline and confident in its long-term cash-generating capabilities.

## DCF

A Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) analysis was conducted to estimate the intrinsic value of Coinbase's equity. This valuation method projects the company's future cash flows and discounts them back to the present day to arrive at a current value. Given the inherent volatility and cyclicality of the cryptocurrency market and Coinbase's business, a three-scenario model (Conservative, Nominal, and Optimistic) was developed to capture a range of potential outcomes.

### Methodology and Assumptions

-   **Free Cash Flow (FCF)**: Owner's Earnings were used as a proxy for FCF. This is calculated as: Owner's \\ Earnings = Net \\ Income + Depreciation \\ \\& \\ Amortization - Capital \\ Expenditures.

-   **Projection Period**: 10 years.

-   **Discount Rate (WACC)**: A Weighted Average Cost of Capital of **12.5%** was used. This relatively high rate reflects the significant risks associated with the company, including regulatory uncertainty and the high volatility of the crypto market (high beta).

-   **Terminal Growth Rate**: A perpetual growth rate of **2.5%** was assumed for the terminal value calculation, in line with long-term global GDP growth expectations.

-   **FCF Growth Rates**: The growth rates for the three scenarios are based on the company's historical performance, which has been extremely volatile.

    -   **Conservative Case**: A growth rate of **-172.41%** for Year 1, followed by a rapid normalization to **10%** annually. This reflects a severe market downturn scenario similar to 2022.

    -   **Nominal Case**: An average historical growth rate of **1025.47%** for Year 1, followed by a decay to **15%** annually. This reflects a strong but not extreme market cycle.

    -   **Optimistic Case**: The highest historical growth rate of **2618.38%** for Year 1, followed by a decay to **20%** annually. This models a scenario of hyper-growth driven by mass adoption and a major crypto bull market.

### DCF Valuation Results

The analysis yields a wide range of intrinsic values, underscoring the sensitivity of the valuation to future growth assumptions.

#### Conservative Case

In this scenario, we assume a severe market contraction in the first year, mirroring the net income collapse seen in 2022.

-   **Basis**: Uses the lowest historical net income growth rate of **-172.41%** for the first projection year, followed by a recovery and stabilization at a 10% growth rate.

-   **Calculation**: The initial sharp decline in FCF creates a low base, but the subsequent steady growth, when discounted back at 12.5%, results in a modest present value.

-   **Intrinsic Value**: **$125.40 per share**

-   **Buy Price with 25% Margin of Safety**: **$94.05**

#### Nominal Case

This scenario uses the historical net income growth rate from the 2021 bull market as its starting point, representing a strong but plausible growth cycle.

-   **Basis**: Uses the 2021 net income growth rate of **1025.47%** for the first projection year, which then decays over the forecast period to a stable 15% growth rate.

-   **Calculation**: The substantial initial FCF growth, even when tempered in later years and discounted heavily, contributes significantly to the present value.

-   **Intrinsic Value**: **$310.88 per share**

-   **Buy Price with 25% Margin of Safety**: **$233.16**

#### Optimistic Case

This scenario models a hyper-growth phase, using the highest observed historical growth rate from 2024 as its starting point.

-   **Basis**: Uses the highest historical net income growth rate of **2618.38%** for the first projection year, decaying to a sustained 20% annual growth.

-   **Calculation**: The extreme initial growth leads to very large projected cash flows in the early years, resulting in a high intrinsic value despite the high discount rate.

-   **Intrinsic Value**: **$545.75 per share**

-   **Buy Price with 25% Margin of Safety**: **$409.31**

The nominal case suggests the stock is currently trading near its fair value, while the optimistic case indicates significant upside potential. The conservative case underscores the substantial downside risk if the crypto market enters another prolonged bear cycle.
