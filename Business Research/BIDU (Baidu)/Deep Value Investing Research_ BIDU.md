# Deep Value Equity Research: Baidu, Inc. (NASDAQ: BIDU)

## 1. Executive Summary: The Asymmetric Dislocation

Baidu, Inc. (NASDAQ: BIDU; HKEX: 9888) presents one of the most enigmatic and potentially asymmetric risk-reward profiles in the global technology sector today. Once monolithically defined as "China's Google," the company has spent the last decade in a painful, capital-intensive metamorphosis, pivoting from a pure-play search engine to a diversified artificial intelligence ecosystem. This transition is occurring against a backdrop of extreme geopolitical friction, domestic macroeconomic deflation, and a profound crisis of confidence among global investors regarding Chinese equities.

Currently trading at valuation multiples that imply terminal stagnation—approximately 10x forward earnings and hovering near book value—Baidu is priced as a legacy asset in secular decline.<sup>1</sup> The market’s pricing mechanism appears to be efficiently discounting the deterioration of the core advertising business, which contracted by 18% year-over-year in the third quarter of 2025.<sup>3</sup> However, this bearish consensus largely assigns zero or negative value to the company’s burgeoning non-search assets, specifically the AI Cloud business and the Apollo Go autonomous driving unit, both of which are exiting their gestational investment phases and entering periods of scalable monetization.<sup>3</sup>

The investment thesis for Baidu is not predicated on a resurgence of the legacy search monopoly, but rather on the mispricing of its optionality in Generative AI (GenAI) and autonomous mobility. With Apollo Go achieving unit-level profitability in key markets like Wuhan and scaling to over 3.1 million driverless rides per quarter <sup>3</sup>, and the AI Cloud segment growing at double-digit rates driven by enterprise GenAI adoption <sup>5</sup>, the intrinsic value of Baidu's "non-search" assets is obscured by the conglomerate discount and the headline noise of accounting adjustments.

This research report executes a rigorous deep value examination of Baidu. It disaggregates the conglomerate’s financials to isolate the performance of emerging growth engines, scrutinizes the validity of the "value trap" narrative, analyzes the forensic accounting implications of recent asset impairments highlighted by investors like Michael Burry, and models the long-term cash flow potential of its autonomous driving fleet in a bifurcated semiconductor world.

## 2. Strategic Pivot and Business Model Evolution

### 2.1 The Erosion of the Search Monopoly

Founded in 2000, Baidu established its initial dominance through internet search, effectively capturing the Chinese market following Google's exit. For nearly two decades, its business model was predicated on the high-margin, capital-light mechanics of auction-based online marketing (P4P).<sup>6</sup> However, the mobile internet era introduced fierce competition from "super apps" like WeChat (Tencent) and algorithmic feed platforms like Douyin (ByteDance), eroding Baidu's monopoly on user attention and advertising budgets.<sup>7</sup>

The decline in the core search business is structural, not merely cyclical. In the third quarter of 2025, Baidu Core’s online marketing revenue plummeted 18% year-over-year to RMB 15.3 billion ($2.16 billion).<sup>3</sup> This contraction reflects a fundamental shift in advertiser preference toward closed-loop ecosystems that offer higher conversion rates for e-commerce, primarily ByteDance's platforms. Baidu’s traditional inventory—search results—is being cannibalized by its own transition to AI-generated answers, which reduce ad load in the short term to preserve user experience.<sup>8</sup>

### 2.2 The "All in AI" Capital Reallocation

Recognizing the saturation of the search market, Baidu initiated a strategic pivot dubbed "All in AI." This was not merely a branding exercise but a fundamental capital reallocation strategy that has consumed billions in free cash flow over the last decade. The company invested heavily in full-stack AI capabilities, ranging from chip design (Kunlun) and deep learning frameworks (PaddlePaddle) to foundation models (ERNIE) and application layers (Search, Autonomous Driving).<sup>9</sup>

The result is a bifurcated revenue structure within Baidu Core:

1.  **Legacy Ads:** A declining cash cow used to fund innovation.

2.  **AI Growth Engines:** Including AI Cloud and Apollo Go, which grew 21% year-over-year in Q3 2025, reaching RMB 9.3 billion.<sup>3</sup>

This "Non-Online Marketing" segment now accounts for nearly 38% of Baidu Core’s revenue, up significantly from previous years. The structural thesis for Baidu rests entirely on whether this non-advertising revenue can scale fast enough to offset the decay in the advertising business. The introduction of the "AI-Native" reporting view in late 2025—disclosing that AI-powered businesses generated roughly RMB 10 billion in revenue in Q3 2025, growing over 50% YoY—provides the first tangible metric to value this growth wedge separately.<sup>10</sup>

## 3. Financial Forensic Analysis

A granular analysis of Baidu's financial performance reveals a company struggling to decouple its future growth from its legacy decay, complicated by aggressive accounting maneuvers that have drawn the attention of short sellers.

### 3.1 Revenue Stagnation and Mix Shift

Historically, Baidu was a growth compounder. From 2015 to 2018, revenue surged from $10.5 billion to $15.3 billion.<sup>11</sup> However, the period from 2019 to 2024 shows marked stagnation, with revenue oscillating between $15.5 billion and $19 billion. The most recent data from Q3 2025 shows total revenues declining 7% YoY to RMB 31.2 billion ($4.38 billion).<sup>3</sup>

The divergence between the two core segments is stark:

| **Segment**              | **Q3 2025 Revenue (RMB Billions)** | **YoY Growth** | **Driver**                                                          |
|--------------------------|------------------------------------|----------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Online Marketing**     | 15.3                               | -18%           | Macro weakness, competition from ByteDance, AI transition friction. |
| **Non-Online Marketing** | 9.3                                | +21%           | AI Cloud adoption, Apollo Go scaling.                               |
| **iQIYI**                | 6.7                                | -8%            | Content cost rationalization, subscriber fatigue.                   |

This mix shift has profound implications for margins. Online marketing is historically a high-margin business (60%+ gross margins), while AI Cloud and hardware-heavy autonomous driving initiatives carry lower structural margins in their early scaling phases.

### 3.2 The "Michael Burry" Critique: Depreciation and Impairment

Baidu’s profitability profile has been a subject of intense scrutiny, particularly regarding its accounting for long-lived assets. In 2024, Baidu extended the estimated useful life of its servers from 5 to 6 years. This accounting policy change artificially boosted net income by lowering depreciation expenses, contributing to a 50% rise in net income in 2024 despite lackluster top-line growth.<sup>12</sup>

Investors like Michael Burry have flagged this as a potential red flag, suggesting quality of earnings deterioration. These concerns were validated in Q3 2025 when Baidu reported a staggering operating loss of RMB 15.1 billion, driven primarily by a non-cash impairment charge of RMB 16.2 billion ($2.27 billion) related to long-lived assets.<sup>14</sup>

Management attributed this write-down to a "proactive" retirement of legacy compute infrastructure that no longer meets the efficiency requirements for training advanced Generative AI models.<sup>13</sup> While management frames this as a modernization effort, forensic analysis suggests it is a "catch-up" recognition of the obsolescence that the 2024 accounting change sought to delay. The rapid evolution of AI chips—from older GPUs to newer architectures required for Large Language Models (LLMs)—rendered older server farms economically unviable faster than the accounting schedules anticipated.

**Deep Value Implication:** For the value investor, this impairment is a "kitchen-sinking" event. While it destroys quarterly GAAP earnings, it cleans up the balance sheet and aligns the asset base with the reality of the AI compute cycle. Adjusted EBITDA, which excludes such non-cash charges, remained positive at RMB 8.7 billion ($1.24 billion) with a 26% margin <sup>3</sup>, indicating that the underlying cash-generating capability of the business remains intact despite the accounting noise. The stock's reaction—trading down to near book value—suggests the market has fully priced in this accounting correction.

### 3.3 Balance Sheet Strength and Capital Allocation

Despite the operating loss, Baidu maintains a fortress balance sheet, a hallmark of a classic value investment. As of September 30, 2025, the company held RMB 144.5 billion ($20.59 billion) in cash, cash equivalents, and short-term investments.<sup>15</sup>

This liquidity provides a massive buffer against operational volatility and funds aggressive shareholder returns. The company has repurchased approximately $2.3 billion in shares under its 2023 program.<sup>16</sup> With a market capitalization hovering around $40 billion, the cash position represents roughly 50% of the company's equity value. This implies that the market is assigning an enterprise value of only \~$20 billion to a business generating over $25 billion in annual revenue with leading positions in AI and autonomous driving.

However, free cash flow (FCF) has been volatile due to heavy CAPEX cycles associated with AI infrastructure build-outs. In Q3 2025, FCF excluding iQIYI was RMB 2.4 billion ($342 million).<sup>15</sup> While lower than historical peaks due to the AI investment cycle, the business remains FCF positive. The "value" argument hinges on the belief that this CAPEX is not maintenance spend but growth investment that will yield high returns on invested capital (ROIC) as the AI businesses mature.

## 4. The AI Cloud Engine: Infrastructure as the New Moat

Baidu's pivot to AI is most visibly monetized through its AI Cloud division. Unlike Alibaba or Tencent, which focus on general-purpose cloud computing, Baidu has positioned itself as the specialized provider for "AI computing."

### 4.1 Growth Amidst Market Saturation

The broader Chinese cloud market has engaged in a "race to the bottom" price war—initiated by Alibaba and matched by Tencent and ByteDance—eroding margins for commodity IaaS (Infrastructure as a Service). Baidu has largely sidestepped this commoditization by focusing on the PaaS (Platform as a Service) and MaaS (Model as a Service) layers.

In Q3 2025, AI Cloud revenue grew 21% YoY to RMB 6.2 billion.<sup>8</sup> More impressively, the AI Cloud Infrastructure sub-segment grew 33%, with subscription-based revenue for AI accelerators surging 128%.<sup>3</sup> This growth outpaces the general cloud market and validates Baidu's differentiation strategy. Enterprises are not coming to Baidu for commodity storage or compute; they are coming for the **Qianfan** platform—a development environment that allows companies to build and fine-tune applications on top of the ERNIE foundation models.<sup>7</sup>

### 4.2 The ERNIE Bot Ecosystem and API Economy

ERNIE Bot (Wenxin Yiyan) has established itself as a top-tier contender in China's "War of a Hundred Models." With over 200 million users and daily API calls exploding to 1.5 billion <sup>17</sup>, ERNIE has achieved critical mass.

**Second-Order Insight:** The high volume of API calls is a leading indicator of future cloud revenue stickiness. As enterprises integrate ERNIE into their workflows (e.g., customer service bots, code generation, document analysis), they become locked into Baidu's ecosystem. The switching costs for an enterprise that has fine-tuned a model on Baidu's proprietary data and infrastructure are significantly higher than for standard cloud hosting. This suggests that Baidu's cloud revenue quality is improving, shifting from one-off projects to recurring, high-margin consumption revenue.

Comparing the domestic landscape, while ByteDance's Doubao has gained significant traction in consumer mobile downloads <sup>19</sup>, Baidu retains an edge in complex enterprise deployments where data privacy, hybrid cloud integration, and industry-specific fine-tuning are paramount. Alibaba's Qwen is a formidable open-source competitor, but Baidu's integration of search data gives ERNIE a unique advantage in real-time information retrieval and hallucination reduction.<sup>9</sup>

## 5. Apollo Go: The Economics of Autonomous Mobility

Perhaps the most mispriced option embedded in Baidu's stock is Apollo Go, its autonomous ride-hailing service. For years, robotaxis were viewed as a cash-burning R&D project. In 2025, the narrative shifted fundamentally toward commercial viability.

### 5.1 Achieving Unit Economic Profitability

The pivotal moment came in mid-2025 when Baidu announced that Apollo Go had achieved unit-level profitability in Wuhan.<sup>4</sup> This is a watershed moment for the global AV industry, placing Baidu alongside or arguably ahead of Waymo in terms of commercial scaling economics. Unit profitability means that the revenue from a ride covers the direct costs: energy, vehicle depreciation, and remote safety supervision.

The path to profitability was paved by two critical levers:

1.  **Vehicle Cost Reduction:** The 6th generation robotaxi (RT6) costs just RMB 204,600 (\~$28,500) to manufacture.<sup>20</sup> This is a game-changer. Competitors' vehicles often cost upwards of $100,000. By vertically integrating the vehicle design and leveraging China's EV supply chain, Baidu has collapsed the depreciation cost per mile—the single biggest expense in the robotaxi P&L.

2.  **Safety Driver Removal:** Apollo Go has moved to 100% fully driverless operations in key cities. The ratio of remote safety officers to vehicles is expanding from 1:1 to 1:3 and targeting 1:10.<sup>22</sup> As this ratio scales, the labor cost per ride collapses, creating a software-like margin profile.

### 5.2 Scale and Growth Velocity

Apollo Go is not a pilot program; it is a commercial utility. In Q3 2025, it provided 3.1 million fully driverless rides, accelerating 212% YoY.<sup>3</sup> With a cumulative total surpassing 17 million rides, Baidu has amassed a data advantage that creates a virtuous cycle: more miles lead to better safety algorithms, which allow for lower supervision ratios, which lower costs, which allow for lower fares and higher volume.

The success in Wuhan serves as a "cookie-cutter" model for expansion. Baidu plans to deploy 1,000 RT6 vehicles in Wuhan alone by year-end 2025.<sup>20</sup> If Baidu can replicate the Wuhan unit economics across its 22+ city footprint, Apollo Go transitions from a valuation drag to a massive contributor of recurring cash flow. Furthermore, the global expansion into markets like Abu Dhabi and Switzerland <sup>3</sup> suggests Baidu aims to be a technology exporter, licensing its autonomous stack to global operators—a high-margin business model distinct from operating a heavy-asset fleet.

## 6. Semiconductor Sovereignty and Geopolitical Risk

The "elephant in the room" for any Chinese AI thesis is the U.S. export control regime on advanced semiconductors. NVIDIA's H100 and upcoming Blackwell chips are effectively banned. While NVIDIA offers degraded "China-only" chips (H20), the performance gap is widening, and recent reports suggest even the H20 may face future restrictions.<sup>24</sup>

### 6.1 The Kunlun Chip Defense

This geopolitical constraint paradoxically strengthens Baidu's competitive position relative to domestic peers that rely solely on stockpiled NVIDIA chips. Baidu has been developing its own AI accelerator, the **Kunlun** chip, for years.

The Kunlun II and upcoming Kunlun III chips are designed specifically for AI workloads. While they may not match the raw throughput of an NVIDIA H100 in isolation, they offer a sovereign, sanction-proof alternative for domestic clients.<sup>26</sup> Baidu has deployed over 30,000 third-generation Kunlun chips in production clusters. By designing the chip to be compatible with standard deep learning frameworks, Baidu mitigates the software lock-in of NVIDIA's CUDA.

### 6.2 Clustering and System-Level Performance

Baidu is countering the raw hardware disadvantage through system-level innovation. The company plans to cluster thousands of Kunlun chips (Tianchi systems) to achieve performance parity for inference tasks.<sup>26</sup> This strategy mirrors Huawei's Ascend approach but benefits from Baidu's deep integration with the software layer (PaddlePaddle and ERNIE).

**Insight:** In a scenario where U.S. sanctions tighten further to exclude H20 chips, Baidu’s vertical integration (Chip + Cloud + Model + App) becomes a survival moat. Competitors who rely on third-party clouds or foreign silicon may face existential disruptions, whereas Baidu controls its own destiny.

## 7. Corporate Governance and Culture

### 7.1 Ownership and Control

Baidu operates under a dual-class share structure. Class A shares (1 vote) are publicly traded, while Class B shares (10 votes) are held by insiders. Co-founder and CEO Robin Li holds approximately 18% of the equity but controls 59% of the voting power.<sup>27</sup> This concentration of power ensures stability in strategic direction—allowing for long-term bets like autonomous driving—but renders minority shareholders largely powerless to effect change or force a sale.

### 7.2 The "Wolf Culture" and "996" Reality

Baidu's corporate culture has been described as intense, aligning with the broader "996" (9 am to 9 pm, 6 days a week) work ethic prevalent in Chinese tech. The company explicitly embraces "Wolf Culture," prioritizing aggressive execution and resilience. However, this has led to controversies, such as the PR crisis involving a VP who made harsh comments about employee well-being ("I'm not your mother-in-law").<sup>29</sup>

While this intensity drives rapid product iteration (e.g., ERNIE Bot updates), it poses a risk to talent retention. In the global war for AI talent, researchers have options. If Baidu's culture is perceived as toxic compared to more flexible environments or startups, it risks losing the human capital essential for its AI leadership.

## 8. Macroeconomic and Regulatory Context

### 8.1 The Deflationary Headwind

China's economy is grappling with structural deceleration, characterized by weak consumer sentiment, high youth unemployment, and a real estate crisis. This deflationary environment directly impacts Baidu's core ad business, as SMEs (Small and Medium Enterprises)—Baidu's bread and butter—slash marketing budgets.<sup>7</sup> The government's recent stimulus measures (the "Beijing Put") aimed at stabilizing asset prices and boosting consumption have yet to fully translate into ad spend recovery.<sup>30</sup>

### 8.2 Regulatory Landscape

The "tech crackdown" of 2020-2023 has largely subsided, replaced by a more supportive stance as Beijing recognizes the strategic importance of AI in the geopolitical rivalry with the US. Baidu, as a national champion in AI and autonomous driving, aligns with the government's "New Productive Forces" initiative. Regulatory approvals for fully driverless robotaxi operations in major cities (Beijing, Wuhan, Shanghai) indicate strong state support for Baidu's strategic direction.<sup>3</sup>

However, data security and generative AI content regulations remain strict. Baidu must navigate the "black box" problem of LLMs to ensure generated content aligns with socialist core values, a compliance burden that adds cost and complexity relative to Western peers.

## 9. Valuation Analysis: Sum-of-the-Parts (SOTP)

To determine if BIDU is a deep value opportunity, we utilize a Sum-of-the-Parts (SOTP) framework to isolate the value of the hidden assets.

**Assumptions:**

-   **Search/Ads (Core):** Valued at a distressed 6x P/E on estimated $2.5B net income. This implies a business in permanent decline but generating cash.

-   **AI Cloud:** Valued at 3x Trailing Sales ($3.5B revenue). A discount to US peers (trading 5-8x) due to China risk, but premium for AI growth.

-   **Apollo Go:** Valued at $8 Billion. Conservative compared to Waymo's historical valuations ($30B+), acknowledging the China discount but recognizing unit profitability.

-   **iQIYI:** Valued at market cap (\~$2.2B).

-   **Net Cash/Investments:** $15 Billion (applying a 25% conglomerate discount to the \~$20B headline number).

The market is effectively assigning **negative value** to the AI investments or assuming the cash pile will be incinerated without return.

## 10. Risks and Counter-Thesis

A deep value thesis is incomplete without a rigorous examination of the risks that justify the cheap valuation.

-   **The "Value Trap" Risk:** If the 18% decline in ad revenue accelerates or fails to stabilize, the cash engine funding the AI transition breaks. Baidu could burn through its cash pile trying to compete in AI without ever achieving the necessary scale to offset the ad loss.

-   **Technological Decoupling:** If U.S. sanctions tighten to include all forms of advanced compute (cutting off workarounds), Baidu's Kunlun chips may not be sufficient to train next-generation models (GPT-5 equivalent), widening the gap with US peers and making its AI ecosystem inferior.

-   **Capital Allocation Failure:** Despite the buybacks, Baidu has a history of questionable investments (e.g., YY acquisition attempt). If management pivots to another capital-intensive "shiny object" instead of returning cash, the value gap may never close.

## 11. Conclusion and Recommendation

Baidu represents a classic deep value opportunity: a high-quality asset base trading at a distressed price due to cyclical headwinds, structural fears, and complex accounting noise. The market sees a dying search engine; the data reveals a birthing AI giant.

The impairment charge in Q3 2025, while painful, marks a capitulation point that likely sets a floor for earnings quality going forward. With a cash-rich balance sheet, a profitable and scaling robotaxi business, and a resilient cloud division, Baidu offers a compelling margin of safety.

Recommendation: BUY.

The current valuation of \~10x P/E and \~1.0x P/B offers an asymmetric entry point. Downside is protected by the net cash position (approx. 50% of market cap) and the floor value of the search cash flow. Upside is levered to the successful commercialization of Apollo Go and the AI Cloud, which the market is currently pricing as free options.

Investors should monitor the stabilization of online marketing revenue in Q4 2025 and Q1 2026 as the primary signal that the turnaround is taking hold.
