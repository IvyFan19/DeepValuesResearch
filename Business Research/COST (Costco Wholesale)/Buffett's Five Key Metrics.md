​​Buffett's five key metrics are essential for evaluating a company's financial health and long-term investment potential. Here's what each metric means:

### **1. Return on Invested Capital (ROIC)**

-   **Definition:** Measures how efficiently a company generates profit from the capital invested in its business.

-   **Formula:
    > **ROIC=NetOperatingProfitAfterTaxes(NOPAT)InvestedCapital

-   **Why It Matters:**

    -   Buffett prefers companies with a high and consistent ROIC, as it indicates the company can generate substantial returns from its investments.

    -   A high ROIC relative to the cost of capital indicates the company is creating value for shareholders.

### **2. Top Line (Revenue)**

-   **Definition:** The total revenue generated from the company's core business activities before any expenses are deducted.

-   **Formula:
    > **Revenue=TotalSalesorIncome

-   **Why It Matters:**

    -   Buffett values companies with consistent and growing revenues, as this indicates strong demand for their products or services.

    -   Sustainable revenue growth is a sign of a durable competitive advantage.

### **3. Bottom Line (Net Profit or Net Income)**

-   **Definition:** The profit remaining after deducting all expenses, taxes, and costs from revenue. It appears as the final line on the income statement.

-   **Formula:
    > **NetIncome=TotalRevenue-TotalExpenses

-   **Why It Matters:**

    -   Buffett looks for companies that not only generate revenue but also translate it into solid, growing profits.

    -   The bottom line reflects operational efficiency and the company's ability to manage costs.

### **4. Equity (Shareholders' Equity or Book Value)**

-   **Definition:** The value of assets minus liabilities, representing the residual interest of shareholders in the company.

-   **Formula:
    > **Equity=TotalAssets-TotalLiabilities

-   **Why It Matters:**

    -   Buffett values companies that consistently increase shareholders' equity over time, indicating retained earnings are effectively reinvested.

    -   It shows the company's net worth and financial stability.

### **5. Free Cash Flow (FCF)**

-   **Definition:** Cash generated by the company after accounting for capital expenditures (CapEx), reflecting the ability to generate cash from operations.

-   **Formula:
    > **FCF=OperatingCashFlow-CapitalExpenditures

-   **Why It Matters:**

    -   Buffett favors companies with robust and growing FCF, as it indicates that the business generates enough cash to pay dividends, reinvest in the business, or reduce debt.

    -   FCF is essential for evaluating the company's ability to create shareholder value without relying on external funding.

-   ### **Free Cash Flow (FCF): In 1988, Coca-Cola's FCF yield was approximately 12.8%, with a three-year average FCF of about $1.1 billion. ([<u>Nasdaq</u>](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/coca-colas-valuation-warren-buffetts-1988-purchase-2013-04-07?utm_source=chatgpt.com))**

-   ### **ROIC: As of May 2025, Apple's ROIC stands at 47.18%, significantly higher than its 5-year average of 39.93%, indicating exceptional capital efficiency. ([<u>FinanceCharts</u>](https://www.financecharts.com/stocks/AAPL/value/roic?utm_source=chatgpt.com))**

-   ### **Net Income (Bottom Line): The company reported a net income of $97.3 billion, reflecting strong profitability. ([<u>www.alphaspread.com</u>](https://www.alphaspread.com/security/nasdaq/aapl/summary?utm_source=chatgpt.com))**

-   ### **Shareholders' Equity: Apple's equity has been bolstered by retained earnings and share repurchases, enhancing shareholder value.**

-   ### **Free Cash Flow (FCF): Apple generated approximately $98.5 billion in FCF, showcasing its ability to produce substantial cash after capital expenditures. ([<u>www.alphaspread.com</u>](https://www.alphaspread.com/security/nasdaq/aapl/summary?utm_source=chatgpt.com))**

### **Buffett viewed Apple not just as a tech company but as a consumer products firm with a loyal customer base and strong brand, aligning with his preference for companies with durable competitive advantages.([<u>Wikipedia</u>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkshire_Hathaway?utm_source=chatgpt.com))**

### **💻 IBM - Acquired in 2011**

### **In 2011, Buffett invested about $10.7 billion in IBM, acquiring a 5.5% stake. ([<u>CNN Money</u>](https://money.cnn.com/2011/11/14/news/companies/buffett_ibm/index.htm?iid=EAL&utm_source=chatgpt.com))**

-   ### **ROIC: IBM's ROIC was moderate, reflecting its challenges in transitioning to new business models.**

-   ### **Revenue (Top Line): The company faced declining revenues, with figures dropping from $92.7 billion in 2014 to $79.1 billion in 2017. ([<u>Wikipedia</u>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM?utm_source=chatgpt.com))**

-   ### **Net Income (Bottom Line): IBM's net income decreased from $12 billion in 2014 to $5.7 billion in 2017, indicating profitability challenges. ([<u>Wikipedia</u>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM?utm_source=chatgpt.com))**

-   ### **Shareholders' Equity: The company's equity position weakened over time, partly due to share buybacks and restructuring costs.**

-   ### **Free Cash Flow (FCF): In 2010, IBM generated $16.3 billion in FCF, but this declined in subsequent years as the company faced headwinds. ([<u>GuruFocus</u>](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/161230/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))**

### **Buffett eventually sold Berkshire's entire stake in IBM by 2018, acknowledging that the company's prospects did not align with his expectations.**
