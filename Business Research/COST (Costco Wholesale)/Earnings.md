### **1. Five ways a company measures what it really earns**

| **Metric**                    | **Where it sits on the financials**   | **How it's calculated**                                                             | **What it tells you**                                                           | **Common caveats**                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
|-------------------------------|---------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Gross profit**              | top of the income statement           | Revenue - Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)                                                 | Pricing power and direct‑cost efficiency                                        | Ignores R&D, marketing, overhead                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| **Operating profit (EBIT)**   | middle of the income statement        | Gross profit - operating expenses (SG&A, R&D, D&A, other operating items)           | Core profitability of the business model, before financing & taxes              | Still accrual‑based; excludes capital intensity                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| **Net earnings (Net income)** | "bottom line" of the income statement | Operating profit - interest ± other gains/losses - taxes                            | Ultimate accounting profit available to common shareholders                     | Includes many non‑cash items (goodwill impairments, fair‑value marks, etc.) ([<u>Investopedia</u>](https://www.investopedia.com/ask/answers/101314/what-are-differences-between-gross-profit-and-net-income.asp?utm_source=chatgpt.com)) |
| **Free cash flow (FCF)**      | cash‑flow statement                   | Operating cash flow - capital expenditures                                          | Real cash left for buybacks, dividends, debt service & M&A                      | Very volatile for working‑capital‑heavy firms; growth cap‑ex treated the same as maintenance                                                                                                                                             |
| **Owner's earnings**(Buffett) | economic concept, not GAAP            | Net income + non‑cash charges - *maintenance*cap‑ex ± working‑capital normalisation | Sustainable cash that could be taken out *without* hurting competitive position | Company must estimate "maintenance" cap‑ex-rarely disclosed; analysts usually approximate it with long‑run average cap‑ex                                                                                                                |

> **Key differences
> ***Accrual vs. cash*: Gross, operating and net profits are accrual‑accounting numbers; FCF and owner's earnings are cash‑based.
> *Scope*: Each step downward includes more kinds of costs, so the figures generally shrink (gross &gt; operating &gt; net &gt; owner's earnings).
> *Signal*:
> • Rising **gross** margins signal pricing power or lower COGS.
> • Expanding **operating** margins show cost discipline and scale.
> • Higher **net** margins reflect efficient financing & tax structure.
> • Strong, growing **FCF / owner's earnings** underpin dividends, buybacks and debt reduction.

### **3. Company‑by‑company pulse‑check**

-   **Microsoft** - Cloud & AI keep gross margin near an all‑time‑high 69 %. FCF covers \~84 % of net income-a very "clean" conversion, signalling ample room for buybacks and a rising dividend.

-   **Nvidia** - Every metric is in hyper‑growth mode: gross profit, operating profit and FCF have risen &gt;5× in two years, driven by AI datacentre GPUs. Margins above 75 % give it a cash‑machine profile unusual for chip makers.

-   **Apple** - Revenue is flat, yet FCF topped **$100 B** for the third straight year thanks to services mix and tight working‑capital cycles. Owner's earnings ≈ FCF because maintenance cap‑ex is a small slice of cash flow.

-   **Amazon** - AWS and advertising lifted gross profit to **$312 B** while head‑count and fulfilment cuts doubled operating margin. Positive FCF is back after the pandemic‑driven warehouse build‑out.

-   **Alphabet** - Operating income rebounded to **$112 B**; despite record AI cap‑ex, Alphabet still minted **$73 B** of FCF-evidence that the ad engine funds its "other bets."

-   **Meta** - Ruthless cost control pushed net margin to \~38 %, even as Reality Labs losses persist. FCF now runs ahead of cap‑ex by nearly **$20 B**.

-   **Broadcom** - VMware acquisition reset the P&L (one‑off charges), so net income dipped, but underlying FCF hit a record **$19 B** as AI networking chips take off.

-   **Tesla** - Price cuts to defend share sliced gross margins by \~11 ppt in two years; net income has halved and FCF fell below **$4 B**. High growth cap‑ex means owner's earnings are smaller still-an early‑cycle warning sign.

-   **Berkshire Hathaway** - Operating earnings, Buffett's preferred profit yard‑stick, set a record **$118 B**. Insurance float and higher rates helped; reported net swings with unrealised equity gains.

-   **JPMorgan** - Net income crossed **$57 B**, its best ever, as NIM widened. FCF is negative because bank cash‑flow statements treat deposit inflows/outflows as working capital-net income is the cleaner profitability gauge here.

### **4. Take‑aways for investors**

1.  **Gross‑to‑FCF "cascade"**: Companies that preserve a high share of gross profit all the way down to FCF (Microsoft, Nvidia, Apple) have the widest strategic optionality and lower need for outside capital.

2.  **Growth vs. durability**: Nvidia's 55 % net margin is exhilarating-but also the most cyclical. Apple's and Microsoft's steadier margins signal *durable competitive advantage*.

3.  **Watch margin direction**: Falling gross margins at Tesla and Broadcom foreshadow the profitability pressure you later see in net earnings and FCF.

4.  **Owner's earnings matter most when cap‑ex is heavy**: For Tesla or Amazon (logistics), subtracting *maintenance*cap‑ex gives a truer picture than raw FCF; for the software‑heavy giants, FCF already approximates owner's earnings.

5.  **Banks/insurers need special lenses**: Net interest margin (JPM) and underwriting plus investment income (Berkshire) are better "operating profit" proxies than the manufacturing‑style metrics used for tech and retail names.

**Bottom line:
**The *Magnificent Tech* names are not only the biggest in the S&P 500-they are also generating record cash after a fresh wave of AI‑driven cap‑ex. Nvidia leads in growth; Apple and Microsoft lead in sheer cash harvest. Amazon and Meta have repaired margins impressively, while Tesla shows how quickly those margins can compress in a volume‑driven industry. For long‑term, value‑oriented analysis, track **owner's earnings** alongside headline profits-the metric Buffett uses to judge whether today's record numbers really translate into wealth for shareholders.
