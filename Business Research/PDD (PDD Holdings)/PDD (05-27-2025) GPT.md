## **Business Model Overview**

PDD Holdings operates **Pinduoduo**, a leading Chinese e-commerce platform, and **Temu**, its fast-growing international marketplace. Pinduoduo's core model is **social commerce**: it leverages group-buying and social sharing (often via Tencent's WeChat) to offer deep discounts on a wide range of products, especially in agricultural produce and daily goods. This group-buying model encourages users to team up for bulk deals, driving viral growth in lower-tier cities with highly price-sensitive consumers. Pinduoduo generates revenue primarily through **online marketing services** (advertising and merchant promotion fees) and **transaction services** (commissions and payment fees) on its marketplace. In FY2024, these two segments contributed roughly equal portions of revenue - about 50.2% from online marketing & other services vs. 49.8% from transaction services - reflecting a shift from prior years when advertising was dominant. The platform's **customer base** has grown explosively: Pinduoduo reached \~882 million annual active buyers by 2023, making it one of China's largest e-commerce apps, second only to Alibaba in user count. These buyers are attracted by Pinduoduo's **ultra-low prices** and gamified shopping experience, which emphasize collective purchasing for discounts. On the international front, **Temu** (launched September 2022) follows a similar discount-driven marketplace model in overseas markets. Temu's growth has been astonishing - the app has amassed over **100 million** active users in the U.S. within a year and **250+ million global downloads in 2023**, consistently topping app store charts for shopping in the U.S. and abroad. Temu connects price-conscious consumers in North America, Europe, and other regions with Chinese manufacturers, cutting out middlemen to offer bargains (its tagline is "Shop Like a Billionaire"). In essence, PDD's business model monetizes *high-volume, low-margin* transactions by subsidizing prices to drive user engagement, then earning fees from merchants for advertising exposure and sales on the platform.

**Income Sources:** Pinduoduo's **main revenue streams** are (1) *online marketing services* - merchants pay for ad placements, product listings, and other traffic-boosting tools, which comprised roughly **RMB197.9 billion** ($27.1B) in 2024 revenue; and (2) *transaction services* - commission fees and payment service charges on each order, contributing **RMB195.9 billion** ($26.8B) in 2024. A small portion of revenue historically also came from **1P merchandise sales**(direct product sales by Pinduoduo itself), but this was a short-term initiative around 2019-2020 and is no longer material. **Figure: Revenue Breakdown 2024** - *Online marketing \~50%, Transaction fees \~50%*. *(Pie chart not shown)*. This balanced mix in 2024 represents a significant change from earlier years: for example, in 2022, online marketing was \~85% of revenue while transaction fees were \~15%. The rapid increase in transaction fee revenue (up +108% in 2024) reflects **surging order volume** and **international GMV** via Temu, whereas online ad revenue (up +29% in 2024) grew more moderately. Pinduoduo's scale is immense - in 2024 it facilitated *over 38 billion orders* (estimated from annual GMV growth) and served nearly **900 million active buyers**, with **14.2 million active merchants** on the platform by end of 2023. These users are not on subscriptions; instead, **merchant advertising spend** and **take-rate on transactions** drive PDD's income. The **value proposition** for merchants is access to Pinduoduo's huge user base and social viral reach, while consumers benefit from low prices (often subsidized). This volume-driven model resulted in FY2024 revenue of **$53.96 billion**, a 59% YoY increase, outpacing all major peers.

## **Short-Term & Long-Term Headwinds and Risks**

**Short-Term Headwinds:** PDD faces several near-term challenges. In China, **consumer spending recovery has been uneven**, hindered by a lingering property crisis and cautious post-pandemic sentiment. Even as Pinduoduo's value-for-money focus helped it outperform peers in 2022-2023, the broader economic headwinds are now catching up. Q1 2025 results underscored this: revenue grew just +10% YoY (to ¥95.67B) - the slowest growth since IPO - missing expectations. Management cited **"slower domestic consumption"** and an escalating **price war** in Chinese e-commerce as factors. Competitors like Alibaba and JD.com have aggressively slashed prices and introduced subsidies, forcing Pinduoduo to respond in kind to retain users. This intensified competition pressures **margins** and could erode Pinduoduo's unique edge in lower-tier cities as rivals encroach with similar discount strategies. At the same time, **global trade frictions** are a short-term risk for Temu. The U.S.-China tariff situation remains fluid; recently, new U.S. tariffs on low-value imports (de minimis shipments) were threatened, which could raise Temu's costs. Although a temporary 90-day reprieve was announced in May 2025, the uncertainty around tariffs and supply chain regulations has created "significant pressure" on Temu's merchants. Temu has relied on exploiting the duty-free threshold (&lt;$800 parcels) to keep prices ultra-low, so any tightening of that loophole (or broader U.S. protectionist moves) is a **short-term headwind** for international growth. Another immediate risk is **profitability pressure**: PDD's Q1 2025 net profit fell -47% YoY, with operating margin dropping sharply due to **higher marketing and fulfillment expenses** (management invested heavily in promotions and coupons to support merchants and fuel Temu's expansion). These elevated costs - part of a ¥100B merchant support initiative - are strategic for long-term ecosystem health but sacrifice near-term earnings, spooking some investors. In sum, **slowing growth**, **rising competition**, and **margin compression** form PDD's short-term storm.

**Long-Term Risks:** Over a longer horizon, PDD must navigate structural and regulatory risks. One key risk is **market saturation in China** - Pinduoduo already serves \~900 million buyers (nearly the entire addressable online shopping population). Future domestic growth will depend on increasing **average spend per user** (which lags Alibaba's) and expanding into new categories or services. If Chinese consumption remains sluggish or if user growth plateaus, Pinduoduo's high-growth story could permanently downshift. **Competitive dynamics** will likely remain challenging: Alibaba has reorganized and revitalized its Taobao/Tmall businesses (even launching a Taobao Deals app targeting Pinduoduo's demographic), and newer entrants (e.g. ByteDance's Douyin e-commerce) are capturing user attention. Prolonged price wars could structurally reduce profit margins for all players in the long run. Another long-term risk is **regulatory and political**. In China, the tech crackdown of 2020-2021 raised the specter of antitrust action - Pinduoduo thus far escaped heavy penalties (unlike Alibaba's $2.8B fine) but regulators may still scrutinize its agricultural supply chain practices or merchant subsidies. The government could also pressure PDD to invest in national priorities (as seen with PDD's "10 Billion Agriculture Initiative"), potentially at the expense of shareholder returns. Internationally, **geopolitical tensions** pose a risk to Temu: Western governments are increasingly wary of Chinese apps over data security and IP infringement concerns. In 2024, the EU placed Temu under its strict Digital Services Act scrutiny due to issues with counterfeit and unsafe products. Beyond policy, **reputation and trust** are long-term factors: Temu has seen controversies around product quality, worker conditions, and data privacy. On the financial side, **currency risk** (PDD's revenue is mostly in RMB, but Temu incurs costs globally) could impact reported results as the USD/CNY rate fluctuates. Lastly, **key-man risk** exists: although founder Colin Huang is no longer in management, his strategic vision shaped PDD - the current team must prove they can innovate beyond his leadership. Overall, while PDD's growth runway is significant (especially overseas), it faces long-term headwinds from **regulatory pressures**, **international trade barriers**, and the need to **balance growth with profitability**.

## **Cost Structure Analysis**

Pinduoduo's cost structure is characterized by **high variable costs** tied to its rapid growth and user acquisition strategy, whereas fixed costs (like G&A) are relatively low. The largest cost component by far is **sales and marketing (S&M)**expense, reflecting the company's heavy spending on promotions, coupons, and advertising to attract and retain users. In 2024, *sales & marketing expense was RMB111.3 billion (\~$15.25B)*, up +35% YoY. This amounted to **\~28% of revenue**in 2024, a considerable ratio but actually lower than in earlier years (for context, S&M was 69% of revenue in 2020 and over 100% of revenue in Pinduoduo's early years). Early on, Pinduoduo aggressively **subsidized group deals** and gave out cash incentives, resulting in S&M exceeding revenue (e.g. in 2018-2019, selling expenses grew 102%-150% annually, outpacing revenue). Over time, as user base and organic traffic grew, the S&M intensity has moderated - but it remains PDD's largest cost, given ongoing investments (e.g. Temu's marketing blitz, including a $21M Super Bowl ad in 2024). **Fulfillment and operational costs** are another significant variable cost. Pinduoduo, unlike Alibaba, does not run its own logistics network; however, it **bears costs for payment processing, cloud services, and merchant support**. In 2024, *cost of revenue* was RMB153.9B ($21.08B), up 68%, mainly due to higher fulfillment fees and payment processing charges as order volumes surged. These costs scale with transaction count - e.g. each order has a payment processing fee and often a subsidy to keep the price low, making them largely variable **per unit (per order)** expenses. **Labor and R&D**costs are also notable: PDD's *research & development expense* was RMB12.66B ($1.73B) in 2024, having grown consistently (78% growth in 2019, and still +16% in 2023) as the company invests in AI, agricultural tech, and platform improvements. R&D and **general & administrative** (G&A) are relatively fixed costs (infrastructure, headcount) - for instance, G&A was only RMB7.55B ($1.03B) in 2024, under 2% of revenue. Pinduoduo runs a lean corporate structure, with **management emphasizing frugality** (Colin Huang famously limited executive perks to align with value-for-money culture). Thus, **fixed overhead** is small compared to the massive variable spending on marketing. **Cost per user or per order:** While PDD doesn't disclose cost per order directly, we can infer that the **fulfillment & S&M cost per order** has been decreasing over time due to scale. For example, in 2019 PDD spent an average of \~$4 in S&M to acquire each active buyer (total S&M $3.8B for \~0.9B buyers) - by 2024, S&M per active buyer has likely dropped closer to \~$17 annually ($15.25B for \~0.9B users), indicating improved efficiency (though still high, as these include subsidies). **Trends:** Over 2015-2020, PDD's cost structure was marked by exploding marketing outlays, which drove large losses. From 2021 onward, there's been a clear pivot: PDD curtailed the growth of S&M spend (it rose +5.5% in 2021 vs +50% in 2020) even as revenue doubled, leading to its first profits. By 2022-2023, the company was highly profitable, indicating **scalability** - revenue grew \~84% in 2023 while S&M grew 35%, and operating margin expanded dramatically (from -15% in 2020 to +28% in 2023). However, with Temu's launch, PDD is again in investment mode: Q1 2025 saw operating expenses jump \~19% (mainly marketing) on just 10% revenue growth, compressing operating margin short-term. **Summary:** Pinduoduo's cost structure is predominantly variable, driven by volume - as it scales globally, maintaining cost discipline (especially on marketing subsidies) will be crucial. The company has signaled it will continue **investing heavily in its ecosystem and supply chain** (e.g. funding farmers, subsidizing merchants), so investors should expect marketing and fulfillment costs to remain a substantial portion of revenue in the near future.

## **Intrinsic Value Estimation (Warren Buffett Style)**

To estimate PDD's **intrinsic value** using a Buffett-style discounted cash flow (DCF) approach, we'll project **owner earnings** (approximated by free cash flow) and discount them at a required rate of return, applying a margin of safety. PDD's trailing 12-month free cash flow (FCF) is about **$16.57 billion** (FY2024), reflecting its robust profitability. We make conservative and moderate growth assumptions:

-   **Conservative Scenario:** Assume PDD's owner earnings grow at **15% annually** for the next 5 years (much lower than recent 50%+ growth, acknowledging a maturation or macro slowdown), then taper to a terminal growth of **2%**(below GDP growth, per Buffett's preference of &lt;3%). Using a **15% discount rate** (to demand a high return given China risk) and no margin of safety in the model, the intrinsic value per share comes out around **$140-$150**. Specifically, projecting FCF from \~$16.6B in 2024 to \~$33.3B in 2029, then terminal growth 2%, and discounting at 15% yields a present value of about **$144 per share**【82†】. Now apply a **margin of safety**: at 30% MOS, one would require a buy price 0.7× intrinsic (**$100**); at 50% MOS, \~**$72**. PDD's current market price (around $119) is about 17% below the \~$144 fair value estimate, implying roughly a **17% margin of safety** is already present in this conservative scenario. In other words, at $119 the stock offers an expected return slightly above 15% if it can maintain 15% earnings growth.

-   **Moderate Scenario:** Assume PDD can grow owner earnings faster in the near term - say **20-25% annually for 5 years** (which is plausible given Temu's ramp-up and monetization of its huge user base), before settling to a **3% terminal growth**. Using a lower discount rate of **10%** (Buffett often uses \~10% as a hurdle for established large caps) and 0% MOS, the intrinsic value skyrockets. Our model with \~20% growth for 5 years and 3% terminal, at 10% discount, suggests a value on the order of **$300-$350+ per share**【86†】. This reflects the power of compounding: PDD's FCF in 5 years could be \~$41B/year under such growth, and with a modest terminal multiple, the equity value is enormous. Even with a 50% margin of safety applied, this scenario would justify a price around $150-$175. The current price $119 would imply an **expected return** well above 10% - in fact, solving for the discount rate that equates the DCF value to $119 (with these growth assumptions) yields an **implied IRR of \~24%**【85†】. In essence, the market may be pricing in a lot of risk or a deceleration in growth, because if PDD achieves \~20%+ sustained growth, today's price offers potentially very high returns (a sign of market skepticism or a mispricing).

-   **Terminal Multiple Check:** Another Buffett-like sanity check is to estimate PDD's **owner earnings in 10 years**and apply a modest multiple. If PDD grows FCF even at 10% annually for 10 years, FCF would be \~$43B in 2034. A terminal multiple of 10× (with 0% growth implied) on that would give \~$430B enterprise value. Meanwhile, if one expects 15% growth for 10 years, the owner earnings in year 10 could be \~$67B; even an 8× multiple (stringent) yields \~$536B, which discounted gives a value far above today's price.

**Expected Return Estimation:** If we set **margin of safety to 0%** (i.e., assume current price = intrinsic value) and solve for the discount rate given realistic growth forecasts, we can infer the market's implied return. Using a moderate growth case (15% for 5 years, 3% terminal), we found the market price \~$119 implies \~**17.5% discount rate** (expected return)【88†】. If one uses a more optimistic growth assumption, the implied required return goes even higher (\~24% as noted under a 20% growth model). This suggests the market is demanding a very high risk premium for PDD - likely due to geopolitical and execution risks. For an investor requiring, say, a 12% return, PDD appears undervalued under most growth scenarios. If one required a 20% return, the current price would only be justified if growth falls to low double-digits quickly.

**Warren Buffett's criteria** (discount 10-15%, terminal g &lt;3%) - under those, PDD's intrinsic value range is broad but generally above the current price.
