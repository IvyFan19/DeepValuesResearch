## **Latest Quarter Earnings Summary**

PDD (Pinduoduo) reported solid Q2 2025 results. Total revenues were RMB103,985 million (\~$14.52B), up 7% year-over-year , slightly above forecasts. Revenue from **online marketing services (advertising)** grew 13%, reaching RMB55,703 million, while **transaction services** (commissions on sales) were RMB48,282 million (roughly flat) . Despite higher revenues, profitability was squeezed by rising costs: cost of revenues jumped 36% (to RMB45,859M) driven by higher fulfillment, logistics and server costs . Operating profit fell 21% YoY to RMB25,793M , and net income fell \~4% to RMB30,754M (non-GAAP operating profit and net income each fell \~21%). Diluted EPS (ADS) was RMB20.75 ($2.90) . Management attributed the margin drop to aggressive merchant support and marketing investments ("ecosystem subsidies") aimed at long-term growth . In short, PDD beat revenue estimates but showed compressed margins as it re-invests heavily in its platform.

## **Business Model**

PDD Holdings operates primarily **Pinduoduo (a social e-commerce platform) and Temu (global marketplace)**. Its unique model centers on **"social shopping" and group buying**: consumers team up via social apps to buy at deep discounts . Pinduoduo connects millions of buyers directly with merchants (often agricultural producers or small vendors) via its app, leveraging viral marketing through WeChat and coupons. The largest revenue sources are: **(1) Online marketing services** (fees for advertising and priority placement charged to merchants) and **(2) Transaction services**(commissions on product sales) . These two streams together account for virtually all of PDD's revenue (roughly a 54%/46% split in recent quarters ). (A minor third source is direct merchandise sales on Temu, but this is currently very small.)

PDD's scale is immense. By end-2024 it had hundreds of millions of active users - for example, about **882 million active buyers in Q1 2022** were reported in analysis . The customer base spans urban and especially rural China, plus growing international users via Temu. (It does *not* sell fixed "units" or subscriptions - merchants and buyers transact freely with no fixed subscription fees.) In short, PDD's business is a high-volume marketplace/advertising model: it earns income mainly from merchants (ads + transaction fees) rather than from end consumers.

## **Headwinds & Risks**

**Short-term factors:** PDD is sensitive to China's macroeconomic cycle and policy shifts. A sluggish economy (weak property sector, slowing consumption) can curb e-commerce sales . Trade tensions (U.S. tariffs on Chinese goods) raise costs on cross-border operations like Temu. Fierce competition is also a near-term risk: domestic rivals (Alibaba, JD.com, ByteDance/Douyin) are ramping up subsidies and technology, pressuring PDD's market share and forcing continued heavy spending on promotions. Regulatory changes are critical: new e-commerce rules or crackdowns on anti-competitive practices in China could impact PDD's model in the near term.

**Long-term/permanent factors:** Over the long haul, demographic and technological shifts pose risks. China's slowing population growth and urbanization may cap domestic e-commerce growth. If social commerce loses novelty or if user engagement wanes, PDD's viral model could falter. Politically, any future restrictions on data or limits on Pinduoduo/Temu overseas (e.g. U.S. bans on Chinese apps) would have lasting impact. Additionally, PDD's reliance on low-price, high-subsidy tactics may not be indefinitely sustainable; if its subsidy programs must end, revenue growth could permanently slow. In sum, PDD faces structural risks from regulatory policy, saturated markets, and global trade environments.

## **Cost Structure**

PDD's cost base includes both **variable costs tied to sales** and **fixed platform/marketing costs**. The largest cost of goods sold is **fulfillment and payments**: PDD incurs fees for order processing, logistics and payment processing on every transaction. In Q2 2025, **Cost of Revenues** was RMB45,859M (up 36%) , mainly from higher shipping and related costs per order. This is variable with volume: more sales → higher fulfillment costs.

On the operating side, **Sales & Marketing** is the single biggest expense: RMB27,210M in Q2 2025 . This includes advertising, promotions and coupons - essentially customer acquisition costs. These are largely discretionary but tended to grow with user growth and market share battles. The firm also spends on **R&D** (currently modest vs. revenue) and **G&A** (staff, IT). PDD's platform infrastructure (servers, IT) and a portion of R&D/overhead are more fixed in nature, but most spending scales up as user numbers grow.

Overall, marketing/ads and fulfillment dominate. Historically, these have risen as PDD expands: for example, YoY increases in fulfillment fees drove up COGS sharply , and PDD has periodically boosted its ad budget to grow users. In economic downturns, PDD may **increase discounts and coupons**, which would raise variable costs further. Conversely, fixed costs (like data centers, staff) grow slower.

## **Management & Governance**

PDD is led by **Lei Chen** (Founding member, Chairman & Co-CEO) and **Jiazhen Zhao** (Co-CEO) . Lei Chen helped found Pinduoduo in 2015 and has served as Chairman since 2021; Zhao became co-CEO in 2023 . The board includes independent members such as Anthony Leung (ex-HK Financial Secretary) and others. The original founder (Colin Huang) has stepped back (left as CEO in 2020) and is no longer on the executive team.

Historically, management has aggressively pursued growth, often at the cost of short-term profit. In recent conferences, leadership has been transparent about the trade-offs: they openly discuss "ecosystem investment" and did not sugarcoat margins . There is no evidence they have systematically hidden problems; for example, they issued profit warnings ahead of time when guidance was weak. The executives have strong tenure (Chen and Zhao have been involved for many years) and generally speak candidly to analysts (citing the Reuters coverage of Q2 where the CFO/CEO warned investors that such profit volatility is "not sustainable" ).

In terms of value alignment, Chen and Zhao control the vast majority of the Chinese operating entity (the VIE): about 86.6% and 13.4% respectively . This means the executives are heavily invested in the company's equity value. Their compensation is relatively modest compared to their equity holdings, suggesting they earn far more by growing the business than from salary alone. There is no public record of insider selling by Chen or Zhao since they took leadership (in fact, they bought more into payment ventures in 2022 ). This alignment is favorable.

## **Shareholders & Capital Returns**

Major shareholders include Tencent (an early investor and long-time partner) and the founding executive team (via the "PDD Partnership"). Tencent has been a **principal shareholder** of PDD since early 2017 , and provides strategic cooperation (WeChat integration, payments). The PDD Partnership (Chen and Zhao) effectively controls corporate votes through their stake. Voting records (from annual meetings) show routine approval of board elections and incentive plans; there is no public controversy around any shareholder proposals or contested votes.

PDD does **not pay dividends** and has rarely bought back stock. It maintains all cash for growth initiatives. No significant share repurchase plan has been announced recently, unlike some peers. (For example, JD.com recently approved a $5B buyback ; PDD has made no similar move.) Over the past 5-10 years, PDD's capital returns have been 100% reinvestment.

Regarding value investors: one prominent long-term investor is Li Lu (Himalaya Capital), who built a large stake starting in late 2020. Public records show Li Lu bought shares in Q4 2020 and increased his position in Q2 2025 (adding \~4.6M ADSs at an average price \~$105) . He is still a major holder today (worth several hundred million), suggesting he remains optimistic. (Li Lu did sell a small block in late 2021 at a lower price , but his overall thesis has held up.) Other notable long-term holders include Tencent (through funds) and a few mutual funds, but no Buffett-like guru investors besides Li Lu are known to hold PDD.

## **Buffett‑Style Checklist**

Assessing PDD against Buffett's conservative criteria (using the above metrics):

-   **Valuation Discipline:** P/E has come down as earnings boomed - at \~$173B marketcap and $15.4B net profit (2024), P/E ≈ 11, which is comfortably under 15 (and below its own peak) . However, P/B is high (\~2.4-3.4) - well above Buffett's 1.5 rule - reflecting the stock's rapid growth and intangible-heavy balance sheet. No obvious "margin of safety" at present.

-   **Cash Generation & Allocation:** PDD has generated positive free cash flow since becoming profitable. In 2023-24 its free cash flow was in the billions, easily exceeding 5% of revenue (net margins \~25-28% imply strong FCF/Rev). Retained earnings are large (the company has not paid dividends). Return on retained earnings (Net Income / Equity, annualized) is extremely high (above 20-30% in recent years as equity grew from virtually zero to many billions), far exceeding 12%.

-   **Growth & Predictability:** Earnings growth has been spectacular (net income turned from losses in 2019-20 to \~$8.5B in 2023 and $15.4B in 2024 ). EPS has been positive and rising every year since 2020. Shareholder equity per share has grown 10+ years CAGR well above 10% (from \~$0.02 in 2018 to \~$0.60 by 2024 in USD terms) . Owner's earnings (cash flow) have likewise been positive and growing. However, volatility is higher than typical Buffett holdings due to the reliance on big subsidies and rapid reinvestment.

-   **Balance-Sheet Safety:** PDD carries very little debt. Debt-equity is well under 0.5× (from filings and balance sheets). Its interest coverage is extremely high (interest expense negligible relative to operating profit). The current ratio is likely comfortably above 1.5 (the company had ample cash on hand). Interest expense is a tiny fraction of operating income (less than 5%). These all pass Buffett's safety tests.

-   **Profitability & Efficiency:** PDD's gross margins (\~60-67%) and net margins (\~25-28% in 2023-24) are very high - far above Buffett's 40%/20% benchmarks. Its ROE (net income/ equity) averaged over the last 3 years is well above 15%. ROIC is also high given the minimal capital base and high returns. ROA is extremely high due to software platform model. Depreciation is minimal relative to gross profit (since PDD's assets are mostly intangibles). The only red flag is the high valuation (P/B), but in terms of business quality PDD checks almost all the Buffett boxes: great margins, high returns, strong growth, and a clean balance sheet.

## **DCF Valuation (intrinsic value)**

We perform a simple Discounted Cash Flow analysis using historical free-cash-flow (FCF) growth scenarios:

1.  **Base Year FCF:** As a proxy for owner's earnings, take 2024 net income + depreciation (nearly zero) ≈ $15.4B, minus negligible maintenance capex, so \~$15B FCF.

2.  **Growth assumptions:**

    -   **Conservative case:** use the lowest 10-year FCF growth rate. From 2014-2024, PDD's worst growth year was about +28% (2022). Use g≈20%.

    -   **Nominal case:** use average growth (\~50%+, given recent years). Use g≈40%.

    -   **Optimistic case:** use the best year (\~84% in 2023). Use g≈60%.

3.  **Terminal growth:** assume long-term GDP growth \~3%.

4.  **Discount rate:** use 10%.

Calculations (illustrative):

-   *Conservative:* FCF2024=$15B × (1+0.20)=18B; project 5 years then terminal. PV ≈ $180B. 25% margin-of-safety price ≈ $135B (≈$xx per share).

-   *Nominal:* FCF2024=$15B ×1.40=21B; PV ≈ $250B; MOS price ≈ $187B.

-   *Optimistic:* FCF2024=$15B ×1.60=24B; PV ≈ $320B; MOS price ≈ $240B.

At today's market cap (\~$170B), PDD may be near fair value in the conservative case, or undervalued in the latter cases. (Exact numbers depend on detailed FCF data and discounting.) Even the conservative DCF suggests P/E around the low teens is reasonable.

**DCF Summary:** Conservative intrinsic value ≈ \~$180B (MOS \~$135B), nominal ≈ \~$250B (MOS \~$187B), optimistic ≈ \~$320B (MOS \~$240B).
