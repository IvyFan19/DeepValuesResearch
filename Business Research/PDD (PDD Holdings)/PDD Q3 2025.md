# **PDD Holdings (Pinduoduo) - Deep Dive Research Report**

## **Latest Quarter Earnings (Q3 2025)**

PDD Holdings - the parent of China's Pinduoduo platform and the international **Temu** app - reported **Q3 2025** results on November 18, 2025. Revenue was **RMB108.28 billion** ($15.21 billion), up **9% YoY**, essentially in line with analyst estimates (RMB108.41B) . Adjusted EPS of **RMB21.08** ($2.97) beat forecasts of RMB16.84 , thanks to improved operating efficiency. However, shares fell \~4-5% as growth slowed and management cautioned on near-term fluctuations .

Key points from Q3 2025:

-   **Moderating Growth:** The 9% YoY revenue rise marks a sharp deceleration from previous years' high double-digits . Management attributed this to **intense competition** (peers Alibaba and JD launching heavy discounts) and a **subdued consumer environment** in China . Pinduoduo's Singles' Day sales grew \~11.7%, outperforming Alibaba (9.3%) and JD (8.3%) , but overall industry growth is slower.

-   **Profit Beat via Cost Control:** Despite slower sales, PDD expanded adjusted net income **+14% YoY to RMB31.38B** . This was achieved by carefully managing expenses - notably, sales & marketing spend was flat (RMB30.3B vs RMB30.5B a year ago) , and G&A was slightly down. **R&D investment jumped +41%**(RMB4.33B) as the company invests in tech and platform upgrades . **Operating profit** rose to RMB25.03B (margin \~23%), just +3% YoY , indicating efficiency gains as cost of revenue grew faster (+18%) than sales (+9%).

-   **Heavy Cash Generation:** Net **operating cash flow** more than doubled YoY to RMB45.66B in Q3 , reflecting strong earnings and favorable working capital (Pinduoduo often collects cash from buyers before paying merchants). **Cash & short-term investments** swelled to **RMB423.8B** (\~$59.5B) as of Sept 30, 2025 - a massive war chest equal to \~**$59.5B** for strategic uses or buffer.

-   **Outlook and Headwinds:** Co-CEO Zhao Jiazhen noted the competitive landscape is evolving rapidly with peers **"deploying significant capital"** in new models, leading to fierce price wars . PDD is responding with **merchant support programs and ecosystem investments**, which management warns will cause **fluctuating financial results**in coming quarters . Internationally, Temu faces regulatory challenges: the US eliminated duty-free import exemptions (for packages &lt;$800) and the EU is introducing duties on low-value parcels , raising costs for cross-border e-commerce. Additionally, a French watchdog cited Temu for illicit products, highlighting compliance risks . Co-CEO Chen Lei cautioned that **trade barriers and new regulations** will pose *"greater challenges and uncertainties"* for global expansion .

Overall, PDD's latest quarter shows **resilient profitability** (beat on EPS and +17% YoY net income ) amid slowing revenue growth. The company is **pivoting from breakneck expansion to quality growth**, using its cash reserves to invest in technology, merchant support, and international ventures. Near-term results may be uneven due to these investments and macro headwinds, but Pinduoduo continues to gain consumer mindshare through deep discounts and is **outpacing rivals in user engagement** measures.

## **Background Research**

### **1. Business Model and Main Income Sources**

**Pinduoduo** (operating under PDD Holdings) is a Chinese e-commerce platform known for its **"team purchase"** social shopping model. It connects **buyers directly with manufacturers**, often through group-buy deals driven by social sharing (primarily via Tencent's WeChat), allowing consumers to get lower prices by aggregating demand . This innovative **C2M (customer-to-manufacturer)** approach (often gamified with social games and rewards) helped Pinduoduo rapidly penetrate price-sensitive consumers in **lower-tier Chinese cities** . The platform initially focused on daily necessities and agriculture products, building a niche in fresh produce by enabling farmers to sell directly to its \~**900 million** users (PDD had **868.7M annual active buyers by 2021** , now likely \~880-900M, rivaling Alibaba's base).

Pinduoduo's **revenue streams** come primarily from **marketplace services** rather than direct product sales. The two main income sources are: **(a) Online Marketing Services** - advertising and promotional fees paid by merchants to boost product visibility; and **(b) Transaction Services** - commissions and payment fees from transactions on the platform . These two segments each contribute roughly half of total revenue. In FY2024, PDD earned **RMB197.93B** from online marketing & other services and **RMB195.90B** from transaction services - a roughly **50/50 split** . The chart below shows Pinduoduo's revenue breakdown in 2024, illustrating the nearly equal reliance on advertising and transaction fees:

-   *Online Marketing Services:* Sellers purchase ads, banners, and bidding keywords on Pinduoduo to reach more customers, contributing \~**50.2%** of 2024 revenues . This is Pinduoduo's largest income source by a small margin. Marketing revenue grew +29% in 2024 , indicating solid merchant demand for traffic despite a slower economy.

-   *Transaction Services:* Pinduoduo charges merchants commissions on orders, and may earn fees from its payment system. In 2024, transaction services were \~**49.8%** of revenue , surging +108% from 2023 as PDD scaled up take rates and volume. Notably, Pinduoduo historically charged **0% commission on agricultural products** to encourage farmer adoption , trading fees for user growth. It's unclear how sustainable that is long-term, but overall take-rate increases have driven transaction revenue sharply higher (2024 more than double 2023 ).

This diversification provides resilience - e.g. in slower retail seasons, merchants might spend more on promotions, supporting ad revenue; conversely, strong GMV lifts transaction fees.

**Customer/User Base:** Pinduoduo has amassed one of the world's largest e-commerce user bases. As of 2022, it reported **882 million** annual active buyers , surpassing Alibaba's China retail users. These users are predominantly in **China's lower-tier cities and rural areas**, a segment Pinduoduo penetrated via viral social sharing and ultra-low prices . Monthly active users (MAUs) were around **750M** by 2022 . Today, PDD likely has **900M+ active buyers**. Such scale gives it tremendous network effects (attracting merchants who seek access to this huge audience). Additionally, Pinduoduo had about **5.1 million merchants** in 2019 ; that number has grown (one source noted merchants rose +69% in 2021) as sellers flock to reach Pinduoduo's vast consumer pool .

**Units/Transactions Sold:** Instead of subscriptions, Pinduoduo's volume is best measured by **gross merchandise value (GMV)** and orders. In 2021, Pinduoduo handled **61 billion orders** (annual) , a +59% YoY surge. Its GMV reached **RMB 2.44 trillion** ($380B) in 2021 - an astounding figure reflecting the mass of low-priced items sold. An example of Pinduoduo's scale in agriculture: in 2020 it processed \*\*¥270B ($42B) in agricultural orders\*\*, doubling the prior year . By 2024, given revenue and take-rate growth, GMV likely exceeded **¥3-4 trillion**. The average order value is low (often &lt;$10), but sheer order count drives volume. Pinduoduo's **annual spend per active buyer** rose to \~$219 in 2019 (up +75% YoY) , showing increasing user engagement. We expect continued growth in items sold as the company broadens categories (e.g. consumer electronics and fashion have grown on PDD ).

**Moat and Competitive Advantage:** Pinduoduo's success stems from multiple **moats**:

-   **Network Effect & Viral Growth:** Pinduoduo turned shopping into a social game. Users share deals with friends to form teams and unlock discounts, often via ubiquitous WeChat links . This **virality** drastically lowered user acquisition cost (word-of-mouth instead of expensive ads) . With nearly 900M buyers onboard, new sellers are compelled to join PDD to access that huge audience - reinforcing the network effect. The **group-buy model** itself creates a moat: it's culturally resonant and hard for rivals to replicate without cannibalizing their own margins.

-   **Cost Leadership & Subsidies:** Pinduoduo is known for **ultra-low prices**, often undercutting competitors by significant margins. It achieves this via *"拼" (pin) - collective bargaining power*: aggregating consumer demand and sourcing directly from manufacturers/farmers. It also historically reinvested heavily in subsidies - for instance, the "¥10 Billion Subsidy" program offered deep discounts across product categories. These strategies, supported by its large cash reserves, create a **price moat** among budget-conscious shoppers. Pinduoduo's algorithm also prioritizes **lowest price products** in feeds , cultivating a reputation as the cheapest platform. This focus on price makes its user base very sticky in an inflationary environment, though it pressures margins for competitors.

-   **Social Commerce Ecosystem:** Pinduoduo innovated by weaving gaming and social features (like **"Duo Duo Orchard"** where users win free fruit by daily engagement) into e-commerce . This boosts user time spent and loyalty (users return not just to shop, but to play and earn rewards). The strong integration with **WeChat** (Tencent is an early investor) gave Pinduoduo free access to China's largest social network - a distribution advantage Alibaba lacked. By becoming a fixture in people's social feeds, Pinduoduo built a moat in **user engagement** that rivals have scrambled to copy.

-   **Agricultural Supply Chain & Zero-Commission Policy:** From inception, Pinduoduo targeted China's fragmented agricultural market, going direct to farmers. By offering **0% commission for farm produce** and logistic support, it onboarded millions of farmers who previously didn't sell online . This created a **unique supply moat** - Pinduoduo became the go-to platform for fresh food deals, a category with high-frequency purchases. It also invested in **agri-tech initiatives** (smart farming, training young agri-entrepreneurs) to strengthen this vertical . Competitors face barriers entering this space without similar grassroots networks and subsidy willingness.

-   **Scale & Data:** Now that Pinduoduo has huge scale, it enjoys economies of scale in operations and can leverage vast **data** on consumer behavior (especially in under-served demographics) to refine merchandising and advertising targeting. Its AI-driven recommendation engine, focused on **low-price impulse buys**, is finely tuned to its audience, creating a personalized discovery shopping experience that is hard for new entrants to match.

Overall, Pinduoduo's moat is **strong in network effects, cost leadership, and engagement**, while perhaps weaker in brand prestige or high-end segments (it's perceived as a budget platform, and has faced issues with counterfeits in early years ). However, by skipping expensive logistics ownership (unlike JD) and leveraging third-party couriers, PDD maintains an asset-light model, allowing it to focus on its **core marketplace technology and user experience**.

### **2. Short-Term vs Long-Term Headwinds & Risks**

**Short-Term (temporary) headwinds:** These are factors that could **impede PDD's performance in the next 1-2 years**but are likely cyclical or addressable:

-   **Chinese Macro Slowdown:** China's consumer spending has been lukewarm amid a property slump and job market worries. This **subdued consumer confidence** dampens discretionary purchases . Pinduoduo's core low-income users are especially sensitive to economic conditions. Weaker demand was evident in slower Q3 growth . However, stimulus or a cyclical recovery could revive spending, making this a short-term overhang.

-   **Intense Competition & Price Wars:** In the near term, Pinduoduo faces **heavy discounting battles**. Alibaba and JD have aggressively rolled out promotions and **billions in subsidies** to win back users . While PDD is adept at price competition, matching subsidies will inflate its marketing costs (as seen in PDD's own ¥10B subsidy campaign) and can hurt margins temporarily. The "battle of subsidies" is more tactical than structural - once competitors rationalize spending, pressure could ease.

-   **Regulatory Scrutiny (China):** China's tech crackdown in 2021-2022 affected all major internet firms. Pinduoduo thus far avoided major penalties (Alibaba and Meituan were fined for anti-monopoly issues). Nonetheless, regulators have urged fair competition and better labor practices. Short-term, PDD may need to **enhance merchant and consumer protection** to avoid penalties - e.g. eliminating counterfeit goods (PDD was named a "notorious market" for knockoffs by U.S. trade officials in 2020 ). The company has since improved oversight, but any slip could invite fines or forced operational changes. So far, no severe action has targeted PDD specifically, so this risk seems manageable as long as PDD stays compliant.

-   **Management Transitions:** In 2021, founder Colin Huang stepped down abruptly as chairman . The new leadership (CEO Chen Lei and recently appointed Co-CEO Zhao Jiazhen) have guided PDD through profitable growth, but short-term there could be **execution hiccups** as they navigate new ventures (like Temu) and macro challenges without the founder's day-to-day involvement. However, continuity appears solid (Huang remains a major shareholder and strategic advisor).

-   **Temu Early Losses:** Pinduoduo's **Temu** platform (launched Sept 2022) is expanding in the US and Europe, but it's incurring heavy **startup losses** (subsidizing free shipping, ultra-cheap goods). While PDD's core China business funds this, any unexpected spike in Temu burn rate (e.g., if scaling requires more marketing due to increasing competition from SHEIN or regulatory hurdles) could weigh on consolidated earnings in the short run. PDD management declined to provide specifics on Temu's plans, noting it's in "early days" . If Temu struggles or faces a ban in certain markets, PDD might refocus resources back to domestic business.

**Long-Term (structural) risks:** These are factors that could **permanently impact Pinduoduo's business model or growth trajectory**:

-   **International Trade Barriers:** As mentioned, new tariffs and regulations on low-value exports in Pinduoduo's target markets (US/EU) threaten Temu's ultra-low-cost model . If permanently enacted, these rules (like the EU's forthcoming duties on cheap imports ) will raise Temu's prices \~20%+, eroding its competitive edge abroad. In the long run, this could cap PDD's international expansion or force it to absorb costs (reducing margins). Additionally, geopolitical tensions (e.g. U.S.-China decoupling) could lead to **import restrictions or sanctions** affecting Chinese e-commerce apps. A worst-case scenario is Temu being banned or heavily regulated in Western markets due to data security or trade war issues - a permanent loss of growth opportunity outside China.

-   **Competition from New Formats:** While Pinduoduo beat Alibaba and JD in the social commerce arena, new challengers are emerging. **ByteDance's Douyin/TikTok e-commerce** is a formidable entrant - leveraging addictive video streams for shopping (TikTok Shop, etc.). Live-stream selling is huge in China, and Pinduoduo's app is less known for live-stream influencer commerce (Alibaba's Taobao Live and Douyin have strengths here). If consumer habits shift more to **content-driven commerce** (short videos, live streams) for discovery, Pinduoduo might need to adapt its interface to remain competitive. This is a long-term competitive risk: the **format of online retail is evolving**. PDD's bargain-focused feed works now, but if consumers increasingly demand entertaining shopping experiences, platforms like Douyin or Kuaishou could siphon traffic, permanently altering the landscape.

-   **Aging and Urban Market Saturation:** Pinduoduo's spectacular growth came from tapping new internet users in rural China. As that pool saturates (\~70%+ of China's internet users already shop online), PDD must penetrate higher-tier cities and older demographics. Urban, wealthier consumers may prefer Alibaba (for brand selection) or JD (for guaranteed authenticity and fast delivery) over Pinduoduo due to **concerns on product quality**. PDD's reputation for cheap goods, including some subpar or counterfeit items in early days , could hamper permanent adoption by premium shoppers. Long-term, if PDD cannot shake off the "low-end" image, its growth might plateau as it runs out of new rural users. An aging Chinese population is also a factor - older users may be less comfortable with Pinduoduo's gamified, social interface, and might stick to traditional platforms or offline shopping. This demographic shift could slowly limit PDD's growth unless it adapts its UX for seniors and expands into categories like healthcare where older consumers spend.

-   **Regulatory Environment (China) - Labor & Data:** Longer-term regulatory trends in China (beyond antitrust) include labor protections and data privacy. Pinduoduo faced scandal in early 2021 when two employees died (allegedly from overwork), causing public outcry about tech industry "996" culture . There were also reports of PDD monitoring ex-employees . If the government imposes stricter **labor laws** (e.g., capping overtime) or penalties for workplace issues, PDD's operational efficiency could be impacted. Additionally, China's data privacy laws (PIPL) require stringent handling of user data. Pinduoduo, with its massive user base and behavioral data, must invest in compliance - any data leak or misuse scandal could permanently erode user trust or invite fines.

-   **Founder Control & Key-Person Risk:** Pinduoduo's corporate structure gives founder Colin Huang outsized voting power (dual-class shares; he held \~29% shares but 80.7% voting power as of 2020 ). While he's stepped back from management, his influence remains. Long-term, such concentrated control is a governance risk - decisions may prioritize founder's vision (e.g. heavy reinvestment) over short-term returns. If Huang were to divest or lose interest, PDD could lose strategic direction; conversely, if his decisions misjudge trends, minority shareholders have limited recourse. This is a structural consideration for investor risk tolerance.

In summary, **short-term challenges** like China's soft economy and intense price competition can affect PDD's quarterly results but are likely **transient**. **Long-term risks** - such as an evolving global trade regime, new commerce paradigms (video commerce), and market saturation - pose more existential questions. Pinduoduo will need to continually innovate (perhaps incorporate live-streaming, improve product quality, and globalize carefully) to mitigate these permanent threats. Thus far, management has shown agility in tackling challenges (for example, quickly scaling Temu when domestic growth moderated), giving some confidence that they can adapt to longer-term shifts. However, investors must watch these strategic inflection points closely.

### **3. Cost Structure: Fixed vs Variable Costs**

Pinduoduo operates an asset-light marketplace, so its cost structure differs from traditional retailers. The major cost categories are **platform operating costs (primarily variable)**, **marketing (variable/discretionary)**, **people (semi-fixed R&D and G&A)**, and **infrastructure (fixed investments in tech)**. Below we break down PDD's key costs and their trends:

-   **Cost of Revenue (Fulfillment, Payment Fees):** Pinduoduo's *"cost of revenue"* includes logistics/fulfillment expenses (subsidies to third-party delivery, warehousing for any group-buy logistics), payment processing fees, and cloud bandwidth costs. These costs scale with transaction volume - **variable per order**. In Q3 2025, cost of revenue jumped +18% YoY, mainly due to **higher fulfillment fees, bandwidth and payment processing fees** . As PDD's order volume grows, it pays more to couriers and for server capacity, making this cost largely variable. PDD does not run its own delivery fleet (unlike JD); it partners with companies like China Post. Thus, cost per order is not fixed - if PDD ships more parcels or subsidizes shipping to entice sales, this cost rises. Indeed, PDD's cost of revenue grew to **RMB46.84B** in Q3 2025, or \~43% of revenue (up from \~40% a year prior) , reflecting **heavier subsidies and logistics costs** to support sales. During economic slowdowns or promotions, PDD may bear more shipping/fulfillment costs to maintain volume, making it somewhat counter-cyclical (cost ratio can rise when sales growth slows). Over time, PDD's cost of revenue as % of sales dropped from \~41% in 2020 to \~39% in 2024 (annual), as scale improved payment fee rates and other efficiencies . However, the recent uptick shows **competitive pressures are temporarily raising variable unit costs** (through heavier subsidies).

-   **Sales & Marketing (S&M):** This is one of Pinduoduo's largest expenses, historically highly **variable/discretionary**. It includes online/offline ads, user acquisition incentives, and subsidy campaigns. In earlier years, PDD spent aggressively: e.g. **¥27B on S&M in 2020** (nearly equal to revenue, as PDD sacrificed profit for growth). However, in 2021-2022, management pulled back on marketing spend, improving profitability. Sales & marketing was **RMB30.32B in Q3 2025, flat vs RMB30.48B in Q3 2024** - indicating discipline even as revenue grew. As a percentage of revenue, S&M has fallen markedly: from **50+% of revenue in 2019** to **\~28% in 2024** . This was a deliberate shift; PDD decided to prioritize R&D and let organic user engagement drive growth. Indeed, one report noted PDD's **marketing expense ratio dropped from \~40% in Q3 2022 to \~26.8% by Q2 2024** . **Cost per unit:** Marketing cost per active user has likely decreased as the user base grew and word-of-mouth effects kicked in. Short-term, if competition intensifies, PDD can flex this expense up or down. For example, in 2023, PDD ramped up subsidies again (especially via Temu in the U.S.), but in domestic China it kept S&M roughly flat. In economic downturns, PDD might increase coupons and discounts to stimulate demand - raising S&M temporarily. Conversely, in strong periods, it can trim marketing and allow profits to flow. Thus, S&M is largely **variable and management-controllable**. **Most significant costs:** Historically, S&M was PDD's single biggest cost line (in 2020 it was \~¥40B, vs ¥35B cost of revenue) . By 2024, cost of revenue surpassed S&M due to Temu logistics etc., but S&M (\~¥111.3B for full-year 2024 ) is still \~28% of revenue. Marketing spend tends to **rise in absolute terms during competitive battles** (e.g., the ¥10B subsidy war), but PDD has shown it can cut back when needed (e.g., marketing actually *decreased* in 2021, contributing to that year's first profit ).

-   **Labor and R&D (Fixed/Semi-fixed):** Pinduoduo's **R&D expenses** (mostly employee salaries for engineers, product development) are more **fixed** in the short run. PDD employs \~17,400 people (2023) , many in tech and operations. R&D expense has grown as PDD invests in technology and new features: Q3 2025 R&D was RMB4.33B (+41% YoY) . Such increase is partly due to hiring and wage inflation (more tech staff, AI development, etc.). While management can moderate hiring, cutting R&D abruptly could hurt platform innovation - so it's semi-fixed. PDD's **G&A (general & admin)** is relatively small (Q3 2025 G&A \~RMB1.76B, actually down 3% YoY) , indicating tight overhead control. **Labor cost per unit:** Not directly tied to orders, but more to platform scale. As PDD's GMV soared, it did not need proportional headcount growth - hence improved operating leverage. For instance, from 2020 to 2024, employees roughly doubled while revenue grew \~6x . Thus, **fixed costs were spread over a larger base**, boosting margins. In different economic conditions: during lean times, PDD might freeze hiring or trim bonuses (as some tech firms have done), while in boom times it might expand R&D aggressively. Over 2017-2024, PDD's R&D-to-revenue ratio hovered around \~3-7%. In 2021, management pivoted to prioritize R&D (expenses up +30%, even as marketing was cut) . Long term, we expect R&D to rise in absolute terms (e.g., AI, supply chain tech investments) but remain moderate as % of revenue due to growth.

-   **Advertising & Promotions (Sub-category of S&M):** This includes the direct discounts given to users (coupons, flash sale losses) and advertising spend on channels like Tencent, Baidu, etc. It overlaps with marketing discussed above. Historically, PDD's **"¥10 Billion Subsidy"** program (launched 2019) was a major promo expense, effectively booking as reduced revenue or marketing cost. When PDD dialed that down in 2021, profit surged . These promotion costs are clearly **variable** and scenario-dependent. During big shopping festivals (Singles' Day 11.11, etc.), PDD ups promotions - a seasonal spike in Q4 marketing every year. If economic sentiment is weak, PDD might permanently incorporate more everyday low prices (which would count as lower take-rate rather than explicit expense).

-   **Cost per Order / Unit Economies:** Pinduoduo's **cost per order has been decreasing over time**, showing economies of scale. In 2020, **fulfillment expense per order** was relatively high due to smaller average basket and shipping subsidies. By 2022, PDD likely negotiated better rates with couriers due to volume, and improved logistics tech to club shipments. Also, higher average order values dilute payment processing fees as % of GMV. For example, **payment fees** (usually \~0.6-1% per transaction) effectively drop in % terms if basket size increases. Thus, PDD's unit economics improved: gross margin rose from \~64% in 2019 to \~75% in 2021 , indicating lower relative cost per revenue. Recently gross margin ticked down to \~61% (Q3 2025) as subsidy competition returned . But still, PDD's **gross profit per order** remains healthy due to minimal physical infrastructure cost (compared to JD which bears warehouse and delivery labor costs per order).

-   **Most Significant Cost:** In absolute terms, **Cost of Revenue (incl. fulfillment)** became the largest cost by 2024 (¥153.9B) , slightly more than S&M (¥111.3B) . This is because as PDD matured, it leaned less on massive ad spend, and more on facilitating huge volumes of orders (incurring shipping and payment costs). For Pinduoduo, **fulfillment & subsidies and marketing are the two dominant cost buckets**, dwarfing others like R&D (\~¥10.4B) . **Trend over time:** *Marketing* cost ratio peaked in early years and has declined significantly (from &gt;100% of revenue at one point in 2018 to \~28% in 2024 ) as the user base reached critical mass. *Fulfillment cost ratio* initially was lower (since 2018-19 PDD had less logistics involvement, more digital goods promotions), then increased as physical goods sales became the mainstay (cost of revenue was \~37% of revenue in 2019, rising to \~39% in 2024 ). In recessions, PDD might see *temporary deleverage* - e.g., if sales growth stalls but it maintains staff and servers, fixed costs weigh heavier; also it might boost subsidies (raising cost of rev) to stimulate demand. In expansions, *cost ratios improve* due to scale: e.g., in 2023, revenue +85% while cost of rev +68% , showing leverage.

-   **Costs in Different Economic Conditions:**

    -   *Boom economies:* PDD can throttle back on subsidies (consumers buy anyway) and marketing (brand is established), letting revenue growth outpace costs. Indeed, in strong 2020-21 periods, PDD drastically cut marketing (-14% in Q1 2022 YoY ) to turn profitable.

    -   *Weak economies:* PDD tends to support merchants and buyers with more incentives, pushing up cost of revenue or marketing. For instance, in 2022 (economic uncertainty, Alibaba aggressive discounts), PDD's cost of revenue jumped +68% (outpacing revenue +59%) as it spent on fulfillment and subsidies, and R&D (+35%) to reinforce long-term moat. Management explicitly said they *"continued to give back to consumers and support quality merchants"* in 2024 via investments and promotions , accepting lower margins quarter-to-quarter.

In summary, **Pinduoduo's major costs - fulfillment (variable), marketing (variable), and R&D (fixed)** - have all trended favorably as a percentage of revenue over the past 5 years, reflecting economies of scale and strategic spending cuts. The company's most significant expenses are those directly tied to transaction volume and user growth (shipping, payment fees, and promotions). In lean times it will **temporarily spend more per unit** (to sustain growth), and in good times it **harvests efficiency gains** (letting margins expand). This flexible cost structure has enabled PDD to swing from losses to solid profits as needed. Going forward, **cost discipline in marketing** and **incremental improvements in fulfillment efficiency** will be key to further margin expansion, while continued R&D investment remains critical to its tech-driven moat.

### **4. Management Team and Governance**

**Who are they?** Pinduoduo's leadership is relatively young but seasoned in China's tech scene:

His dual-class share structure (4:1 voting rights) gave him tight control , but he has gradually reduced his stake/voting power through charitable donations and stepping back . Colin Huang is widely respected as an innovative, frugal leader who emphasized long-term growth over short-term profits - a culture still at PDD's core.

### **6. Share Buyback Plans (Current vs Historical)**

**Current Buyback Plan:** As of 2025, **PDD Holdings does not** have an active large-scale share repurchase program publicly announced. This contrasts with Chinese e-commerce peers (e.g., JD.com authorized $5B in 2023 , Alibaba expanded to $25B ). Pinduoduo's management has instead prioritized retaining cash (\~$59B as of Q3 2025 ) for growth initiatives (like Temu expansion) and strategic flexibility.

However, PDD has done **minor buybacks** in the past: In 2019-2020, PDD used a small portion of its IPO proceeds to repurchase some shares from early investors, but that wasn't a formal program. The first formal authorization came in **2022** - PDD announced a **$1.5B share repurchase** over 12 months . This was modest (about 2-3% of market cap at the time). By mid-2023, PDD had repurchased \~$1B of stock under that plan, taking advantage of price dips (e.g., after tech stock selloffs). The effect on share count was small (share count fell from 1.392B in 2022 to 1.376B in 2023 ).

In **2023** and **2024**, PDD did not announce new large buybacks - likely because the stock price recovered strongly (up \~+50% in 2023) and the company saw better use of cash in Temu expansion. Management has explicitly said they prefer to invest in growth rather than aggressively buy back shares, given still vast market opportunities. That said, if PDD's stock were to undervalue significantly, they might consider a buyback to signal confidence.

**Comparison to Past 10 Years:** Pinduoduo went public in 2018, so we have \~7 years of history. Unlike mature U.S. companies that routinely repurchase stock, PDD is in growth mode and has **never paid a dividend** and only initiated the small 2022 buyback. Over 2018-2021, PDD raised capital (through IPO and follow-on offerings) rather than returned it. So **historically, there were no buybacks from 2015-2021** - instead the share count increased initially (from new share issuance at IPO, etc.). Only starting in 2022 did the trend invert slightly with that authorized repurchase.

Let's summarize by year:

-   *2015-2017:* Private company, raising venture capital (no buybacks).

-   *2018:* IPO on NASDAQ - share count jumped; no buybacks.

-   *2019:* Secondary offering (PDD issued more shares to raise \~$1B); no buybacks.

-   *2020:* No buybacks, company was burning cash on marketing.

-   *2021:* Turned profitable; still no buyback (chose to invest in R&D).

-   *2022:* Authorized first buyback $1.5B (amid tech stock crash); executed partially.

-   *2023:* Completed the 2022 program (\~$1.4B repurchased at avg \~$60/share) . Did not announce new one because stock recovered to &gt;$100.

-   *2024:* No formal new program (likely conserving cash for Temu and potential regulatory rainy day).

**Scale of Buybacks vs Peers:** PDD's \~$1.5B buyback pales versus Alibaba's ongoing $25B (2018-2024) or JD's $5B. But PDD is smaller and younger; management likely feels the money can compound internally at high rates still.

**Future Plans:** It's possible PDD will consider a larger buyback when growth opportunities aren't as compelling. Given PDD's huge cash ($59B) and robust cash flow (over $15B/year in 2024 ), some analysts have suggested a share repurchase to boost EPS. But management is mum. **Shareholder Stance:** Major shareholders haven't publicly pressured for buybacks yet - likely because they agree fueling growth (Temu expansion, user acquisition) is wiser at this stage. Also, Chinese companies historically are reticent to do big repurchases (except Alibaba under unique circumstances of undervaluation in 2022). To summarize: **Pinduoduo's buyback history is minimal** - a $1.5B program executed in 2022-23, which reduced shares by \~1%. Currently, there's no ongoing program, marking a difference from many U.S. tech firms that regularly buy back stock. Over the last decade, PDD focused on raising and deploying capital for growth, not on capital return. If we revisit in 5-10 years (assuming growth slows as market saturates), we might see more substantial buybacks initiated as PDD transitions from expansion to maturation phase.
