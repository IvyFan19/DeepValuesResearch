# **Background Research**

**Business Model:** Alibaba Group is a Chinese tech conglomerate generating revenue mainly from e-commerce and cloud services. Its core business is domestic retail commerce (Taobao and Tmall), which accounted for nearly half of 2023 revenue. Other segments include international e-commerce (AliExpress, Lazada, Trendyol), logistics (Cainiao), local consumer services (Ele.me, Freshippo), and digital media/entertainment. *Alibaba's 2023 revenue by segment. Domestic retail (Taobao/Tmall) dominates, with cloud, international retail, and logistics each contributing about 10-11%.*Alibaba's customer base is enormous: it reported **1.31 billion annual active customers** in FY2023. For example, China retail GMV and user engagement remain high (Taobao/Tmall GMV grew \~4% in late 2024). The 88VIP loyalty program had \~49 million members by Q4 2024. Subsidiaries like Cainiao (logistics) and Alipay/Ant Financial (payments, not publicly listed here) broaden the ecosystem, though Ant's IPO remains on hold.

**Customer Base & Volume:** Alibaba serves **hundreds of millions** of merchants and consumers. In Q4 FY2024, total active consumers on its China retail marketplaces reached 1.315 billion. Taobao/Tmall collectively handle over 1 trillion yuan in gross merchandise value (GMV) annually. Its international marketplaces (AliExpress, Lazada, etc.) and new retail stores (Freshippo) add tens of millions more customers. The company also provides cloud computing services to over 3 million customers globally (Alibaba Cloud), though user-counts for cloud services are not regularly disclosed.

**Risks (Short- & Long-term):** Alibaba faces significant **political/regulatory risks**. U.S.-China tensions threaten its U.S. ADR listing - in 2025 U.S. lawmakers urged the SEC to delist "Chinese companies including Alibaba" on national security grounds. In China, past anti-monopoly fines ($2.75B in 2021) and new data/tech regulations mean heightened scrutiny. Market analysts note that weak Chinese consumer income and spending have slashed Alibaba's growth; for example Q2 2024 revenue grew only 3.9% YOY (vs \~30% three years prior). Economically, a Chinese economic slowdown or COVID resurgence could depress consumer demand (evidenced by mid-2024 slowdown). **Industry** risks include fierce competition (e.g. Pinduoduo's rapid growth) and global trade frictions. Geopolitically, cloud and technology segments face US export controls (e.g. AI chip restrictions) which have already delayed an Alibaba Cloud spin-off. **Short-term** risks are macro volatility (inflation, interest rates) and seasonal demand swings. **Long-term** risks include China's slowing growth/aging population, potential tech decoupling, and the absence of Alibaba from major U.S. indices if delisted.

**Costs (Fixed vs. Variable):** Alibaba's **fixed costs** include data centers, warehouses, and R&D. It employs \~250,000 people (large headcount) and invests heavily in infrastructure (e.g. cloud servers and logistics facilities). **Variable costs**are largely marketing, promotions (especially Singles Day campaigns), and order fulfillment. Historically, marketing and promotional spend ran around 10-15% of revenue; for example mid-2022 marketing expense was \~12% of revenue. Logistics costs (Cainiao) are partly passed to merchants, but fuel and labor inflation can raise per-delivery costs. In economic **upturns**, Alibaba ramps marketing to capture demand; in **downturns** it cuts costs (as it did in 2022 to protect margins). Depreciation of tech assets (a fixed cost) grows with new capex. Overall operating margins have recently expanded: gross margin \~37%, operating margin \~13-15%, implying substantial fixed overhead but strong operating leverage when growth resumes.

**Share Buyback:** In 2021-22 Alibaba launched its first big repurchase programs. Through May 2025 about $20.1B of the $25B authorization remains unused. In prior years (2014-2020) Alibaba had virtually no buybacks, so this is a major new capital-allocation policy. The aggressive repurchases (often at prices around $80-$100 per ADS) signal management's confidence that the shares are undervalued relative to intrinsic business value.

**Performance Interpretation:** Over the past decade Alibaba's **revenue** has soared from only \~$8.5B (FY2014) to over $126B (FY2023), roughly 25% CAGR. Growth accelerated mid-decade (2015-2021) on booming e-commerce, but has cooled in recent years as market saturation and macro pressures set in. In 2022-23 revenue actually dipped (\~6%). **Margins:** Gross margin has held steady around \~36-38% (FY2023 gross profit \~$46.4B on $126.5B revenue). Operating margins have improved modestly; operating income was $15.0B in FY2023 (\~11.9% of sales) and $17.2B in FY2024 (\~13%). Net income has been more volatile: a windfall $22.94B in 2021 (high investment gains) plunged to \~$9.77B in 2022, recovering to $10.56B in 2023. Net margin is now \~8-9%. Consequently, **valuation multiples** have compressed: trailing P/E fell from &gt;20x in 2018-20 to the mid-teens today (2023 P/E ≈19) as earnings growth stalled. **Return on Equity (ROE)** has declined to \~6-7% (2023) because of rising retained earnings and large cash balance, down from \~14% during the prior boom years. **Debt:** The balance sheet is very strong - Alibaba has negligible net debt. FY2024 cash was \~$84.4B vs. long-term debt \~$19.6B (debt/assets \~8%). So **leverage** is low (Debt/Equity \~0.13; Debt/Assets \~0.08). **Shareholder equity** has grown from \~$6.6B (2014) to \~$152.6B (2024). Overall, the company retains ample liquidity and no dividend, but has modest returns given its scale.
