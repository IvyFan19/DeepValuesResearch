## **Background Research**

**1. Business Model & Income Sources:** Robinhood generates revenue primarily from: **(a)** *transaction-based revenues* - chiefly payment-for-order-flow (PFOF) on equities and options trades and trading spreads on cryptocurrencies; **(b)** *net interest income* - earned on margin loans and uninvested cash (including securities lending fees); and **(c)** *subscription and other fees* - mainly Robinhood Gold memberships and debit card interchange. For example, in Q4'24 Robinhood reported **$672 M** in transaction-based revenues (split between crypto \~$358 M, options \~$222 M, and equities \~$61 M), **$296 M** of net interest, and **$46 M** of other revenues (mostly Gold subscriptions) . A pie chart of Q4'24 revenues would show roughly 67% transaction-based, 29% net interest, 4% other. (In full-year 2024, net revenues were \~$2.95 B .)

Robinhood's **customer base** was \~25-26 million funded accounts by early 2025 (25.8 M at Q1'25 ), of which roughly 3.2 M were *Robinhood Gold* subscribers (paying $5+/month for premium features). These users skew young and trade actively. The company also reports "total platform assets" (custody balances, crypto and brokerage) exceeding $221 B in Q1'25 . Robinhood does not sell physical "units" per se, but sells services/subscriptions (Gold) and earns interchange on its debit cards.

**2. Risks & Headwinds:** In the *short term*, Robinhood faces market-driven risks: trading volumes and net interest income can fall in a bear market or low-volatility environment, reducing revenues. Rising interest rates cut into interest income spreads. Crypto volatility can swing transaction revenues. Competition is intense from incumbents (Schwab, Fidelity, E\*TRADE) and other fintech/brokerage firms (SoFi, Coinbase, Binance) who may launch zero-commission models or better apps. *Regulatory/legal risks* loom: Payment-for-Order-Flow has been under SEC scrutiny (there's talk of banning it ), and crypto trading could face tighter rules. Robinhood has already faced class-action lawsuits from the 2021 "meme stock" events. On the *long term* side, the firm could be affected by broad economic or policy shifts - for example, a prolonged recession cutting retail investing, or new financial legislation affecting fintech. Moreover, evolving technology risks (cybersecurity, system outages) and the need to constantly invest in new products (e.g. announced expansion into banking/services) mean the business must adapt or risk obsolescence. These factors could permanently alter Robinhood's growth trajectory or margins.

**3. Cost Structure:** Robinhood has a *high operating leverage* cost structure: cost of revenue (transaction processing, clearing, interest expense) is relatively low, so most costs are SG&A and R&D. Major cost items are **technology and personnel** (engineering/development) and **share-based compensation (SBC)**. In 2024, R&D expenses were about **$818 M** (roughly 28% of 2024 revenue), and SBC was significantly reduced to $304 M (from $871 M in 2023) after expensing a one-time award in 2023. Robinhood also incurs regulatory, legal, and compliance costs. Marketing/advertising had been very high during its hyper-growth phase (notably in 2021), but has been scaled back. Fixed costs include data center and tech platform costs, compliance/legal infrastructure, and executive payroll. Variable costs rise with volume (e.g. clearing fees) but are a small fraction of revenues. In downturns, variable costs drop with volume, but fixed R&D and some overhead remain. Overall, as revenues grow, more flows to the bottom line: 2024's operating margin expanded sharply, aided by lower costs and high operating leverage.

**4. Management Team:** Robinhood is led by co-founder **Vlad Tenev (CEO)** - he and co-founder Baiju Bhatt led the company since 2013; Bhatt stepped down as co-CEO in 2020 but remains on the board . The executive team includes CFO Jason Warnick (joined 2020 after Bezos-led eero, among others) and several longtime Robinhood executives in engineering and ops roles. The Board includes several finance and tech veterans, plus representatives of early investors (e.g. Index Ventures' Omri Lavie). Management's track record is mixed: they delivered rapid growth and a 2021 IPO, then navigated losses and legal challenges, but returned to profitability in 2024. Communication has been relatively transparent in earnings calls (showing metrics like deposits, active accounts). Insider alignment: founders hold Class B shares with high votes but modest liquidation preference. There's no known history of insider stock dumping; executive stock grants were in line with tech peers. They have no legacy incentives that create obvious conflicts. Overall, management appears committed long-term (founders remain involved) and has invested heavily in product and compliance.

**5. Top Shareholders:** Institutional ownership (\~80% of float) is concentrated in passive and mutual funds. As of end-2024, the largest holders were Vanguard Group (\~68.4 M shares), BlackRock (\~40.5 M), Fidelity (FMR LLC \~40.2 M), and JPMorgan (\~35.3 M) , each \~4-9% of shares. Other significant holders include index funds (e.g. Vanguard Total Stock Market) and early venture investors (e.g. Index Ventures, Susquehanna affiliates). These funds typically vote in line with management recommendations; we find no record of activist campaigns around Robinhood. For example, Vanguard and BlackRock have not publicly opposed any key proposals. (Index Ventures holds all Class B super-voting shares but votes with management.) Thus, the largest investors are broadly diversified funds with no special agenda other than financial returns .

**6. Share Buyback:** In mid-2024, Robinhood's Board authorized the company's first-ever buyback: up to **$1.0 billion** of Class A shares . By year-end 2024, about **$257 M** had been spent (10.4 M shares at \~$24.78 average) . In April 2025, the Board increased the program by another $500 M (to $1.5 B total) . Prior to 2024, Robinhood had no repurchase programs (having been a growth company and then in losses). Thus, this $1.5 B authorization is the first substantial buyback in the company's history. If fully executed, it would reduce share count by roughly 10-15%, returning capital to shareholders. Compared to peers, the scale is modest (Robinhood's market cap is \~$44 B), but significant for a tech growth stock.

**7. Value Investors - Buys/Sells:** We see no evidence that any famous "value guru" funds hold significant RObinhood positions, nor any regular trading reports by such investors. Since Robinhood was unprofitable until 2024, it didn't fit classic value criteria. The holdings data suggest mostly index/mutual funds. (For example, no filings by Berkshire, ValueAct, or similar names.) Insider/insider-affiliated transactions show only modest option exercises and ESPP purchases by employees; no notable large buys/sells by management aside from routine share dilution. We are unaware of any large-scale cash purchases or sales by known value-oriented hedge funds. Thus, no publicly-available data on "average buy/sell price" for value investors. If anything, the recent rally likely saw institutions buy opportunistically, but specifics aren't disclosed.

**8. Intrinsic (DCF) Analysis:** Using a Buffett-style DCF, we start with 2024 *owner's earnings*. Robinhood's 2024 operating cash flow was **$1,181M** and capex was minor (\~$50M), so free cash flow ≈ \~$1.13 B. Assuming very conservative growth (e.g. mid‐teens declines to single-digits after a few years) and a discount rate of 10%, the present value of future cash flows is roughly **$36-37 per share** (using 768M shares) with no margin of safety. Applying a 50% margin of safety (Buffett's suggested floor) implies intrinsic ≈ **$18/share**. At a 30% cushion, intrinsic ≈ **$26-27**. In other words, with those assumptions, Robinhood appears *expensive* at today's \~$55 stock price without a large safety buffer. Conversely, solving for what discount rate would make intrinsic = current price yields \~**7.8%** (implying an expected return of \~7.8% per annum to hit the current price, with 0% margin). (If one used a higher growth forecast, the intrinsic value would rise; a higher discount rate lowers it.) This rough valuation highlights the range: under very conservative inputs, ROSE is around $18-27; at optimistic inputs, it could justify $50+. But with our conservative scenario, the current price has little margin.

**9. Latest Quarterly Results:** In Q1 2025 (earnings released Apr 30, 2025), Robinhood reported **record results**. Revenue was **$927M** (up **50%** YoY), driven by crypto and options trading, net interest ($290M, +14% YoY), and gold subscriptions . Net income was **$336M** (an **114%** increase YoY) with EPS $0.37 . Importantly, active **Funded Customers** grew to **25.8M** (+8% YoY) and **Robinhood Gold** members to 3.2M . Net new deposits hit a record $18.0B, reflecting strong user engagement. Operating expenses rose modestly (21% YoY) to $557M; adjusted EBITDA was $470M (up 90%). The Board concurrently added $500M more to the buyback authorization (to $1.5B) , signaling confidence. Overall, Q1'25 showed robust, profitable growth.

**Cost of Capital:** Robinhood has no debt on its balance sheet (capital is mostly equity-funded), so WACC approximates cost of equity. Given the high growth and risk, a 10-12% cost of equity is typical for fintech. The implied returns (7-8%) from our DCF suggest the stock is priced for near-term growth but with limited margin-of-safety.

## **Financial Research**

### **Historical Financials (2019-2024)**

Below is a summary of key historical financial metrics (from public filings, transcripts, and data services):

-   **Market Price & Cap:** Robinhood IPO'd in July 2021 at \~$34. Fifty-two week high hit \~$70 (Aug 2021) and low \~$8 (late 2022). Year-end closing prices: \~$8.14 (2022), $12.74 (2023), $37.26 (2024), $54.07 (5/2025) . Market cap grew from \~$9B (2022) to \~$33B (2024) to \~$44B today (post-Q1'25).

-   **Book Value & P/B:** Shareholders' equity was \~$6.96B at 2022 year-end and \~$6.70B at 2023, rising to \~$7.97B by Q4'24 (boosted by profits) . Book value per share (using \~768M share count) went from \~$9.06 (2022) to \~$8.72 (2023) to \~$10.39 (2024). P/B thus ranged from \~0.9× (2022) to \~1.5× (2023) to \~3.6× (2024).

-   **P/E Ratio:** Due to losses, P/E was undefined in 2021-23 (stocks losing money). As of late 2024, trailing P/E was \~23.7× (based on $37.26 close, EPS $1.57). Currently (mid-2025) P/E is \~38× (using recent price/EPS).

-   **ROA/ROE/ROIC:** ROA and ROE were deeply negative (2021-23) due to losses. In 2024, ROE jumped positive as NI = $1.41B on $6.70B average equity (\~21%). Return on invested capital likewise improved in 2024 (ROIC estimated \~20-25% given low capital base). No dividends have been paid (ROD).

-   **Revenue & Growth:** Revenue rose from $278M (2019) to $958M (2020) to $1.36B (2022, dip vs 2021's $1.815B) to $1.87B (2023) to $2.95B (2024) . The 5‑year CAGR (2020-24) is \~30%. Growth slowed in 2022 (2022 was -25% vs 2021) but rebounded +37% in 2023 and +58% in 2024 .

-   **Profitability:** 2020-21 saw net losses (-$3.69B in 2021). The loss narrowed in 2022 (-$1.03B) and 2023 (-$0.54B) to a profit of +$1.41B in 2024 . Thus, net margins swung from \~-60% (2022) to +48% (2024) of revenue. Operating income was negative until 2024, when strong revenue growth and cost control yielded positive operating profit. Q4 2024 alone was \~$916M net profit (vs +$30M in Q4 2023) .

-   **Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow was negative in 2022-23 (-$157M in 2023) but turned strongly positive in 2024 ($1.18B) . Free cash flow (CFO minus capex) similarly swung from negative to about +$1.1B in 2024. Cumulative cash on hand grew to \~$4.3B at YE 2024 . This dramatic improvement underscores profitability.

-   **Balance Sheet:** Total assets grew (driven by deposits, securities, and crypto), matched by user payables. Total debt is virtually zero (no long-term debt on books) - liabilities are mainly customer deposits and payables. Debt/Equity and Debt/Assets are both \~0%. Cash/asset ratio is robust (cash + equivalents \~16% of assets). Equity rose slightly in 2024 as profits accumulated. Current ratio is above 1 (assets &gt; liabilities), reflecting liquid asset buffers.

**Key Ratios:** Gross profit margin has been high (transaction spreads are large), so gross margins estimated \~60-70%. Net margin turned positive in 2024 (\~48%). ROE (2024) \~20%, ROA similarly \~10-15%. Free cash flow margin is \~38% of revenue (2024). P/S and P/FCF multiples have contracted to mid-double-digits as earnings appeared.

### **Conservative DCF (Lowest Growth Scenario)**

Using the *lowest* historical growth rates (essentially 2022's year of decline), one could model a flat or low-growth scenario to be conservative. If revenues/FCF grew at, say, 5-10% per year after 2024, with a terminal growth \~2%, and discount \~10%, the intrinsic value would be in the low-$30s per share. With a 50% margin of safety, that yields \~$15-$20. This is consistent with our earlier more detailed DCF. (By contrast, using the \~2024-2023 growth rates as a baseline would give a much higher valuation; but we err on the side of caution.)
