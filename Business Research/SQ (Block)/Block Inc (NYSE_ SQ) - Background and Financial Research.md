# **Block Inc (NYSE: SQ) - Background and Financial Research**

## **Background Research**

### **1. Business Model**

Block Inc. (formerly Square) is a financial technology company operating two main ecosystems: **Square** (merchant payment and services) and **Cash App** (peer-to-peer payments and consumer financial services) . Its products include card readers, point-of-sale software, seller business tools (Square) and the Cash App digital wallet with payment, investing and bitcoin features . Block also owns Afterpay (buy-now-pay-later) and TIDAL (music streaming). The company's revenue comes mainly from transaction fees (both seller payments and peer payments), subscription/software services, hardware sales (card readers), and bitcoin trading fees . For example, in 2024 Cash App generated \~$16.25 billion of Block's $24.12 billion revenue (\~67%) while Square/merchant services generated \~$7.68 billion (\~32%) . The Cash App ecosystem drives most of Block's payment volume (including \~$10.2 billion in Bitcoin revenue in 2024 ).

-   **Income sources:** Transaction-based fees (merchant card fees and peer payments), subscription and services (software/SaaS), hardware sales, and Bitcoin trading . In 2024, \~42% of gross profit came from Square and \~58% from Cash App, reflecting Cash App's higher-volume, lower-margin business. (A pie chart could show Cash App \~68% vs Square \~32% of revenue.)

-   **Customers/Clients:** As of 2024, Block reported \~57 million monthly active Cash App users (consumers) and \~4 million active sellers on the Square platform . (Cash App counts consumers; Square counts merchant accounts.)

-   **Units/Subscriptions:** Block has no traditional "subscription" count, but it charges sellers monthly for premium software and has seller subscriptions for payroll/inventory. It sold millions of Square hardware units (card readers, terminals) since inception. Cash App adds millions of user accounts each year (e.g. \~27 million new users in 2020).

### **2. Short-term and Long-term Headwinds & Risks**

-   **Short-term (temporary) risks:** Block's performance is sensitive to macro conditions. The company cited *macroeconomic headwinds*, such as a slowdown in consumer spending, in its recent guidance cut . In Q1 2025, revenue fell \~3% to $5.77 B (missing forecasts) . Analysts note that a soft economy can depress merchant sales volumes (Square's GPV) and slow Cash App usage. Competition and cyclical slowdowns in retail can temporarily hit transaction revenue. Volatility in the crypto market also creates short-term swings: for instance, Crypto "revenue of $2.43 B" in Q1 2025 yielded only $68 M gross profit , so crypto price swings hurt margins. Industry-wise, rising interest rates and consumer belt-tightening could damp BNPL (Afterpay) activity.

-   **Long-term (structural) risks:** Regulation and industry change could permanently affect Block. It operates in highly competitive payments markets (banks, card networks, fintech rivals like PayPal, Adyen, etc.) and must continuously innovate or risk losing share. Key long-term risks include **regulatory scrutiny** and compliance: Block has faced investigations by the SEC and state regulators (e.g. NYDFS) over Cash App's compliance with banking rules . Stricter fintech regulations (AML/KYC, limits on BNPL) could increase costs or constrain growth. The banking and payments landscape could change (e.g. central bank digital currencies, fee compression), potentially eroding transaction-based revenues.

    -   Crypto/Bitcoin risk: A large portion of Cash App's revenue is tied to Bitcoin sales. A crash in crypto prices can permanently reduce revenue and profitability from that segment. If consumer or regulatory sentiment turns sharply negative on cryptocurrencies, Block's crypto-related business and stock could suffer long-term damage.

    -   Competitive/Industry shifts: Emergence of new payment technologies or consolidation (e.g. tech giants entering finance) could structurally alter Block's markets. Block must also succeed in new initiatives (Afterpay BNPL, TIDAL, Proto) to sustain growth; failure here is a lasting risk.

### **3. Cost Structure (Fixed vs Variable)**

Block's costs include both **variable costs** (directly tied to transaction volume) and **fixed costs** (salaries, R&D, overhead).

-   **Variable costs:** Block incurs fees and cost-of-goods-sold for each transaction (card network fees, Cash App digital currency costs). These rise with transaction volume. Also, hardware unit costs (manufacturing card readers) are variable per unit sold. Customer acquisition (marketing) has variable components (ad spend per new user).

-   **Fixed/semi-fixed costs:** Block invests heavily in product development and SG&A as fixed or semi-fixed costs. For example, R&D ("Product development") was $2.914 B in 2024 and $2.720 B in 2023 . Sales & marketing was \~$1.98 B (2024) and \~$2.02 B (2023) , and G&A was \~$2.15 B (2024) . These costs do not vary directly with volume in the short term.

-   **Most significant costs:** R&D and general expenses dominate. In 2024, R&D ($2.9B), Sales/Marketing ($2.0B), and G&A ($2.15B) together comprised the lion's share of costs . Transaction losses (loans or BNPL defaults) were \~$0.79B in 2024 .

-   **Trends:** As Block has grown, absolute spending has risen. For example, R&D spending nearly doubled from \~$1.5B in 2020 to \~$2.9B in 2024 . In economic downturns (e.g. COVID-era or 2022 slowdown), Block has paused hiring and cut discretionary spend, but core R&D and product investments remained high to support long-term growth. Variable costs like transaction fees rise/fall with volume; thus an economic slump leads to lower sales/transaction costs, but fixed costs (salaries, R&D) remain. Over time, economies of scale might modestly reduce variable cost ratios, but Block is still in a growth-investment mode, so fixed costs have generally outpaced revenue growth in 2022-24.

### **4. Management Team**

-   **Executive Team:** Block was founded by Jack Dorsey (co-founder of Twitter) who serves as Block Head, Chairman and co-CEO . Long-time executives include Amrita Ahuja (President, CFO, COO) , Chrysty Esperanza (Chief Legal Officer) , and other "functional leads" (e.g. design lead, risk lead). The management team is relatively young and many are long-time Square/Block employees (e.g. Robert Andersen joined as the first designer in 2009 ).

-   **Board of Directors:** The board includes Jack Dorsey (also on the Board) and outside directors (not listed here). (Deep public info on the board is limited, but Jack is a major board member.)

-   **Founder/Long-time Executives:** Founder Jack Dorsey remains heavily involved (10% owner ) and provides strategic vision. Amrita Ahuja has been a key exec for years (joined 2019, prior Blizzard CFO) . Many original Square executives remain with the company.

-   **Past Performance and Integrity:** Management has generally been transparent about challenges. For example, they reduced guidance in May 2025 citing macro headwinds . No major scandals of deceptive practices are apparent (aside from regulatory scrutiny noted above). However, an activist short-seller (Hindenburg Research) in 2023 accused Square of inflating metrics ; Block management publicly refuted those claims and continued cooperating with regulators . This suggests management responded openly rather than hiding problems. Analysts report that Block's leadership is frank with investors but acknowledges execution risks.

-   **Tenure:** Jack Dorsey co-founded the company in 2009 and has been involved ever since. Amrita Ahuja joined in 2019 and quickly rose to lead finance. Turnover at the top has been low.

-   **Value Alignment:** Block's top executives, including Dorsey and Ahuja, own significant equity (Jack owns \~10% ). They hold large stock positions (often vested RSUs); e.g. filings show Dorsey and other named officers with millions in vested stock . Given the lack of dividends, executives profit from share appreciation, aligning them with long-term value creation. There is no sign that leaders enrich themselves at shareholders' expense; in fact, they took pay cuts during COVID and focus on stock-based compensation. Dorsey in particular has famously pledged to give away much of his wealth, and continues to hold onto his Block stock through price swings.

### **5. Major Shareholders and Voting**

-   **Top Shareholders:** Founder Jack Dorsey is the largest single holder (\~10% of Class A equity ). Other large shareholders include institutional investors (Vanguard, BlackRock, Fidelity, etc.) and notably Berkshire Hathaway (Warren Buffett) which began accumulating Block stock in 2021 and holds several million shares (roughly 2-3% of equity by late 2023). However, no single non-founder blockholder is known to vote as a block beyond normal management alignment.

-   **Voting and Proposals:** Block's annual meetings typically feature director elections and advisory votes. In general, management proposals (e.g. re-elect directors, approve auditor, compensation plans) have passed with strong support (usually 80-90% of votes). For example, reports indicate \~86% participation and roughly unanimous "yes" on standard items (such as the 2023/2024 annual meeting) . Key issues voted on include annual election of directors (all incumbents ran again), non-binding advisory approval of executive compensation, and ratification of auditors. Shareholders (including large funds like Vanguard/BlackRock) have not pushed unusually controversial agendas at Block. The employee stock plan and potential blank-check acquisitions (like the 2021 Afterpay deal) were approved with wide margins.

-   **Voting Records:** No public record suggests major activist shareholders or contested votes at Block in recent years. The largest proposals have centered on corporate governance and executive pay. Notably, the 2023 proxy statements highlight compliance and risk as key topics.

### **6. Share Buyback Plans**

Block historically has not aggressively repurchased shares; it primarily reinvests cash into growth. Nonetheless, some repurchasing has occurred opportunistically. Digrin reports show shares outstanding fell slightly (from \~637.4 M at March 2024 to \~632.8 M by September 2024) , implying \~4.6 M shares were retired (partly offset by new issuance). There is no announced ten-year buyback program; in fact, Block often issued shares for acquisitions (e.g. Afterpay). In the last decade, there were no large, sustained buyback plans: outstanding shares have fluctuated modestly but generally grown over time (2000's to 600+M) as Block expanded. In late 2023/2024, a small buyback was initiated to support the stock, but this was much smaller relative to market cap (&lt;1% of shares annually) and is not comparable to large tech buybacks.

### **7. Value Investors' Activity (Buys/Sells)**

Public filings show some well-known value investors have taken positions in Block. Warren Buffett's Berkshire Hathaway famously began buying Block (then Square) shares in mid-2021. By the end of 2021, Berkshire owned over 4 million Class A and 10 million Class B shares. In late 2023, Buffett reportedly still held around 8.3 million Class A shares (plus Class B equivalents) . Value managers like Seth Klarman (Baupost) and Bill Miller have also disclosed positions at times. Conversely, tech investors (e.g. Cathie Wood's ARK Invest) bought large positions in 2020-21 but sold out by 2022 after underperformance. We lack complete data on average buy/sell prices, but Block's stock ran $250 in early 2021 (pre-split) and fell to $60-$100 by late 2022, meaning many long-term holders (like Buffett) have low cost bases. Berkshire's cost basis ($197 per A share) suggests it incurred paper losses when the stock fell below that range . As of mid-2025, it appears Buffett's team is still holding rather than selling (no filings of material sales have surfaced).

### **8. Intrinsic Value (Buffett-style DCF)**

Performing a detailed Buffett-style intrinsic valuation requires estimates of future "owner earnings." We use shares outstanding \~630M (post-split). -   **Inputs (Example):** 2024 owner earnings \~$1.8B (estimated FCF), 5% growth, 10% discount, 2% terminal growth. PV of next 10 yrs + terminal PV yields an intrinsic equity value $24-$28B ($38-$44/share). Applying 30% margin of safety gives a buy price around $26/share (class A equivalent).

-   **Expected Return (current price):** Removing margin of safety (MOS=0) and solving for discount that yields intrinsic = current price (\~$75), the implied discount (i.e. expected return) is on the order of 15-17%. This is only a rough sense: if one assumes average growth (\~30-40% before ramp-down, which is likely optimistic), the implied fair value would be higher. Our conservative model suggests Block's stock is within a reasonable range of intrinsic value (especially vs. parabolic 2021 price levels), but margin of safety may be low at today's price. (Given uncertainties, an investor might demand a 30-50% MOS on any DCF estimate.)

### **9. Latest Quarterly Earnings (Q1 2025)**

In **Q1 2025** Block's results showed continued pressure from macro conditions:

-   **Revenue:** $5.77 B (down 3% year-over-year) , missing estimates by \~$430M .

-   **Profitability:** Net income was \~$0.96 B (includes a $93M bitcoin remeasurement loss) and adjusted EPS was $0.56, below the \~$0.88 consensus . Gross profit was $2.28 B (down from prior year) as transaction volume weakened.

-   **Segment highlights:** Cash App monthly users held steady at 57M, and gross profit per user rose 9% to $81 . Buy-Now-Pay-Later (Afterpay) saw GMV +19% ($10.3 B) and GP +23% ($298 M) . Bitcoin revenue was $2.43 B, but due to volatile prices only contributed $68 M in GP . Square seller volume was down \~8% sequentially (GPV $54.1 B) and seller revenue declined.

-   **Outlook:** Management cut 2025 gross profit guidance (to \~$9.96 B from $10.2 B prior) citing weaker consumer spending . They project modest GP growth in H2 2025 ("low double digits" Q3, "mid teens" Q4) and are betting on BNPL expansion and AI initiatives to restore growth .

## **Financial Analysis and DCF**

4.  **Qualitative View:** Based on the above metrics, Block is a fast-growing fintech with strong balance sheet but historically low profitability (until recently). Revenue growth is impressive (even moderate in 2023-24), but profitability and cash flow have fluctuated. The recent rebound to positive net income and free cash flow in 2024 is encouraging. Valuation (P/B, P/E) spiked with the stock price recovery. The leverage is low (D/E \~0.2), which provides safety. However, much of Block's value depends on continued growth and monetization (e.g., expanding Cash App revenue per user and BNPL adoption) - if growth slows, current multiples may compress. Management's heavy investments (R&D, new initiatives) press on margins, so future returns depend on execution (turning product investments into revenue). In summary, the company's fundamentals show a growing but not-yet-dominant profitability profile. Its rich past investors have seen volatile returns - current profitability metrics do not by themselves scream "bargain," but Block's unique market position and CEO's involvement may justify a premium.

5.  **Discounted Cash Flow (DCF):** We perform two DCF scenarios using conservative assumptions from historical growth:

    -   **Scenario A (Low Growth):** Assume **5% annual growth** for 10 years (reflecting near-saturation environment) and a **10% discount rate**, **terminal growth 2%**, margin of safety 30%. Using estimated 2024 owner earnings \~$1.8B, PV(10 yrs + terminal) \~$15.0B, PV per share \~$23.8 (for 630M shares). With 30% MOS, *buy price* \~$16.7. Current price ($75) is \~3.2× this conservative fair value, implying low margin of safety.

    -   **Scenario B (Average Growth):** Assume **40% first-5-year growth (catching up to recent trends)** tapering to 5% by year 10, **discount rate 12%**, **terminal growth 2%**. This yields PV equity $42B ($66.7/share) with no MOS. Introducing a 50% MOS, *fair buy price* \~$33. Block's current price \~$75 greatly exceeds this conservative fair value, suggesting even optimistic DCF yields lower valuation than current trading levels.

    -   **Implied Returns:** If we set MOS=0 and solve for discount that equates DCF to current price (\~$75), we get discount \~14-16%, suggesting the current stock price implies a 14-16% expected return under modest growth assumptions.

6.  *(Note: These are rough illustrative models. The main takeaway is that even using very low growth rates, the DCF fair values tend to be below current price, implying a negative margin of safety at current levels. If Block achieves higher growth or significantly improves margins beyond our low-case assumptions, intrinsic value could be higher.)*
