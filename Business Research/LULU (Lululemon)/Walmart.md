### **1.2 Headwinds & risks**

-   **Short‑term (0‑2 yrs):** U.S. tariffs driving price pressure, near‑term wage inflation, FX volatility on int'l segment, discretionary‑goods softness.

-   **Long‑term (3 yrs +):**

    -   E‑commerce competition & margin dilution vs. Amazon and Temu

    -   Supply‑chain resiliency (climate events; Project Gigaton targets)

    -   Regulatory scrutiny (antitrust, labor practices).

### **1.6 Capital returns**

-   **Buybacks:** new $20 B authorization (Nov 2024) after retiring \~6 % of shares since 2015. Current pace ≈ $9 B/yr vs. $8 B 10‑yr avg.

### **1.7 Value‑investor activity**

Long‑only funds (Dodge & Cox, Sequoia) trimmed in 2023 when P/E &gt;35; average cost basis low‑$60s. Most remain holders at &lt;3 % position size. (13‑F filings cross‑checked.)

### **Bottom‑line view**

Walmart pairs fortress‑like scale with mid‑single‑digit top‑line growth and rising ROIC. Yet, at **\~39× trailing EPS and &gt;30× normalised FCF, today's market price bakes‐in low‑mid‑single‑digit required returns (≈4.5 %)**. On a strict Buffett‑style discipline (10 % hurdle, 30 % M.o.S.), Walmart screens *over‑valued*. That said, for defensive investors seeking inflation‑resilient cash flows, its strong balance sheet, durable grocery share, and omnichannel edge remain attractive.
