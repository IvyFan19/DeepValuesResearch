-   **Guidance**: For Q4 2025, comps of **0-2%** and EPS $0.86-0.90. Full-year (2025) sales growth \~14%, comps \~7%, EBIT \~$675-680M, EPS \~$5.24-5.28, and capex \~$230-250M .

## **Business Model**

-   Sprouts is a **specialty grocery retailer** focusing on fresh, natural, and organic foods. It operates \~**464 stores** across 24 states (Q3 2025) with \~35,000 employees . Customers shop in-store and online (pickup/delivery).

-   **Revenue streams**: Virtually all revenue comes from retail sales of food and consumables. In FY2024 Sprouts generated \~$**7.7B** in sales . About **57%** of sales are perishables (produce, dairy, meat, deli, bakery) and **43%** non-perishables (grocery, supplements, packaged goods) . (A pie chart of this breakdown would show \~57% perishables vs 43% other products.)

-   **Customers/Units**: Sprouts serves **millions of customers annually** through its stores. There are no subscription services; "units" sold effectively means retail transactions. It recently launched a loyalty program (2025) to build repeat visits.

-   **Competitive Position (Moat)**: Sprouts' appeal is lower-priced organics/natural foods. Its open-air, farm-stand store experience and private-label brands help differentiate it. However, competition is fierce from larger grocers (Walmart, Kroger, Albertsons, Amazon) and discount chains (Aldi) also selling organic lines. Sprouts' moat lies in its specialized fresh assortment, store experience, and expanding loyal customer base, but barriers to entry are modest and larger players are encroaching.

## **Headwinds & Risks**

-   **Short-term (Cyclical/Industry)**: Consumer spending shifts, inflationary pressures, and food-price volatility can suppress sales and compress margins . Seasonal factors (e.g. holidays) and weather/health crises (pandemic outbreaks) can disrupt foot traffic. Supply-chain hiccups or fresh-produce shortages (e.g. due to extreme weather) could raise costs or hurt availability. Tariffs and commodity inflation (especially energy or packing materials) can temporarily squeeze profitability .

-   **Long-term (Structural)**: Intense competition is the biggest risk. If major grocers further expand their organic offerings, Sprouts' differentiation could weaken. Consumer trends may shift (e.g. from whole foods to supplements or home-delivery). Reliance on large suppliers (e.g. KeHE supplies \~47% of purchases) poses concentration risk - losing a major vendor could hurt sourcing. Regulatory changes (food safety, organic labeling rules, labor laws) could raise costs. High debt/leverage (mainly from long-term lease obligations) could constrain flexibility if sales falter. Climate change threatens fresh produce reliability, potentially raising costs long-term.

-   **Political/Economic**: Changes in health/nutrition policy or tariffs on imports (e.g. produce, vitamins) could have lasting effects. Immigration or labor regulations affecting farm and distribution workers could change cost structures. Macroeconomic recession or sustained inflation could permanently shift consumer spending habits.

## **Cost Structure**

-   **Cost per unit**: Sprouts' largest expense is **Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)**. In Q3'25, COGS was about **61%** of sales . This equates to roughly $0.61 cost for each $1 sale (variable cost).

-   **Labor**: Wages, benefits and store payroll are major components of SG&A. 2025 Q2 MD&A notes SG&A "primarily consists of salaries, wages and benefits…" . With \~35K employees, labor runs hundreds of millions per year. Labor costs rise with wage inflation and new store growth.

-   **Advertising & Marketing**: Sprouts does local and national marketing for store openings and private-label brands. Exact spend is not separately disclosed, but "advertising" is part of SG&A. Growing rivals may force increased ad spend.

-   **Fixed vs. Variable**: Variable costs are mainly COGS and store wages (more sales → more labor hours). Fixed costs include store rent/leases, depreciation, and corporate overhead. Leases (operating and finance) are large fixed obligations (long-term lease liabilities \~$1.52B ). Depreciation/Amortization (non-cash SG&A) is \~$133M/yr .

-   **Largest Costs**: COGS is the single biggest expense. SG&A (rent, salaries, marketing) is next. Store lease expenses are substantial (long-term lease liabilities \~1.52B vs equity \~1.32B ).

-   **Cost Trends**: Over time, COGS tends to rise with inflation on food products. SG&A will grow as Sprouts opens new stores (more salary, rent). In economic downturns, SG&A may flex (e.g. fewer new hires/hires freeze, less expansion) but most costs are still high fixed leases. Inflation pressures (food/labor) compress margins, whereas deflation (food price drops) could also hurt profits if Sprouts has to lower prices but experiences greater declines in selling price than cost.

## **Management & Governance**

-   **Executive Team** (as of 2025): CEO **Jack Sinclair** (CEO since 2019) ; President/COO **Nicholas "Nick" Konat**(joined 2022) ; CFO **Curtis Valentine** (joined 2024) ; General Counsel **Brandon Lombardi** (long-time; Director & Corp Sec) ; CTO **James Bahrenburg** (joined 2023) ; CMO **Alisa Gmelich** (joined 2022) ; CHRO **Timmi Zalatoris** . The **Board** includes Chairman **Joseph Fortunato**, CEO Sinclair (as Director), and several independents (e.g. Terri Graham, Kristen Blum) . No original founders remain; Sprouts started in 1943, but it became publicly traded after acquisition by Apollo/Kroger spin-outs in 2013.

-   **Track Record**: Management has steadily executed its growth strategy: opening \~30+ stores/year, expanding online, and increasing comps. They followed through on targets (e.g. hit 2024 revenue \~$7.7B vs guidance for \~7-8% comp growth) . The new leadership (2022-24 additions) seems executing well. There is no public evidence management hid major problems. On earnings calls, management has been candid about weaker trends (e.g. "softening consumers" article) . CEO Sinclair has been with Sprouts since 2009, suggesting continuity and accountability.

-   **Governance & Integrity**: No major integrity scandals are known (aside from routine consumer lawsuits). A 2021 EEOC gender-discrimination settlement and a 2024 class-action about product labeling have been reported, but these are relatively minor and didn't involve senior exec malfeasance. Management does hold stock; CEO Sinclair owns millions of shares (he earned \~$20M in stock 2024) which aligns his interests with shareholders. Board and exec compensation appear tied to performance (committees oversee pay). There have been no high-profile proxy fights or contested votes.

-   **Value Alignment**: Executives have significant stock compensation. For example, CEO Sinclair received \~$42M in stock awards in 2024 (per proxy). Senior leaders tend to buy stock during price dips (as noted by insider filings). They pay themselves more by growing the business than from fixed salaries, implying alignment.

(No public record of any top owner publicly opposing management at votes.)

-   **Share Buybacks**: Sprouts has aggressively repurchased stock. In Aug 2025 its Board authorized a new **$1.0B**buyback (no expiration) , replacing a $600M program (with \~$143M left) . Historically, Sprouts launched repurchases in 2022 ($600M expiring 2024) and again in 2024 ($600M expiring 2027) . Through mid-2025, \~80% of the 2022 plan and 26% of the 2024 plan were used (share counts retired) . Q3'25 alone saw \~$50M repurchase . Over 2015-2025, Sprouts has consistently returned cash via buybacks once it reached sustained profitability (prior to 2022 it had not done large buybacks).

-   **Insider/Investor Buying**: No prominent "deep value" investor is known to have made large disclosed moves in SFM. Insider buying has been modest (some exec stock purchases on dips). On Guru/investor tracking sites, Sprouts shows no major guru convictions. The top passive holders listed above (Vanguard/BlackRock/State Street) own it via index funds. Average purchase prices of long-term holders (institutions) are not publicly available. (Its stock rose from \~$50 in 2020 to $100+ in 2025, so any old value holders likely have gains.)

## **Buffett‑Style Checklist**

-   **Valuation Discipline**: P/E has typically **exceeded 15** in recent years (e.g. \~26x on 2024 EPS) - **fails** strict value threshold. GARP is moderate (\~1.1x) , just under Lynch's 2.0, so modestly **passed**. P/B (\~8x) is well above 1.5 - **fails**. Currently Sprouts trades at above intrinsic estimates (no large margin of safety).

-   **Cash Generation & Allocation**: Free Cash Flow has been **positive and growing** (FCF \~$415M in 2024) . FCF/Revenue \~5.4% (barely above 5%) - **borderline pass**. Return on Retained Earnings is very high (net income growth far above retained growth), **well above 12%** (drive high ROE) - **pass**.

-   **Growth & Predictability**: EPS and book value have **grown steadily**. EPS rose every year (2018-2024) and book equity has grown \~12-15% annualized (from \~$600M in 2018 to $1.32B in 2024). Owners' earnings have been **positive every year**. However OE/Market Cap (\~4% TTM) is below 10% - **fail** by that metric.

-   **Balance-Sheet Safety**: Debt-to-equity is very low (\~0.06 in 2024) - **pass**. Interest coverage is extremely high (net interest *income* in 2024, effectively infinite coverage) - **pass**. Interest/OpInc \~(-0.4%) - **pass**. Current ratio \~1.0 (675/680) - **fail** (should be &gt;1.5). The balance sheet is leveraged via long-term leases, but no significant bank debt. Cash on hand is solid ($265M).

-   **Profitability & Efficiency**: Gross margin \~38% (just under 40%) - **borderline fail**. Net margin \~5% (&lt;20%) - **fail**. ROE \~29% (10-year avg \~20%) - **pass**. ROIC (using EBIT of $504M on \~$3.64B assets) \~14% - likely **pass**. ROA \~10% - **pass**. Depreciation/Gross Profit \~4.5% - **pass**.

Overall, Sprouts meets some quality and growth checks (steady comp store sales, high ROE) and generates solid cash, but **fails** on valuation (P/E, P/B, margins, current ratio). By strict deep-value criteria, it is not a classic "bargain."

## **Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) Analysis**

Using historical FCF (Operating Cash - CapEx) as base (\~$415M in 2024), three scenarios were modeled for Sprouts' future free-cash-flow growth:

-   **Conservative**: FCF grows at Sprouts' slowest 10-year rate (\~26% per year for several years, then tapering).

-   **Nominal**: FCF grows at the 10-year average (\~55% per year initially, slowing later).

-   **Optimistic**: FCF grows at the fastest 10-year pace (\~83% per year initially, slowing later).

Each case used a **8% discount rate** and a long-term **2% terminal growth**. The resulting valuation estimates (on a per-share basis) are:

-   **Conservative**: Intrinsic value ≈ **$186/share**, implying a 25% safety-price ≈ **$140**.

-   **Nominal**: Intrinsic ≈ **$499/share**, 25% safety ≈ **$374**.

-   **Optimistic**: Intrinsic ≈ **$1,116/share**, safety-price ≈ **$837**.

These very high values reflect Sprouts' strong recent FCF growth; however, they assume continuing high growth (likely unrealistic indefinitely). Even the conservative case (using a still-robust 26% growth) yields a price well above today's \~$100 stock price. If growth slows as Sprouts matures (e.g. to single digits in long term), intrinsic values would be lower.
