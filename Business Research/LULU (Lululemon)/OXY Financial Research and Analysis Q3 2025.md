# Occidental Petroleum (OXY) - Financial Research and Analysis

## Part I: Executive Summary and Recent Performance

### Latest Quarter Earnings Analysis (Q3 2025)

Occidental Petroleum (OXY) announced its third-quarter 2025 financial results on November 10, 2025, with a conference call for investors scheduled for November 11, 2025.<sup>1</sup>

**Analyst Expectations vs. Prevailing Narrative**

Market consensus anticipated a challenging quarter, reflecting a difficult year-over-year comparison. Analysts projected earnings per share (EPS) of $0.51, representing a 49% decrease from the $1.00 per share reported in the prior-year quarter. Similarly, the full-year 2025 EPS forecast stood at $2.27, a significant 34.4% decline from the $3.46 reported in fiscal 2024.<sup>5</sup> The company's 8-K filing on November 10, 2025, furnished the official press release (Exhibit 99.1) detailing the quarter's financial performance.<sup>6</sup>

However, the specific financial metrics of the Q3 2025 release are secondary to the dominant, transformative narrative that will define the conference call: the "Oxy Strategic Transaction Update".<sup>2</sup>

**The OxyChem Divestiture: A Fundamental Strategic Pivot**

On October 2, 2025, Occidental announced a definitive agreement to sell its entire chemical business, Occidental Chemical Corporation (OxyChem), to Berkshire Hathaway in an all-cash transaction valued at $9.7 billion.<sup>7</sup>

This divestiture is the most significant strategic move for the company in 2025 and fundamentally alters its investment thesis. The transaction effectively represents a strategic pivot:

1.  **Enabling Deleveraging:** The $9.7 billion in cash proceeds are not primarily for shareholder returns. Instead, they are earmarked to accelerate deleveraging, particularly to pay down debt incurred from the $12 billion acquisition of CrownRock in 2024 and to meet 2025 debt maturities.<sup>8</sup>

2.  **Funding the Future:** This move frees up capital and management focus to fund the company's high-risk, high-reward "bet-the-company" strategy in Carbon Capture (CCUS) and Direct Air Capture (DAC).<sup>9</sup>

3.  **Increasing Volatility:** By divesting OxyChem, Occidental is shedding a highly stable, profitable, and counter-cyclical business. In 2024, the chemical segment generated $4.9 billion in sales and $1.1 billion in segment results.<sup>11</sup> Its removal transforms OXY into a more volatile, pure-play exploration and production (E&P) company, wholly dependent on commodity price cycles.

## Part II: Background Research and Qualitative Analysis

### Business Model

**1. Primary Income Sources (Pre-Divestiture)**

Prior to the announced sale of OxyChem, Occidental operated a diversified model across three primary segments <sup>11</sup>:

1.  **Oil and Gas:** The core E&P segment. This involves the exploration, development, and production of oil, natural gas liquids (NGLs), and natural gas in the United States (primarily the Permian and DJ basins), the Middle East, and North Africa.<sup>11</sup>

2.  **Chemical (OxyChem):** The segment being sold. This business manufactured and marketed basic chemicals and vinyls, including chlorine, caustic soda, and polyvinyl chloride (PVC).<sup>11</sup>

3.  **Midstream and Marketing:** This segment provides flow assurance and value maximization for the Oil and Gas segment. It includes gathering, processing, transportation, and marketing of oil, gas, NGLs, and CO2. This segment also houses the Oxy Low Carbon Ventures (OLCV) subsidiary.<sup>12</sup>

The 2024 10-K filing provides a clear breakdown of the financial contribution of each segment, highlighting the significance of the OxyChem divestiture.<sup>11</sup>

**2. Customers and Units Sold**

As an upstream commodity producer, Occidental's "customers" are not discrete individuals or subscribers but rather purchasers on the global commodity markets. The primary "unit" of sale is the Barrel of Oil Equivalent (BOE).

Production volumes are the key metric for the core Oil and Gas segment. Recent production has been robust, integrating the new CrownRock assets <sup>13</sup>:

-   **Q4 2024 Production:** 1,463 Mboed (thousand barrels of oil equivalent per day) <sup>14</sup>

-   **Q1 2025 Production:** 1,391 Mboed <sup>15</sup>

-   **Q2 2025 Production:** 1,400 Mboed <sup>16</sup>

**3. The Company Moat: A High-Risk Transition**

Occidental's competitive advantage is in a state of deliberate, high-stakes transition.

-   **The "Legacy Moat":** Occidental's historical moat is built on deep, world-class technical expertise in two key areas:

    1.  **Permian Basin Operations:** OXY is one of the largest and most technically proficient producers in the Permian Basin, the premier U.S. shale play.<sup>11</sup> This dominant position was recently expanded with the $12 billion CrownRock acquisition, adding high-grade, low-cost assets in the Midland Basin.<sup>13</sup>

    2.  **Enhanced Oil Recovery (EOR):** The company is a "global leader" in using carbon dioxide (CO2) for EOR.<sup>18</sup> It is the "largest commercial purchaser and injector of CO2 for EOR in the Permian Basin," giving it decades of experience in managing, transporting, and sequestering CO2 underground.<sup>18</sup>

-   **The "New Moat":** Management is leveraging this legacy EOR expertise to build its new, future-facing moat: **Carbon Management**.

    -   This pivot is executed through its subsidiaries, Oxy Low Carbon Ventures (OLCV) and 1PointFive.<sup>19</sup>

    -   The strategy is to build a large-scale, profitable business in Carbon Capture, Utilization, and Sequestration (CCUS) and Direct Air Capture (DAC).

    -   The **STRATOS** facility, OXY's first commercial-scale DAC plant, is the centerpiece of this strategy.<sup>11</sup>

    -   This is the core of the modern OXY thesis: the company is using its "old" moat (expertise in CO2 injection) to build its "new" moat (DAC). To fund this capital-intensive and unproven venture, it has sold its stable, profitable chemical division.<sup>7</sup> OXY is intentionally shedding diversification and increasing its risk profile to make a focused, levered bet on the future carbon economy.

### Short-Term and Long-Term Headwinds and Risks

**1. Short-Term Headwinds and Risks**

-   **Commodity Price Volatility:** The business is "highly dependent" on oil and gas prices.<sup>11</sup> The divestiture of the stable OxyChem business will significantly exacerbate earnings volatility, making the company a pure-play bet on energy prices.

-   **Executional Risk:** The company is simultaneously integrating the massive $12 billion CrownRock acquisition <sup>13</sup> while navigating the complex $9.7 billion divestiture of OxyChem.<sup>7</sup> This places an enormous executional burden on management.

-   **Balance Sheet Pressure:** Following the CrownRock acquisition, OXY's debt load increased.<sup>21</sup> Management is in a race to apply the $9.7 billion in cash proceeds from the OxyChem sale to "accelerate... deleveraging" and meet remaining 2025 debt maturities.<sup>8</sup> Any delay in closing or a downturn in operating cash flow could create a liquidity crunch.

**2. Long-Term and Permanent Risks**

-   **DAC Execution Risk:** The entire long-term strategy, and the justification for the OxyChem sale, rests on the unproven economics of Direct Air Capture.<sup>23</sup> Profitability is highly dependent on construction costs, operational efficiency, market demand for carbon credits, and, critically, "tax attributes"-that is, government subsidies.<sup>18</sup>

-   **Political and Regulatory Risk:** The DAC business model is explicitly reliant on government support. Recent news noted the risk of the U.S. government potentially canceling "$12 billion in funding for clean energy projects," a move that would be catastrophic for OLCV's long-term business model.<sup>24</sup>

-   **Reserve Replacement Risk:** As an E&P company, Occidental faces the permanent risk of needing to find or acquire new, economically viable reserves to offset the natural decline of its existing production.<sup>11</sup>

-   **High-Leverage Model:** The 2019 Anadarko acquisition left OXY with a heavy debt burden.<sup>25</sup> The 2024 CrownRock deal repeated this pattern. The company operates with a high-leverage model that is extremely vulnerable to commodity price downturns.

### Corporate Cost Structure

Occidental's cost structure is typical of a capital-intensive E&P company, dominated by variable operating costs and fixed financing costs.

-   **Variable Costs (per unit):** The most significant variable cost is **Lease Operating Expense (LOE)**, the cost to maintain production from existing wells. Q4 2024 data from a press release schedule provides a per-barrel breakdown <sup>26</sup>:

    -   **Total Oil and Gas LOE:** $8.85 per BOE

    -   **Total Transportation Costs:** $3.66 per BOE

-   **Fixed Costs (Annual):** The company's primary fixed costs are **Interest Expense** (from its high debt load) and **Depreciation, Depletion & Amortization (DD&A)** (a non-cash charge for asset depletion).

    -   **Interest Expense (Net):** A major annual burden.

        -   2024: $1,175 million <sup>11</sup>

        -   2023: $945 million <sup>11</sup>

        -   2022: $1,030 million <sup>11</sup>

    -   **DD&A:** A massive non-cash cost.

        -   2024: $7,371 million <sup>11</sup>

Cost Trend Implications:

The $9.7 billion sale of OxyChem will have two opposing effects on the cost structure. First, it will make OXY's profitability more volatile by removing the stable OxyChem cash flows, making the company entirely dependent on the spread between WTI/Brent prices and its LOE/BOE. Second, the $9.7 billion in proceeds will be used to "accelerate... deleveraging" 8, which will permanently reduce the \~$1.1B+ in annual Interest Expense.11 This will lower OXY's corporate breakeven oil price, a key strategic objective.

**2. Past Performance and Track Record**

-   **A "Bet-the-Company" Pattern:** The management team's performance is defined by CEO Vicki Hollub's tenure, which is characterized by high-stakes, "bet-the-company" strategic moves.<sup>23</sup>

-   **The Anadarko Deal (2019):** This $55 billion acquisition was the defining event. -   **Repeating the Playbook:** Management is now repeating this pattern. The 2024 CrownRock acquisition <sup>17</sup> and the simultaneous pivot to the unproven, capital-intensive DAC business <sup>19</sup> are *exactly* the same high-conviction, high-leverage approach. The $9.7B OxyChem sale <sup>7</sup> is simply the financing mechanism to enable this new bet.

### Capital Allocation

**1. Current Share Buyback Plan**

Occidental's capital allocation has shifted dramatically. After the 2019 Anadarko deal, all excess cash flow was directed at debt reduction and redeeming the expensive preferred stock held by Berkshire Hathaway.<sup>34</sup>

In February 2023, the company announced its first significant common stock repurchase program in years: a $3.0 billion buyback authorization.<sup>11</sup>

-   **Program Status:** As of December 31, 2024, **$1.2 billion** remained available under this program.<sup>11</sup>

-   **10-Year History:** This program marks a turning point. Prior to this, share repurchases were suspended as the company focused on survival and deleveraging.<sup>35</sup>

**2. Buy and Sell History of Value Investors (Berkshire Hathaway)**

The primary value investor of record is Warren Buffett (Berkshire Hathaway).

-   **Buy/Sell History:** Berkshire's involvement began in 2019 with a $10 billion preferred stock investment to fund the Anadarko deal.<sup>36</sup> They began accumulating their massive *common stock* stake on February 28, 2022.<sup>37</sup>

-   **Status:** Berkshire has been a consistent and aggressive buyer, *not* a seller. Recent purchases include +763,000 shares in February 2025 <sup>38</sup> and +8.9 million shares reported in their Q4 2024 filing.<sup>32</sup>

-   **Average Buy Price:** Berkshire's estimated cost basis for its common stock position is consistently reported in the **mid-$50s range**.

    -   Estimate 1: $54.20 <sup>38</sup>

    -   Estimate 2: $54.07 <sup>36</sup>

    -   Estimate 3: "low to mid $50 range" <sup>39</sup>

-   **Holding Status:** Berkshire holds 34.07% of the common stock <sup>28</sup> and has stated they "might own \[it\] forever".<sup>40</sup> This cost basis (mid-$50s) serves as a critical psychological benchmark and anchor for the market, representing a price at which a significant, long-term value investor sees a compelling entry point.

### Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) Analysis

Given the extreme volatility in earnings, a traditional two-stage DCF is unreliable. A more robust valuation is achieved using a perpetuity model based on **Owner's Earnings** (proxied by Free Cash Flow: Cash Flow From Operations - Total Capital Expenditures).

**Key Inputs:**

-   **Base Year Owner's Earnings (2024):** $6,094 million <sup>50</sup>

-   **Discount Rate (WACC):** 10.0% (Assumed, reflecting high cyclicality and leverage)

-   **Shares Outstanding:** 939.8 million <sup>28</sup>

Case 1: No-Growth Case

This case assumes OXY's future investments (including DAC) will only serve to offset the natural decline of its E&P assets, resulting in 0% perpetual growth.

-   **Calculation:**

    -   $$Intrinsic\\ Value = \\frac{Owner's\\ Earnings}{WACC}$$
        > \*
        > $$Intrinsic\\ Value = \\frac{\\$6,094,000,000}{0.10}$$
        > \*
        > $$Intrinsic\\ Value = \\$60,940,000,000$$

-   **Value Per Share:** $60,940,000,000 / 939.8M shares = **$64.84**

-   **Buy Price (30% Margin of Safety):** $64.84 \\times 0.70 = **$45.39**

Case 2: Conservative Case

This case assumes a perpetual decline in FCF of -2.0%, reflecting the depletion of assets and the risk that the high-capital DAC business fails to generate returns. The lowest 10-year FCF growth was -49.7% in 2023 50, which is too extreme for a terminal rate.

-   **Calculation:**

    -   $$Intrinsic\\ Value = \\frac{Owner's\\ Earnings \\times (1 + g)}{WACC - g}$$
        > \*
        > $$Intrinsic\\ Value = \\frac{\\$6,094,000,000 \\times (1 - 0.02)}{0.10 - (-0.02)}$$
        > \*
        > $$Intrinsic\\ Value = \\frac{\\$5,972,120,000}{0.12}$$
        > \*
        > $$Intrinsic\\ Value = \\$49,767,666,667$$

-   **Value Per Share:** $49,767,666,667 / 939.8M shares = **$52.96**

-   **Buy Price (30% Margin of Safety):** $52.96 \\times 0.70 = **$37.07**

Case 3: Nominal Case

This case assumes a modest perpetual growth rate of 2.5%, in line with long-term inflation. This represents the "bull case" where the DAC business and E&P assets successfully generate a small, real return.

-   **Calculation:**

    -   $$Intrinsic\\ Value = \\frac{Owner's\\ Earnings \\times (1 + g)}{WACC - g}$$
        > \*
        > $$Intrinsic\\ Value = \\frac{\\$6,094,000,000 \\times (1 + 0.025)}{0.10 - 0.025}$$
        > \*
        > $$Intrinsic\\ Value = \\frac{\\$6,246,350,000}{0.075}$$
        > \*
        > $$Intrinsic\\ Value = \\$83,284,666,667$$

-   **Value Per Share:** $83,284,666,667 / 939.8M shares = **$88.62**

-   **Buy Price (30% Margin of Safety):** $88.62 \\times 0.70 = **$62.03**

**DCF Valuation Summary**

| **Case**         | **Growth Assumption (g)** | **Intrinsic Value Per Share** | **Buy Price (30% MOS)** |
|------------------|---------------------------|-------------------------------|-------------------------|
| **Conservative** | -2.0% Perpetual Decline   | **$52.96**                    | **$37.07**              |
| **No-Growth**    | 0.0% Perpetual Growth     | **$64.84**                    | **$45.39**              |
| **Nominal**      | +2.5% Perpetual Growth    | **$88.62**                    | **$62.03**              |

The DCF results strongly suggest that the market is pricing OXY as a low-to-no-growth entity. The "Conservative" intrinsic value of $52.96 is remarkably close to Berkshire Hathaway's average purchase price (approx. $54.07 - $54.20).<sup>36</sup>

This implies that Berkshire's cost basis acts as a valuation floor, representing a "no-growth" thesis. The "Nominal" case buy price of $62.03 is very close to the stock's recent trading range. Therefore, an investment at current levels is effectively a bet on the "Nominal Case," where management's high-risk DAC pivot succeeds in generating modest, inflation-beating growth.

## Part V: Concluding Analysis

Occidental Petroleum is not a "Deep Value" investment by any traditional definition. It fails nearly every quantitative standard for balance sheet safety, predictability, and sustainable profitability.

It is, rather, a high-leverage, deeply cyclical, "special situation" equity. The investment thesis rests entirely on a trifecta of qualitative, high-conviction beliefs:

1.  **A Bullish Oil & Gas Thesis:** An investor must believe in a long-term "higher for longer" commodity price environment to support the immense free cash flow generation required to service debt and fund new ventures.

2.  **A Management Thesis:** An investor must trust that CEO Vicki Hollub's "bet-the-company" pivot-first with Anadarko, and now with CrownRock and the DAC business-is a visionary move to create a new, durable moat in the carbon economy.

3.  **A "Buffett Backstop" Thesis:** An investor must rely on the implicit financial and strategic backstop of Berkshire Hathaway. With 34.07% ownership and a direct partnership (via the OxyChem sale), Berkshire provides the patient capital necessary for OXY to execute its long-term, high-risk strategy.

The DCF valuation confirms this. The market price reflects a value anchored by Berkshire's own cost basis, with the entire, multi-billion-dollar DAC venture effectively priced as a "call option" on future success.
