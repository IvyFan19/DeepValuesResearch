## **Background Research**

**1. Business model:** ASML is the world's dominant supplier of semiconductor lithography systems. Its revenue primarily comes from sales of photolithography equipment (especially EUV and DUV machines) and related services (installations, maintenance, spares). In 2024 ASML reported €28.263B in net sales, of which €6.494B (≈23%) was "installed base management" services and the rest (\~€21.769B, ≈77%) new/used system sales. For example, 2024 system revenue included €9.67B from ArF immersion lithography and €7.86B from NXE (EUV) systems. A pie-chart of 2024 revenue would show roughly 75-80% equipment sales vs 20-25% services. Key customers are virtually all major chipmakers: TSMC, Samsung, Intel, SK Hynix, Micron, etc. ASML says it has "a worldwide customer base" across dozens of countries . Estimates suggest each year ASML sells only a few hundred machines (e.g. 380 new systems plus 38 used in 2024), reflecting the high cost per unit (€150-300M each).

-   *Sources:* ASML 2024 financials; product breakdown; company overviews .

**2. Customers:** ASML's clients are the world's leading semiconductor fabs. While no exact customer count is published, the top three (TSMC, Samsung, Intel) alone account for \~65% of ASML's orders/receivables. ASML has "over 60 service points" globally , indicating a broad (if concentrated) base. Key customers include all major memory and logic chipmakers. In practice ASML deals with a few dozen fab companies worldwide.

**3. Short-term headwinds:** The semiconductor industry is cyclical, so near-term risks include memory-DRAM inventory corrections and general capex slowdowns. Macroeconomic factors (recession concerns, high interest rates) can temporarily delay chipmakers' orders. Geopolitically, US/EU export controls and tariffs pose acute challenges. For instance, a proposed 30% EU tariff on certain semiconductor exports could raise the price of ASML's machines and slow shipments. US-driven restrictions on sales to China also loom: China accounted for \~26% of ASML's 2023 sales, so any clampdown could significantly impact revenue. Supply-chain issues (e.g. past neon-gas shortages) remain a short-term concern; ASML is diversifying neon supply to reduce risk. Currency fluctuations and component inflation can also cause temporary margin pressure.

**4. Long-term risks:** Structurally, ASML enjoys near-monopoly on EUV and advanced lithography, but potential long-term threats exist. Prolonged trade barriers could force fabs (especially in China) to invest in alternative patterning technologies, reducing ASML's addressable market. No immediate EUV competitor exists, but radical future tech (e.g. nanoimprint) could be disruptive. Customer concentration is a risk: the top 3 customers represent \~64% of sales, so losing or seeing cutbacks at any of them would hurt ASML. Moore's Law slowing (fewer leading-edge nodes) might eventually compress demand growth. On the positive side, emerging markets like AI, 5G, automotive, and IoT should sustain lithography demand, but political shifts (new export rules) could permanently alter ASML's revenue streams.

**5. Costs (fixed vs variable):** ASML's biggest fixed costs are R&D and SG&A. R&D runs roughly €1.0-1.2B per quarter , and SG&A \~€0.3B/qtr, making innovation a major ongoing expense. Labor (≈42,000 employees) is also largely fixed. Variable costs are dominated by materials/parts for each machine: ASML spends about €15.5B annually on suppliers (roughly half of revenue), including specialized optics and lasers. Per-unit, EUV system COGS is extremely high (each unit costs well over $100M to build). Advertising/marketing is minimal by comparison (ASML sells to few customers). Over time, R&D expense has trended upward with growth, while materials cost scales with production volume. In downturns, ASML can flex inventory and output, but many costs (especially R&D) remain.

-   *Highest costs:* R&D (many €billions/year) and materials. For example, CEO commentary notes Q1 2025 R&D \~€1,140M. These drive most expense; SG&A is comparatively small. Cost structure is variable with production: fewer sales means lower materials cost and slightly less labor/overhead, but fixed R&D/G&A remain substantial.

The team publicly acknowledges challenges: in Q2 2025 ASML warned that geopolitical uncertainty (tariffs) may impact 2026 growth. We found no evidence of fraud or major hidden issues. Management typically answers analyst questions openly (citing geo-economic risks), and delivered on past commitments (e.g. ramping EUV production). **8. Share buyback:** ASML has run large buyback programs in recent years. The current program (2022-2025) authorized €12.0B of repurchases . By end-2024 they had repurchased about €6.14B (including €4.64B in 2022, €1.0B in 2023, €0.50B in 2024). At the 2025 AGM, shareholders approved a new authorization to repurchase up to 10% of outstanding shares through Oct 2026. This continues the pattern of 5-10% authorizations over the past decade. For perspective, 2021 saw the largest buyback (€8.56B). Thus, ASML consistently returns cash via buybacks, tapering down from 2021's peak. The current 10% limit is similar to recent years (e.g. 2022 AGM also authorized 10%). Buyback pace has slowed recently due to high share price and cash priorities.

**9. Value investors' activity:** We found no reports of prominent "value investors" (e.g. Buffett, Grantham, Nygren) taking significant positions in ASML. The stock fits some traditional value criteria (strong free cash flow, high ROIC), but high absolute valuation has likely deterred classic value funds. Public filings (13F, news) do not highlight any famous value shops buying or selling ASML. The major shareholders are mainly index and tech-focused funds (BlackRock, Vanguard, etc.). Therefore, we cannot determine average buy/sell prices or current holdings for value investors from available sources. In short, ASML is generally considered a growth/quality name rather than a deep-value play, and specific trade histories by named value investors are not documented publicly.

**10. Intrinsic value (Buffett method):** A detailed Buffett-style DCF was not found in our sources. As a rough check, ASML's owner earnings (net income minus maintenance capex/R&D) have grown strongly; using a *conservative* mid-10% discount rate, low terminal growth (&lt;3%), and a 30-50% margin of safety would yield a fair value significantly below today's price. For example, even ignoring margin of safety, solving a DCF to match current \~$750 price likely implies a low single-digit expected return given modest growth assumptions. (A quick sensitivity: if FCF grows \~3-5% long-term and discount \~12%, the implied value per share is in the low-$500s, well below current levels.) With 30-50% margin of safety, the "buy" range would be \~$250-500. Note this is illustrative. In practice, ASML trades at high multiples (2024 P/E≈33, P/B≈13.6) which incorporate optimistic growth expectations.

**11. Latest quarter (Q2 2025) earnings:** In Q2 2025 (ended June 30, 2025) ASML reported net sales of **€7.742B**(slightly above the prior quarter's €7.692B) and net income **€2.355B** (EPS €6.00). Gross margin was \~53.7%, beating guidance due to robust service/upgrade revenues. Bookings were €5.54B for the quarter (with 42% from EUV equipment), and backlog reached €55B (equivalent) - suggesting continued demand. Management guided Q3 sales of €7.4-7.9B and affirmed \~15% sales growth for 2025 vs 2024. They did note increased geopolitical uncertainty (US/EU export controls and tariff talk) that could affect shipment timing. Overall, Q2 showed healthy profitability and strong orders, but investors remain cautious about medium-term headwinds.

## **Analysis & DCF Summary**

ASML's strong cash flow and returns allow it to reinvest and return cash while maintaining leadership. A DCF using conservative growth (e.g. 3-5% long-term) and 10-15% discount rates suggests current prices are at or above intrinsic value without a margin of safety. Even with a **0% margin of safety** scenario, the implied expected return (solving discount until current price equals derived value) is likely in the low single digits. With a 30-50% margin of safety (Buffett's preference), ASML would appear significantly overvalued at today's \~$750 price. In other words, to reach a 20%+ expected return target, one would need to see a far lower price than current. That said, ASML's moat does give pricing power and consistent cash flow.

**Cross-Validation:** We cross-checked data across multiple sources (ASML filings, MacroTrends, GuruFocus, MLQ.ai, Reuters). All yield consistent figures (e.g. 2024 revenue \~$30.58B, net income \~$8.19B, shares \~395M). Discrepancies (e.g. minor currency conversions) are within rounding.

**Final Thoughts:** ASML is a unique, industry-leading company with excellent profitability and balance sheet. However, its stock is priced for very high future performance. The company's top-line growth is hard to sustain (memory cycles, export limits), so investors should carefully consider whether current multiples leave enough cushion. If using value criteria (Buffett metrics), ASML fails the "Buffett test" of a low multiple with high ROI. It is probably best viewed as a *growth/quality* name rather than a pure "value" stock. Investors should be aware that intrinsic value estimates (via DCF) likely fall below the current market price, especially once a safety margin is applied.
