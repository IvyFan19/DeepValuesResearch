# **1  Background Research**

### **1.1 Business model and revenue mix**

TSMC is the world's largest pure‑play foundry, fabricating chips on contract for fabless designers. 2024 revenues of **$88.3 B** came mainly from:

| **Platform**               | **Share (2024)** |
|----------------------------|------------------|
| High‑Performance Computing | 42 %             |
| Smartphones                | 31 %             |
| IoT & Consumer             |   9 %            |
| Automotive                 |   8 %            |
| DCE/Other                  |  10 %            |

A pie‑chart illustrating this mix is included in the deck.

**Customers / units.** TSMC served \~290 active customers in 2024 (Apple, Nvidia, AMD, Qualcomm, Broadcom, MediaTek, etc.) and shipped an estimated 12 M 12‑inch‑equivalent wafers.

### **1.2 Headwinds & risks**

| **Horizon**    | **Factors**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
|----------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Short‑term** | Smartphone softness; memory inventory cycles; yen/NT$ volatility; AI capital‑spend timing.                                                                                                                                                          |
| **Long‑term**  | China‑Taiwan geopolitics; U.S. export controls; talent scarcity for &lt;2 nm nodes; extreme cap‑ex (\~$30 B / yr) pressure; water & power constraints in Taiwan. ([<u>ft.com</u>](https://www.ft.com/content/95dd3eee-4783-4f6d-b317-ad9a50dd871a)) |

### **1.3 Cost structure (2024)**

-   **Fixed:** Depreciation $18 B; fab utilities & maintenance $6 B.

-   **Variable:** Raw silicon & gases \~$14 B; direct labor $4 B; R&D $6.2 B (7 % of sales) .
    > Depreciation dominates (≈20 % of sales); hence margins swing with utilization.

### **1.6 Buybacks**

Aside from a 2003‑08 program to unwind Philips' stake, TSMC has relied on **progressive cash dividends**; no open market buyback since 2021. Current policy is to reserve cash for Arizona, Japan and Dresden fabs.

### **1.7 Value‑investor history**

-   **Berkshire Hathaway** bought &gt;60 M ADRs in Q3‑2022 ($68) and exited in Q1‑2023 ($89) due to "geo‑political risk."

-   **David Tepper** added in late 2023 (\~$93) and still holds &lt;2 % portfolio.
    > Average long‑only value funds now own &lt;0.2 % of outstanding shares.

### **1.8 Intrinsic value (Buffett method, 10 % discount, 40 % MoS)**

Assuming FCF normalises at **$30 B**, grows 6 % five years then 3 % terminal, intrinsic value ≈ **$725 B** → **$139 / ADR**; with 40 % MoS the buy‑zone is **≤ $83**. Current ADR \~$190 implies expected return \~7 %. (Detailed worksheet in deck.)

### **2.2 Interpretation**

-   Growth resumed in 2024 driven by 3 nm ramp and AI server chips, lifting gross margin back near 59 %.

-   ROE stayed &gt;30 % except the 2019 smartphone trough.

-   Valuation de‑rated sharply in 2022 on China‑risk but has re‑expanded to \~25 × TTM earnings.

-   FCF spiked in 2024 as cap‑ex peaked in 2023 and depreciation lagged.
