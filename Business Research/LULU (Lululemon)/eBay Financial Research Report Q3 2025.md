## **Executive Summary & Investment Thesis**

This report provides a comprehensive financial analysis of eBay Inc. (eBay), a company at a critical crossroads. The core investment thesis for eBay is defined by a deep and persistent conflict between its quantitative financial profile and its qualitative business fundamentals.

**The Quantitative Bull Case:** eBay operates as a high-margin, capital-light e-commerce platform that generates substantial and consistent free cash flow. The company's primary method of returning value to shareholders is a massive, multi-billion dollar annual share repurchase program. This program has been so aggressive that it has reduced the company's outstanding share count by nearly half over the last decade, mechanically driving significant earnings-per-share (EPS) growth, even in periods of stagnant revenue. Rigorous discounted cash flow (DCF) analysis, anchored by the company's strong cash generation, suggests the stock is undervalued if it can achieve even modest, low-single-digit growth.

**The Qualitative Bear Case:** The company's core marketplace appears to be in a state of secular decline. Its "Active Buyer" count, a critical metric of platform health, has faced persistent erosion, marking a significant drop from its 2018 peak. Having effectively ceded the broad "new-item" e-commerce market to competitors like Amazon, eBay is now facing a multi-front war in its "non-new" and niche categories from aggressive, low-cost international players (e.g., Temu, Shein) and specialized vertical platforms.

**The Management & Value Trap Conflict:** This analysis reveals a significant disconnect between corporate actions and executive behavior. While the *company* deploys billions of dollars of shareholder capital to execute buybacks (signaling the stock is undervalued), the *executive management team*-including the CEO and former CFO-has been systematically selling its personal stock holdings and has not executed a single open-market purchase.

Ultimately, eBay presents as a potential "value trap." An investment in the company is not a bet on high-growth e-commerce; it is a bet on financial engineering. The bull case rests entirely on the premise that management can continue to repurchase shares at a rate that outpaces the fundamental erosion of its core business.

## **Analysis of Latest Quarter (Q3 2025) Earnings**

eBay's financial results for the third quarter of 2025, which ended September 30, 2025, surpassed analyst expectations on key metrics. However, a deeper analysis reveals profitability pressures and a reliance on monetization rather than user growth.

### **Headline Financial Results**

The company reported strong top-line and bottom-line figures, beating consensus estimates:

-   **Revenue:** $2.8 billion, representing a 9% year-over-year (Y/Y) increase as reported and an 8% increase on a foreign exchange (FX) neutral basis.

-   **Gross Merchandise Volume (GMV):** $20.1 billion, reflecting a 10% Y/Y increase as reported and an 8% increase on an FX-neutral basis.

-   **Earnings Per Share (EPS):** The company delivered GAAP EPS from continuing operations of $1.28. Non-GAAP EPS was $1.36, a 14.3% Y/Y increase, which exceeded analyst expectations.

### **Key Growth Drivers & Operational Highlights**

The headline GMV growth was driven by two key strategic areas, rather than by broad-based user growth:

1.  **Advertising Revenue:** This remains the company's primary growth engine. Total advertising offerings generated $525 million, marking a significant 25% Y/Y increase. This revenue stream now accounts for 2.6% of total GMV, indicating that eBay is successfully increasing its "take rate" by monetizing its existing seller base more effectively.

2.  **Focus Categories:** Management's strategy to pivot toward "enthusiast buyers" in niche verticals showed positive results. GMV within these "focus categories" (such as collectibles, luxury goods, and refurbished items) grew by over 15%. To support this, eBay expanded its "Authenticity Guarantee" program to include luxury apparel and accessories in the U.K..

3.  **Strategic Acquisitions:** The company completed its acquisition of Tise, a community-based resale marketplace, in an effort to attract younger, Gen Z and Millennial, demographics.

### **Profitability, Cash Flow, and Capital Return**

While non-GAAP metrics were strong, GAAP profitability declined, reflecting the high cost of the company's strategic initiatives.

-   **Operating Margins:** The GAAP Operating Margin *decreased* to 20.4%, a notable contraction from 23.1% in the same period last year. This signals that investments in AI, authentication services, and platform technology are pressuring "real" profitability. The Non-GAAP Operating Margin was 27.1%.

-   **Cash Flow:** The business continues to be a strong cash generator, producing $934 million in operating cash flow and $803 million in free cash flow from continuing operations.

-   **Capital Return:** eBay returned $757 million to shareholders during the quarter. This consisted of $625 million in share repurchases and $132 million in cash dividends.

### **Forward Guidance (Q4 2025 & Full-Year 2025)**

The company provided the following guidance for its continuing operations:

-   **Q4 2025 Guidance:**

    -   **Revenue:** $2.83 billion to $2.89 billion

    -   **GMV:** $20.5 billion to $20.9 billion

    -   **Non-GAAP EPS:** $1.31 to $1.36

-   **Full-Year 2025 Guidance:**

    -   **Revenue:** $10.97 billion to $11.03 billion

    -   **GMV:** $78.9 billion to $79.3 billion

The quarterly results demonstrate that eBay's growth is primarily being driven by extracting more revenue from its existing transactions (via ads) rather than by expanding its user base. The declining GAAP operating margin is a critical counter-point to the headline beats, showing that this strategy is capital-intensive and comes at the cost of "real" profitability.

## **Qualitative Analysis: Business Fundamentals and Risks**

### **Business Model & Competitive Moat**

eBay operates a global two-sided e-commerce marketplace, connecting buyers and sellers in approximately 190 markets. Unlike a traditional retailer (like Amazon), eBay is a technology platform that facilitates Consumer-to-Consumer (C2C) and Business-to-Consumer (B2C) transactions, holding no inventory itself.

#### **1. Main Sources of Income**

The company's revenue is generated through two primary channels:

1.  **Marketplace Fees (Primary):** This is the commission, or "take rate," eBay charges on transactions. This includes insertion fees (for listing an item) and final value fees (a percentage of the total sale amount). For the full year 2023, eBay's take rate was 13.81%.

2.  **Advertising Revenue (Secondary & Growing):** This stream consists of "Promoted Listings" and other advertising services. Sellers pay eBay to have their listings featured more prominently, increasing visibility. This is the company's fastest-growing segment, surging 25% in Q3 2025.

#### **2. Customer Base**

As of 2024, eBay's platform connects over 130 million active buyers with approximately 20 million sellers. However, this "active buyer" count has been a point of significant weakness, experiencing 13 consecutive quarters of decline from its 2018 peak, a fact that recent minor year-over-year upticks tend to obscure.

#### **3. How many units/subscriptions do they sell?**

eBay does not sell "units" or "subscriptions" as its primary business. It facilitates transactions. The key volume metric is **Gross Merchandise Volume (GMV)**, which represents the total value of all goods sold on the platform. For the full year 2024, eBay's GMV was approximately $75 billion, and it is guiding to roughly $79 billion for 2025.

#### **4. What's the moat of this company?**

eBay's competitive advantage (its "moat") is built on three pillars:

1.  **The Network Effect:** This is the classic marketplace moat. 20 million sellers are on the platform because 130 million buyers are there, and buyers are present because of the vast selection provided by sellers. This two-sided ecosystem creates high barriers to entry for a generalist competitor.

2.  **Niche Brand Dominance:** While eBay has lost the "new, in-season" commodity market, it maintains a powerful and defensible brand identity in "non-new" and unique-item categories. This includes used goods, refurbished electronics, collectibles (like trading cards), auto parts, and high-end luxury items (watches, handbags).

3.  **Proprietary Data:** With 30 years of transaction and pricing history for unique and collectible items, eBay possesses a massive, proprietary dataset that is now the foundation of its AI-driven pricing, search, and authentication tools.

### **Short-term and Long-term Headwinds and Risk**

Despite its moat, eBay faces significant and intensifying risks.

#### **1. Economic, political or industry factors that could temporarily impact the business**

-   **Economic Factors:** As a platform for discretionary goods, eBay is sensitive to weak consumer spending, particularly in its key international markets like the United Kingdom and Germany.

-   **Foreign Exchange (FX):** With over 50% of its GMV generated in international markets, the company's U.S. dollar-denominated results are subject to constant FX volatility, as seen in the 1-2% gap between reported and FX-neutral growth in Q3 2025.

-   **Industry Factors:** Intense, price-driven competition from new C2C players and Chinese marketplaces (e.g., Temu, Shein) poses a direct threat to eBay's low-to-mid-range C2C market.

#### **2. Economic, political or industry factors that could permanently impact the business**

-   **Competition (Amazon):** The primary long-term threat remains Amazon, whose superior logistics and fulfillment network have made it the default for new-item e-commerce. eBay's strategic pivot to "non-new" items is a tacit admission that it cannot compete with Amazon on logistics.

-   **User Base Erosion:** The most critical long-term risk is the secular decline of its user base. The platform's active buyer count is shrinking, indicating that it is failing to attract new, younger users to replace those who are leaving. If this trend continues, the "network effect" that forms its moat will begin to unwind.

-   **Regulatory Scrutiny:** eBay faces persistent regulatory risks, including potential government crackdowns on "de minimis" shipping loopholes that benefit foreign sellers, new laws regarding online sales tax collection, and increased platform liability for counterfeit goods and product safety.

### **Company's Fixed and Variable Costs**

eBay's cost structure is that of a technology platform, not a retailer. Its costs are primarily related to processing transactions, acquiring traffic, and paying for talent.

-   **Cost of Net Revenues (Primarily Variable):** This is the largest single cost category, representing 28% of net revenues in 2024. It includes:

    -   Payment processing costs (bank fees, credit card interchange)

    -   Costs of authentication and shipping services

    -   Customer support and site operations staff

-   **Operating Expenses (Fixed and Variable):**

    -   **Sales and Marketing (S&M):** A mix of variable costs (traffic acquisition like paid search and affiliate marketing) and fixed costs (brand campaigns and staff salaries).

    -   **Product Development:** A largely fixed cost, consisting of salaries and compensation for engineers and R&D teams building out AI and platform features.

    -   **General and Administrative (G&A):** Fixed overhead, including executive compensation, corporate facilities, legal, and finance.

    -   **Provision for Transaction Losses:** A variable cost to cover fraud, chargebacks, and the "eBay Money Back Guarantee".

#### **5. How do these costs trend over time? In different Economic conditions?**

Costs are trending upwards, not for aggressive expansion, but for defensive purposes. The 2024 10-K filing noted a $163 million increase in S&M expenses and a 2% rise in Cost of Net Revenues. These increases are not for capturing new markets but are the cost of *staying relevant*. They are spent on subsidizing shipping (to compete with Amazon), buying ad traffic (to replace lost organic traffic), and building out trust services (like authentication) to prevent users from defecting to specialized vertical competitors.

In a recessionary environment, eBay may benefit from a "trade-down" effect as consumers seek used goods. ## **Stewardship: Management and Shareholder Analysis**

### **Management Team**

An assessment of eBay's management reveals a team of industry veterans executing a challenging strategic pivot. However, their personal financial actions appear to contradict the company's capital allocation strategy.

#### **1. Who are they?**

-   **Current Executive Team:**

    -   **Jamie Iannone (President and CEO):** Iannone is an eBay veteran (2001-2009) who returned to the company as CEO in April 2020. He previously led e-commerce and digital initiatives at Sam's Club and Walmart.

    -   **Peggy Alford (SVP, Chief Financial Officer):** Alford was appointed CFO in May 2025, replacing Steve Priest. She brings extensive experience from PayPal, where she was Executive Vice President of Global Sales.

    -   **Key Executives:** The leadership team includes Cornelius Boone (SVP, Chief People Officer), Julie Loeger (SVP, Chief Growth Officer), Mazen Rawashdeh (SVP & Chief Technology Officer), Jordan Sweetnam (SVP, Chief Commercial Officer), and Samantha Wellington (SVP, Chief Legal Officer).

-   **Current Board of Directors:**

    -   **Paul S. Pressler (Chair):** Mr. Pressler has been a director since 2015 and serves as Chair of the Board. He is the former President and CEO of The Gap, Inc., and a 15-year veteran of The Walt Disney Company.

    -   **Other Key Directors:** The board includes Adriane M. Brown (Chair, Compensation and Human Capital Committee), Perry M. Traquina (Chair, Risk Committee), and Zane Rowe (CFO of Workday, Inc.).

-   **Founder or long-time executive on the team?**

    -   **Pierre Omidyar (Founder):** Pierre Omidyar founded eBay in 1995. He stepped down from the board of directors in 2020 but retains the title of "director emeritus". His influence is passive but significant; he still owns approximately 6% of the company, and a large portion of his estimated $9-10 billion net worth remains tied to eBay stock.

#### **2. Past Performance**

-   **Does management follow through on goals and promises?** The record is mixed.

    -   **Goal:** The stated goal is a "tech-led reimagination" focused on "enthusiast buyers" and AI.

    -   **Execution (Failure):** On the primary goal of growing the user base, management has not succeeded. The "Active Buyer" count has seen a multi-year decline from its 2018 peak, which is a critical failure.

    -   **Execution (Success):** Management *has* successfully executed its secondary goal of monetizing this shrinking base via high-growth advertising revenue and has grown its "focus categories".

-   **Does management hide problems from investors?**

    -   **Major Red Flag:** Management's credibility was significantly damaged on October 31, 2025, when the company was forced to file an 8-K to correct a public statement made by CEO Jamie Iannone. On a Fox Business interview, Iannone "incorrectly" stated that GMV guidance was "4% - 6% for next year," when in fact, that guidance was only for the *next quarter*. This is a serious unforced error that suggests either a lack of preparedness or an attempt to mislead the market.

    -   **Transparency:** The company heavily emphasizes non-GAAP financial metrics (which beat expectations) while downplaying the GAAP operating margin, which *declined* from 23.1% to 20.4% in the latest quarter. This practice, while common, obscures the "real" profitability pressures from its strategic investments.

#### **3. Value Alignment**

This is the most critical area of concern in this analysis.

-   **Do they have a significant portion of their wealth invested?**

    -   Management's compensation is heavily weighted toward equity (PBRSUs and RSUs) to "align executives' incentives with the long-term interests of our stockholders".

-   **Do they acquire stock during short-term declines?**

    -   **No. They sell.** Analysis of insider trading data reveals a complete lack of value alignment.

    -   In the six months leading up to February 2025, company insiders executed **0 purchases** and **28 sales** of eBay stock.

    -   The sellers include the highest levels of management: President and CEO (Jamie Iannone), the former SVP & CFO (Stephen Priest), the SVP & Chief Product Officer, and the VP & Chief Accounting Officer.

    -   This selling has continued, with multiple sales by the CEO and other executives reported through August 2025.

    -   This creates a profound contradiction: the company, via its board-authorized buyback program, is aggressively purchasing stock, signaling it is undervalued. Simultaneously, the top executives, who have the clearest view of the internal business trends (such as the declining user base), are systematically selling their personal shares. This suggests management is using shareholder capital to support the stock price while reducing their own personal exposure.

-   **Current Plan:** As of September 30, 2025, eBay had approximately $1.4 billion remaining on its repurchase authorization. This is part of a massive, rolling program that was increased by $2.0 billion in February 2024 and another $3.0 billion in December 2024. In Q3 2025 alone, the company spent $625 million on repurchases.

-   **10-Year History:** The company has executed one of the most aggressive buyback programs in the market, spending tens of billions of dollars over the past decade. This has reduced the diluted weighted-average shares outstanding from approximately 1.191 billion in 2015 to 0.501 billion in 2024-a reduction of nearly 58%.

#### **Key Financial Trend Analysis (from Graphs 1-39)**

-   **Profitability (Graphs 12-18):** The most significant trend is the divergence between Net Income and Operating Income. The **Net Income (Graph 16)** is extremely volatile, showing massive spikes (2016, 2020, 2021) and deep losses (2017, 2022). This volatility is *not* related to the core business; it is driven by non-operating gains and losses on equity investments (e.g., Adevinta, Adyen). In contrast, **Operating Profit (Graph 14)** is highly stable and consistently positive, hovering between $1.7B and $2.9B. The **Operating Margin (Graph 15)** is exceptionally strong, consistently averaging over 20%. This demonstrates the core marketplace is a durable, high-margin business.

-   **Cash Generation (Graphs 19-20):** The **Free Cash Flow (Graph 19)** graph is the star of the bull case. Like Operating Profit, it is consistently strong and positive, averaging over $2.1 billion annually. The **FCF Margin (Graph 20)** is impressive, consistently landing above 18%. This reinforces the "cash cow" status of the business.

-   **Valuation (Graphs 3-4):** The **P/E Ratio (Graph 3)** and **P/B Ratio (Graph 4)** are rendered effectively useless by accounting distortions. P/E is chaotic due to the aforementioned Net Income volatility. P/B is skewed to appear expensive (P/B &gt; 7.0) *only* because the "Book Value" denominator has been decimated by the share buyback program.

-   **Capital Allocation (Graphs 32, 33):** The **Shareholder's Equity (Graph 32)** graph shows a dramatic decline from its peak, falling from over $10.5B in 2016 to just $5.1B in 2024.<sup>3</sup> This is a visual representation of the tens of billions of dollars in capital that have been returned to shareholders via buybacks, effectively "hollowing out" the balance sheet's equity.

### **Deep Value Investing Analysis**

This section evaluates eBay against a rigorous checklist of deep value investing standards. The analysis reveals that eBay is a "broken" value stock: it fails many simple standards *because* of its aggressive (and value-accretive) financial engineering, requiring an analyst to look deeper.

#### **1. Valuation Discipline**

-   **Price to Earning ratio (P/E) $\\leq$ 15:** **FAIL.** The TTM P/E ratio is volatile and unreliable due to non-operating gains/losses in Net Income.

-   **PEG Ratio (Peter Lynch) &lt; 1:** **FAIL.** The TTM PEG ratio is approximately 1.17. With non-GAAP EPS growth at 14.3% and a forward P/E around 15-18x, the PEG ratio is slightly above 1.0.

-   **Price to Book ratio (P/B) $\\leq$ 1.5:** **FAIL.** The P/B ratio is approximately 7.8x. This metric is irrelevant for eBay, as shareholder equity has been intentionally depleted by decades of share repurchases.

-   **Margin of Safety: Pay $\\geq$ 25% below intrinsic value:** **(See DCF Analysis).** This analysis suggests the stock is currently trading *above* its no-growth intrinsic value, but *below* its modest-growth value. The margin of safety is therefore dependent on future growth, which is uncertain.

#### **2. Cash Generation & Allocation**

-   **Free-Cash-Flow (FCF): Positive each year; FCF/Revenue &gt; 5%:** **PASS.** FCF has been consistently and strongly positive for the last 10 years. The FCF/Revenue (FCF Margin) has averaged over 20%, far exceeding the 5% standard.<sup>4</sup>

-   **Return on Retained Earnings $\\geq$ 12%:** **FAIL (Not Calculable).** This metric is distorted to the point of being useless. The company has negative retained earnings growth in several years (due to net losses) and its primary method of "reinvestment" is share buybacks, not growing the equity base.

#### **3. Growth & Predictability**

-   **EPS Record: Positive & rising every year for 10 yrs:** **FAIL.** GAAP EPS (based on Net Income) was negative in 2017 and 2022.<sup>5</sup>

-   **Shareholder-equity: 10-year CAGR in book value per share $\\geq$ 10% and no single year of contraction:** **FAIL.** Book value per share has a *negative* 10-year CAGR due to the aggressive buyback program.

-   **Owner's-earnings (OE): positive every year, OE / Market Cap $\\geq$ 10%:** **PASS.** Owner's Earnings (proxied by FCF) are consistently positive. The FCF Yield (FCF / Market Cap) was $1.956B / $31.0B = 6.3% in 2024. While not 10%, it is substantial.

#### **4. Balance-Sheet Safety**

-   **Debt-to-Equity &lt; 0.5:** **FAIL.** The D/E ratio is high (2.75 in 2024). Like P/B, this metric is meaningless. The "Equity" denominator is artificially suppressed. A better metric is Debt-to-EBITDA, which is more stable.

-   **Interest Coverage (EBIT / Interest) $\\geq$ 3$\\times$:** **PASS.** This is a key metric. In 2023, Operating Income was $1.941B <sup>5</sup> and Interest Expense was $263M. This represents an excellent interest coverage ratio of \*\*7.4$\\times$\*\*.

-   **Interest Expense / Operating Income $\\leq$ 15%:** **PASS.** In 2023, this ratio was 13.5% ($263M / $1,941M), passing the safety standard.

-   **Current Ratio &gt; 1.5:** **FAIL.** The Current Ratio in 2024 was 1.24. While below the 1.5 standard, it is not at a distress level, though it has declined.

#### **5. Profitability & Efficiency**

-   **Gross Margin $\\approx$ 40%:** **PASS.** eBay's gross margin is consistently exceptional, averaging over 70% for the past decade.

-   **Net Profit Margin $\\approx$ 20%:** **FAIL (in spirit).** Net Margin is too volatile. However, the **Operating Margin** (a much better metric for core business profitability) is consistently *above* 20%, so the business **PASSES** on core profitability.

-   **Return on Equity (ROE) $\\geq$ 15% average for 10 yrs:** **PASS.** ROE is consistently high, often above 30% or 40%. This is, again, inflated by the low equity base but demonstrates efficient profit generation.

-   **Return on Invested Capital (ROIC) $\\geq$ 12% 10-yr avg:** **PASS.** ROIC is a strong indicator of a high-quality business and has consistently averaged above 12% (e.g., TTM 20.2%, 2021 20.5%).

-   **Return on Assets (ROA) $\\geq$ 6%:** **PASS.** The 10-year average ROA is well above 6%, though it has dipped in specific years due to net losses.

-   **Depreciation / Gross Profit $\\leq$ 10%:** **PASS.** Depreciation (a component of CapEx) is very low relative to the high Gross Profit, reflecting the capital-light platform model.

**Value Checklist Conclusion:** eBay fails most "simple" balance sheet and growth tests. An analyst *must* look through these distortions to see the "true" company: a high-margin (GM &gt; 70%), high-ROIC (&gt; 15%), safely-levered (Interest Coverage &gt; 7x) cash cow (FCF Margin &gt; 18%).

### **Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) Analysis**

This DCF analysis models three scenarios to determine the intrinsic value of eBay's stock based on its historical cash-generating capabilities, not speculative analyst forecasts.

**Key Assumptions:**

-   **Base Free Cash Flow (FCF):** $1,956 million (Full-year 2024 FCF).

-   **Discount Rate:** **8.4%**. This is based on an analyst-calculated Cost of Equity, which is appropriate for a DCF valuation given the company's capital structure.

-   **Shares Outstanding:** 501 million (2024 average).

-   **Terminal Growth Rate:** 2.0% (a long-term proxy for inflation).

#### **Case 1: No-Growth (Perpetuity) Case**

-   **Methodology:** This case assumes eBay's FCF stops growing immediately and forever (0% growth). This is a simple perpetuity calculation to find the value of the business as a mature, non-growing cash cow.

-   **Calculation:**

    -   *Intrinsic Value = Base FCF / (Discount Rate - Growth Rate)*

    -   *Intrinsic Value = $1,956,000,000 / (0.084 - 0.0)*

    -   *Intrinsic Value = $23,285,714,286*

    -   **Intrinsic Value per Share = $23,286M / 501M = $46.48**

#### **Case 2: Conservative Case (0% Growth, 10-Year DCF)**

-   **Methodology:** This case uses the *lowest* FCF growth rate from the past 10 years, which was -34.6%.<sup>4</sup> Using this rate is not feasible as it results in a near-zero valuation. Therefore, we have substituted a **0% FCF growth rate** for 10 years, followed by a 0% terminal growth rate, as a more realistic "conservative" scenario.

-   **Calculation:**

    -   *Projected FCF (Years 1-10):* $1,956 million (flat)

    -   *PV of Projected FCF:* $13,446 million

    -   *Terminal Value (at Year 10):* $1,956M / 0.084 = $23,286 million

    -   *PV of Terminal Value:* $23,286M / (1.084^10) = $10,361 million

    -   *Total Intrinsic Value = $13,446M + $10,361M = $23,807 million*

    -   **Intrinsic Value per Share = $23,807M / 501M = $47.52**

#### **Case 3: Nominal Case (Average Growth, 10-Year DCF)**

-   **Methodology:** This case uses the 10-year *average* FCF growth rate. As calculated from the 10-year data <sup>4</sup>, this average is -3.73%. Using a negative average is analytically flawed as it punishes the company for a single volatile spinoff event in 2015. Therefore, we have substituted a modest **2.0% annual growth rate** (in line with terminal growth) as a more rational "nominal" or base case.

-   **Calculation:**

    -   *Projected FCF (Years 1-10):* FCF grows at 2.0% annually.

    -   *PV of Projected FCF:* $14,642 million

    -   *Terminal Value (at Year 10):* ($1,956M \* (1.02^11)) / (0.084 - 0.02) = $37,926 million

    -   *PV of Terminal Value:* $37,926M / (1.084^10) = $16,879 million

    -   *Total Intrinsic Value = $14,642M + $16,879M = $31,521 million*

    -   **Intrinsic Value per Share = $31,521M / 501M = $62.91**

| **DCF Scenario**         | **Growth Assumption**       | **Intrinsic Value per Share** | **Buy Price (30% Margin of Safety)** |
|--------------------------|-----------------------------|-------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| **Case 1: No-Growth**    | 0% FCF Growth (Perpetuity)  | **$46.48**                    | **$32.54**                           |

Conclusion of DCF Analysis:

This DCF analysis, based on historical cash flows, indicates that at a year-end 2024 price of $61.95, the market was pricing eBay at a modest-growth valuation (close to our Case 3).

However, at the current price (November 2025) of approximately **$81.01**, the stock is trading significantly *above* even the nominal-growth intrinsic value. This model contradicts analyst reports (which use high forward-looking estimates) and suggests that, based on its established historical performance, the stock is **overvalued**.

## **Conclusion**

The financial research on eBay Inc. presents a stark dichotomy. On one hand, the company is a high-margin, high-ROIC business that generates billions in predictable free cash flow. It uses this cash to execute one of the most aggressive share buyback programs in the market, which has been the primary engine of EPS growth and shareholder returns for a decade.

On the other hand, this financial strength masks a deteriorating core business. The platform's active user base is in a long-term decline, and it is fighting a costly defensive war against larger (Amazon) and newer (Temu) competitors. The costs of this war are evident in the company's declining GAAP operating margins.

The most significant finding is the profound misalignment between the company's capital allocation and its management's personal actions. The company is spending billions of shareholder dollars to buy stock, while the CEO and other top executives are systematically selling their own shares.

This evidence strongly suggests eBay is a "value trap." The company's quantitative metrics (FCF, margins, ROIC) are backward-looking and reflect a stronger past. The qualitative trends (user decay) and management's insider selling are forward-looking and suggest a weaker future. Our historical DCF model confirms that at current 2025 prices, the stock appears overvalued, pricing in a growth recovery that its historical data does not support.
