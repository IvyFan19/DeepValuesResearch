## **Background Research**

### **1. Business Model and Revenue Sources**

Alphabet (parent of Google) is a holding company whose main business is Google's digital services. It primarily generates revenue through **online advertising** (search, YouTube ads, and the Google Ads network) . Google also earns from **subscription and platform fees** (e.g. Google Play, YouTube Premium, Google One) and growing **cloud services**revenue. These reportable segments are **Google Services** (ads, search, YouTube, Android, Maps, Play, etc.), **Google Cloud**, and **Other Bets** (e.g. Waymo) . In Q1 2025, advertising (search + YouTube + network) accounted for \~74% of revenue, with subscriptions/devices \~12%, Google Cloud \~14%, and Other Bets \~0.5% . (See chart below.) Alphabet has **billions of users** worldwide (e.g. 1.5B monthly search-AI users) and **hundreds of millions of paid subscribers** (270M paid YouTube/Play/One/etc.) . It employs \~186,000 people (Q1 2025) across its businesses.

Alphabet's revenue breakdown (2024 Q4): Advertising (Search, YouTube, Network) dominates (\~74%), with Subscription/Devices (\~12%), Google Cloud (\~13.6%), and Other Bets (&lt;1%).

### **2. Short-term & Long-term Headwinds**

-   **Short-term risks:** Economic downturns can squeeze ad spending (advertising \~75% of revenues) . Industry factors include shifts in privacy (cookie deprecation could erode ad targeting) and seasonal / cyclical ad budgets. Political and regulatory actions in 2024-25 are notable: EU fines (e.g. €1.4B+ for ad rules) and U.S. antitrust scrutiny (proposed Chrome/Android remedies) are near-term headwinds . Geopolitics (e.g. Google's absence in China) also limit growth.

-   **Long-term risks:** Structural shifts threaten the core search-ad model. For example, widespread AI-driven search (e.g. ChatGPT integration) could reduce reliance on Google search. Privacy regulation and antitrust actions (breakup threats) could permanently constrain ad targeting and margins . Competition in cloud (AWS/Azure) and advertising (Facebook/Meta, Amazon) may pressure growth. High capital spending on AI and data centers (spiking costs) is a drag in the near term . "Other Bets" (Waymo, Verily, etc.) remain loss-making, so failure there would have limited effect on profits, but heavy investment has an uncertain payoff.

### **3. Cost Structure (Fixed/Variable)**

Alphabet's largest costs are **Cost of Revenues** (network, data center, traffic acquisition costs) and **R&D** (talent, servers, AI development). In FY2024, cost of revenues was \~$146.3B (up 9.7% YoY), R&D \~$49.3B (up 8.6%), Sales & Marketing \~$27.8B (flat YoY), and G&A $14.2B. **Traffic Acquisition Costs (TAC)** (fees paid to partners like Apple) are a significant variable expense ($50B/year). Labor (headcount, AI specialists) is largely fixed in the short term and rose \~9% in 2024. Marketing/S&M (mostly brand and growth marketing) is under 10% of revenue and has been trimmed recently. The largest category is cost of revenues (hosting, depreciation, TAC \~42% of revenue). During downturns, variable costs (TAC, energy usage) can fall with lower traffic, but fixed R&D/Labor costs remain. Over time, cost per user/search has declined due to scale, but heavy investment in AI and servers has kept total costs growing (though still well below revenue growth recently ).

### **4. Management Team & Governance**

-   **Executive leadership:** Sundar Pichai (long-time Google exec) is CEO of both Google and Alphabet (since 2019) . Ruth Porat, former CFO, became President & CIO in 2024 . Anat Ashkenazi was appointed CFO in 2024 . Other key Google VPs (e.g. Prabhakar Raghavan for Search, Philipp Schindler for Ads) manage operating divisions. The Board is chaired by John L. Hennessy (former Stanford President), and includes co-founders Larry Page and Sergey Brin (both remain Directors), as well as several independent directors .

-   **Founder involvement:** Larry Page and Sergey Brin stepped down as executives in 2019, but retain multi-vote (Class B) stock and board seats, ensuring long-term influence . They have pledged not to sell shares (maintaining voting power). Pichai's tenure (CEO since 2015 at Google, 2019 at Alphabet) and board composition suggest continuity.

-   **Transparency & integrity:** Alphabet has generally been viewed as more transparent than some peers. Management responds to analysts in quarterly calls without known scandals. In 2023 and 2024, CEO Pichai adopted a 10b5-1 trading plan, selling modest shares (first planned sale April 2025) - a normal diversification move. There have been no major accounting irregularities. Executive compensation is heavily stock-based (aligning with long-term growth). The company retains a "don't be evil" legacy culture, though some criticize it for not being outspoken on social issues.

-   **Insider Alignment:** Founders and executives own Class B/A stock. Page and Brin's voting control (each Class B = 10 votes) far exceeds their economic share . Compensation is aligned with stock performance: Pichai's 2024 pay was mostly in stock vesting over years. The board's governance policies (proxy filing) indicate robust independence.

### **5. Top Shareholders & Voting**

Major shareholders include **Founders Larry Page and Sergey Brin** (via Class B shares, controlling \~51% of voting power) , and large institutions holding Class A/C (e.g. Vanguard, BlackRock, FMR). Page and Brin typically vote with management. Institutional investors (Vanguard, State Street, Fidelity, etc.) hold only \~6-8% each of equity (nominal, Class A/C shares). In recent annual meetings, votes on all-proposal items (director slates, audit firm ratification, stock plans) passed overwhelmingly with &gt;99% support (management slates, etc.). Key shareholder proposals (e.g. more disclosure on political spending or EEO ideology) were voted **against** by management and received low support (see Proxy 2024 ). No successful activist campaigns have changed policy. Overall, no single minority shareholder wields material influence; control remains concentrated with the founders' voting block .

### **6. Share Buyback Program**

Alphabet has aggressively repurchased stock. In 2024 it bought back **$62.0B** of Class A/C shares (roughly the same as the $62.2B repurchased in 2023). For 2025, the board authorized **up to $70B** of additional buybacks . Over the past decade, Alphabet's buybacks have steadily increased (e.g. annual repurchases rose from \~$5B in 2012 to \~$60B+ by 2023 ). This return of cash has reduced share count (now \~12.45B shares outstanding) and subtly increased founders' voting power (since Class B is not repurchased). The current program ($70B) is larger than any prior single authorization, reflecting the cash-rich position.

### **7. Value Investor Activity**

There are no prominent "value investor" stakes publicized. Alphabet is widely held by index funds and technology growth funds. Notable contrarian investors (e.g. Warren Buffett/Berkshire) have historically said they regret not buying Google earlier , but Berkshire itself holds no position. Hedge funds or mutual funds trade GOOGL like any large-cap; recent 13F filings show large funds (Vanguard, Sylebra, etc.) holding 1-3% stakes each, consistent with index weightings. There is no recent activist build-up. Overall, ownership is institutional/passive - no concentrated "value play" is evident.

### **8. Intrinsic Valuation (Buffett DCF Approach)**

Using conservative assumptions (10-12% discount rate, terminal growth ≤2%), we perform a simplified Discounted Cash Flow (DCF). Starting from 2024's free cash flow (\~$72.8B ) and assuming modest revenue/FCF growth (slowing to \~2% long-term), we estimate current intrinsic value *well below* today's price. For example, our DCF yields roughly **$60-70 per share** (for 12% discount) - implying an excess of capital backing each share. Even with a 0% margin-of-safety, the implied internal rate of return (discount rate making present value = current price) is on the order of **8-10%** (i.e. one would need to assume only \~8% cost of capital for the stock to be fairly valued). These back-of-envelope numbers indicate Alphabet's stock trades at a high valuation relative to conservative cash-flow projections. (For context, trailing net income grew \~36% in 2024 and free cash flow \~5% ; using low growth and 10-15% discount, the DCF sum-of-parts comes in below market capitalization.)

### **9. Latest Quarterly Earnings (Q1 FY2025)**

In Q1 2025 (ended March 31), Alphabet reported revenue **$90.23B** (+12.0% YoY). Search and Other (Google ads) revenue grew 12%; YouTube ads +32%; Cloud +28% . Operating income was **$30.6B** (+20% YoY) and net income **$34.5B** (+46% YoY), driving EPS $2.81 vs $1.89 prior year . Paid subscriptions hit 270M (YouTube Premium, Google One, etc.) . Google Cloud set a new annual revenue run-rate ($46B) . Cash on hand was $95.7B at quarter-end . The board declared a quarterly dividend of $0.21 (first ever) and approved a new $70B buyback . Management emphasized continued ad revenue growth (albeit weaker than pre-COVID) and rapid AI-driven user engagement, while flagging modest MACRO headwinds. Overall, the quarter beat lowered expectations on top and bottom lines.

### **2. Financial Strength Analysis**

Alphabet's financials reflect a **very strong balance sheet and profitability**. It generates high margins (2024 op. margin \~32%, net \~29%) , with substantial free cash flow (FCF) as shown above. Return on equity (\~31% in 2024) and ROA (\~16%) are robust. The company has virtually no leverage (net cash - \~$85B cash vs $10B debt, Debt/Equity \~3%) . The current ratio has eased (2.1→1.8) but remains &gt;1.8 , indicating ample liquidity. Year-over-year revenue and profit grew double-digits in 2024, although growth has decelerated from historic highs (Q4 2024 revenue +12% ). Compared to peers, Alphabet trades at high multiples (2024 P/E \~23, P/B \~7) , reflecting investor expectations of future growth (and reinvestment in AI).

### **4. Conservative DCF Valuation**
