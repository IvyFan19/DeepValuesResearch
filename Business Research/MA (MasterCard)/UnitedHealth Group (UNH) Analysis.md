## **Background Research**

**Business Model.** UnitedHealth Group operates two segments: UnitedHealthcare (insurance) and Optum (health services). The vast majority of revenue (\~78% in 2023) comes from insurance premiums and fees, with smaller contributions from products and services (e.g. pharmacy benefits, care management) and investment income. For example, 2023 revenue was $290.8B from premiums, $42.6B from products and services, and only \~$4.1B from investments.

*Chart: 2023 revenue breakdown by category (premiums vs products/services vs investment income), based on 2024 10-K data.*

UnitedHealthcare serves tens of millions of people. As of late 2024, UHC's medical membership was \~50.7 million (49.3M domestic + 1.3M international). In addition, \~9.4M seniors (Medicare/retirement) and \~7.4M Medicaid/community members were served. In total, UHG claims "over 147 million customers worldwide" (Sept. 2022 figure). Each customer is covered under one or more insurance "policies" or benefit contracts rather than a discrete product. The company also runs pharmacy and care businesses (OptumRx, Optum Health) that serve large patient volumes, though insured "members" remain the core unit of sale.

**Key Headwinds & Risks.** UHG faces **economic and industry headwinds** as well as regulatory/political risks. In the near term, rising health-care costs and high utilization are pressuring margins. For example, Q1 2025 saw a sharp spike in Medicare Advantage utilization (outpatient and physician services) beyond expectations . The company noted that new CMS risk-adjustment models and reduced MA payment rates (from previous federal cuts) have increased its medical loss ratio (the percentage of premiums paid out in claims). Indeed, 2024's medical care ratio was \~85.5% (vs \~83.2% in 2023) due to "Medicare funding reductions, member mix, and Medicaid redeterminations". Such changes - along with the end of pandemic-era Medicaid enrollment guarantees - can reduce membership and raise claims costs (Q4 2024 saw Medicaid membership fall sharply).

Longer-term political/regulatory risks include health reform changes (ACA, drug pricing laws, surprise billing rules, etc.), Medicare/Medicaid policy shifts, and antitrust scrutiny (e.g. the Change Healthcare merger review). Industry trends like telehealth expansion and new entrant competition (e.g. large tech firms or startups) could also pressure margins or market share. Conversely, an aging population and employer-based insurance growth support demand. Geopolitically, UHG's exposure is mainly domestic (90% of business in U.S.), so national healthcare policy is most relevant. Economic downturns can hurt employer-paid coverage and thus membership, although health spending is relatively inelastic. Finally, operational risks (cybersecurity, data breaches, fraud) are material - UHG noted a large cyberattack cost in 2023. In sum, major risks include health-cost inflation, government payment and enrollment changes, and regulatory shifts.

**Cost Structure.** UHG's **largest costs are medical claims and benefits** paid to providers. In 2023, medical care costs ("incurred claims") were \~$241.9B out of $371.6B revenue (\~65%), driving an \~83% medical loss ratio. These claims costs are largely variable (driven by member utilization and acuity). Non-claims operating expenses (technology, administration, salaries, etc.) were \~$54.6B (14.7% of revenue). Optum's cost-of-goods (mostly pharmacy and clinical operations) was \~$38.8B in 2023. In contrast, **advertising and marketing expenses are quite small** relative to revenue; estimated UHG ad spend is under $100 million/year . UHG employs \~440,000 people worldwide (2023) - a huge labor base - but much of the labor cost is in medical care delivered by providers (not UHG payroll). Fixed costs include corporate overhead, IT systems, and branch offices; variable costs are mainly provider payments. Over the past decade operating expenses have scaled with revenue, keeping the operating margin in the high single digits. For example, UHG's operating cost ratio has been roughly 14-15% of revenue; in Q1 2025 it was 12.4% (down from 14.1% in 2024 due to some timing effects) . In recessions, claims costs may grow slower, but UHG's profitability tends to be resilient due to diversified mix. Overall, **claims costs are by far the biggest expense**, followed by salaries/IT and cost of products (pharmacy drugs, etc.).

**Management & Governance.** Sir **Andrew Witty** (former CEO of GlaxoSmithKline) has been CEO of UHG since Feb 2021 . John Rex (formerly CFO) is now President (effective 2024) and long-time Dirk McMahon was President/COO until retiring April 2025 . The Board is chaired by **Stephen J. Hemsley** (former UHG CEO) . The lead independent director is Michele Hooper, and other directors include former executives like ex-Mayo Clinic CEO John Noseworthy and industry veterans (Charles Baker, Bill McNabb, etc.) . Founder **Richard T. Burke Sr.** (Charter Med founder) retired from the board in 2022 after 45 years . In general, UHG's leadership has delivered consistent growth and regularly sets multi-year targets (Witty has reiterated a goal of high-teens EPS growth). However, recent performance has deviated from targets (hence Q1 2025 guidance cut).

UHG emphasizes governance and accountability: directors are mostly independent, CEO succession is planned, and compensation includes heavy equity incentives. A recent proxy notes that *all* executives and directors met stock ownership guidelines and at least one-third of vested shares must be held for a year . Insider buying is limited; in fact CEO Witty has sold stock in 2022-23 (typical when no insider purchases are reported). Overall, management's interests are aligned via stock-heavy pay and insider holding policies, though ultimate accountability is to shareholders. Communication has been active: UHG provides quarterly guidance and holds earnings calls. Its track record is mixed - it has met long-term targets over decades, but required mid-course adjustments (as in early 2025).

**Major Shareholders.** UHG stock is widely held by mutual funds and institutions. Top holders include Vanguard (\~9% of shares), BlackRock (\~8%), State Street, Fidelity (FMR), and Capital World Investors . These index managers generally support management's slate and proposals. For example, UHG's advisory "say-on-pay" received \~96% shareholder approval in 2023 . The only material shareholder proposal in 2024 (requiring more political spending disclosure) was opposed by the board and failed , indicating major holders sided with management. No activist shareholder currently has a controlling interest; renowned value investors appear to hold at most modest stakes. (Warren Buffett's Berkshire Hathaway briefly held \~1.18M UNH shares in 2006 - under 0.2% of its portfolio - but has since exited.) Large public and retirement funds (via index exposure) dominate voting.

**Share Buybacks.** UHG has a longstanding share repurchase program. In 2024 the company repurchased about **$8.94 billion** of stock (17.0M shares at \~$529.85 avg) . This compares to $8.00B in 2023 (16.2M shares at $493.79) . Over the past decade, annual buybacks ranged from \~$4-7B (see below). The board still had \~33 million shares remaining under its authorization at end-2024 . Roughly speaking, UHG allocates a steady \~2-3% of market cap per year to repurchases, while also raising its dividend. By comparison, in 2019 UHG bought \~$5.5B, dipped during COVID (2020 \~$4.25B) and ramped up to $7.0B by 2022 . The recent $9B/year level is at the high end historically. Repurchases are opportunistic: UHG will accelerate them when valuation seems attractive. Overall, buybacks plus rising dividends have returned \~$16B+ annually to shareholders in recent years .

**Value Investors (Buys/Sells).** High-profile value investors have had limited roles. Warren Buffett's Berkshire briefly entered UNH in 2006 (1M shares) but sold by 2007. Another activist, Bill Ackman's Pershing Square, built a sizable UNH stake around 2021-22 (reportedly \~12% of his fund) but later reduced it; he cited valuation as a reason (no formal press release). Other famous value funds like Klarman's Baupost or Greenblatt's Gotham have not publicly disclosed major UNH bets. In short, most UNH stock is held by broad-based funds, not concentrated in a single "guru" portfolio.

**Intrinsic Value (Buffett Method).** Roughly applying Buffett's owner-earnings DCF approach: assume conservative growth and discount. UHG's free cash (operating cash flow minus maintenance capex) was about $24B in 2024. Even using low growth (5-8% initially, tapering to \~2% terminal) and a 10-15% discount rate, the implied intrinsic value is several hundred dollars per share. For example, a 10% discount yields \~$430/share, and 15% yields \~$260/share (using \~915M shares outstanding ). Applying a 30-50% margin of safety (Buffett-style) would imply a "buy" range roughly $130-300. At today's \~$400 price, this suggests valuation is in the ballpark of intrinsic if optimistic, but may lack a large margin of safety at higher discount rates. (Precise valuation is sensitive to growth and rate assumptions.)

**Latest Quarter (Q1 2025) Highlights.** UnitedHealth's Q1 2025 results showed revenue $109.6B (↑9.8% YoY) and operating earnings $9.1B . GAAP EPS was $6.85 ($7.20 adj), up from $0.55 (loss) a year ago due to deferred Medicare cost adjustments. The medical care ratio rose to \~84.8% (vs 84.3% in 2024) due to Medicare funding effects, which squeezed margins despite revenue growth . Management cut full-year 2025 EPS guidance from $26.50 to $24.65-25.15 (GAAP) , citing unexpectedly high utilization and MA risk adjustments. Cash flow remained strong: Q1 ops cash was $5.5B, and the company returned \~$5B to shareholders (dividends + buybacks) . For reference, full-year 2024 revenue was $400.3B (↑8%) with $32.3B op earnings and $23.1B net profit (EPS $23.86).

## **Financial Research**

**Historical Financials (2014-2023).** Over the past decade, UNH's growth was dramatic. Revenue grew roughly 3-4× (2014: $127.1B; 2023: $373.3B). Operating income and net earnings similarly expanded. Key metrics (2014 vs 2023) include: ROE 18%→25%, ROIC 18%→23%, and net profit margin 5%→6%. The balance sheet remains strong with shareholder equity growing and debt kept at moderate levels (2023 D/E around 0.4). Dividends have steadily risen (from $3.20 in 2014 to $6.40 in 2023) . The stock price has also risen markedly (from about $188 in 2014 to $450+ in 2023).

**Recent Valuation & Leverage.** Today UHG trades around a mid-20s P/E. Historical P/E (trailing) hovered 15-20x for much of 2010s, jumping to \~26x in 2021 then dipping to \~21x in 2023 . As of 2024 year-end the P/E spiked (low earnings) to \~32.5× . Price/book is about 5-6× (book value \~$80/share). ROA and ROE remain high (\~10-15% ROA, \~20-25% ROE). Debt-to-equity is modest (\~0.4), and debt/asset \~0.2, reflecting low net leverage. Current ratio has been stable around 1.1-1.2 (ample liquidity). Free cash flow has grown in line with profits (FCF \~$20B in 2023).

**Financial Health Assessment.** UnitedHealth appears financially healthy. Profitability (ROIC \~20%+) is strong, cash flow generation is robust (FCF yield \~5-6% on market cap), and the balance sheet is solid (debt kept to \~2× EBITDA). Valuation is not overly rich given growth - at recent prices P/E \~20-25x and P/B \~5x - but it is not obviously undervalued. The dividend payout ratio is moderate (\~27% of earnings). The company has consistently met or beat analyst estimates, but the recent guidance cut shows sensitivity to cost trends. Overall, the fundamentals (growth, margins, cash flow) justify a premium valuation, but investors should watch execution against rising cost pressures.

**Discounted Cash Flow (DCF).** A simple DCF (Buffett-style owner-earnings) using conservative assumptions (10-15% discount rate, terminal growth &lt;3%) yields an intrinsic value in the $260-430/share range. For example, discounting projected free cash flows (starting \~$26B in 2025 and tapering growth) at 10% gives \~$430/share; at 15% it gives \~\~$260/share. Applying a large (50%) margin of safety would suggest valuing the stock in the $130-215 range. Given the current \~$400 price, this implies the stock may be fairly valued to slightly expensive depending on assumptions.
