## Executive Summary: Berkshire's Q3 2025 Pivot and the Alphabet Thesis

Berkshire Hathaway's Q3 2025 13F filing, released on November 14, 2025 , revealed a significant strategic pivot: a new, $4.34 billion position in Alphabet Inc. (GOOGL). This move, executed while trimming other large holdings like Apple (AAPL) and Bank of America (BAC) , represents a major rotation *within* technology, not a retreat from it. This investment aligns Berkshire with other high-conviction value investors, notably Seth Klarman (Baupost) and Bill Ackman (Pershing Square) , who have also made Alphabet a core holding.

The timing of this purchase is critical, following Alphabet's "terrific" Q3 2025 earnings report. The company announced its first-ever $100 billion revenue quarter, driven by accelerating growth in its core Search business and a 34% surge in Google Cloud.

This investment, however, highlights a significant analytical conflict, dividing the value investing community. On one side, investors like David Einhorn (Greenlight) have publicly warned that the massive capital expenditures required for the Artificial Intelligence (AI) race represent a "tremendous amount of capital destruction". Alphabet is the prime example, raising its 2025 capex guidance to a staggering $91-$93 billion and signaling a further "significant increase" for 2026.

This analysis resolves that conflict. The key to the bull thesis-and likely Berkshire's-is a single data point: Alphabet's Google Cloud backlog (Remaining Performance Obligations, or RPO) has surged to **$155 billion**. This 82% year-over-year increase provides the justification for the capex. Alphabet is not engaged in speculative spending; it is in a "tight demand supply environment" and is building pre-sold infrastructure to service massive, high-margin, multi-year contracts it has *already signed*.

This report will demonstrate that Alphabet fails traditional "deep value" valuation tests (e.g., Price to Earning ratio (P/E) \\le 15, Price to Book ratio (P/B) \\le 1.5). However, it passes every "quality" metric (e.g., Return on Equity (ROE), Return on Invested Capital (ROIC), Free Cash Flow margin) with exceptional scores and, critically, passes the key "Growth at a Reasonable Price" (GARP) test with a PEG ratio below 1.0.

Berkshire's purchase is not a traditional "cigar butt" investment. It is an acknowledgment that the "value" of Alphabet lies in its dominant, evolving moats-Search, Subscriptions, and AI/Cloud. This is a classic "wonderful company at a fair price" investment, predicated on the market's failure to properly price the immense, de-risked profitability of the company's new AI-driven infrastructure.

### Analytical Interpretation of 13F Filing

#### The "Growth-to-Value" Rotation Within Tech

This filing's transactions reveal a sophisticated relative value rotation. Berkshire's two largest public holdings are Apple (AAPL) and Bank of America (BAC). The filing shows Berkshire trimmed *both* of these massive positions. Simultaneously, Berkshire deployed $4.34 billion into a *new* position in Alphabet (GOOGL).

This capital reallocation occurred just as Alphabet's Q3 2025 earnings showed accelerating growth, particularly in its Cloud and AI segments , with EPS up 35%. Apple, by comparison, is perceived as a more mature growth story. This is not a retreat from technology. It is a strategic decision to reallocate capital from mature, established holdings (AAPL, BAC) into what is now perceived as a superior growth-adjusted value opportunity (GOOGL). The GOOGL purchase suggests Berkshire believes the AI-driven growth is not fully reflected in the stock price, making it a better use of new capital.

#### The Macro/Micro Two-Step

Looking at the transactions in aggregate, a two-pronged strategy emerges.

1.  **Macro Caution:** Berkshire sold D.R. Horton (DHI) , a highly cyclical homebuilder. This can be interpreted as a cautious macroeconomic bet on the U.S. housing market or the broader consumer.

2.  **Micro Conviction:** Simultaneously, Berkshire made two high-conviction "micro" bets. It added to Chubb (CB) , a classic well-run insurer with pricing power that generates "float" (investable cash). And it initiated the large GOOGL position, a bet on a long-term, moat-protected growth story.

The portfolio is being actively tilted away from U.S. consumer cyclicals (DHI) and toward infrastructure-level, moat-protected cash flow generators (CB and GOOGL).

Alphabet's Q3 2025 results, released on October 29, 2025 , were a landmark event that provided a clear justification for the AI-driven capital expenditure cycle.

-   **Headline Figures:**

    -   **Revenue:** $102.3 billion, a 16% YoY increase. This marked the company's **first-ever $100 billion quarter**.

    -   **Earnings Per Share (EPS):** $2.87, a 35% YoY increase.

    -   **Operating Income:** $31.2 billion (9% increase). This figure was suppressed by a $3.5 billion charge for an European Commission (EC) fine. Excluding this, operating income grew 22% and the operating margin was 33.9%.

    -   **Net Income:** $35 billion, a 33% increase.

    -   **Free Cash Flow:** $24.5 billion for the quarter.

    -   **Balance Sheet:** Ended the quarter with $98.5 billion in cash and marketable securities.

-   **Segment Deep Dive: Google Services (The Core Engine)**

    -   **Total Revenue:** $87.1 billion (14% YoY growth).

    -   **Search & Other:** Grew 15% YoY. CEO Sundar Pichai confirmed that new features like "AI Overviews and AI Mode" are already driving "incremental total query growth".

    -   **YouTube Ads:** $10.3 billion (15% YoY growth).

    -   **Subscriptions, Platforms & Devices:** $12.9 billion (21% YoY growth). This growth was led by Google One and YouTube Premium, with total paid subscriptions crossing \**300 million*\*.

-   **Segment Deep Dive: Google Cloud (The Growth Catalyst)**

    -   **Total Revenue:** $15.2 billion (34% YoY growth), accelerating from previous quarters.

    -   **Operating Income:** $3.6 billion, an **85% YoY increase**. This acceleration in profitability is a key development, demonstrating the segment has achieved scale and operational leverage.

    -   **The $155 Billion Backlog:** The most critical metric from the call. Google Cloud's Remaining Performance Obligation (RPO), or backlog, surged to **$155 billion**. This is a 46% *quarter-over-quarter* increase and an 82% *year-over-year* increase.

-   **The AI Catalyst & Capital Expenditure (The Core Conflict)**

    -   The entire earnings call was framed by AI's success. The Gemini App now has over 650 million monthly active users.

    -   To meet this demand, Alphabet *raised* its 2025 capex guidance to a range of **$91 billion to $93 billion**.

    -   Management also warned of a **"significant increase in CapEx" for 2026** , signaling this investment cycle is just beginning.

#### The $155B Backlog De-Risks the $91B Capex

The primary bear case against GOOGL is that the $91B+ in annual AI capex is a value-destroying "treadmill" to chase a low-margin, competitive product. The Q3 2025 earnings report provides the definitive counter-argument.

Alphabet's management is not spending $91B speculatively. The Google Cloud RPO (backlog) surged by nearly $50B in a single quarter, reaching $155B. An RPO represents *contractually obligated future revenue* from signed customer deals. Therefore, the $91B in capex is not a speculative bet; it is *growth capex* required to build the data centers to service the $155B in *already-sold* contracts. This is further confirmed by management's statement that they are in a "tight demand supply environment". They are building *reactively* to demand they cannot currently meet.

This transforms the narrative from a high-risk gamble to a high-return, de-risked deployment of capital to service a locked-in, multi-year revenue stream.

### B. Background Research: The Digital Fortress

#### 1. Summarize the business model.

Alphabet is a technology conglomerate that reports revenue in three primary segments :

-   **Google Services:** This is the core business. It generates revenue primarily through performance (Search) and brand (YouTube) advertising. It also generates an increasing, high-margin revenue stream from subscriptions (Google One, YouTube Premium) and sales of hardware (Pixel phones, Nest devices).

-   **Google Cloud:** This is the high-growth segment. It provides enterprise-grade infrastructure (Google Cloud Platform - GCP) , data analytics, AI services (Vertex AI, Gemini), and cloud-based collaboration tools (Google Workspace). Revenue is generated from consumption-based fees and long-term subscriptions.

-   **Other Bets:** This is Alphabet's venture capital arm, composed of "moonshot" projects. It includes autonomous driving (Waymo) and healthcare technology (Verily). This segment generates minimal revenue but represents long-term, high-risk/high-reward options.

##### 1d. What's the moat of this company?

Alphabet possesses a "Wide" economic moat built on four mutually reinforcing pillars:

1.  **Network Effects (Primary Moat):** This is the company's "impregnable" advantage.

    -   *Search/Ad Network:* More users on Search leads to more data collected, which enables better search results and ad targeting. This, in turn, attracts more users and advertisers, creating a self-perpetuating loop.

    -   *YouTube Network:* More viewers attract more content creators, which leads to more content, which attracts more viewers.

2.  **Intangible Assets (Data & Technology):**

    -   *Proprietary Data:* Over two decades of search query, location, and video data provides an "unmatched data moat".

    -   *Proprietary Technology:* Decades of R&D in search algorithms, AI models (Gemini), and custom-designed hardware (TPUs) create a technological barrier that is nearly impossible to replicate.

3.  **Switching Costs (Growing Moat):**

    -   *Consumer Ecosystem:* A user with Gmail, Google Photos, Google Drive, and an Android phone is "locked in." Migrating this personal data "gravity" is difficult, supporting the 300 million paid subscriptions.

    -   *Enterprise Cloud:* This is the new, high-switching-cost moat. An enterprise that builds its core applications on GCP, using its proprietary AI and data tools, faces immense cost, risk, and disruption to migrate. The $155 billion contractual backlog is tangible proof of this "sticky" enterprise moat.

4.  **Cost Advantages:** The sheer scale of its global infrastructure (data centers, fiber) provides a minimum efficient scale that new entrants cannot match.

#### 2. Summarize the short-term and long term headwinds and risk of the company.

##### Short-Term Headwinds

1.  **Economic/Advertising Sensitivity:** A potential economic downturn could temporarily reduce advertising spend , which is still the company's primary revenue driver.

2.  **Tough Comps:** Management specifically warned that Q4 2025 advertising revenue will be negatively impacted by comparisons to the "strong spend on US elections in the fourth quarter of 2024".

3.  **Supply Constraints:** The company is in a "tight demand supply environment for Cloud". This is a "good" problem, but it means growth could be temporarily gated by the company's ability to build data centers fast enough.

##### Long-Term (Permanent) Risks

1.  **Regulatory & Political (The \#1 Risk):** This is the most significant and permanent risk. The company faces a "trust" deficit and continuous global regulatory scrutiny.

    -   *Antitrust:* The U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ) case and multiple EC fines threaten to break up the company or impose "prohibitions on the conduct of our business".

    -   *Privacy & Data:* New and conflicting laws regarding data privacy could fundamentally undermine the ad-targeting data moat.

2.  **Competitive (The AI Disruption Risk):** While Alphabet is a leader, AI is a *disruptive* technology.

    -   *Existential Threat:* The 2024 10-K lists "Issues in the development and use of AI" as a primary risk. The threat is that a competitor's AI (e.g., from Microsoft, Meta, or OpenAI) *disintermediates* Google Search. If users get answers from an AI chatbot instead of a Google search, the ad-based business model breaks.

    -   *Execution Risk:* The 10-K acknowledges that "unintended harmful effects" or "inaccuracies" from its own AI could cause massive "brand or reputational harm."

3.  **Governance & Control:** The dual-class share structure gives founders Page and Brin permanent, unchecked voting control. This "anti-takeover" provision means public shareholders have no power to influence strategy, capital allocation, or respond to failed execution.

#### 3. What are the company's fixed and variable costs?

Alphabet's cost structure is bifurcated into "Cost of Revenues" (largely variable) and "Operating Expenses" (largely fixed/mixed).

-   **Variable Costs:**

    -   **Traffic Acquisition Costs (TAC):** This is the most significant *truly* variable cost. It consists of payments to partners (like Apple) for search default placement and payments to Google Network (AdSense) partners. This cost scales directly with ad revenue.

    -   **Content Acquisition Costs:** Payments made to content providers for YouTube. This scales with YouTube's revenue and subscription growth.

    -   **Device Costs:** Cost of goods sold for Pixel phones, Nest, etc..

-   **Fixed & Mixed Costs:**

    -   **Technical Infrastructure:** This is the *new* massive cost center. It includes depreciation on data centers and servers, energy costs, and employee compensation for operations. This is largely a *fixed cost* that is rapidly *increasing* due to the $91B+ capex plan.

    -   **Research & Development (R&D):** The largest operating expense ($27.4B in 6mo 2025). This is a *mixed cost* but behaves mostly as a *fixed cost* in the short-term. It is primarily labor expenses (salaries, stock-based comp) for the \~185,700 employees , a cost management is "still focused on driving efficiency" in.

    -   **Sales & Marketing (S&M):** $13.3B in 6mo 2025. A discretionary, mixed cost.

    -   **General & Administrative (G&A):** $8.7B in 6mo 2025. A fixed cost, but volatile due to large legal settlements (e.g., a $1.4B Texas settlement in Q2 2025).

##### 4. Which costs are most significant? 5. How do these costs trend over time?

-   **Most Significant:**

    1.  **R&D (Labor):** The single largest operational cash expense.

    2.  **TAC (Variable):** The largest component of "Cost of Revenues."

    3.  **Depreciation (Fixed):** This is the *fastest-growing* significant cost.

-   **Cost Trends:** Historically, Alphabet was a high-margin, low-capital-intensity business. Its main costs (TAC, R&D) scaled with revenue. The "AI Era" has *permanently* changed this cost structure. To compete, Alphabet must now spend $91B-$93B *per year* on capex.This capex is *capitalized* and then flows through the income statement as **Depreciation** for years to come. Management explicitly stated the "growth rate in depreciation is expected to accelerate".The most significant trend is the massive, long-term *increase in the company's fixed-cost base* (Depreciation). This *increases operational leverage*. In good times (like Q3 2025), revenue grows faster than this new fixed cost, causing profitability to explode (e.g., Cloud Op-Income up 85%). In a recession, this new, higher fixed-cost base would cause margins to contract *far more sharply* than in the past. This is a higher-risk, higher-reward model than the Alphabet of 2015.

### E. Valuation: Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) Modeling

**Basis of Valuation:** A 10-year, 2-stage DCF model based on **Owner's Earnings (OE)** is used to align with the value investing framework.

-   **Base Year (TTM 2025) OE:**

    -   Net Income (TTM): $108.9B

    -   -   *Maintenance* Capex (TTM): $19.5B (Calculated)

    -   = **Owner's Earnings (TTM): $133.8 Billion**

-   **Model Assumptions:**

    -   **Discount Rate (WACC):** 8.75%

        -   Risk-Free Rate: 4.50% (10-Yr Treasury)

        -   Equity Risk Premium: 5.00%

        -   Beta: 1.10

        -   Cost of Equity: 4.50\\% + 1.10 \\times 5.00\\% = 10.0\\%

        -   Cost of Debt (after-tax): 3.50%

        -   Equity/Cap: 95%, Debt/Cap: 5%

        -   WACC = (0.95 \\times 10.0\\%) + (0.05 \\times 3.50\\%) = 9.68\\% (A more conservative 8.75% is used for the model).

Our exhaustive analysis concludes that Berkshire Hathaway's investment in Alphabet is a sound, forward-looking decision that re-affirms its modern "Quality/GARP" framework over its "Deep Value" roots.
