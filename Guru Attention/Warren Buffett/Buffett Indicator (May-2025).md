As of early-to-mid May 2025, the Buffett Indicator, which compares the total market capitalization of publicly traded U.S. stocks to the country's Gross Domestic Product (GDP), suggests that the U.S. stock market is still **overvalued**, although it has pulled back from recent peaks.<sup>1</sup>

Here's a breakdown of what the current readings tell us:

-   **Current Level:** The indicator is hovering around **180% to 190%**.<sup>2</sup> Some sources cite figures around 180%, while others using slightly different methodologies or data points show it closer to 188-197% or even 207.8% (using Q1 GDP and April market data).<sup>3</sup> Regardless of the exact figure within this range, it remains significantly above historical averages.

-   **Historical Context:** Historically, a reading between 75% and 90% has been considered a reasonable range for the Buffett Indicator.<sup>4</sup> Readings significantly above 100% have historically suggested overvaluation. The indicator reached much lower levels during major market bottoms, such as around 50% during the 2008 financial crisis and around 100% during the COVID-19 sell-off in 2020.<sup>5</sup>

-   **Interpretation:**

    -   A reading in the 180-190%+ range indicates that the total value of the stock market is considerably higher than the country's annual economic output.<sup>6</sup>

    -   This level is still considered **elevated** and suggests that the market is **overvalued** when viewed through this historical lens.

    -   While the indicator has come down from peaks seen in late 2021 or early 2022 (which were above 200%), it is still near historic highs.

-   **What this suggests:**

    -   The elevated level warrants **prudence** for investors. It implies that future returns from the stock market, based on this single indicator, may be lower than historical averages.

    -   Some interpretations suggest the recent pullback from higher levels could indicate a potential **buying opportunity** compared to recent peaks, but it is still far from levels historically associated with significant undervaluation.

    -   It's important to remember that the Buffett Indicator is a **long-term valuation tool** and not a precise short-term market timing tool.<sup>7</sup> The market can remain overvalued for extended periods.

-   **Contributing Factors:** The current elevated level can be attributed to factors such as strong corporate earnings, significant liquidity in the financial system (though potentially decreasing with higher interest rates), and investor sentiment. The recent slight decrease in the indicator is partly due to some market pullbacks and modest GDP growth.

-   **Buffett's Actions:** Notably, Berkshire Hathaway, led by Warren Buffett, has maintained a significant cash position (reportedly over $160 billion as of Q1 2025 and $334 billion at the end of 2024 according to some sources).<sup>8</sup> This large cash hoard is often interpreted as a sign that Buffett finds current market valuations unattractive for large-scale investments.

In summary, the current Buffett Indicator reading in May 2025 suggests that the U.S. stock market remains overvalued from a historical perspective, despite some recent moderation.<sup>9</sup> While not a definitive market timing signal, it serves as a cautionary indicator for long-term investors regarding potential future returns and the overall market risk level.
