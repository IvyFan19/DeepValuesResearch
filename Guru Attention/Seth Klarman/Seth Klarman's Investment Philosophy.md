# **Seth Klarman's Investment Philosophy**

Seth Klarman is a classic *value investor* who emphasizes **capital preservation**, patience and disciplined analysis. He was heavily influenced by Benjamin Graham (often called the father of value investing) and David Dodd. Klarman "closely follows the investment philosophy of Benjamin Graham," seeking deeply **undervalued, unpopular securities** with a margin of safety . In practice this means buying assets "at a significant discount to underlying economic value" so that his downside is protected . Klarman explicitly defines *risk* as the potential for permanent loss, not mere market volatility, echoing Graham's view that one must avoid loss in the first place . As he notes, **volatility can be a value investor's friend**, creating extreme mispricings and thereby opportunities . In sum, Klarman's key principles include:

-   **Margin of Safety:** Only invest when market price is well below intrinsic value. The "margin" acts as a buffer against errors or bad news . This was the centerpiece of his book *Margin of Safety* (1991), now a cult classic .

-   **Risk Aversion:** Prioritize avoiding loss over chasing gains. Klarman argues that "the way to get return is by avoiding and shunning risk" , in contrast to modern finance's focus on volatility. He maintains large cash reserves in lean times, viewing cash as an option to deploy into future bargains.

-   **Contrarian Value:** He targets **out-of-favor** or misunderstood assets. By patiently studying balance sheets and fundamentals, he aims to buy when others are fearful. Klarman has said that value investing is "in your blood" - it requires extraordinary patience and discipline . Like Warren Buffett, he often reminds investors to be "fearful when others are greedy, and greedy when others are fearful."

-   **Fundamental Analysis:** Klarman shuns indexation and market-timing; instead, he insists on deep research of each company's business and balance sheet. He warns against short-term "formulas" and psychology-driven decisions . At Baupost, he oversees a team of analysts who "dig deep" into ideas, seeking confidence that they know more than the market .

-   **Flexibility and Opportunism:** Baupost has an "open mandate," allowing Klarman to invest across asset classes, sectors, and geographies . He looks for catalysts (restructurings, spin-offs, M&A, etc.) that might unlock value. For example, he has famously bought distress in financial firms, spun-off biotech companies, or debt at steep discounts, provided the risk/reward is favorable.

In summary, Klarman's philosophy is a modern restatement of Graham-Dodd value investing: focus on **price**, not momentum; demand a margin of safety; be patient and skeptical of consensus; and always protect capital . These ideas also pervaded the updated edition of *Security Analysis* he edited in 2023, reinforcing timeless investment principles .

# **Career Overview and the Baupost Group**

After graduating Cornell (BA Econ, 1979) Klarman worked briefly for Mutual Shares (a small value fund run by Max Heine and Michael Price), where he honed his value mindset . He earned his MBA (1982, Harvard Business School, Baker Scholar) and immediately co‐founded the **Baupost Group** in 1982 with Professor Bill Poorvu and a few classmates. Poorvu seeded Baupost with \~$27 million in capital when Klarman was just 25 . (The name "Baupost" was originally an acronym from the founders' names .) Klarman took a modest salary ($35K) but was given full control over investments, proving his skill from the start .

Under Klarman's leadership Baupost grew rapidly. By the late 2000s it managed tens of billions of dollars; today its assets under management are roughly **$25-30 billion**. Over the first 26 years Baupost generated an astonishing \~20% annual return . Klarman's success has drawn comparisons to Buffett (he has been called the "Oracle of Boston" ) and in 2008 he was inducted into Institutional Investor's Hedge Fund Hall of Fame.

Baupost became known for non‑traditional, distressed, and special-situation investments. Klarman famously sat on large cash cushions (often 30-50%) waiting for crises to deploy (for example during 2008-09) . The firm opportunistically bought bonds of troubled companies (such as Washington Mutual, Sallie Mae, CIT Group) or equity spin-offs at deep discounts . In one notable example he bought a biotech spinoff (FacetBiotech) at \~$9 when it had \~$17 in net cash per share, later selling at a handsome profit when a takeover bid emerged . Throughout, risk management and fundamental analysis remained paramount.

Today Baupost is a major hedge fund managing global investments. It describes its strategy as **"opportunistic, value-oriented"** with an open mandate to invest in anything selling below intrinsic value . The firm looks for mispriced, complex or illiquid assets, from equities and bonds to real estate and derivatives, anywhere in the world. As one profile notes, Baupost seeks **"out of favor"** assets with good prospects, catalysts for value realization, or selling below reasonable expectations due to market inefficiencies . In practice, Klarman and his team deploy extensive fundamental research and a contrarian mindset to find these opportunities, always with an eye to capital preservation.
