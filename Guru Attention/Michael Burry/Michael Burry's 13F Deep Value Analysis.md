## I. Executive Summary: A Portfolio of High-Conviction Extremes

The Q3 2025 13F filing from Scion Asset Management, managing a portfolio with a reported notional value of $1.38 billion, marks a dramatic strategic pivot. The portfolio is not a diversified collection of equities but rather a highly concentrated, bifurcated structure built on two distinct and nearly opposing theses:

1.  **A Massive Macro-Short:** An approximately $1.1 billion directional bet *against* the Artificial Intelligence (AI) sector. This is comprised of large put option positions on Palantir ($PLTR) and Nvidia ($NVDA), representing a combined \\approx 80% of the portfolio's reported notional value.

2.  **An Idiosyncratic Long Book:** A small, highly selective basket of long positions-in both common stock, preferred stock, and call option form-focused on non-AI, contrarian sectors. These include financials, healthcare, and traditional energy.

This filing reveals a major portfolio reshuffling. Scion exited all option positions held in the previous quarter and liquidated numerous equity stakes. In their place, the fund established these massive new put positions alongside new call options and new equity holdings.

Significantly, this portfolio does not appear to be a hedge. Scion's Q1 2025 filing included a disclosure that its puts "may serve to hedge long positions". The Q3 2025 filing conspicuously contains no such qualifying statement. This absence signals a clear, high-conviction, and directional bearish bet against the AI-driven market.

## II. The $1.1 Billion "AI Bubble" Short: A Forensic Thesis

The dominant feature of Scion's portfolio is its massive bearish wager against AI. The fund opened substantial new put option positions against AI leaders Nvidia ($NVDA) and Palantir ($PLTR). These trades target two of the market's highest-flying stocks, which had seen extreme year-to-date gains of 54% and 174%, respectively, at the time of the filing.

This trade aligns directly with public warnings from Dr. Burry. He posted "sometimes, we see bubbles" on the social media platform X and, more pointedly, shared a graphic from a Bloomberg News story regarding "circular financing" concerns surrounding Nvidia and other AI-related firms.

This public "circular financing" post provides the analytical key to the short thesis. The bet is not merely that $NVDA is "expensive" on a traditional price-to-earnings basis; it appears to be a forensic argument questioning the *legitimacy and sustainability* of its reported revenues. This "circular" or "reflexive loop" is a classic hallmark of a market top, reminiscent of the "vendor financing" and "telecom capacity swaps" that masked underlying weakness before the 2000-2002 market crash.

The mechanism of this "circular" loop, as detailed in public reports, functions as follows:

1.  **Nvidia Invests in AI:** Nvidia announces a $100 billion investment in OpenAI.

2.  **AI Buys from Nvidia:** OpenAI, a primary recipient of this capital, uses the funds to place massive orders for Nvidia's high-margin chips. This spending is then booked as revenue by Nvidia.

3.  **Cloud Providers Buy from Nvidia:** Concurrently, cloud providers like Oracle spend tens of billions of dollars on Nvidia's chips to build out their AI infrastructure.

4.  **AI Buys from Cloud Providers:** OpenAI (through its partnership with Microsoft Azure) then becomes a major *customer* of this new cloud infrastructure, effectively "renting" the same chips Oracle just bought.

This loop can artificially inflate revenues, margins, and valuations for all participants, creating a "dangerous bubble" where reported demand is not purely organic but rather a function of interconnected capital flows. Scion's $1.1 billion short position is a direct bet that this entire structure is fragile, unsustainable, and poised for a sharp correction.

## III. Defensive & Cyclical Longs: The "Barbell" Call Options

As a counterbalance to the massive tech short, Scion established new bullish *call option* positions in Halliburton ($HAL) and Pfizer ($PFE). These positions are not random; they represent the ideological and economic opposite of the AI short, creating a sophisticated "barbell strategy."

-   **Halliburton ($HAL):** A $61.5 million call position on a traditional, cyclical energy services company. This is a leveraged bet on "real assets" and a potential inflationary economic environment.

-   **Pfizer ($PFE):** A $153 million call position on a defensive, "fallen angel" healthcare giant. This is a leveraged bet on a non-cyclical, deep-value cash flow stream.

This portfolio construction forms a "barbell" designed to profit from market dislocation.

-   **Side 1 (Short):** A massive bet on a *deflationary* event-the "AI bubble" bursting, likely leading to a broad, tech-led market correction.

-   **Side 2 (Long):** Leveraged bets (via calls) on *inflationary* assets (Energy/HAL) and defensive, deep-value stalwarts (Healthcare/PFE).

Scion is thus positioned to profit if the AI bubble bursts, but *also* profits if persistent inflation and "old economy" fundamentals re-assert their dominance. This asymmetric structure is designed to capitalize on market extremes, regardless of which direction the market breaks.

## IV. Forensic Analysis: Scion's New & Added Equity Positions

The remainder of this report provides a deep forensic analysis of the four long equity and equity-like positions identified in the Q3 2025 filing: SLM Corporation ($SLM), Molina Healthcare ($MOH), Lululemon Athletica ($LULU), and Bruker Corporation Preferred Stock ($BRKRP).

Each stock will be stress-tested against the 5-category, 20-point "Deep Value / Great Company" framework. However, a crucial analytical exception must be made for the Bruker position.

### Analyst Exception: The Bruker ($BRKRP) Special Situation

Before proceeding to the scorecards, the Bruker position must be analytically separated. Scion's filing shows a *liquidation* of its entire common stock position in Bruker ($BRKR). In its place, the fund purchased a new $13.1 million position in **$BRKRP**.

Research identifies $BRKRP as the "6.375% Mandatory Convertible Preferred Stock, Series A". This is not a "deep value" common stock investment; it is a "special situation" play. Scion has intentionally swapped its common equity risk for a fixed-income-like instrument. This preferred stock provides a high, secure 6.375% dividend and has a mandatory conversion feature, binding it to the company's future equity value.

This trade represents a capital structure arbitrage. Scion is de-risking its exposure to $BRKR's common stock volatility while capturing a high, fixed-income-like yield. Therefore, applying traditional value metrics (P/E, P/B, ROE) to this preferred stock is analytically invalid. This position is excluded from the 20-point "Great Company" analysis, as it represents a different asset class entirely.

The following analysis will focus on the three remaining common stock positions: $SLM, $MOH, and $LULU.

## VIII. Final Concluding Thesis: A Portfolio of Extremes

The Scion Asset Management Q3 2025 portfolio is a masterclass in high-conviction, asymmetric portfolio construction. It is a portfolio of extremes, bifurcated between one giant macro-short and a handful of uncorrelated, idiosyncratic longs.

The **short thesis** ($1.1 billion in puts on $NVDA and $PLTR) is not a simple valuation call. It is a sophisticated, forensic-accounting-driven bet that the AI revenue boom is artificially inflated by a "circular financing" loop. This thesis mirrors the mechanics of the 2000-2002 dot-com bust and represents a directional bet on a major market dislocation.

The **long book** acts as a curated "anti-AI" portfolio, diversified by thesis type:

-   **GARP / "Great Companies":** Molina Healthcare ($MOH) and Lululemon ($LULU) both **PASS** the rigorous "Great Company" framework. They are high-quality, wide-moat compounders (high ROE, high ROIC, safe balance sheets) that were acquired *after* their stock prices fell to "deep value" valuation levels (P/E &lt; 15, PEG &lt; 1).

-   **Deep Value / "Cigar Butt":** SLM Corp ($SLM) **FAILS** the "Great Company" framework. It is a high-risk, high-leverage "cigar butt" that is statistically cheap but fails catastrophically on balance-sheet safety metrics.

-   **Special Situation / Arbitrage:** The Bruker ($BRKRP) preferred stock purchase, paired with the sale of the $BRKR common stock, is a pure capital structure arbitrage play, not a traditional value investment.

This 13F filing demonstrates that Scion Asset Management is not operating as a simple, diversified "buy and hold" value fund. It is acting as a macro-timing specialist, using deep forensic analysis to place massive, asymmetric bets on market dislocations (the AI short) while simultaneously bottom-fishing for high-quality, non-correlated compounders ($MOH, $LULU) and high-risk special situations ($SLM).
