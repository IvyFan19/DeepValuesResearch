# Himalaya Capital's Q3 2025 Portfolio: A Masterclass in Disciplined Concentration and the "Barbell" Thesis

## I. Himalaya Capital Q3 2025 Portfolio Overview: The Power of Inactivity

An analysis of the Form 13F-HR filing submitted by Li Lu's Himalaya Capital Management LLC on November 12, 2025, for the quarterly period ending September 30, 2025, reveals a masterclass in disciplined, long-term, concentrated value investing. The filing, which discloses the firm's U.S.-listed public equity holdings, shows a portfolio that is both highly concentrated and, for this quarter, entirely static. This lack of trading activity is not a sign of passivity but rather the ultimate expression of the firm's core philosophy: to act as long-term business owners and swing hard only at the "fat pitches".

### A. Key Portfolio Metrics (as of September 30, 2025)

The Q3 2025 filing details a U.S. public equity portfolio with the following characteristics:

-   **13F Portfolio Value:** $3,230,264,839.

-   **Number of Holdings:** 9.

-   **Concentration:** 100% of the disclosed portfolio is concentrated in these nine holdings, with the top five positions accounting for 87.25% of the total value.

This extreme concentration is a hallmark of the investment principles of Charles Munger, a known mentor to Li Lu, and Warren Buffett, whose philosophy Himalaya explicitly embraces. It demonstrates a profound conviction in a select few ideas rather than broad diversification.

### C. Q3 2025 Change Analysis: Inactivity as an Active Strategy

The most significant finding from the Q3 2025 13F filing is the complete absence of trading activity. Himalaya Capital "has not disclosed any changes to the portfolio in the latest (2025 Q3) SEC report(s)". Filings confirm a 0.00% change in the share count for all nine holdings during the quarter.

This inactivity is a critical strategic decision when viewed through the lens of Li Lu's stated philosophy. Citing the baseball player Ted Williams, Li Lu's approach is to divide the "strike zone" of investments into "seventy-seven cells" and "insist on swinging only at balls in his 'best' cells". He notes that a common problem for investors is "swinging too often".

The third quarter's inactivity follows a period of significant portfolio maneuvering in the first half of 2025. Q2 2025 saw Himalaya initiate a massive new position in PDD Holdings, making it an 18%+ portfolio cornerstone. This purchase was partially funded by trimming the Bank of America stake by nearly 25%. Q1 2025 involved trims to both Alphabet (GOOG) and Bank of America (BAC).

Therefore, the "no-change" Q3 filing represents the "waiting" period. Li Lu has swung hard at his "fat pitches" (the new PDD position, the re-weighting of BAC) and is now exercising the discipline to let those theses compound, avoiding the temptation to "reach for the 'worst' spots".

### D. The "Iceberg" Context: AUM vs. 13F Disclosures

The most critical context for interpreting this report is the significant discrepancy between Himalaya's reported Assets Under Management (AUM) and its 13F portfolio value. The firm's AUM is listed as $14.99 billion. The 13F portfolio, however, accounts for only $3.23 billion of that total.

This implies that approximately $11.76 billion, or 78.5% of Himalaya's managed assets, is *not* captured in this 13F filing. Given that SEC 13F-HR forms only require the disclosure of U.S.-listed securities, and Himalaya's philosophy explicitly states a focus on "publicly traded companies in Asia and North America" , it is virtually certain that this undisclosed capital is invested in Asian markets (e.g., Hong Kong, mainland China).

This portfolio must not be mistaken for Li Lu's entire strategy. It is merely the U.S.-listed component. The holdings in PDD Holdings (a Chinese company listed in the U.S.) and East West Bancorp (a U.S.-China "bridge bank") are the visible "tips" of a much larger, undisclosed "iceberg" thesis on the US-China economic relationship and the Asian growth story.

## II. Core Holdings Analysis: The Buffett-Munger Framework Applied

The core of the Himalaya portfolio-the top five positions comprising 87.25% of the 13F value-represents Li Lu's highest-conviction ideas. Each of these is a "bet-the-farm" decision and merits a deep analysis based on the three pillars of the Buffett-Munger-Graham philosophy that Li Lu espouses: a substantial **Economic Moat**, "trust-worthy" **Management**, and **Long-Term Growth Potential**.

### A. Alphabet Inc. (GOOGL/GOOG): The Indispensable Digital Utility

*Holding:* 37.62% of portfolio (GOOGL: 19.14%, GOOG: 18.48%). This is, by a slight margin, Li Lu's largest conviction bet, held across both the voting (GOOGL) and non-voting (GOOG) share classes.

*1. Economic Moat (Wide)* Alphabet possesses one of the widest economic moats in the modern economy, rated as "Wide" by analysts. This moat is not a single attribute but a trinity of reinforcing competitive advantages:

-   **Network Effects:** The core of the moat is the "self-reinforcing cycle". Google's dominant 90%+ global search market share provides an "unmatched data moat." More user searches generate more data, which is used to refine the search algorithms, which delivers better results, which attracts more users, which in turn attracts more advertisers. This powerful network effect is replicated across its other properties. YouTube's value increases as more creators join the platform, which attracts more viewers, which attracts more advertising dollars. The Android operating system and its Google Play store create a similar flywheel, locking in billions of users and millions of developers.

-   **Intangible Assets (Data & Brand):** The "Google" brand is a globally recognized verb, synonymous with search itself. This brand strength is a formidable intangible asset. The data it collects from its billions of users is a proprietary asset that no competitor can easily or cheaply replicate.

-   **Evolving Moat (AI & Infrastructure):** The legacy data moat is now evolving. It serves as the fuel for Alphabet's new, dominant moat: Artificial Intelligence. Alphabet is executing a "full stack approach to AI". It possesses the massive, proprietary datasets (from Search, YouTube, Android), the cutting-edge models (Gemini), and, crucially, the "in-house chips" (TPUs) to train and run these models. This vertical integration means Google can deploy AI features like "AI Overviews" at a scale, speed, and cost that competitors reliant on third-party infrastructure cannot match. Li Lu is not just betting on digital advertising; he is betting on the dominant, vertically-integrated AI infrastructure company for the next decade.

**2. Management & Capital Allocation ("Trust-worthy People")** For a "trust-worthy" management, Himalaya is endorsing a dual-leadership team:

-   **Sundar Pichai (CEO):** Pichai provides the core engineering vision. His "calm leadership" and "transformational" style have steered the company through the "digital era" and into the "generative AI era". His track record is exceptional, having delivered a Total Shareholder Return (TSR) of 517% during his tenure. He is now leading the company's aggressive "pivot to AI".

-   **Ruth Porat (President & CIO):** Porat, who joined as CFO from Morgan Stanley in 2015 , is widely credited with instilling financial discipline. Her recent promotion (in 2023-24) to President and Chief Investment Officer is a major, Munger-esque catalyst. She now oversees all corporate investments, "Other Bets," and the venture arms (GV and CapG). This move signals a new era of *capital allocation* discipline, focused on rationalizing or monetizing the company's various moonshot projects, a long-held desire of value investors.

This management team demonstrates its shareholder alignment through a classic "growth and return" capital allocation strategy. They are investing *aggressively* to widen the AI moat, with capital expenditures projected to be as high as $91 billion to $93 billion for data centers and servers. Simultaneously, they are returning massive amounts of capital to shareholders through "substantial share buyback programs". This blend of aggressive reinvestment and large-scale returns is the hallmark of a mature, confident, and shareholder-aligned management.

**3. Long-Term Growth** Alphabet's growth potential remains vast, driven by three AI-infused pillars:

-   **Core Ads:** Google Search remains the dominant revenue driver. The "global rollout of AI Overviews" demonstrates its ability to integrate generative AI directly into its core product, defending its advertising franchise against the threat of standalone chatbots.

-   **Google Cloud:** This is the company's fastest-growing segment, "challenging AWS and Azure". After years of investment, the segment is now reliably profitable and continues to grow revenue at a "double-digit" pace, with AI-related services acting as a "key driver" of this growth.

-   **Subscriptions & Services:** This segment provides crucial revenue diversification. Led by strong growth in Google One and YouTube Premium, Alphabet has crossed 300 million paid subscriptions.

### B. PDD Holdings (PDD): The High-Growth, High-Risk Conundrum

**Holding:** 18.85% of portfolio. This is a new, massive position initiated in Q2 2025 , making it one of Li Lu's highest-conviction bets.

**1. Economic Moat (Narrow, but Evolving)** PDD's economic moat is rated as "narrow" and is best understood as two distinct, synergistic businesses:

-   **Pinduoduo (Domestic China):** This is the company's "main business" and the established "cash-flow engine." It is a social commerce platform in China that built its moat on a network effect derived from its unique "group buying" model. It generates substantial, high-margin revenue from "online marketing services" and "transaction services" within China.

-   **Temu (International):** This is the high-growth, international-facing business. It operates as a "loss leader" whose primary strategic goal is capturing global market share. Its model is built on "removing middlemen" and sourcing products "directly from manufacturers". As of 2025, Temu is *not* profitable; it is in a "blitz-scaling" phase, heavily "subsidized by PDD" and its domestic profits.

Li Lu's investment thesis appears to be a bet that the enormous profits from the Pinduoduo quasi-monopoly in China can be successfully redeployed to build a *new, global moat* with Temu. This new moat would be based on a ruthlessly efficient, direct-from-factory supply chain and, eventually, a global network effect.

**2. Management & Capital Allocation ("Trust-worthy People")** This holding presents the central contradiction in Li Lu's portfolio. The "trust-worthy people" principle, a cornerstone of his philosophy, is severely tested by PDD.

-   **The Red Flags:** PDD is notoriously opaque and secretive. Its corporate structure is a complex Variable Interest Entity (VIE) based in the Cayman Islands. Critically, its domestic Pinduoduo app was suspended from the Google Play Store in 2023 due to the discovery of "malware". This raises "serious doubts" about data privacy and security. Furthermore, analysts have pointed to "accounting red flags," such as capital expenditure growth that is "more than 10 times the regional and sector median".

-   **Management's "Confession":** In a bizarre and unconventional move, management has publicly made statements citing the team's "gradual aging and lack of certain capabilities," admitting they "miss macro opportunities". They have explicitly warned investors that "profitability will affect it as a result" of their new investments and that "high revenue growth is not sustainable".

Li Lu, one of the world's most sophisticated value investors, is not blind to these issues. His massive 18.85% position suggests a non-obvious, contrarian interpretation. This is not a bet on Western-style governance or transparency.

This investment must be interpreted as a bet on *ruthless competence* over *communication*. The management team, led by data scientists like Chairman and Co-CEO Lei Chen , is likely engaging in "strategic opacity." The public "confessions" of "aging" and "lack of capabilities" are a form of "sandbagging." They serve as a smokescreen to scare off short-term, momentum-driven investors. By publicly warning that profits will be "affected" , they give themselves the air cover to conduct a margin-crushing, multi-billion-dollar global expansion (Temu) in plain sight. Li Lu, acting as a long-term "business owner" , is signaling that he trusts their *execution* and *strategy* far more than he values their *transparency*.

**3. Long-Term Growth** The growth story *is* Temu. PDD is using the "funny money" from its highly profitable domestic arm to fund Temu's hyper-aggressive global land grab. The bet is that Temu can leverage its direct-from-factory supply chain to become a global e-commerce giant on par with Amazon or Shein. Having already expanded to over 90 markets by April 2025 , the growth potential is enormous, albeit fraught with regulatory and execution risk.

### C. Bank of America (BAC): The "Responsible Growth" Financial Monolith

**Holding:** 16.66% of portfolio. This is a long-standing core holding that was trimmed by approximately 25% in Q2 2025.

**1. Economic Moat (Wide)** Bank of America's moat is built on "too-big-to-fail" scale and deep regulatory entrenchment.

-   **Scale and Deposit Base:** The primary source of its moat is its massive, low-cost deposit base. As one of the largest financial institutions in the U.S. , it provides "unmatched convenience" by serving nearly 70 million consumer and small business clients. This scale provides a significant funding advantage over smaller competitors.

-   **Diversification:** The business is a well-diversified monolith. It operates across four major segments: Consumer Banking, Global Wealth & Investment Management (GWIM), Global Banking, and Global Markets. This diversification provides stability, as weakness in one segment (e.g., market-sensitive investment banking) can be offset by strength in others (e.g., stable consumer banking).

**2. Management & Capital Allocation ("Trust-worthy People")** In stark contrast to PDD, Bank of America's management is the *definition* of "trust-worthy" and "transparent."

-   **Moynihan's Doctrine:** The entire corporate strategy is encapsulated by CEO Brian Moynihan's doctrine of "Responsible Growth". This philosophy, which Moynihan reportedly repeats "at every internal meeting" , is built on four tenets:

    1.  Grow and win in the market ("no excuses").

    2.  Be client-focused.

    3.  Grow within the firm's risk framework.

    4.  Be sustainable (supporting teammates and communities).

-   This doctrine is a public-facing commitment to shareholders that the bank will not repeat the reckless, capital-destroying mistakes of the pre-financial-crisis era. Moynihan's leadership is credited with transforming the bank into a "simpler, more efficient company".

-   **Capital Allocation:** This "Responsible Growth" strategy translates directly into a clear, predictable, and shareholder-first capital return policy:

    -   **Dividends:** A regular, growing quarterly cash dividend, increased by 8% in July 2025 to $0.28 per share.

    -   **Buybacks:** A massive, $40 billion stock repurchase authorization, also announced in July 2025.

The trim of \~25% in Q2 2025 should not be misinterpreted as a vote of no confidence. It was a rational *source of funds*. In the *same quarter*, Li Lu initiated the massive PDD position. This is a classic, disciplined capital allocation move: trimming a "wonderful company at a fair price" (BAC) to fund what was perceived as a "wonderful company at a wonderful price" (PDD). The remaining 16.66% stake is still a massive, high-conviction bet on Moynihan's stable, compounding machine.

**3. Long-Term Growth** Growth at BAC will be steady and disciplined, not explosive. It will be driven by "winning in the marketplace" through operational excellence, "deepening client relationships" , and leveraging its leadership positions in wealth management (Merrill) and corporate and investment banking.

### D. Berkshire Hathaway (BRK.B): The Philosophical Anchor

**Holding:** 13.97% of portfolio.

**1. Economic Moat (Unique)** While some analysts have recently lowered their moat rating for Berkshire to "Narrow" due to its size , the Buffett-Munger-Li Lu perspective would almost certainly disagree. Berkshire's moat is unique and structural:

-   **The Insurance "Float":** This is the core moat. Berkshire's vast insurance operations (GEICO, Berkshire Hathaway Reinsurance, etc.) generate an enormous "float"-premiums collected upfront that will not be paid out in claims for months or even years. As of September 30, 2025, this float stood at approximately $176 billion. This float acts as a massive, "interest-free source of capital" that Berkshire's managers can invest for the shareholders' benefit.

-   **The Conglomerate Structure:** The conglomerate structure, often maligned, is a "huge and enduring" advantage for Berkshire. The company owns a diversified collection of wide-moat, cash-gushing operating businesses (e.g., BNSF Railroad, Berkshire Hathaway Energy). It can take the excess cash generated by these businesses and allocate it to *other* subsidiaries or investments that offer the highest prospective returns, all with extreme tax efficiency.

**2. Management & Capital Allocation ("Trust-worthy People")** A 14% holding in Berkshire Hathaway is a direct statement of philosophical alignment. Li Lu is, in effect, outsourcing 14% of his capital allocation to his mentors. This is the ultimate "trust-worthy people" bet.

With Charlie Munger deceased and Warren Buffett in his 90s, a long-term bet on Berkshire today is *not* a bet on Buffett. It is a bet on the *culture* he built and the *successors* he chose. Li Lu's unyielding position signals a high-conviction endorsement of **Greg Abel**, Buffett's designated successor as CEO. Abel, the former CEO of Berkshire Hathaway Energy , is lauded by Buffett as a "great manager" who has proven his ability to lead and allocate capital rationally within the Berkshire culture.

**3. Long-Term Growth** Berkshire's growth will be "lumpy" and slow, not linear. It will come from the internal compounding of its existing assets and the "patient capital" approach of deploying its record $381.7 billion cash hoard into the next "fat pitch" (e.g., a large-scale acquisition) when market dislocations provide an opportunity.

### E. East West Bancorp (EWBC): The Niche "Bridge" Bank

**Holding:** 9.15% of portfolio. This is another high-conviction bet, which Himalaya added to as recently as Q2 2023.

**1. Economic Moat (Niche)** EWBC's moat is not derived from its size, but from its *specialization*. It operates in a "niche financial market".

Its primary focus is on "the United States and China". It functions as the premier "bridge bank" for businesses and high-net-worth individuals who operate in both economies. This is a highly complex, specialized field that requires deep cultural, linguistic, and regulatory expertise in two vastly different systems. This specialization creates high switching costs for its clients and deters larger, unfocused competitors like Bank of America from attempting to dominate its niche.

**2. Management & Capital Allocation ("Trust-worthy People")** Li Lu's trust in EWBC's management can be inferred from the position's 9.15% size and its longevity in the portfolio. This investment is squarely within Li Lu's own "circle of competence." As a Chinese-born, U.S.-based investor , Li Lu understands the US-China economic corridor-and the unique risks and opportunities it presents-more intimately than almost any other institutional manager. He is uniquely positioned to vet the bank's client base, risk management, and leadership team.

**3. Long-Term Growth** The long-term growth thesis is a bet that despite political rhetoric about "de-coupling," the *economic* and *financial* ties between the United States and China will remain deep, complex, and profitable. EWBC provides the essential "picks and shovels" for this bi-lateral trade and investment flow. Its growth will be tied to the continued (if volatile) economic integration of the two superpowers.

## III. Analysis of Ancillary Holdings (The "Fat Pitch" Tail)

The four smaller positions in the portfolio, accounting for the remaining 12.75%, offer further context on Li Lu's disciplined, "fat pitch" investment style.

### A. Occidental Petroleum (OXY)

**Holding:** 2.15% of portfolio. This position was initiated in Q2 2024.

**Buffett-Style Analysis:** This holding is a clear "coat-tailing" investment. Berkshire Hathaway has a massive, well-publicized stake in OXY, including preferred stock and warrants, which it accumulated with a very bullish outlook on US energy. Li Lu's 2.15% position is an expression of alignment with his mentor's high-conviction bet. It is a bet on the long-term price of oil, the strategic value of US energy assets, and the specific, "trust-worthy" management of OXY's CEO, Vicki Hollub, whom Buffett has praised.

### B. Apple Inc. (AAPL)

**Holding:** 0.87% of portfolio.

**Buffett-Style Analysis:** Apple is a quintessential "wide moat" company, possessing arguably the most powerful consumer ecosystem and brand in history. However, Li Lu's position is *tiny* and has been *repeatedly trimmed*. Records show a -65.19% reduction in Q1 2025 and a -58.18% reduction in Q3 2024. This is clearly not a "buy" thesis; it is a "legacy" holding. The dwindling 0.87% stake suggests Li Lu views Apple as a "wonderful company at a *full* price." The aggressive trims have likely served as a "source of funds" to finance other ideas that (in his view) are more undervalued, such as Alphabet (with its more compelling AI growth story) and PDD (with its explosive e-commerce growth).

### C. Sable Offshore Corp. (SOC)

**Holding:** 0.73% of portfolio. This was a new buy in Q3 2024.

**Buffett-Style Analysis:** This is the most "Graham-like" holding in the portfolio. SOC is a micro-cap energy company, and this 0.73% position is a rounding error for a $15 billion fund. It does not fit the "wide moat" thesis of the core portfolio. Instead, it likely represents an opportunistic "special situation" or "cigar butt" style investment-a "fat pitch" that is simply too cheap to ignore, regardless of its size. This small holding is philosophically significant: it demonstrates that Li Lu, despite his fund's massive scale, remains a "pure" value hunter willing to swing at any pitch in his "sweet spot" , no matter how small.

## IV. Concluding Insights: The Himalaya Doctrine

The Q3 2025 filing, characterized by its complete lack of activity, provides a powerful, high-conviction snapshot of the "Himalaya Doctrine." This 9-stock portfolio is not a random assortment of U.S. equities; it is a carefully constructed, interlocking set of theses that reflects its manager's unique worldview.

### Insight 1: The "Barbell Strategy" of Moats

The portfolio is structured as a "barbell," balancing stability and risk:

-   **On one side of the barbell (approx. 68% of the 13F):** "Fortress America." This side consists of **Alphabet**, **Bank of America**, and **Berkshire Hathaway**. These are transparent, dominant, wide-moat, and shareholder-friendly U.S. institutions. They are managed by "trust-worthy" teams (Pichai/Porat, Moynihan, Abel) who are executing clear, rational capital allocation plans. This side provides bedrock stability and predictable compounding.

-   **On the other side of the barbell (approx. 19%):** "High-Growth China." This side is a single, massive bet on **PDD Holdings**. This holding is the ideological opposite: opaque, high-risk, hyper-growth, and with significant governance "red flags".

-   **The "Connector":** The "bar" connecting these two disparate ends is **East West Bancorp** (9.15%), the specialty bank that literally and figuratively "bridges" the two economies.

This structure allows Li Lu to anchor his portfolio in the stable, predictable compounders of the U.S. while taking a massive, concentrated, and asymmetric bet on the Chinese growth story he understands so intimately.

### Insight 2: The "Trust-worthy People" Thesis is Nuanced

This portfolio reveals a sophisticated, non-obvious interpretation of the "trust-worthy people" principle:

-   For the "Fortress America" holdings (BAC, BRK, GOOG), "trust-worthy" means what Buffett and Munger traditionally mean: transparent, candid, shareholder-aligned managers who are rational capital allocators.

-   For the "China" holding (PDD), it means something entirely different. It means a management team that is *ruthlessly competent* at executing a business strategy (led by data scientists ) and building a global giant, even if they are *intentionally opaque* and strategically "sandbag" to do so. Li Lu is making a clear, high-stakes bet on *competence* over *communication*.

### Insight 3: The Portfolio is a Reflection of the Man

Ultimately, this portfolio is a direct reflection of Li Lu's unique identity. He is a Chinese-born investor who became a protégé of Charlie Munger in the United States. His portfolio holds what he perceives as the "best of America" (Alphabet's AI dominance, Bank of America's stability, Berkshire's capital allocation culture) and the "best of China's growth" (PDD's e-commerce machine), connected by the "bridge" (EWBC) that understands both.

The Q3 2025 filing, in its profound *lack of activity*, is the ultimate statement of conviction. The major bets have been placed. The "fat pitches" -the Q2 2025 initiation of PDD-have been hit. Li Lu is now exercising the final and most difficult discipline of a true value investor: *patience*.
