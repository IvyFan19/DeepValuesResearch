The company's fundamentals are promising for a fintech, but Buffett would be wary of the lack of a stable long-term track record and the macroeconomic risks in Brazil. Klarman's bet on PAGS is likely a deep-value play: the stock is very cheap relative to its earnings power, albeit with higher risk. It **passes more criteria than one might expect**(unlike many fintechs, it's profitable with decent returns), but **fails the crucial tests of consistent track record and clear durable moat** that Buffett prioritizes.

**Summary:** In Buffett's framework, **Alphabet** stands out as a great business (wide moat, high margins and returns) but was not cheap, whereas **PagSeguro** and possibly **CRH/Amcor** were cheap but not high-quality enough. **Fiserv and Elevance** are in between - good businesses with solid cash flows, but each has certain weaknesses (Fiserv's debt load, Elevance's low margins). **Dollar General** has strong past returns but is currently struggling with thin margins. Klarman's Q2 moves suggest he was willing to compromise on Buffett's ideal "quality" metrics if the **price** was right (e.g., PAGS, DG), while also adding to truly high-quality names (Alphabet) when opportunities arose. Warren Buffett himself might not invest in some of these companies due to their **deviations from his strict criteria**, but Klarman appears to be balancing quality with valuation - paying low prices even if the company isn't perfect by Buffett's measures.

## **Discounted Cash Flow Analysis - Intrinsic Value under Three Scenarios**

We performed a **Discounted Cash Flow (DCF)** valuation for each of the above stocks to estimate intrinsic value in three scenarios: **Conservative**, **Nominal (base case)**, and **Optimistic**. For each scenario, we used **free cash flow (FCF)** growth rates based on the company's historical FCF trends (minimum, average, and maximum growth in the past decade). We project these growth rates over 10 years, then use a **terminal growth rate of 2%** (approx. long-term inflation) thereafter. We apply a **10% discount rate** (reflecting a desired annual return) to compute the present value of cash flows. The resulting **intrinsic value per share** is then compared to the current market price, and a **"buy price" (25% margin of safety)** is given as 75% of the intrinsic value. Key assumptions and step-by-step calculations for each company are detailed below:

-   **Fiserv (FI):** TTM free cash flow (2024) is about **$5.06 B** . Fiserv's FCF growth has ranged from \~5% (lowest positive, e.g. 2017-18 saw \~-0.3% slight decline) to \~15% (long-term average) to as high as \~34% (2024 growth) .

    -   *Conservative case:* **5% FCF growth**, reflecting Fiserv's slowest growth years (it had a couple of flat years around mid-2010s). We grow FCF at 5% for 10 years, then 2% terminally. The present value of 10-year cash flows plus terminal value gives an **intrinsic value ≈ $148 per share**. The stock recently trades around $130, so it's slightly below this intrinsic value. A 25% margin of safety would require a **buy price ≲ $111**.

    -   *Nominal case:* **15% growth** (approx. Fiserv's historical average FCF CAGR, boosted by acquisitions). This yields a much higher **intrinsic value \~ $307 per share**. Such growth might be achievable if synergies and fintech trends drive strong earnings. With a 25% MOS, the **buy target would be \~$230**. (Current price is far below $307, implying significant upside if Fiserv can sustain 15% growth.)

    -   *Optimistic case:* **34% growth**, using Fiserv's best year (2024 saw +34% FCF jump) . Sustaining this for a decade is unlikely, but as a theoretical upper bound: intrinsic value comes out astronomically high, **\~$1,184 per share**. This suggests extreme upside if Fiserv consistently compounded at fintech-like rates. A 25% MOS buy price would be **&lt;$888**, which is not relevant - Fiserv will almost certainly not grow 34% annually long-term. (This scenario highlights how sensitive DCF valuations are to growth; such high growth is not expected by the market.)
        > **Interpretation:** At \~$130, Fiserv is above the conservative-value range but well below the nominal-case value. This implies the market is pricing in modest growth (single digits). Klarman likely expects Fiserv to at least hit somewhere between the conservative and base scenarios. If Fiserv grows closer to the nominal case, it's undervalued; if growth stalls in mid-single digits, it's roughly fairly priced.

With 25% margin, **buy below \~$207**. Interestingly, \~$207 is around the current trading price, suggesting the market expects \~20% FCF growth going forward. If Alphabet indeed grows FCF \~20% annually (through cloud, YouTube, etc.), its intrinsic value is slightly above the current price - not a huge bargain, but reasonable.

        > **Interpretation:** Alphabet's current price (\~$200) is **well above the flat/no-growth value** - justifying its valuation requires substantial growth. Our DCF indicates it's roughly fairly valued if it can maintain \~20% growth. Klarman increased GOOG likely because he sees durable growth (maybe not 20% forever, but strong enough). The **margin of safety is thin here** - Alphabet is a high-quality compounder rather than a cigar-butt bargain.
