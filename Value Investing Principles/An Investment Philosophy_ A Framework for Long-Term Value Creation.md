### An Investment Philosophy: A Framework for Long-Term Value Creation

##### 1.0 Introduction: Our Guiding Philosophy

Our investment approach is founded on a clear and unwavering mission: the disciplined, long-term ownership of exceptional businesses. We do not engage in speculation, attempt to time the market, or rely on complex financial models that create an illusion of precision. Instead, this philosophy serves as the foundational framework for every investment decision we make, ensuring that capital is allocated rationally, patiently, and with a profound understanding of the assets we acquire.Our central creed is that **"Investing is simple, but not easy."** Success is not a function of possessing an exceptionally high IQ or mastering arcane formulas. Rather, it is the product of sound temperament and the discipline to avoid the common errors that ensnare most market participants—errors rooted in emotion, impatience, and speculation.This document outlines the core tenets of our philosophy—the specific, interconnected principles that guide our analysis, our actions, and our mindset.

##### 2.0 Core Tenets of Our Approach

Our philosophy is built upon a set of interconnected principles that govern how we identify opportunities, execute investments, and manage our mindset. These tenets are not a checklist but a holistic system designed to ensure a rational, repeatable, and resilient investment process that stands the test of time and market cycles.

###### 2.1 The Primacy of the Business

-   **Principle 1: Operate Strictly Within a "Circle of Competence."** We only invest in businesses we can fundamentally understand. The critical factor is not the size of this circle of competence, but the clarity of its boundaries; we must know what we don't know. If we cannot explain in a single, simple sentence how a company makes money, we will not invest.

-   **Principle 2: Distinguish Price from Intrinsic Value.** We recognize the fundamental difference between a stock's market price and a business's intrinsic worth, guided by the axiom: "Price is what you pay; value is what you get." A falling market price for a fundamentally sound and profitable company represents a growing opportunity, not a rising risk. Our attention is fixed on the company's earning power, not the daily quotes on a screen.

-   **Principle 3: Prioritize Wonderful Companies at Fair Prices.** It is far better to pay a fair price for a great business than a cheap price for a fair one. This is a foundational lesson in long-term compounding. Time is the friend of a wonderful company and the enemy of a mediocre one; therefore, our strategy is to partner with exceptional enterprises for the long haul.

###### 2.2 The Discipline of Execution

-   **Principle 4: Demand a Margin of Safety.** The margin of safety is the bedrock of our risk management. As one would build a bridge with a 30,000-pound capacity to handle a 10,000-pound truck, we purchase assets at a significant discount to our conservative estimate of their intrinsic value. This gap provides a crucial buffer against misjudgment, error, and the inevitable uncertainties of the future.

-   **Principle 5: Embrace Patience and Inactivity.** Excitement is the enemy of sound investment. Frequent trading erodes returns through transaction costs and taxes, while superior results often come from calculated inaction. We view the market as a baseball game with no called strikes; we can wait indefinitely for the perfect investment "pitch" that lies squarely in our sweet spot before swinging with conviction.

-   **Principle 6: Ignore Market and Macroeconomic Noise.** We deliberately disregard short-term market movements and macroeconomic forecasts, viewing them as distracting and unproductive noise. Time spent speculating on central bank policy is time taken away from analyzing a company's financial health and competitive position. A great business, purchased at the right price, will endure and prosper through multiple economic cycles.

###### 2.3 The Psychology of Investing

-   **Principle 7: Maintain Unwavering Emotional Stability.** Emotional control is far more critical than intellect in the investment arena. We are guided by a contrarian creed: "Be fearful when others are greedy, and greedy when others are fearful." This requires resisting the two primary emotional traps that destroy capital: buying at market peaks due to a fear of missing out and selling in troughs due to panic.

-   **Principle 8: The Ultimate Investment is in Oneself.** The most valuable asset anyone can possess is their own talent and knowledge. Continuous learning—whether in professional skills, communication, or personal health—is a non-depreciating asset that inflation cannot erode. This is the one investment that no one can ever take away from you.These principles of business analysis, disciplined execution, and psychological fortitude are not standalone rules; they are the building blocks of a coherent strategy for capital allocation and true risk management.

##### 3.0 Portfolio Construction and Risk Management

A sound philosophy must translate into a coherent strategy for capital allocation and risk control. Our principles directly shape the structure of our portfolio and our definition of what constitutes true investment risk.

###### 3.1 A Prudent Approach to Capital Allocation

-   **Focused Diversification** For investors who do not have the time or expertise to analyze individual businesses, broad diversification through index funds is a perfectly sensible strategy. However, for an active investor seeking to achieve superior returns, over-diversification is a "protection against ignorance." Our strategy is to make significant, concentrated investments in our very best ideas, following the logic of putting our eggs in a few baskets and watching those baskets very carefully.

-   **Absolute Avoidance of Leverage** We are unequivocal in our prohibition on the use of leverage or margin. Leverage magnifies both gains and losses, introducing the one risk that is entirely unacceptable: the permanent loss of capital. A minor market fluctuation, when combined with debt, can force a total and irreversible loss. As the mathematical certainty states, any number multiplied by zero is still zero.

###### 3.2 Redefining Investment Risk

In our framework, risk is not market volatility. A fluctuating stock price is not risk; it is an opportunity. The only risk that truly matters is the **permanent loss of capital** . These three principles—Margin of Safety, Circle of Competence, and Absolute Avoidance of Leverage—form a defensive triad, working in concert to protect capital from the only risk that matters: permanent impairment.Ultimately, this disciplined definition of risk is what anchors our philosophy and enables our unwavering commitment to the long-term.

##### 4.0 Conclusion: A Commitment to the Long-Term

Our investment framework is built on a foundation of logic, business discipline, and emotional fortitude, not on attempting to outsmart the market's daily whims. We focus on owning pieces of excellent businesses and holding them for long periods, allowing the power of compounding to work on our behalf.The principles outlined here are simple to understand but exceedingly difficult to execute, precisely because they run contrary to basic human nature. Our competitive advantage is not derived from complexity or a secret formula, but from the consistent avoidance of the value-destroying mistakes that ensnare most market participants.This unwavering commitment to our philosophy is how we create sustainable, durable, and meaningful value for our clients and stakeholders.
