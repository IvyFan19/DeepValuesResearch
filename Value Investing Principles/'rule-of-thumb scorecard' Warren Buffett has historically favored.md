Below is a concise "rule-of-thumb scorecard" that investors often use when they want to know whether a company is the sort of durable, high-quality franchise Warren Buffett has historically favored. Think of the numbers as **minimum hurdles, not ironclad laws**-Buffett will waive (or tighten) a metric if the qualitative story justifies it, but meeting most of them puts a firm well inside the "great-company" zone.

| **Area**                         | **Metric**                                   | **Buffett-style Threshold**                    | **Why It Matters**                                            |
|----------------------------------|----------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Profitability & Efficiency**   | Return on Equity (ROE)                       | ≥ 15 % **average for 10 yrs**                  | High, durable profitability shows a moat and able management. |
|                                  | Return on Invested/Total Capital (ROIC/ROTC) | ≥ 12 % 10-yr avg                               | Demonstrates the firm earns well above its cost of capital.   |
|                                  | Return on Assets (ROA)                       | ≥ 6 %                                          | Confirms efficient use of the balance-sheet.                  |
|                                  | Gross Margin                                 | ≳ 40 %                                         | Sign of pricing power and brand strength.                     |
|                                  | Net Profit Margin                            | ≳ 20 %                                         | Converts revenue into owner earnings at an elite clip.        |
|                                  | Depreciation ÷ Gross Profit                  | ≤ 10 %                                         | Buffett dislikes cap-ex-hungry businesses.                    |
| **Balance-Sheet Safety**         | Debt-to-Equity                               | &lt; 0.5                                       | Keeps financial risk and interest drag low.                   |
|                                  | Interest Coverage (EBIT ÷ Interest)          | ≥ 3×                                           | Ensures debt service is easily covered.                       |
|                                  | Interest Expense ÷ Operating Income          | ≤ 15 %                                         | "Great businesses don't need much debt."                      |
|                                  | Current Ratio                                | &gt; 1.5                                       | Liquidity cushion for shocks.                                 |
| **Growth & Predictability**      | EPS Record                                   | **Positive & rising every year for 10 yrs**    | Shows earnings are steady and forecastable.                   |
| **Cash Generation & Allocation** | Free-Cash-Flow (FCF)                         | Positive each year; **FCF ÷ Revenue &gt; 5 %** | Confirms profits translate into real cash.                    |
|                                  | Return on Retained Earnings                  | ≥ 12 %                                         | Management reinvests profits at high rates.                   |
| **Valuation Discipline**         | Price-to-Book                                | ≤ 1.5                                          | Simple yard-stick for "cheap vs. asset value."                |
|                                  | Price-to-Earnings                            | ≤ 15 (vs. firm's own history)                  | Limits over-paying for even great firms.                      |
|                                  | Margin of Safety                             | Pay ≥ 25 % below conservative intrinsic value  | Buffett's built-in risk buffer.                               |

### **Don't forget the**

### **qualitative**

###  **filters**

-   **Durable economic moat**-brand, network effects, cost edge, regulatory licenses, etc.

-   **Straight-shooting, rational managers** who allocate capital wisely.

-   **Understandable business model** you can forecast a decade out; Buffett famously won't touch what he can't grasp.

### **How to use the scorecard**

1.  **Screen** your universe for firms clearing most of the hurdles.

2.  **Dig into annual reports** (and Buffett's own letters) to verify consistency across cycles.

3.  **Value the short-list** yourself (DCF, owner's-earnings, EVA, etc.) and insist on the 25 % margin of safety before buying.

4.  **Monitor** the metrics annually; a great company remains great by compounding, not by flashing one good year.

Following this checklist won't guarantee Berkshire-level performance, but it puts you in the habit of insisting on the twin pillars of Buffett's style: **exceptional economics and a rational price.**

### **Where the "Buffett hurdles" come from**

| **Metric**                                            | **Typical Screen**                                                                  | **Primary Source(s)**                                                                                       | **Rationale**                                                                                                     |
|-------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Return on Equity (ROE)**                            | ≥ 15 % every year *and* ≥ 20 % 10-yr avg                                            | Buffett-style stock screens used by AAII and other researchers                                              | Shows durable pricing power and management discipline.                                                            |
| **Shareholder-equity (book-value) growth**            | ≥ 10 % CAGR over the last decade                                                    | Even Berkshire's own book value per share is judged on a \~10 % annual compounding yard-stick               | Signals that retained earnings are being reinvested at attractive rates.                                          |
| **Return on retained earnings**                       | ≥ 12 %                                                                              | Buffett's "$1 of retained earnings should create ≥ $1 of market value" dollar-test (1984--present letters). | Rewards CEOs who add value with each incremental dollar kept.                                                     |
| **Owner's Earnings ≡ FCF after "maintenance cap-ex"** | • Positive every year for 10 yrs • **Owner-earnings yield ≥ 10 %** ("Ten-Cap" rule) | Definition in the 1986 letter ; 10 % "Ten-Cap" yard-stick popularised by Buffett disciples (Phil Town)      | Converts accounting profit into real, distributable cash; 10 % yield gives a built-in margin above the long-bond. |
| **Valuation sanity-check**                            | **P/E ≤ 15** *and* **P/B ≤ 1.5** (or P/E × P/B ≤ 22.5)                              | The Benjamin Graham rule that Buffett still quotes for quick triage                                         | Keeps investors from paying fancy multiples, even for elite businesses.                                           |

#### **How the thresholds were pieced together**

1.  **Buffett's own words and letters** - The 1986 letter introduces "owner earnings," later letters benchmark book-value growth and stress high returns on *retained* capital.

2.  **Reverse-engineering his purchases** - Long-held positions (Coca-Cola, Moody's, Apple, etc.) all clear these bands over multi-year windows; academic and practitioner screens simply quantified what the holdings show in practice.

3.  **The AAII "Buffettology" and similar filters** - Independent back-tests settled on ROE 15 %-20 %, low leverage, and rising EPS as the sweet spot; those numbers are what survive robustness checks today.

4.  **Benjamin Graham legacy rules** - Buffett says he is "15 % Graham, 85 % Fisher," so he still respects Graham's 15× earnings / 1.5× book guard-rails when sanity-checking price.

### **Adding the missing pieces**

| **Area**                        | **Practical "Buffett bar"**                                                                                                                                                | **Quick test you can run**                                                                                                                                  |
|---------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Shareholder-equity momentum** | 10-year CAGR in **book value per share ≥ 10 %** *and* no single year of contraction                                                                                        | Pull 10 years of balance-sheet data; compute geometric mean of BVPS growth and check for any negative years.                                                |
| **Owner's-earnings power**      | 1️⃣ Compute owner earnings (OE) each year: Net Income + D&A - Maint. Cap-Ex - Δ Working Cap 2️⃣ **OE positive** every year 3️⃣ Purchase only if **OE ÷ Market Cap ≥ 10 %** | Most screeners list "free cash flow"; adjust cap-ex to maintenance level if possible. Then divide latest 12-mo OE by current market cap-the "Ten-Cap" test. |

*Passing both* tells you:

-   The company adds ≥ 10 cents of book value for every dollar you left in it - **retained earnings are compounding at double-digit rates**.

-   If you bought the whole business today you'd receive a ≥ 10 % pre-tax cash yield - **price is not overstretched**.

### **Putting it all together**

1.  **Start with quality**: require high, stable ROE/ROIC, double-digit equity growth, fortress balance sheet.

2.  **Confirm cash economics**: owner-earnings positive and translating into cash with minimal reinvestment needs.

3.  **Insist on a fair or better price**: classic Graham multiple limits *plus* a 10 % owner-earnings yield give you the margin of safety Buffett demands.

These are **guidelines, not gospel**-Buffett will occasionally bend them for an extraordinary moat (e.g., Apple trades well above 1.5 × book). But the closer a company hews to the grid above, the more it looks like the "wonderful businesses at fair prices" that have filled Berkshire's portfolio for six decades.
