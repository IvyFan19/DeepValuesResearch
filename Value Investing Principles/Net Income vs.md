### **Net Income vs. Owner's Earnings - What's the Difference?**

| **Aspect**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | **Net Income (a.k.a. "earnings," "bottom-line")**                                                                                           | **Owner's Earnings1**                                                                                                                                              |
|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **What it tries to measure**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | *Accounting* profit after all expenses (COGS, SG&A, interest, taxes, depreciation, etc.)                                                    | *True* cash available to owners after running the business and keeping it in good working order                                                                    |
| **Core formula**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | Revenues - Expenses (GAAP accrual basis)                                                                                                    | Net Income+ Non-cash charges (Depreciation, Amortization, etc.)± Changes in working capital- Maintenance Cap-Ex                                                    |
| **Why they diverge**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Includes many non-cash items and can be swayed by GAAP estimates (e.g., depreciation schedules, stock-based comp, impairments, tax quirks). | Strips out accrual noise and deducts only *necessary* capital spending, so it tracks the cash an owner could theoretically pull out without hurting the franchise. |
| **Investment use-case**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Good for P/E and trend analysis, but can overstate economic profit for cap-ex-heavy firms or understate it for highly depreciated assets.   | Buffett-style valuation, discounted cash-flow models, assessment of dividend/buy-back capacity.                                                                    |
| 1 In practice analysts often approximate Owner's Earnings with Free Cash Flow (FCF = Cash From Operations - Total Cap-Ex). Buffett would ideally replace *Total* Cap-Ex with *Maintenance* Cap-Ex, but maintenance data isn't disclosed, so FCF is the closest public proxy. |                                                                                                                                             |                                                                                                                                                                    |

### **Concrete Examples (latest completed fiscal year)**

| **Company (FY)**    | **Net Income** | **Free Cash Flow ≈ Owner's Earnings** | **Gap ($ B)** | **Key Driver of the Gap**                                                                                                         |
|---------------------|----------------|---------------------------------------|---------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Apple (FY 2024)     | **$93.7 B**    | **$108.8 B**                          | **+15.1**     | Large non-cash depreciation on already-paid-for fixed assets and relatively modest cap-ex keep cash flow above accounting profit. |
| Microsoft (FY 2025) | **$101.8 B**   | **$71.6 B**                           | **-30.2**     | Heavy cloud/datacenter build-out (cap-ex) plus sizeable working-capital swing for AI hardware purchases.                          |
| Amazon (FY 2024)    | **$59.2 B**    | **$38.2 B**                           | **-21.0**     | Enormous fulfillment- and AWS-related cap-ex drags owner earnings well below net income.                                          |
| Alphabet (FY 2024)  | **$100.1 B**   | **$72.8 B**                           | **-27.3**     | AI-driven data-center cap-ex and stock-based comp add-backs partly offset each other, but cap-ex still dominates.                 |
| NVIDIA (FY 2025)    | **$72.9 B**    | **$60.9 B**                           | **-12.0**     | Massive working-capital build (inventory for Blackwell GPUs) and cap-ex for new fabs temper explosive GAAP profit.                |

**Take-aways**

1.  **Direction of the gap matters more than size.
    > ***Positive* gaps (Apple) indicate GAAP is conservative; *negative* gaps (Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet, NVIDIA) warn that reported earnings over-state true cash a shareholder can pocket.

2.  **Cap-ex intensity drives most differences today.
    > **The AI-infrastructure race is pushing even historically "asset-light" firms (GOOG, AMZN, META) into cap-ex-heavy territory, shrinking owner's earnings relative to net income.

3.  **Owner's Earnings is the better yard-stick for valuation.
    > **When you discount future cash flows or gauge dividend/buy-back capacity, start with owner's earnings, not net income, especially for companies in high-investment phases.

#### **Quick mental checklist for estimating Owner's Earnings from a 10-K/10-Q**

Cash from Operations

- Maintenance Cap-Ex (if unavailable, use total Cap-Ex as conservative proxy)

= Free Cash Flow ≈ Owner's Earnings

Then sanity-check by:

-   Adding back one-time restructuring charges if truly non-recurring

-   Adjusting for large acquisitions financed in cash

-   Normalizing cyclic working-capital swings (e.g., holiday inventories for retailers)

Use this cash-based lens and you'll see why investors like Warren Buffett often ignore headline EPS and head straight to the cash-flow statement.
